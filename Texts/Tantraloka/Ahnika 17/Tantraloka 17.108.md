## Mūla

शिवात्मत्वेन यत्सेयं शुद्धता मानसादिके ।
नियमो रञ्जनं कर्तृभावः कलनया सह ॥ ^mula

śivātmatvena yatseyaṃ śuddhatā mānasādike |
niyamo rañjanaṃ kartṛbhāvaḥ kalanayā saha || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 08 - Chapter Seventeen#^pg206|p. 186]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=206>))
>
> The pure state within the mind (manas) and the rest (up to Nature is realized by contemplating) the intention (of the mind), the ascertainment (of the intellect), and the self-arrogation (of the ego), as well as the light (of sattva), the attachment (of rajas), and the abiding state (of tamas), as being Śiva. (with 17.107cd–108ab)
>
> The six coverings (kañcuka) (that envelop the individual soul) are 1) the restriction (imposed by Karma and causality) (niyama), 2) attachment (rañjana), 3) the sense of being an agent (kartrbhāva), along with 4) the passage of time (kalanā), 5) the awareness (of finite sensations) (vedana), and 6) sleeping, as it were, in the presence of that aspect of (finite) reality that should be abandoned. In this way, these too are pure for the one who established in a state of oneness with Śiva. The individual soul who has attained Śiva’s state (śivabhāva) is purified. (Finally,) there is no impurity of any kind in the (pure) principles of the Lords of Knowledge (vidyeśa) and the rest. Thus, by the emanation of the pure principles in this way, the disciple is also of that nature (fanmaya). This (teaching) is alluded to in the venerable Mālinīvijayottara. (with 17.108cd–111)
