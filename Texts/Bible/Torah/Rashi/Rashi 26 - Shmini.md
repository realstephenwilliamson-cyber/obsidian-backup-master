# Rashi 26 - Shmini

← [[Rashi 25 - Tzav]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 27 - Tazria]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-1|Leviticus 9:1–11:47]]* · Text: [[Torah 26 - Shmini]]

## Chapter 9

### 9:1

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-1|Leviticus 9:1]]

**It was on the eighth day —** of the installation. That [was] Rosh Chodesh Nison, that very day on which the Tabernacle was set up, and it [= that day] took [the] ten crowns which are taught in Seder Olom.

**and to the elders of Israel** to announce to them that [it was] by Divine command [that] Aharon entered [the Tabernacle] and served in the high priesthood, that they not say that he had entered into it on his own authority.

### 9:2

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-2|Leviticus 9:2]]

**Take, for yourself, a young calf,** to let [Aharon] know that God forgave him by means of this calf for the deed of the [Golden] Calf which he had made.

### 9:4

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-4|Leviticus 9:4]]

**for today Adonoy will appear to you —** to rest His *Shechinoh* on the work of your hands; that is why these sacrifices are obligatory for this day.

### 9:7

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-7|Leviticus 9:7]]

**Approach the altar.** [It was because] Aharon was ashamed and afraid to approach. Moshe said to him: Why are you ashamed? [It was] for this that you were chosen!

**Your sin-offering,** a young calf.

**And your burnt-offering,** the ram.

**The people’s offering,** the kid of goats, young calf and lamb. Wherever it states “ עֵגֶל “ (a calf), [it refers] to a yearling, and from here you learn [this rule].

### 9:11

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-11|Leviticus 9:11]]

**The flesh and the skin, etc.** We do not find an “outer” sin-offering that is burned except this [one] and that of the installation, and all of them by Divine command.

### 9:12

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-12|Leviticus 9:12]]

**[Aharon’s sons] presented,** an expression [denoting] “handing over” and “preparation.”

### 9:15

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-15|Leviticus 9:15]]

**And offered it a sin-offering,** [that is, וַיְּחַטְּאֵהוּ means:] He treated it according to the rule of a sin-offering.

**As the first one,** as his [own] calf.

### 9:16

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-16|Leviticus 9:16]]

**He did it according to the rule,** [the rule] explained in [regard to] a freewill burnt-offering in Vayikro 1.

### 9:17

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-17|Leviticus 9:17]]

**He filled his palm from it.** This is the קְמִיצָה [= separating the fistful].

**Aside from the burnt-offering.** All these he did after the daily burnt-offering.

### 9:19

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-19|Leviticus 9:19]]

**The covering,** [that is,] the fat covering the innards.

### 9:20

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-20|Leviticus 9:20]]

**They put the fats on top of the breasts,** after the “waving” the *kohein* [who did] the waving gave them to another *kohein* to burn them; [thus that which] was above was [now] below.

### 9:22

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-22|Leviticus 9:22]]

**And he blessed them** [with the blessing of] *Birkas Kohanim* — May [He] bless you, May [He] cause [His Face] to shine, May [He] lift up.

**He descended** from the altar.

### 9:23

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-23|Leviticus 9:23]]

**Moshe and Aharon went [in]to, etc.** Why did they enter? I found in the section of Millu’im, in the added בָּרַיְיתָא to our Toras Kohanim: Why did Moshe enter with Aharon? To teach him the ritual of the incense, or perhaps he entered for some other purpose? I can argue [as follows]: Descending and entering require a blessing, just as descending is similar to the [Temple] service, so entering is similar to the [Temple] service. Therefore you have learnt: Why did Moshe enter with Aharon? To teach him the ritual of the incense. Another interpretation: When Aharon saw that they offered all the sacrifices, and all the rituals had been performed, and [still] the *Shechinoh* did not descend for Israel, he was pained, and thought, “I know that God is angry with me and [it is] because of me that the Shechinoh did not descend for Israel”! He said to Moshe: “My brother Moshe, is this [what] you did to me, that I entered and was shamed”? Moshe immediately entered with him, and they begged for mercy, and the *Shechinoh* descended for Israel.

**They went outside and blessed the people.** They said: “’May the pleasantness of Adonoy our God be upon us.’ May it be [His] Will that the *Shechinoh* should rest on the work of your hands.” Because all seven days of the installation [during which time] Moshe set up the Tabernacle and served in it [as a *kohein*], and took it down every day — the *Shechinoh* did not rest on it, and Israel were ashamed, saying to Moshe: “Our master Moshe! All the trouble we went to [in order] that the *Shechinoh* should rest among us, and that we should know that we have achieved atonement for the sin of the [Golden] Calf. Therefore he said to them: “This is what Adonoy commanded you to do and the glory of Adonoy will appear to you. My brother Aharon is worthy and [even] more important that I, for by means of his sacrifices and his service the *Shechinoh* will rest among you, and you will know that the Omnipresent chose him.

### 9:24

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-24|Leviticus 9:24]]

**They raised [their voices],** according to its Targum.

## Chapter 10

### 10:2

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-2|Leviticus 10:2]]

**A fire came forth.** Rabbi Eliezer says: The sons of Aharon died only because they rendered halachic decisions in the presence of their master Moshe. Rabbi Yishmo’el says: Intoxicated with wine they entered the Sanctuary. You should know [that this is true], for after their death He warned the survivors not to enter the Sanctuary [while] intoxicated with wine. [This] may be compared to a king who had a [faithful] member of his household, etc. as it is [explained] in Vayikro Rabbo.

### 10:3

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-3|Leviticus 10:3]]

**It is [as Adonoy spoke], etc.** Where did He speak? “It is there that I will meet with Bnei Yisrael, and it [the Tabernacle] will be sanctified with My Glory.” Do not read: “with My Glory” (בִכְבוֹדִי), but [rather] “with My honored ones” (בִּמְכוּבָּדַי). Moshe Said to Aharon: “My brother Aharon, I had known that the Temple would be sanctified with those singled out as special by the Omnipresent, and I thought [this must mean] either through me or through you; now I see that they are greater than I or you.”

**and Aharon was silent.** He received a reward for his silence; what reward did he receive? The Divine Word was specially [directed] to him, for to him alone was said the *paroshoh* regarding those intoxicated by wine.

**Through those that are near Me,** My chosen ones.

**And in the presence of the entire people I will be glorified.** When the Holy One, blessed is He, passes judgement on the righteous, He is feared and exalted and praised. [People say:] “If so with these, how much more so with the wicked”! And so too it states: “You are awesome, O God, from Your Sanctuaries”! Do not read “from Your Sanctuaries” (מִמִּקְדָּשֶׁיךָ), but [rather] “from Your sanctified ones” (מִמְּקוּדָּשֶׁיךָ).

### 10:4

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-4|Leviticus 10:4]]

**Aharon’s uncle.** Uzziel was the brother of Amram, as it is said, “And the sons of Kehos, etc.”

**Carry your brothers, etc.** As a man who says to another, “Take away the dead [body] from before the bride so as not to disturb the celebration.”

### 10:5

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-5|Leviticus 10:5]]

**In their tunics,** of the dead men. This teaches that their clothes were not burnt, but [only] their souls; something like two threads of fire entered into their nostrils.

### 10:6

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-6|Leviticus 10:6]]

**Do not let [your hair] grow long.** Do not let [your] hair grow; from here [we learn] that a mourner is forbidden [to take] a haircut, but [as for] *you*, do not disturb the Omnipresent’s celebration.

**So that you will not die.** Therefore, if you do so, you will die.

**Your brothers, the entire House of Yisroel.** From here [we learn] that the troubles of Torah Scholars must be mourned by all.

### 10:9

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-9|Leviticus 10:9]]

**Wine or any other intoxicant.** Wine in a manner which intoxicates.

**When you enter the Tent of Meeting.** I [know] only when you enter the Temple [proper], when you approach the [outer] altar — from whence [do I know this]? “Entering the Tent of Meeting” is said here, and it is said regarding the sanctification of the hands and feet [before] entering the Tent of Meeting. Just as there [Scripture] made approaching the [outer] altar equivalent to entering the Tent of Meeting, so here it made approaching the altar equivalent to entering the Tent of Meeting.

### 10:10

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-10|Leviticus 10:10]]

**To distinguish,** in order that you should distinguish between a proper service and [one which is] improper. Thus you learn that if he performed [the Temple] service his service is invalid.

### 10:11

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-11|Leviticus 10:11]]

**And to teach.** This teaches that one who is intoxicated is forbidden to issue [halachic decisions]. Perhaps [such a person] is worthy of death? The verse says: “you and your sons with you. . . and you will not die.” *Kohanim* in their [improper] service [are worthy of] death, but the Sages in their halachic decisions are not [worthy of] death.

### 10:12

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-12|Leviticus 10:12]]

**The surviving,** from death. This teaches that death had been decreed on them too for the sin of [Aharon’s making] the [Golden] Calf, as it is said, “And God was exceedingly angry with Aharon to destroy him,” and “destroying” refers only to the death of children, as it is said, “And I will destroy his fruit above.” But Moshe’s prayer cancelled half [the decree], as it is said, “And I also prayed on behalf of Aharon at that time.

**Take the meal-offering.** Even though you are אוֹנֵן and holy things are forbidden to an אוֹנֵן .

**The meal-offering.** This is the meal-offering of the eighth [day of the installation] and the meal-offering of Nachshon.

**And eat it [as] matzos.** What does this verse teach [us]? Because it is a communal meal-offering and a meal-offering [prescribed only] for [that] time, and there is no similar one for [future] generations, it was necessary to set forth the rule of other meal-offerings.

### 10:13

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-13|Leviticus 10:13]]

**The portion of your sons,** daughters have no portion in holy things.

**For so have I been commanded.** That they should eat it while they are אוֹנְנִים .

### 10:14

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-14|Leviticus 10:14]]

**The breast of the wave-offering** of the communal peace-offerings,

**You shall eat in a pure (undefiled) place.** Did they then eat the first ones in an impure place? Rather, the first ones, which are most holy things, had to be eaten in a holy place, but these did not have to be eaten “within the hangings,” though they had to be eaten within the camp of Israel, which was pure and thus מְצוֹרָעִים were forbidden to enter there. From here [we learn] that offerings of lesser sanctity (קָדְשֵׁי קָדָשִׁים) may be eaten within the entire city [of Jerusalem].

**You and your sons and daughters.** You and your sons [are entitled] to a portion, but your daughters are not [entitled] to a portion. However, if you give them gifts, they are permitted to eat of the breast and shoulder. Or [perhaps] even the daughters [are entitled] to a portion? The verse says: “For [as] your portion and the portion of your sons it is given” — there is a portion for the sons but there is no portion for the daughters.

### 10:15

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-15|Leviticus 10:15]]

**On top of the fats of the fire offering.** From here [we learn] that the fats are below at the time of waving, and [we thus learn] the reconciliation of the verses that they not contradict each other — I have already explained the three of them in [the Sidra of] צַו אֶת אַהֲרן .

### 10:16

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-16|Leviticus 10:16]]

**The goat of the sin-offering.** The goat of the additional sacrifices for Rosh Chodesh. They offered three goats [as] sin-offerings on that day, the he-goat [of the installation], the goat [offered by] Nachshon, and the goat [offered as a sin-offering] on Rosh Chodesh, and of all of them, only this one was burnt. The Sages of Israel are in disagreement about this; some say it is was burnt because of impurity which touched it, and some say it was because of the state of אֲנִינוּת, because it is of the holy offerings ordained for all generations, but regarding holy offerings for the time [of installation] they relied on Moshe, who told them regarding the meal-offering: “Eat it [as] matzoh.”

**Made a detailed inquiry.** Two inquiries: Why was this one burnt? And why were these eaten? That is the version in Toras Kohanim.

**With Elozor and Isomor,** out of respect for Aharon’s honor he turned his face toward the sons to show his anger.

**Saying.** He said to them: Answer my questions!

### 10:17

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-17|Leviticus 10:17]]

**Why did you not eat the sin-offering in a sacred place?** Did they then eat it outside the Holy [Place]? Did they not burn it? What [then did he mean by] saying: “in a sacred place”? Rather, he said to them: “Perhaps it went out “beyond the curtains” and became invalid”?

**For it is holy of holies,** and becomes invalid by being taken [outside]. They said to him, “No.” He [then] said to them: “Since it was in a sacred place, why did you not eat it”?

**And He gave it to you, to bear [the iniquity of the congregation].** For the *kohanim* eat [the sacrificial meat] and the owners find atonement [thereby].

**To bear the iniquity of the congregation.** From here we learn that it was a goat for [the musaf of] Rosh Chodesh, which atones for the sin of [causing] impurity [to] the Tabernacle and its sacred things. [This is the only possibility,] since the sin-offering of the eighth [day] and the sin-offering [brought by] Nachshon did not come for [the purpose of] atonement.

### 10:18

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-18|Leviticus 10:18]]

**Behold, its blood was not brought, etc.** For if it had been brought, you would have had to burn it, as it is said, “And all sin-offerings whose blood is brought,” etc.

**You should certainly have eaten of it.** You should have eaten it, even though you are אוֹנְנִים .

**As I have commanded** you regarding the meal-offering.

### 10:19

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-19|Leviticus 10:19]]

**Aharon spoke.** The term “speaking” is an expression of harshness, as it is said, “The people spoke [against Moshe].” Could it be that Moshe was angered at Elozor and Isomor and Aharon spoke? Thus you know that this was only by way of respect. They thought: It is not proper that our father should sit [silent] [while] we speak in his presence, and it is not proper that a disciple respond to his teacher. Perhaps [they were silent] because Elozor did not have [the ability] to respond? The verse says: “Elozor the *kohein* said to the soldiers,” etc. Behold, when he wished [to], he spoke in the presence of Moshe and in the presence of the princes [of the tribes]. This I found in the Sifrei of Another Character.

**Behold, today they brought.** What did he [mean] to say? Rather, Moshe said to him: “Perhaps you sprinkled its blood as אוֹנְנִים, and an אוֹנֵן who performs the service profanes it”? Aharon said to him: “And did they, who are ordinary people, bring [it]? I brought [it], for I am a *Kohein Godol*, and may bring [a sacrifice while being] an אוֹנֵן .”

**And then such [tragedy] befell me.** Even if the dead were not my sons, but other relatives for whom I am obligated to be an אוֹנֵן as [I am] for these, as for example, all [those] listed in the *paroshoh* of *kohanim*, for whose sake a *kohein* must [allow himself] to become [ritually] unclean.

**[If] I had eaten the sin-offering.** And if I had eaten, Would that have been good [in the eyes of Adonoy]?

**Today,** but the אֲנִינוּת of nighttime is permitted, for [the laws applying to] an אוֹנֵן [relate] only to the day of the burial.

**Would that have been good in the eyes of Adonoy?** If you heard [this law] regarding holy offerings of a specific time, you should not have taken a lenient view regarding holy offerings that are obligatory on all future generation

### 10:20

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-20|Leviticus 10:20]]

**It was good in his eyes.** He admitted [the justice of Aharon’s argument], and was not ashamed to say: “I had not heard [this].”

## Chapter 11

### 11:1

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-1|Leviticus 11:1]]

**To Moshe and to Aharon.** He told Moshe to tell Aharon.

**Saying to them.** He [= Moshe] said that he [= Aharon] should say to Elozor and to Isomor. Or is it that [he was] to tell Israel? When [the verse] says: “Speak to Bnei Yisroel,” that is the Divine Commandment to Israel; How then do I interpret [the phrase] “to say to them”? To the sons [of Aharon], Elozor and Isomor.

### 11:2

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-2|Leviticus 11:2]]

**Speak to Bnei Yisroel.** He equated all of them to be messengers for this Divine Commandment, because all of them were alike in their silence, and accepted God’s decree with love.

**These are the living things.** An expression of “life.” Because Israel is bound to God and worthy of being alive, therefore He separated them from [ritual] uncleanness and decreed *mitzvos* upon them, but did not forbid anything to the nations. [This is] a parable of a physician who came in to visit a sick person, as is set out in the Midrash of Rabbi Tanchuma.

**These are the living things.** This teaches that Moshe would hold the animal [concerned] and show it to Israel: This you may eat and this you may not eat! “This you may eat,” etc. — He also held each species of swarming water creatures and showed them — and so too of fowl: And these you shall consider repulsive from the fowl. And so regarding swarming things: And these shall be unclean to you.

**These are the living things. . .all the animals.** This teaches that animals are within the category of “living things.”

### 11:3

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-3|Leviticus 11:3]]

**All that have cloven hooves.** According to its Targum: “split.”

**hoof.** *Plante* in Old French.

**Completely split.** Which is separated above and below into two nails, according to its Targum: “which has nails” [plural]. For there are [animals] whose hooves are split above but not entirely cloven and separated, for they are joined below.

**Which chews its cud.** Brings up and vomits the food from its intestines and returns it to its mouth to crush and grind it finely.

**Its cud.** Such is its name; it is possible that it is from the root “and like water drawn along,” for it is drawn towards the mouth. Its Targum is פִּשְׁרָא (“dissolved food”), for through the cud the food is dissolved (נִפְשָׁר) and liquified.

**Among animals.** [This is] an [apparently] extraneous word for deriving [a new rule, namely], to permit [as food] the embryo found within the mother’s womb.

**You may eat it,** and not a [ritually] unclean animal. [But] has it not [been included] in the prohibition? Rather, [that one who eats forbidden flesh] transgresses [both] a positive and a negative commandment.

### 11:8

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-8|Leviticus 11:8]]

**You shall not eat of their flesh.** I know only these, [but what of] other [ritually] unclean animals which have no sign of purity [= permissibility], how do I know [this law applies]? You may say: It is a קַל וָחוֹמֶר : If these [animals] which have some signs of purity are forbidden, etc.

**Of their flesh.** There is a prohibition for [eating] their flesh, [but] not on their bones, sinews, horns and claws.

**You may not touch their carcass.** Perhaps Israelites are forbidden [to] touch carcasses? The verse says: “Say to the *kohanim*,” — *kohanim* are forbidden, [but] Israelites are not forbidden. [It is] thus a קַל וָחוֹמֶר . If the [ritual] impurity of a corpse, which is severe, is forbidden only to *kohanim*, the impurity of a carcass, which is less severe, how much more so [does it not affect Israelites]. [Why then] does the verse say: “You shall not touch”? On a [pilgrimage] festival.

### 11:9

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-9|Leviticus 11:9]]

**Fins.** [The limbs] with which it swims.

**Scales.** These are the scales attached to it (as it is said, “And he was dressed in scaled armor.”)

### 11:10

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-10|Leviticus 11:10]]

**Creeping things.** Wherever [this expression is found] it denotes a low creature which creeps and moves along the ground.

### 11:11

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-11|Leviticus 11:11]]

**They shall be repulsive to you.** [This phrase comes] to forbid their mixtures if they have in them [enough to] impart [their] taste.

**Of their flesh.** One is not prohibited from [eating] their fins and scales.

**And their carcasses shall be repulsive to you,** to include gnats [found in liquids] which one has filtered. יַבְחוּשִׁין are *moucherons* in Old French.

### 11:12

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-12|Leviticus 11:12]]

**Anything which does not have, etc.** What does this verse tell [us]? I might know only the permitted status of those which bring its signs [of purity] onto dry land, [but what if] it lost them in the water, how [do I know that they are permitted]? The verse says: “Anything which does not have fins or scales in the water,” thus if it had [them] in the water although it lost them in being brought up [to dry land], it is permitted.

### 11:13

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-13|Leviticus 11:13]]

**They may not be eaten.** To cause one who feeds them to a minor to be liable, for such is its meaning: they may not be eaten through your action. Or perhaps [this extraneous phrase comes] only to prohibit [one to receive] any benefit from them? The verse says: “You may not eat” — they are prohibited to be eaten, but [receiving] benefit [from them] is permitted. Any fowl of which it is said: “to its kind,” “to its kind,” “to its kind,” has among its species some which do not resemble each other either in appearance or name, [but] all of them are of one species.

### 11:16

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-16|Leviticus 11:16]]

**The sparrow hawk.** *Espervier* in Old French.

### 11:17

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-17|Leviticus 11:17]]

**The cormorant.** Our Rabbis explained: This is [the bird] which draws up fish out of the sea. And that is what Onkelos translated [in Targum]: “and שָׁלֵי נוּנָא,” “and fish drawer.”

**The little owl and the eagle owl.** Those are the *chouettes* which scream at night, and they have cheek bones like humans, and another which is similar is called a *hibou*.

### 11:18

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-18|Leviticus 11:18]]

**The horned owl.** That is the *chauvesouris*; it resembles a rat, and flies about at night. The תִּנְשֶׁמֶת mentioned among the creeping things, resembles it, [but] has no eyes, and is called a *talpa*.

### 11:19

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-19|Leviticus 11:19]]

**The stork.** This is the white דַיָּה, *cigogne*, and why is it called “ חֲסִידָה “? Because it does kindnesses with its fellows in regard to food.

**The heron.** That is the “angry דַיָּה,” and it seems to me that this is what is called a *heron*.

**The hoopoe.** The wild cock, which has a double comb, in Old French *herupe*. Why is it called a דּוּכִיפַת ? Because its ornament [= its comb] is tied together, and this is its comb. Onkelos translated [it as] נַגָּר טוּרָא, [“mountain splitter”], so called because of its actions, as our Rabbis explained in Maseches Gittin, in the chapter of Mi She’achozo.

### 11:20

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-20|Leviticus 11:20]]

**Winged insects.** These are thin, lowly creatures which creep on the ground, like flies, hornets, gnats and locusts.

### 11:21

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-21|Leviticus 11:21]]

**on four,** [that is,] on four legs.

**above their legs,** close to its neck it has two leg-like [appendages], aside from its four [true] legs, and when it wishes to fly and leap from the earth, it strengthens itself by these two [appendages] and flies. There are many [of these], such as those called *longouste*, but we are not familiar with them, for [though] four signs of purity [= permissibility] are said regarding them — four legs, four wings, and *karsulayin*, [which are] the “knees“ mentioned here, and its wings cover most of it[s body], all these signs are found in those which are among us, but there are those whose head is long and have no tail, and whose name must be חָגָב (“locust”), and regarding this we are not knowledgable in distinguishing among them.

### 11:23

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-23|Leviticus 11:23]]

**All flying insects, etc.** [This] comes and teaches [us] that if it has five [feet], it is [ritually] clean.

### 11:24

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-24|Leviticus 11:24]]

**And as for these —** that are about to be listed in the section below.

**You can be made unclean.** That is to say, there is uncleanness [which comes] with their touch.

### 11:25

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-25|Leviticus 11:25]]

**Anyone who carries of their carcasses, etc.** Wherever the uncleanness [caused by] carrying is mentioned, it is more severe than [that caused by] touch, for [the former] requires the washing of clothing.

### 11:26

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-26|Leviticus 11:26]]

**Thus every animal which has cloven hooves but its hooves are not [completely] split.** As for example, a camel, whose hoof is split above, but joined below. [This] teaches you that the carcass of an [ritually] unclean animal causes uncleanness, and in the section at the end of the *parshah* it explains [the rule] regarding a [ritually] clean animal.

### 11:27

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-27|Leviticus 11:27]]

**On their paws,** such as a dog, bear and cat.

**They are unclean to you —** in regard to touch.

### 11:29

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-29|Leviticus 11:29]]

**These are unclean to you.** All these impurities are not in regard to the prohibition of eating, but to [ritual] uncleanness itself, to be [made] unclean by their touch, [and the one who touches them] is forbidden to eat תְּרוּמָה and holy things, and to enter the Sanctuary.

**Weasel,** *moustille* [in Old French].

**Toad,** *bot*, which resembles a frog.

### 11:30

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-30|Leviticus 11:30]]

**Hedgehog,** *herisson*.

**Lizard,** *lizard*.

**Slug,** *limace*.

**Mole,** *talpa*.

### 11:32

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-32|Leviticus 11:32]]

**It must be put into water,** and even after its immersion it is unclean for תְּרוּמָה —

**until the evening**, and after that — **it becomes clean** with the setting of the sun.

### 11:33

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-33|Leviticus 11:33]]

**Into which.** No earthenware vessel becomes unclean except through its interior.

**Everything that is within it becomes unclean.** The vessel in turn causes impurity to whatever is in its interior.

**And you shall break it.** [This] teaches that [earthenware vessels] cannot be purified in a *mikveh*.

### 11:34

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-34|Leviticus 11:34]]

**Of all the foods which are eaten.** This refers to the verse above: “All that is within it becomes unclean.” “Of all the foods which may be eaten” — upon which water comes while it is within an unclean earthenware vessel, becomes unclean. (and so too “every beverage which is drunk in every vessel” — and it is within an unclean earthenware vessel, becomes unclean.) We learn many things from here: We learn that food is not ready and prepared to acquire uncleanness until water comes on it [at least] once, and once water comes on at [at least] once, it acquires uncleanness forever, even when dry. [We also learn] that wine and oil, and everything called “a liquid” prepares seeds for uncleanness, just [as] water [does], for so can the verse be expounded: “Upon which water comes” or “every beverage which is drunk in any vessel, impure shall be” — the food. Furthermore, our Rabbis learned from here that a secondary uncleanness cannot cause vessels to be unclean, and thus we learned: Perhaps all vessels can become unclean from the interior of an earthenware vessel? The verse says: “All that is within it becomes unclean” “of all food” — food (or liquid) become unclean from the interior of an earthenware vessel, [but] all vessels do not become unclean from the interior of an earthenware vessel. [This is] because a creeping thing (שֶׁרֶץ) is a Father of Uncleanness and a vessel which becomes unclean from it becomes a Child of Uncleanness and therefore it does not in turn cause uncleanness to *vessels* which are within it. We learnt further that a creeping thing (שֶׁרֶץ) which fell into the interior of an oven while the loaf was within it [but] the creeping thing did not touch the loaf, the oven is a First and the loaf is a Second, and we do not say that we see the oven as though it it is full of uncleanness, and [so] the loaf will be a First, for if you say so, no vessels are excluded from becoming unclean from the interior of an earthenware vessel, for the uncleanness itself touched them on their outside. And we learned further in regard to water coming [on vegetation] that it does not prepare seeds [to acquire uncleanness] unless it fell on [the vegetation] after it had been picked, for if you say that they acquire fitness [to acquire uncleanness] while still attached [to the ground], you have no [foodstuff] which has not had water come on it, and why then does [the verse] say: “upon which water comes”? [It means] after it had been picked. And we learned further that foodstuffs do not cause other [foodstufffs] to become unclean unless [the former] are [at least] an egg’s-volume [in amount], as it is said, “which are eaten” — food which is eaten at one [gulp], and the Sages estimated that the gullet cannot hold more than a hen’s egg [at once].

### 11:35

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-35|Leviticus 11:35]]

**An oven and a hearth.** These are vessels which may be moved, they are of earthenware and have an interior. One leans the pot over the opening of the hollow space, and both of them have their openings above.

**Shall be shattered.** For earthenware vessels cannot be purified by immersion.

**They shall be unclean to you.** That you should not say: I am obligated to shatter them: the verse says: “They shall be unclean to you” — [but] if he wishes to retain them in their unclean state, he is permitted [to do so].

### 11:36

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-36|Leviticus 11:36]]

**A well-spring and a pit** attached to the ground, do not acquire uncleanness. [Moreover,] you have still to learn: **shall be pure —** one who immerses in them [shall be pure] of their uncleanness.

**That which touches their carcasses shall be unclean.** Even if he is within the well-spring or pit while touching their uncleanness, he will be unclean. For you should not propose a קַל וָחמֶר : If [immersion] purifies the impure from their uncleanness, all the more will it save the pure from becoming unclean; therefore it is said: “that which touches their carcasses shall be unclean.”

### 11:37

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-37|Leviticus 11:37]]

**Any sowing seed.** Seed of any seed-plant. זֵרוּעַ is a noun, as [in the verse]: “And they shall give us of the seeds.”

**It remains clean.** The verse teaches you that [foodstuffs] are not prepared and fit to be called “food” to acquire uncleanness until water comes on them.

### 11:38

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-38|Leviticus 11:38]]

**And if water was once placed on seeds,** after it was uprooted, for if you say that there is a [state of] fitness in regard to [plants] still rooted [in the soil], there is no seed which has not been prepared [for uncleanness].

**Water on seed,** whether water or other liquids, or whether it fell on the seed, whether the seed falls into them, all this is expounded in Toras Kohanim.

**And part of their carcasses fell on them —** even after they became dried of moisture, for the Torah was particular only that it should be called “food,” And once there descended on it a [state of] preparedness for acquiring uncleanness it no longer departs from it.

### 11:39

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-39|Leviticus 11:39]]

**Its carcass —** [but] not bones and sinews, and not horns or hooves, and not hide.

### 11:40

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-40|Leviticus 11:40]]

**Whoever carries its carcass.** The uncleanness [caused by] carrying is more severe than the uncleanness [caused by] touch, for one who carries it causes uncleanness [to] clothes, [but] as for one who touches [it], his clothes are not unclean, for it is not said about the latter, “he shall wash his clothes.”

**Whoever eats of its carcass.** Perhaps the eating of it will make him unclean? When it says regarding the carcass of clean bird, “An animal which has died on its own and an animal that was fatally maimed he must not eat to defile himself with it” it — causes uncleanness to clothes while it is eaten, but the carcass of an animal does not cause uncleanness to clothes while it is eaten, if there is no carrying involved. as for example if another person stuck it into his gullet. If so, why does [the verse specify] “whoever *eats*“? To give a measure for one who carries or touches [it] — [that is], as much a[s the minimum amount] for eating — and that is an olive’s-bulk.

**And [he] is unclean until the evening.** Even though he has immersed he [still] requires the setting of the sun [to complete his purification].

### 11:41

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-41|Leviticus 11:41]]

**That crawls on the ground.** [This comes] to exclude the gnats in peas and beans and the mites in lentils, for [these] do not creep on the earth but within the food, but when they emerge into the air and swarm, they become forbidden.

**It may not be eaten.** [This comes] to make liable one who feeds [others] as one who [himself] eats. [The word] שֶׁרֶץ denotes only a [creature] which is low, with short feet, and which appears as [something] creeping and crawling.

### 11:42

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-42|Leviticus 11:42]]

**Whatever crawls on its belly.** This is the snake, and the expression “belly” [denotes] bending low, in that it moves bent down and fallen on its belly.

**Whatever crawls.** To include worms and whatever is similar to anything similar to it.

**Whatever goes on four [legs].** This is the scorpion.

**Whatever.** To include the beetle, *escarbot* in Old French, and whatever is similar to anything similar to it.

**Whatever has many feet.** This is the centipede, a creeping thing which has feet from its head to its tail on either side [of its body], and it is called a *centpies*.

### 11:43

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-43|Leviticus 11:43]]

**Do not make yourselves repulsive** by eating them, since “your souls” is written, and there is no repulsion of the soul by touch. And so too “you shall not make yourselves unclean” — by eating them.

**You will become unclean because of them.** If you become unclean because of them on the earth, I will in turn cause you to become unclean in the World to Come and in the dwelling Above.

### 11:44

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-44|Leviticus 11:44]]

**For I am Adonoy, your God.** Just as I am holy, for I am Adonoy your God, so “shall you be holy” — sanctify yourselves below.

**And you shall become holy before Me,** for I will sanctify you Above and in the World to Come.

**And you shall not make yourselves unclean.** [All these phrases are intended] to make [one eating these creatures] transgress many negative commandments, and every negative commandment [involves] a whipping, and this is what is said in the Talmud: [If] he ate a *putiso,* he receives four [sets of whippings], [if he ate] an ant, he receives five, a hornet, he receives six.

### 11:45

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-45|Leviticus 11:45]]

**For I am Adonoy who brings you up.** In order that you shall accept My commandments I brought you up. Another interpretation: “For I am Adonoy Who brought you up” — in all [the verses which speak of the Exodus] it is written: “I brought you out,” while here it is written “I brought you up.” The school of R. Yishmo’el taught: If I had only brought Israel up from Egypt only that they refrain from becoming unclean with creeping things as [do] the other nations — it would have been sufficient. And this is their advantage over others — this [explains] the expression of “bringing up.”

### 11:47

[[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-47|Leviticus 11:47]]

**To distinguish.** Not only to recite [these teachings], but you must know and recognize and become proficient in them.

**Between the unclean and the clean.** Does one need to pronounce the difference between a donkey and a cow — are they not already explicit? Rather, [this refers] to [the difference] between that which is unclean to you and that which is clean to you — between [an animal] whose wind-pipe has been cut through half-way and when the greater part of has been cut through.

**And between living creatures which may be eaten.** Need one pronounce the difference between a stag and a wild ass? Are they not explicit? Rather, [to distinguish] between [those] in which there have arisen characteristics [which render the animal] forbidden (טְרֵפָה), [but] it is kosher, and [those] in which there have arisen characteristics [which render the animal] forbidden (טְרֵפָה), and it is [indeed] not kosher.
