# Romans 1 (JANT notes)

### 1.1–15: Salutation, thanksgiving, introduction of issues

*[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 286* · Paul introduces his role as a herald of God’s good news about Jesus Christ, the Davidic descendant who, as Israel’s awaited Messiah, will also rescue the Gentiles, as scripture promises. The opening verses contain the formal features of Roman diplomatic correspondence: clarifying the sender, who is represented, by what authority, and to whom it is written. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Servant*, slave. *Apostle*, lit., “one sent”; i.e., envoy. *Set apart*, see [[Isaiah 06#^6-8|Isa 6.8]]; [[Isaiah 49#^49-1|49.1]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-7|52.7–10]]; [[Isaiah 61#^61-1|61.1–9]]; [[Jeremiah 01#^1-5|Jer 1.5]]; *Pss. Sol.* 11.1; 1QH 18.14. *Gospel*, lit., “message of good news.” ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Prophets . . . holy scriptures*, Paul regards the significance of Jesus’ death and resurrection as well as the turning of the Gentile nations to the God of Israel to have been predicted in Israel’s scriptures (for Paul, the Septuagint). ^1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Son*, sonship involves an obligation to reign righteously or be disciplined. Cf. [[II Samuel 07#^7-8|2 Sam 7.8–16]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]] of the Davidic king; Israel is also the son of God ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-22|Ex 4.22–23]]; [[Hosea 11#^11-1|Hos 11.1]]) as are angels ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-2|Gen 6.2]],[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-4|4]]; [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-8|Deut 32.8]]). This royal claim has political implications for the Jewish people ([[Psalms 002#^2-8|Ps 2.8]]; [[Acts 02#^2-36|Acts 2.36]]; [[Acts 13#^13-33|13.33]]; *Pss. Sol.* 17.3,21), and involves an implicit challenge to a Roman emperor regarded as “son of a god” (e.g., Claudius, Nero). *According to the flesh*, by human descent. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Declared to be Son of God*, a Judaic notion of royal as well as priestly authority. It contrasts with the apotheosis tradition by which deceased caesars were hailed as gods. That Jesus is the *Christ* (“anointed one,” cf. Heb “mashiaḥ”) is witnessed by heaven (*spirit*) and earth (*descended from David*, v 3). *Resurrection,* demonstrates God’s action in a way that defies nature, declaring thereby the dawning of God’s reign over all the nations through Israel’s king. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Grace* (Gk “charis” = lovingkindness; or benefaction/favor; Heb “ḥesed”): the concept of benefaction, found throughout the letter, involves a reciprocal relationship between a patron having the resources to give to clients gifts (such as work) that the clients cannot repay in kind; instead clients repay from their means, including expressions of loyalty (faithfulness) and by making known the honor and good deeds of the patron. *Obedience of faith* (or “faithful obedience”), Gk “hypakoē” translates the Heb “shema” (“to hear/listen,” in the sense of “to obey”). *Gentiles*, Gk “ethnē,” literally means “peoples/nations” and usually refers to people other than Israel (Heb “goyim”), although the word can refer to Israel, which is a people/nation as well. When contrasted with Israel/Israelites/Jews, the reference is to “the people of the other nations” or *Gentiles*. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Saints*, Gk “hagioi” or “holy ones”: those set apart \[to God\]. Paul never uses the term “Christians,” an indication that the term was not in use yet, and neither was Christianity a separate identity. *Father*, see [[Psalms 103#^103-13|Ps 103.13]]. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Faith*, better: “faithfulness.” ^1-8

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-11|11]]:** *To strengthen,* lit., “to establish.” The *spiritual gift* is likely Paul’s proclamation of the gospel (1 Cor 12), by which he will “establish” them within his ministry in conjunction with his preaching in the synagogues of Rome. Their anom-alous foundation independent of his ministry is the cause of their present confusion about how they fit into God’s plan for Israel *and* the nations. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-13|13–15]]:** See 15.28. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Reap some harvest*, Paul will execute his ministry, first to the Jews and then to the nations, gaining followers of Christ, according to the plan that he sees unfolding throughout the Diaspora; see 15.14–33. ^1-13-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Debtor*, Paul’s ministry field extends throughout the Western Diaspora, thus to Rome and Spain. *Barbarian*s, those who do not speak Greek. ^1-14

### 1.16–32: Humanity’s evil

*[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-16|Text]], p. 287* · Paul begins to explain the gospel and his commitment to its success, despite peoples’ failure to obey God. God’s faithfulness is demonstrated in Christ’s faithfulness, which rescues everyone. From 1.16 through ch 3, Paul emphasizes the obligation of Gentiles to do what is right/just and their culpability for failing to do so. ^1-16

> [!note] Faith (ROM 1.8) *(p. 287)*
> Paul uses *pistis* and cognates throughout the letter. The word is usually translated “faith,” but the alternative “faithfulness” is a better translation for its uses in the Pauline Epistles. *Pistis* does not signify mere acknowledgment of a truth claim; nor does it stand in contrast to works. Rather, it signifies loyalty and trust, which include appropriate behavior; hence, faithfulness. In 3.3, the NRSV recognizes that *pistis tou theou* is not “faith of God” but “faithfulness of God”; see [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-8|1 Thess 1.8–9]]. Where Paul contrasts faithfulness to deeds, he is actually contrasting two different ways of being faithful: by Gentiles, apart from circumcision and thus apart from Mosaic covenantal obligations; by Jews, including circumcision and concomitant Mosaic covenant obligations; see 3.28–4.25; [[Galatians 03#^3-1|Gal 3.1–5]]. Paul insists that the faithfulness of Christ-following Gentiles should not be measured by faithfulness to complete the rites of proselyte conversion that some other Jewish teachers might advance as necessary to claim membership among the people of God, which he indicates generally by reference to “circumcision” or “works of law”; cf. Josephus, *Ant.* 20.34–48, for a case where two Jewish teachers of the Gentile King Izates of Adiabene disagree along similar lines as to whether he must be circumcised or not to express faithfulness and do the deeds appropriate for those who claim to worship Israel’s God. In chs 6–8 and 11–15, Paul defines the faithful lifestyle expected of Gentiles.

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Not ashamed*, Paul’s audience may have been wavering about the gospel’s claim that the tiny subject-nation of Israel had produced the Lord of the world instead of Caesar, or they were facing the suspicions of others. *Salvation*, protection/preservation from oppressive forces of the present evil age. *Who has faith,* or “who is being faithful.” *The Jew first and also . . . the Greek,* indicates both chronological development and prioritization, and also that, ultimately, everyone will respond to the gospel; see 2.9–10. ^1-16-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Righteousness,* or justice, cf. Heb “tzedek.” *The one who is righteous . . . faith*, see [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]], which can be translated, “But the righteous/just one out of faithfulness will live”; the verse here can refer to believers living faithfully, or to Christ as the “righteous one who lives out faithfulness.” To his faithfulness Jews and Gentiles are obliged to respond in faithful obedience. See [[Isaiah 51#^51-4|Isa 51.4–5]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-10|52.10]]; [[Psalms 098#^98-2|Ps 98.2–3]]; [[Romans 05#^5-18|Rom 5.18–19]]. For Habakkuk, the verse probably refers to Israelites standing together loyally in the face of adversity. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-18|18–19]]:** The alternative to living faithfully is suppressing the truth about God being and doing what is right. *Wrath of God*, is revealed by those who live unjustly, mirroring how God’s justice is revealed by those who live faithfully. ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-20|20–32]]:** The truth about the Creator is subverted by the worship of created beings ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 13#^13-1|Wis 13.1–9]]). The likely contrast is to the Roman regime; cf. 1QpHab 3–7, for Qumran’s indictment of Rome, also appealing to Habakkuk. Many of the listed unrighteous acts were associated with recent emperors; they are also stereotypes of Gentile culture; see 28–32n. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Without excuse*, Gentiles, although without Torah, should have recognized from both creation (nature) and their own consciences the goodness of the divine and the appropriate response. ^1-20-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-21|1.21,22]]:** *They became*, lit., “they were made.” ^1-21

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Honor,* lit., “glorify”; cf. Heb “kavod.” ^1-21-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-23|23]]:** *Images*, representations, such as statues. ^1-23

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-24|24]]:** *Gave them up*, lit., “handed them over.” ^1-24

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-25|25]]:** *Amen*, “it is so,” “let it be so,” indicating acceptance of what has been stated. ^1-25

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-26|26–27]]:** *Natural intercourse* . . . . The verses reflect contemporary Greco-Roman and Jewish gender roles: men were not to take the subordinate, passive (soft) role or women to take the penetrating role of the superior; both same-sex and certain heterosexual relations would undermine this ideal; cf. Diod. Sic.*.* 32.10.4.9; 32.10.9.3; Cicero, *Verr.* 2.1.140; *Philipp*. 6.4; *Cael.* 32, 67; *Clu.* 18; Dio Chrys., *Or.* (*Euboicus)* 7.133–52; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.50; 3.37–42; *Abr.* 135–37; Pseudo-Phocylides*, Sent.* 190–93; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-16|Wis 14.16]]; see also [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-22|Lev 18.22]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-13|20.13]]. Paul could also be appealing to the command in [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-28|Gen 1.28]] to procreate as that which is natural, and thus censoring all non-procreative sexual activity; see [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-1|1 Cor 7.1–17]]. *Due penalty*, recompence. ^1-26

**[[NRSVue Romans 01#^1-28|28–32]]:** God handed over idolaters (including those claiming to be sons of the gods, such as Roman emperors) to unrighteous lifestyles, thereby making manifest their foolishness and weakness instead of ostensible wisdom and power. For indictments of such depraved behavior, including Caligula’s insatiable sexual lust, see Suetonius, *Gaius* 22–58 (a Roman report), and Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 14–15,89–90,97,107,118 (a Jewish report); for Nero, see Suetonius, *Nero* 26–35. ^1-28
