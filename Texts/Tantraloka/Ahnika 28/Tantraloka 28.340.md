## Mūla

देहत्वस्याविशेषेऽपीत्येष प्रश्नो न शाम्यति ।
स्मरन्निति शता हेतौ तद्रूपं प्रतिपद्यते ॥ ^mula

dehatvasyāviśeṣe'pītyeṣa praśno na śāmyati |
smaranniti śatā hetau tadrūpaṃ pratipadyate || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. X
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 10 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v10 06 - Chapter Twenty-Eight, part 4#^pg163|p. 146]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 10.pdf#page=163>))
>
> It is for this reason that the teacher Kallaṭa, who belongs to our own spiritual lineage, (declares that) although there is no difference (in the various possible) bodies (into which the soul may transmigrate), it is said that the reason (why it is some particular one is) the firm stability of the vital breath (there within it). That is a reasonable view. Otherwise (if one does not accept it), a question (would arise) that has no answer, and that is: (if the vital breath is) equally a part of the corporeal state, why is the vital breath firmly stable in (just) one (body)? (with 28.338cd–340ab)
>
> The reason for this is (implicitly stated) by the present participle ‘recollecting’ (which indicates that that continues throughout the process of transmigration, beginning with the quitting of the prior body up to when the soul assumes the next one). (with 28.340c)
>
> It is that form (namely the body) which is conceived first of all and is recollected, for the body, pulsing (and manifest with vitality) (sphuran), is the first be sustained (and occupied) by consciousness. (with 28.340d–341ab)
