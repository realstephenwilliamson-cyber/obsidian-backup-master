# 2 Peter 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 2 Peter 02|2 Peter 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 2 Peter 01|NRSVue]] · [[2 Peter 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 2 Peter - Introduction]]

### 1.1–2: Words of greeting

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 512* ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Simeon Peter*, the author uses both Heb *Simeon* as well as Gk *Peter* (from “Petros,” “rock”), unlike 1 Peter (1.1) where only the name Peter appears. This distinction supports the argument that 1 and 2 Peter were written by different authors. *Servant*, Gk “doulos,” lit., “slave,” a common self-designation for Jesus’ followers (see [[Romans 01#^1-1|Rom 1.1]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]). *Apostle*, literally, “one sent out.” *A faith* may suggest a doctrinal system. *Precious* (Gk “time”), lit., honorable, esteemed. *Righteousness* (Gk “dikaiosyne”), common early Christian term, from Heb “tzedakah” (see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-19|18.19]]; [[Isaiah 56#^56-1|Isa 56.1]]), indicating both fidelity and uprightness. It was one of the four cardinal virtues (the others are courage, wisdom, and self-control \[see [[Titus 02#^2-5|Titus 2.5–6]]\]). *Our God and Savior*, perhaps an early evidence of equation of God with Jesus (see also 1.11; 3.18; [[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1–3]]; [[John 20#^20-28|20.28]]). ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Grace* (Gk “charis”) *and peace,* standard Greek epistolary greetings. *Knowledge* (Gk “gnosis”), a subtheme in the epistle that perhaps contrasts with the stream of thought known as Gnosticism. For 2 Peter, knowledge concerns faith in the actions of the Christ, not secret tenets. ^1-2

### 1.3–11: Exhortation to virtue

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-3|Text]], p. 512* ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Given us everything*, God as presented as a benefactor or patron; this generosity will be contrasted with images of divine wrath (2.4–10; 3.5–7,9–13). *Glory* (Gk “doxa”), mostly used in LXX for Heb “kavod” (e.g., [[Hosea 04#^4-7|Hos 4.7]]), “tehillah” (e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-11|Ex 15.11]]) or “peʾer” (e.g., [[Isaiah 61#^61-3|Isa 61.3]]). ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Precious and very great promises,* of forgiveness of sin and life after death. *Corruption . . . because of lust*, the epistle sees lust as humanity’s principal sin, and it may reflect an early second-century Christian emphasis on continence. *Participants of the divine nature,* language of popular Greek mystery religions. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Support your faith with goodness, and goodness with knowledge*, this list of virtues resembles *m. Sot.* 9.15: “R. Pinchas ben Yair says: ‘Quickness leads to cleanliness, cleanliness leads to purity, purity leads to separation, separation leads to sanctity, sanctity leads to humility, humility leads to fear of sin, fear of sin leads to piety, piety leads to the Holy Spirit.’” This structure, where each element leads to what follows, is common in Hellenistic moral philosophy (see e.g., Philo, *Sacr*. 27; *Leg*. *all.* 1.64; cf. [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-18|Wis 6.18–21]]; [[Romans 05#^5-3|Rom 5.3–5]]). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Self-control* (Gk “sophrosyne”), a principal Stoic virtue. Self-control was also praised by the rabbis (e.g., *m. Avot* 4.1, ” . . . Who is strong? He who overcomes his desire, as it is said, ‘He who is slow to anger is better than the mighty, and he who rules his spirit than he who takes a city’” \[cf. [[Proverbs 16#^16-32|Prov 16.32]]\]). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Love*, the greatest virtue ([[1 Corinthians 13#^13-13|1 Cor 13.13]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Short-sighted and blind*, people in the church who are weak in the faith. *Cleansing of past sins*, likely via baptism (see [[1 Peter 03#^3-21|1 Pet 3.21]]). The notion that sins may be cleansed is common in biblical and rabbinic thought (see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-30|Lev 16.30]]; [[Isaiah 44#^44-22|Isa 44.22]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-25|Ezek 36.25]]; *m. Yoma* 8.9: “just as the mikvah \[ritual bath\] purifies the unclean, so does God purify Israel”). ^1-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Election*, the church adapted Israel’s notion of being a people chosen by God ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-2|Deut 14.2]]; [[Psalms 135#^135-4|Ps 135.4]]; *m. Avot* 3.14). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Eternal kingdom*, the life that begins with the new age. *Lord and Savior*, see 2.20; 3.2,18, cf. [[Philippians 03#^3-20|Phil 3.20]]; Jude 25. ^1-11

### 1.12–15: Peter’s impending death

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-12|Text]], p. 513* ^1-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Keep on reminding*, the author presumes 1 Peter. ^1-12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-13|13]]:** *In this body*, as opposed to the glorious, resurrected body. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-14|14]]:** *My death will come soon*, see [[John 21#^21-18|Jn 21.18]]. Early Christian legend *(Acts of Peter*, second-third centuries) states that Peter was crucified, upside-down, by Nero in 64. ^1-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Make every effort*, either through this letter or by some other document to which his followers would be able to turn and confirm their belief. *Departure* (Gk “exodus”), i.e., death (see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-2|Wis 3.2]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-6|7.6]]; *T. Naph.* 1.1; Josephus, *Ant*. 4.189). ^1-15

### 1.16–18: Peter’s revelation

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-16|Text]], p. 513* ^1-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Cleverly devised myths*, see [[1 Timothy 01#^1-4|1 Tim 1.4]]; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-7|4.7]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-4|2 Tim 4.4]]; [[Titus 01#^1-14|Titus 1.14]], where the reference is to “Jewish” myths. Gk “mythos” could refer to fables or fictions, the latter hinting at false beliefs. *Coming* (Gk “Parousia”), the technical term for the return of Jesus. *Eyewitnesses of his majesty*, the author’s belief is not based on legends but is a result of his eyewitness (see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-19|Ex 20.22]]: “You have seen for yourselves that I spoke with you from heaven”). *Eyewitness* (Gk “epoptai”) was commonly used in Hellenistic literature for initiates into mystery religions (cf. Plutarch, *Alc*. 22). ^1-16-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Majestic Glory*, perhaps referring to biblical (Heb) “kavod” or the rabbinic (Heb) “Shekhinah,” terms for the earthly manifestation of God (Gk “megaloprepes” is found [[LXX Deuteronomy 33#^33-26|Deut 33.26]]\[LXX\]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 08#^8-15|2 Macc 8.15]]). Here the reference is to Jesus’ transfiguration ([[Matthew 17#^17-1|Mt 17.1–9]] || [[Mark 09#^9-2|Mk 9.2–8]] || [[Luke 09#^9-28|Lk 9.28–36]]). *God the Father*, “father” in connection with God is frequent in Jewish sources, especially prayers (see *b. Taʾan.* 25b). *Son, my Beloved*, an allusion to Jesus’ baptism ([[Matthew 03#^3-17|Mt 3.17]]; [[Mark 01#^1-9|Mk 1.9–11]]; [[Luke 03#^3-22|Lk 3.22]]) and transfiguration ([[Matthew 17#^17-5|Mt 17.5]]; [[Mark 09#^9-7|Mk 9.7]]); cf. [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]] and [[Isaiah 42#^42-1|Isa 42.1]]. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-18|18]]:** *We ourselves*, according to [[Matthew 17#^17-1|Mt 17.1]], Peter was taken by Jesus to witness the transfiguration, together with James and John (see 1.17n.). *Voice come from heaven*, a similar term (Heb “bat kol”) is often used in rabbinic sources to express a heavenly voice that initiates divine intervention or instruction. *Holy mountain*, early church fathers (e.g., Origen, Cyril of Jerusalem, Jerome) identified the mountain as Mt. Tabor. ^1-18

### 1.19–21: Prophecy’s authority

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-19|Text]], p. 513* ^1-19

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Prophetic message*, it is unclear whether the author is referring to prophecies in the Tanakh, fulfilled by Jesus, or to a message from God by the Holy Spirit (as, perhaps, in v 21). *Lamp shining*, [[Matthew 05#^5-15|Mt 5.15]] (in reference to disciples); 6.22–23; [[John 05#^5-35|Jn 5.35]]**.** *Day dawns*, probably a reference to Judgment Day (see 2.9; 3.7). *Morning star* (Gk “phosphoros”), in [[Revelation 02#^2-28|Rev 2.28]]; [[Revelation 22#^22-16|22.16]] this refers to Jesus. ^1-19-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Prophecy of scripture*, the early church faced multiple interpretations of both the Scriptures of Israel and its own collection of texts; the author limits interpretations to those in accord with his teachings. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Moved by the Holy Spirit*, charismatic pronouncements, see Acts 2; 1 Cor 12–14. Heb “ruaḥ ha-Kodesh” (holy spirit) is very common in rabbinic sources to describe prophesying (e.g., *t. Sot.* 13.3; *Mek. Shira* 1). *Holy Spirit saints of God spoke* ^1-21
