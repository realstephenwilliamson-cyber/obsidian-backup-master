**1.** There are six factors that prevent [a priest] from reciting the priestly blessings: [an inability] to pronounce [the blessings properly],[^1] physical deformities,[^2] transgressions,[^3] [lack of] maturity,[^4] intoxication,[^5] and the ritual impurity of [the priest's] hands.[^6]  
[An inability] to pronounce [the blessings properly]:[^7] What is implied? Those who cannot articulate the letters properly[^8] - e.g., those who read an *aleph* as an *ayin* and an *ayin* as an *aleph*, or who pronounce *shibbolet* as *sibbolet*[^9] and the like[^10] - should not recite the priestly blessings.  
Similarly, a stutterer or one who speaks unclearly, whose words cannot be understood by everyone,[^11] should not recite the priestly blessing. ^15-1

**2.** Physical deformities: What is implied? A priest should not recite the priestly blessings if he has blemishes on his face, hands, or feet[^12] - for example, his fingers are bent over, crooked, or covered with white spots[^13] - for they will attract the people's attention.  
A person whose spittle always dribbles when he speaks, and also a person who is blind in one eye should not recite the priestly blessings. However, if such a person was well known in his city and everyone was familiar[^14] with the person who was blind in one eye or whose spittle dribbled, he may recite the priestly blessing, for he will not attract their attention.[^15]  
Similarly,[^16] a person whose hands were colored purple or scarlet should not recite the priestly blessings. If the majority of the city's population is involved in such a profession,[^17] he is permitted, for this does not attract the people's attention. ^15-2

**3.** Transgressions: What is implied? A priest who killed someone should never recite the priestly blessings, even if he repents,[^18] as [implied by [[Isaiah 01#^1-15|Isaiah 1:15]] which] states: "Your hands are full of blood,"[^19] and states:[^20] "When you spread out your hands, I will hide My eyes from you."  
A priest who served false gods, even if he was compelled to do so or did so inadvertently[^21] - though he has repented[^22] - may never recite the priestly blessing, as [can be inferred from [[II Kings 23#^23-9|II Kings 23:9]], which] states: "However, the priests of the high places shall not ascend [to God's altar in Jerusalem]." [The recitation of the priestly] blessings is equated to service [in the Temple],[^23] as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-8|Deuteronomy 10:8]]] states: "to serve Him and to bless in His[^24] name."  
Similarly, a priest who converted[^25] to the worship of false gods[^26] - even though he repents[^27] - may never recite the priestly blessing. Other transgressions[^28] do not prevent [a priest from blessing the people].[^29] ^15-3

**4.** [Lack of] maturity: What is implied? A young priest should not recite the priestly blessings until he grows a full beard.[^30]  
Intoxication: What is implied? A [priest] who drank a *revi'it* of wine[^31] at one time should not recite the priestly blessings until the effects of the wine wear off.[^32] [This restriction was imposed] because an association was established between [reciting the priestly] blessing and service [in the Temple].[^33]  
Should [a priest] drink[^34] a *revi'it* of wine on two different occasions[^35] or mix a small amount of water in it, he is permitted [to recite the priestly blessings].[^36] If he drank more than a *revi'it*, even though it was mixed with water or even though he drank it intermittently, he should not recite the priestly blessings until the effects of the wine wear off.[^37]  
How much is a *revi'it*?[^38] [The volume of an area] two fingerbreadths by two fingerbreadths and two and seven tenths of a fingerbreadth high.[^39] Whenever the term "finger" is mentioned as a measure throughout the entire Torah, it refers to a thumbbreadth.[^40] The thumb is called *bohen yad* [in the Torah].[^41] ^15-4

**5.** The ritual impurity of [the priest's] hands: What is implied? A priest who did not wash his hands should not recite the priestly blessing.[^42] Rather, he should wash his hands[^43] to the wrist,[^44] as is done when sanctifying the hands for the service in the Temple,[^45] as \[[[Psalms 134#^134-2|Psalms 134:2]]] states: "Raise up your hands [in] holiness and bless God."  
A[^46] *challal[^47]* does not recite the priestly blessing, for he is not a priest.[^48] ^15-5

**6.** A priest who does not have any of the factors which hinder the recitation of the priestly blessings mentioned above[^49] should recite the priestly blessing, even though he is not a wise man or careful in his observance of the mitzvot. [This applies] even though the people spread unwholesome gossip about him,[^50] or his business dealings are not ethical.[^51]  
He should not be prevented from [reciting the priestly blessings] because [reciting these blessings] is a positive mitzvah incumbent on each priest who is fit to recite them.[^52] We do not tell a wicked person: Increase your wickedness [by] failing to perform mitzvot.[^53] ^15-6

**7.** Do not wonder: "What good will come from the blessing of this simple person?" for the reception of the blessings is not dependent on the priests, but on the Holy One, blessed be He,[^54] as \[[[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-27|Numbers 6:27]]] states: "And they shall set My name upon the children of Israel, and I shall bless them."[^55] The priests perform the mitzvah with which they were commanded,[^56] and God, in His mercies, will bless Israel as He desires. ^15-7

**8.** The people standing behind the priests are not included in the blessing.[^57] Those standing at their sides[^58] are included in the blessing. [Even] if there is a partition[^59] - even an iron wall - between the priests and the people who are being blessed, since they are facing the priests,[^60] they are included in the blessing.[^61] ^15-8

**9.** The priestly blessing is recited [only] when ten people [are present].[^62] The priests can be included in that number.[^63]  
If [the congregation in a particular] synagogue are all priests, they should all[^64] recite the priestly blessing.[^65] Who should they bless? Their brethren in the north and the south. Who will respond "Amen" to their [blessings]? The women and the children.[^66] If more than ten priests remain besides those who ascend to the *duchan*, these ten [priests] respond "Amen"[^67] and the remainder of the priests recite the blessings.[^68] ^15-9

**10.** When there is no priest in the community other than the leader of the congregation,[^69] he should not recite the priestly blessings.[^70] If he is sure that he can recite the priestly blessings and return to his prayers [without becoming confused],[^71] he may [recite the priestly blessing].[^72]  
If there are no priests present at all, when the leader of the congregation reaches [the blessing] *Sim shalom*,[^73] he recites [the following prayer]:  
Our[^74] God and God of our fathers, bless us with the threefold blessing written in the Torah by Moses, Your servant, and recited by Aharon and his sons, the priests, Your consecrated people,[^75] as it is[^76] said:  
May God bless you and keep you.[^77]  
May God shine His countenance upon you and be gracious to you.  
May God turn His countenance to you and grant you peace.  
And they shall set My name upon the children of Israel and I will bless them.[^78]  
The people do not respond "Amen" to these blessings.[^79] He [resumes his recitation of the *Shemoneh Esreh*], beginning the recitation of [the blessing] *Sim shalom*. ^15-10

**11.** A priest who recited the priestly blessings and went to another synagogue and found the congregation in the midst of prayer, before the [recitation of] the priestly blessings, should bless them.[^80] [He may recite the priestly blessings] several times during the day.[^81]  
A priest who does not move from his place to ascend to the *duchan* when the leader of the congregation recites [the blessing] *R'tzey[^82]* should not ascend [to the *duchan*] during that prayer service.[^83] However, if he moved [from his place], even though he did not reach the *duchan* until after the [conclusion of the blessing *R'tzey*],[^84] he may[^85] ascend [the *duchan*] and bless [the people]. ^15-11

**12.** Any priest who does not ascend to the *duchan*[^86] - even though he neglects [the performance] of [only] one commandment - is considered as if he violated three positive commandments, as \[[[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-23|Numbers 6:23-27]]] states: "This is how you shall bless the children of Israel," "Say to them," "And you shall set My name..."  
Any priest who does not recite the priestly blessing will not be blessed, and any priest who blesses [the people] will be blessed, as \[[[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Genesis 12:3]]] states: "And I will bless those who bless you."[^87] ^15-12

Since in the Rambam's time the Jews lived mostly to the east or west of Jerusalem, in most synagogues the *heichal* would be pointed in that direction, and thus, depending on the location of the synagogue, the people standing in one of these directions would be standing behind the priests. Therefore, they would not be included in the blessing.
