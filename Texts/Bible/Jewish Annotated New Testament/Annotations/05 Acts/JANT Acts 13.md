# Acts 13 (JANT notes)

### 13.1–14.28: Paul’s journey in Asia Minor

*[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 248* · The rest of Acts focuses on Paul’s travels through Asia Minor and Greece (chs 13–20), his arrest in Jerusalem and defense before Roman and Jewish authorities (chs 21–27), and his journey to Rome (chs 27–28). ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-1|1–3]]:** The church in Antioch.  ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Prophets*, see 11.27. *Barnabas*, see 4.36n. *Herod the ruler*, Gk “tetrarch,” signifying Herod Antipas, son of Herod the Great and tetrarch of Galilee and Perea 6–37 CE On Luke’s presentation of Antipas, see [[Luke 23#^23-8|Lk 23.8–11]]. ^13-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-2|2]]:** *Holy Spirit*, see 8.29; 10.19; 13.4; 16.6,7. ^13-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Praying they laid their hands*, actions sanctioning ministerial appointment (6.6; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-14|1 Tim 4.14]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-6|2 Tim 1.6]]). ^13-3

### 13.4–12: Preaching on Cyprus

*[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-4|Text]], p. 248* ^13-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-4|4]]:** *Seleucia*, seaport near Antioch. *Cyprus*, other Christians preceded Paul and Barnabas there (11.19). Cyprus was a Senatorial province and important trade center. A sizeable Jewish community lived on the island, but declined considerably after Rome brutally quelled a Jewish uprising against other residents early in the second century (Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 282; Josephus, *Ant* 13.284; 18.131; Dio Cassius 68.32). ^13-4-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-5|5]]:** *Salamis*, seaport on eastern side of Cyprus. *Synagogues of the Jews*, Paul regularly preaches in synagogues (13.14; 14.1; 16.13; 17.1,10,17; 18.4,19; 19.8), which contradicts Paul’s own claims that he preaches only to Gentiles ([[Romans 16#^16-4|Rom 16.4]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-16|Gal 1.16]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-9|2.9]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-2|1 Thess 1.2]]). ^13-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-6|6]]:** *Paphos*, seat of Roman administration on Cyprus, on the southwest coast. *Magician*, see Peter’s meeting with Simon (8.14–24). On Jewish magicians from Cyprus, see the example of Atomus (Josephus, *Ant.* 20.142). *Bar-Jesus*, a striking coincidence that Paul encounters another “prophet” named Jesus, but Jesus/Joshua/Yeshua was a common Jewish name. ^13-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-7|7]]:** *Proconsul, Sergius Paulus*, representing the Roman emperor (18.12; 24.22–27; 25.14). A “proconsul” governed a province, usually for a fixed term of one year, by appointment of the emperor. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-8|8]]:** *Elymas*, meaning uncertain. ^13-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-9|9]]:** *Saul, also known as Paul*, here, and for most of the rest of Acts, the name Paul is used. Luke may have preferred the Hebrew name Saul to this point to emphasize Paul’s Jewish background. *Intently*, see 3.4n. ^13-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-10|10]]:** *Son of the devil*, Elymas is the son not of Jesus but of Satan (see [[John 08#^8-44|Jn 8.44]]). ^13-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-11|11]]:** Paul often performs miracles (14.10; 19.11; 20.9–10; 28.3–6). Paul’s ability to blind the magician reflects God’s power to subdue Satan. ^13-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Lord*, here meaning Jesus. ^13-12

### 13.13–52: Preaching in Antioch in Pisidia

*[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-13|Text]], p. 249* · Another recounting of Israel’s history (cf. Stephen’s speech, 7.2–51). Paul’s speech covers several central elements: Jesus is David’s heir and the culmination of Israel’s sacred narrative (vv 17–25); his death and resurrection bring forgiveness and salvation to those who believe (vv 26–41); Jews reject God’s message and persecute Christians, while Gentiles become believers (vv 44–52). ^13-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Perga in Pamphylia*, major seaport city in southern Asia Minor (today Turkey). *John*, see 15.37–38. ^13-13-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-14|14]]:** *Antioch in Pisidia*, Roman city approximately 100 mi from Perga. *Sabbath day*, Jews regularly gathered on the Sabbath (seventh day, Saturday) to read scripture, both Torah (Pentateuch) and Prophets (Philo, *Spec*. *Laws* 2.60–62; Josephus, *J.W.* 2.289–92; *m. Meg.* 3.6); this was typically followed by preaching, related in the following verses. *Synagogue*, Paul’s typical practice; see 13.5. ^13-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-15|15]]:** *Officials*: Gk “archisynag*ō*gos,” title commonly used for Jewish communal leaders. ^13-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-16|16]]:** *Others who fear God*, Acts makes a point of depicting Gentiles as regular synagogues attendees (16.14; 17.4,12; 18.7). Their presence often allows Luke to contrast Gentiles who accept Paul’s preaching with Jews who reject it. ^13-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-17|13.17–41]]:** **Paul’s speech**. For Paul, Israel’s story culminates with Jesus (vv 17–25). *Chose our ancestors*, see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-37|Deut 4.37]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-6|7.6]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-15|10.15]]. *Uplifted arm*, see [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^6-6|Ex 6.6]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-34|Deut 4.34]]; [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-8|26.8]]. ^13-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-18|18]]:** *Put up with*, some manuscripts read *cared for* (the difference between the two Greek words is one letter). While both meanings fit the context, Stephen’s speech (7.42) suggests Luke meant *put up with*. ^13-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-19|19]]:** *Seven nations*, see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-1|Deut 7.1]]. *Land as an inheritance*, see [[Torah 43 - Masei#^34-2|Num 34.2]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-38|Deut 4.38]]; [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-1|26.1]]; [[Joshua 14#^14-1|Josh 14.1–2]]. ^13-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-20|20]]:** *Four hundred fifty years*, the figure corresponds closely to the chronological reckoning provided in [[I Kings 06#^6-1|1 Kings 6.1]]. ^13-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-21|21]]:** *Asked for a king*, see [[I Samuel 08#^8-5|1 Sam 8.5]],[[I Samuel 08#^8-10|10]]. *Saul,* see [[I Samuel 10#^10-21|1 Sam 10.21]],[[I Samuel 10#^10-24|24]]. *Forty years*, the Tanakh does not specify the length of Saul’s reign, **Chs 13–14:** First missionary journey of Paul. but suggests a much shorter period. The number however agrees with Josephus’s account (*Ant.* 6.378), although it can also often denote a period of great length or fullness ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^7-17|Gen 7.17]]; [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^25-20|25.20]]; [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^26-34|26.34]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-35|Ex 16.35]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-18|24.18]]; [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^13-25|Num 13.25]]). 1 Kings states that Solomon reigned in Israel for 40 years ([[I Kings 11#^11-42|1 Kings 11.42]]). ^13-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-22|22]]:** A pastiche of biblical passages (e.g., [[I Samuel 13#^13-14|1 Sam 13.14]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-25|Ps 89.24]]\[MT 89.25\]). ^13-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-23|23]]:** *Posterity*, Jesus’ legal inheritance of Davidic descent according to Luke came through Joseph ([[Luke 03#^3-23|Lk 3.23–38]]). ^13-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-24|24]]:** *John . . . baptism of repentance*, see [[Luke 03#^3-3|Lk 3.3]]. ^13-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-25|25]]:** See [[Luke 03#^3-16|Lk 3.16]]. ^13-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-27|27]]:** *Did not recognize him*, see 3.17. *Condemning him,* Jews are responsible for killing Jesus (see 2.23). ^13-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-28|28]]:** *No cause*, see [[Luke 23#^23-4|Lk 23.4]],[[Luke 23#^23-14|14–15]],[[Luke 23#^23-22|22]],[[Luke 23#^23-47|47]]. *Pilate*, see 3.13n. ^13-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-29|29]]:** *Tree*, see 5.30; 10.39. 40° 35° ^13-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-39|39]]:** *Set free*, Gk “dikaioo,” literally, “justified”; on the relation between justification and the law, see [[Galatians 03#^3-23|Gal 3.23–25]]; [[Romans 03#^3-28|Rom 3.28]]; [[Romans 08#^8-3|8.3]]. ^13-39

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-41|41]]:** See [[Habakkuk 1#^1-5|Hab 1.5]]. *Perish*, possibly in the sense of total annihilation, as in the flood generation (Josephus, *Ant.* 1.76). *You will never believe*, Paul often encounters Jews who reject Jesus and his evangelists (e.g., 13.36; 18.6; 28.28). ^13-41

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-42|13.42–52]]:** **End of Pisidian mission.** Many Jews respond positively (14.1; 17.4,11–12; 18.8). ^13-42

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-43|43]]:** *Converts*, present at the beginning of Peter’s mission (2.10) and Paul’s. ^13-43

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-45|45]]:** *The Jews*, Acts presents Jewish opposition as persistent (17.5,13; 18.12; 20.3,19), irrational (5.17; 17.5), and virulent (18.6). ^13-45

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-46|46]]:** *Spoke out boldly*, see 4.13. Just as Jesus’ death results from both divine will and Jewish malevolence, so the mission to the Gentiles results from God’s plan (10.1–11.18) and Jewish rejection. ^13-46

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-47|47]]:** See [[Isaiah 49#^49-6|Isa 49.6]]. Paul understands himself and his companions, rather than Israel (*T. Levi* 14.3; *Sib*. *Or*. 14.214), to be the light. ^13-47

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-48|48]]:** *Destined for eternal life*, proven worthy of becoming part of God’s kingdom. ^13-48

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-50|50]]:** *Jews incited*, Jews are the source of enmity against Christians. *Devout women*, Acts often portrays women as important members of the community (see 12.12). ^13-50

**[[NRSVue Acts 13#^13-51|51]]:** *Shook the dust*, see [[Luke 10#^10-11|Lk 10.11]]; see also [[Matthew 10#^10-14|Mt 10.14]]. *Iconium*, 75 mi (120 km) southeast of Antioch (present-day Konya). ^13-51
