# 1 John 3 (JANT notes)

[[JANT 1 John 02|← 1 John 2]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 1 John 04|1 John 4 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 1 John 03|NRSVue]] · [[1 John 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-2|3.2]]:** *We are God’s children* (also 3.1; 4.4; 5.19), by this repeated theme the author assures readers of their status. *We will be like him*, believers become Christ-like in the resurrection ([[John 01#^1-12|Jn 1.12–13]]; [[John 13#^13-15|13.15]]). The Tanakh refers to Israel as God’s children (e.g., [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1–2]]); this imagery conveys a special, intimate relationship that likewise connotes the obligation to obey. ^3-2

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-3|3]]:** *Purify . . . pure*, necessary for coming into contact with God; see [[Isaiah 06#^6-5|Isa 6.5–7]]; [[Malachi 3#^3-2|Mal 3.2]]. ^3-3

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-4|4]]:** *Lawlessness* (Gk “anomia”) may imply the view that Jesus’ followers reject “nomos” (“law,” the LXX translation of “Torah”), a view that Paul also combated (see, e.g., [[Romans 03#^3-31|Rom 3.31]]; [[Romans 06#^6-15|6.15–23]]). ^3-4

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-5|5]]:** *No sin*, see also [[Hebrews 04#^4-15|Heb 4.15]]; [[2 Corinthians 05#^5-21|2 Cor 5.21]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-22|1 Pet 2.22]], where Jesus is likewise referred to as sinless. ^3-5

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-6|6–10]]:** *No one who abides in him sins*, the author sets a very high standard of behavior (see vv 8–10). The main forms of sin here seem to be the failure to love their *brothers and sisters* (2.22). ^3-6

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Child of the devil*, perhaps referring to those who withdrew (2.19); there is no indication here of the identification of Jews as children of the devil, as in [[John 08#^8-44|Jn 8.44]]. *Devil* (Gk “diabolos”) translates “satan” of the Tanakh; in the latter, satan is subordinate to God and acts on his behalf as an accuser in the heavenly court ([[Zechariah 03#^3-1|Zech 3.1–10]]; Job 1–2). By the first century ce, the understanding of devil had transformed into an evil power no longer subordinate to God (see “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682). ^3-8

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-9|9]]:** *Seed*, offspring, here the deeds of love and faith in God. ^3-9

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Love one another* (see 3.14,16,18), love is a principle Johannine theme (see [[John 13#^13-34|Jn 13.34–35]]). ^3-11

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-12|12]]:** *Cain*, the first murderer (Gen 4). Genesis does not explain why God preferred Abel’s offering to that of Cain. Various attempts to explain why Cain murdered Abel are found in Jewish postbiblical traditions. For example, in *Gen. Rab.* 22.7, Cain suggests to Abel that they divide the world between them, with Cain getting all land and Abel getting all chattel; when Abel agrees, Cain accuses him of standing on his land, whereupon Abel points out that Cain is wearing garments of animal skins which belong to him; in the resulting battle, Cain kills Abel. ^3-12

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-13|13]]:** *World hates you*, see [[John 15#^15-18|Jn 15.18–19]]. ^3-13

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-16|16]]:** *Laid down his life*, the rabbinic term “kiddush ha-Shem” denotes martyrdom, particularly the willingness to die a martyr in order to sanctify (“kiddush”) God’s name (“ha-Shem”). The concept is based on [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-32|Lev 22.32]], “You shall not profane my holy name, that I may be sanctified among the people of Israel.” Rabbinic Judaism mandates choosing martyrdom only if one is forced to transgress the commandments against idolatry, unchastity (e.g., incest, adultery), and murder ([[Sanhedrin 74#74a|*b. Sanh*. 74a]]). ^3-16

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-19|19–21]]:** *Hearts*, the Tanakh views the heart as the seat of the emotional and intellectual inner life; Heb for heart, “lev(av),” metaphorically refers to the source of intellectual activities (e.g., [[I Kings 08#^8-48|1 Kings 8.48]]; [[Isaiah 10#^10-7|Isa 10.7]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-26|Ezek 36.26]]). [[Psalms 037#^37-31|Ps 37.31]] states laws of God are written on the human heart. Most rabbinic references to the heart pertain to ethics (e.g*. , m. Avot* 2.9), in which “a good heart” is chosen as the best among many responses to the question: “Which is the good way to which a person should cleave?” because it encompasses all of the other responses (i.e., “a good eye,” “a good neighbor,” “a good companion, “one that sees what will be”), and prayer is called the “service of the heart” (e.g., *y. Ber*. 4.1; 7a). ^3-19

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-20|20]]:** God’s omniscience is suggested by several verses in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Jeremiah 11#^11-20|Jer. 11.20]]; [[Jeremiah 16#^16-17|16.17]]; [[Psalms 007#^7-10|Ps. 7.10]]); rabbinic literature more broadly asserts that God knows all but notes that human beings’ right to choose limits God’s power; see, e.g., [[Berakhot 33#33b|*b. Ber*. 33b]]: “Everything is in the power of Heaven except the reverence of Heaven.” ^3-20

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-21|21]]:** *Boldness* (Gk “parresia”), the confidence to speak one’s mind; see 2.28, “confidence”; 4.17, “boldness” resulting from knowledge of the love of God; 5.14, “boldness” in asking “according to \[God’s\] will.” ^3-21

**[[NRSVue 1 John 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Name*, see 2.12–14n. In creating the world God names things (the light he names “Day,” the darkness “Night,” etc. in [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-5|Gen 1.5]]), and this naming enacts his authority over these things; names had power (demonstrated by the multiple names of deities used in ancient magic spells in order to increase the strength of the spell). The Decalogue prohibits taking the name of God in vain. Indeed, God’s name is considered too sacred to be uttered aloud, thus Jewish readers substitute the word “adonai*”* (“my master”) or “hashem*”* (“the Name”) for the tetragrammaton. ^3-23
