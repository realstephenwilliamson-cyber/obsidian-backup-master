### 13.1–38: Last Supper

*[[NRSVue John 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 205* ^13-1

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Passover*, begins here on Friday night, in contrast to the Synoptics, where it begins on Thursday ([[Matthew 26#^26-17|Mt 26.17]]; [[Mark 14#^14-12|Mk 14.12–16]]; [[Luke 22#^22-15|Lk 22.15]]). This “Last Supper” is not a Passover meal—it lacks the paschal lamb, and there is no institution of the Eucharist (contrast [[Matthew 26#^26-26|Mt 26.26–29]]; [[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22–25]]; [[Luke 22#^22-13|Lk 22.13–20]]). The chronology of the Passion — the sequence of events from the betrayal to the crucifixion—therefore differs from that of the Synoptics. Given that it is unlikely that the Jewish authorities would have interrupted their celebration of the Passover in order to try Jesus, John’s chronology is seen by many as more plausible. ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-2|2]]:** *Devil . . . Judas*, as in the Gospel’s comments about the judgments against the Jews, Judas’s association with the devil is not meant to suggest that he lacks free will, but that his actions demonstrate that he is motivated by the devil, as explained in 8.41–45. ^13-2

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-5|5–10]]:** The foot washing, unique to John, indicates that followers should serve one another. ^13-5

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-7|7]]:** *Later you will understand*, after the crucifixion/resurrection event. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-10|10]]:** *Entirely clean*, the foot washing was not an act of hygiene but a ritual act of purification perhaps analogous to the baptism offered by John the Baptist, and/or the purification needed prior to the Passover sacrifice. ^13-10

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Teacher . . . Lord*, both offer instruction and insight and act with authority. ^13-13

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-14|14]]:** *Lord . . . have washed*, an exemplary act of humility. ^13-14

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-16|16]]:** See [[Matthew 10#^10-24|Mt 10.24]]; [[Luke 06#^6-40|Lk 6.40]]. ^13-16

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-18|18]]:** *Whom I have chosen*, probably the disciples, though later it is God who is accredited with choosing them (see 17.6). *Scripture*, see [[Psalms 041#^41-10|Ps 41.9]]. ^13-18

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-21|21]]:** *Troubled in spirit*, a reference to deep emotion; see 11.33. This is the only point at which John’s Jesus shows any concern about the events that await him; the immediate reference here, however, is to the betrayal and not to his impending death. See, by contrast, the Gethsemane prayer in [[Matthew 26#^26-36|Mt 26.36–46]]; [[Mark 14#^14-32|Mk 14.32–42]]; [[Luke 22#^22-39|Lk 22.39–46]]. ^13-21

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-23|23]]:** *One whom Jesus loved*, first appearance of the anonymous “Beloved Disciple” presented as Jesus’ closest disciple and the eyewitness behind the Gospel; see 19.25–27; 20.10; 21.1–14,20–24. The Gospel subordinates Peter to this Beloved Disciple. *Reclining*, a reference to the Greco-Roman practice of reclining at a communal meal; also adopted as practice at the Passover seder, though in John the final dinner is not a Passover meal. ^13-23

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-27|27]]:** *After he received*, implying that Satan somehow entered into Judas along with the bread. ^13-27

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-30|30]]:** *Night*, see 3.19. ^13-30

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-31|31]]:** *Now . . . . glorified*, the Passion — the series of events culminating in Jesus’ crucifixion —begins. ^13-31

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-33|33]]:** *Little children*, perhaps relating back to the idea of children of God (1.12), as these are the ones who have received him. ^13-33

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-34|34]]:** *New commandment*, the *commandment* (Gk “entolē,” \[LXX\] for Heb “mitzvah,” “commandment, order”) is not “new” in the sense of original: [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]],[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-34|34]] enjoins love of one’s fellow and of the stranger (see “The Concept of Neighbor,” p. 645). “Hillel would say, ‘Be of the followers of Aaron, loving peace, pursuing peace, loving your fellow human beings and bringing them to Torah’ ” (*m. Avot* 1.12). Instead, it is part of the new life to which the disciples are invited (14.15; 15.12–17). Although the “love commandment” is often seen as denoting the love for all humankind, in the context of this Gospel it has a narrower meaning referring to the love among the believers, which is not extended to unbelievers. ^13-34

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-36|36–38]]:** [[Matthew 26#^26-33|Mt 26.33–35]]; [[Mark 14#^14-29|Mk 14.29–30]]; [[Luke 22#^22-33|Lk 22.33–34]]. ^13-36

**[[NRSVue John 13#^13-38|38]]:** See 18.15–27. ^13-38
