### 12.1–6: Woman clothed with the sun

*[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 557* ^12-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-1|1–2]]:** The vivid drama of the *pregnant* woman and the dragon is a *portent* (Gk “semeion”), a symbol to be decoded, rather than a specific heavenly being. The woman should be compared to other symbolic women in apocalyptic visions (*4 Ezra* 9.38–10.54; Herm. *Vis.* ) rather than identified as the Virgin Mary (with whom she came to be linked by the sixth century: Oecumenius, *Comm. on Apocalypse*). She also recalls contemporaneous goddesses like Isis and Astarte. *Twelve stars*, if the woman is Israel, from whom the Messiah is born, these would represent the twelve tribes (see also 2.28n.). *Birth pangs*, draws on the prophetic image of childbirth as a metaphor for the pain and suffering that accompanies the dawning of a new age ([[Isaiah 21#^21-3|Isa 21.3]]; [[Isaiah 26#^26-17|26.17]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-7|66.7]]; [[Jeremiah 04#^4-31|Jer 4.31]]; [[Jeremiah 06#^6-24|6.24]]; [[Jeremiah 13#^13-21|13.21]]). ^12-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-3|3–4a]]:** *Dragon*, identified in v 9 as *Devil*, *Satan*, and *deceiver*, recalls in its *seven heads* and cosmic destruc-tiveness the ancient Semitic chaos-monster Lotan or Leviathan, portrayed in ancient Near Eastern art with seven heads (see [[Psalms 074#^74-13|Ps 74.13–14]]; [[Job 40#^40-25|Job 40.25]]). *Ten horns*, possibly a reference to various rulers, but specific identification is not possible. ^12-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-4|4b]]:** John imagines the dragon receiving the infant directly from the woman’s womb into its mouth. ^12-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-5|5]]:** *Rod of iron*, [[Psalms 002#^2-9|Ps 2.9]] (see 11.18n.). ^12-5

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-6|6]]:** *Wilderness*, here a place of refuge, associated with prophets in flight ([[I Kings 17#^17-3|1 Kings 17.3]]; [[I Kings 19#^19-4|19.4]]; as well as [[Mark 01#^1-12|Mk 1.12–13]]; [[Mark 13#^13-14|13.14]]). *One thousand two hundred sixty days*, see 11.1–2n. The motif of the pregnant woman chased by the dragon, here and in vv 13–17, recalls the story of Leto (and her child, Apollo), pursued by Python. It is not surprising that such local mythology from Asia Minor might inspire visions of a Jewish author from the region. ^12-6

### 12.7–9: War in heaven

*[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-7|Text]], p. 558* · Like the great primal theomachies (divine battles) of Titans versus gods, Baal versus Yamm, and Yahweh versus the dragon ([[Isaiah 27#^27-1|Isa 27.1]]; [[Isaiah 51#^51-9|51.9–10]]; [[Psalms 074#^74-12|Ps 74.12–15]]), the dragon and his angelic army battle with Michael, God’s military angel (Dan 10; 12), and his host. Armies amassing in heaven for the eschatological battle is presumed in [[LXX 2 Maccabees 03#^3-25|2 Macc 3.25–26]]; 1QM; and [[Matthew 26#^26-53|Mt 26.53]]; see also Job 25.2–3. Michael retains his preeminent role as God’s principal warrior and mediator for Israel in Daniel (esp. 12.1), *1 Enoch* (esp. 20), *3 Bar.* 11, and the Qumran scrolls (esp. 1QM 9.15–16; 17.6–8), as well as rabbinic midrash. ^12-7

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-9|9]]:** *Thrown down*, as the other angelic groups called Watchers before the flood, according to the well-known early Jewish tradition (e.g., *1 En*. 6–21). *Ancient serpent*, an occasional term for Satan in rabbinic texts (e.g., *t. Sot.* 1.19; *Sifre Deut*. 323), here uniquely linking Satan to the serpent in [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-1|Gen 3.1–7]] (as also in *2 En.* 31). *Devil . . . Satan . . . thrown down*, cf. [[Luke 10#^10-19|Lk 10.19]], which also links “tread\[ing\]” on serpents with the fall of Satan. ^12-9

### 12.10–12: Satan’s fall

*[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-10|Text]], p. 558* · The song credits God and the Lamb rather than Michael’s forces in defeating Satan (see 12.7–8), indicating that these verses were possibly a secondary addition to the vision itself. *Accuser*, Satan (Heb “adversary, accuser”); see [[Job 01#^1-6|Job 1.6–12]] and “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682. Rabbinic midrashic texts often imagine Satan as a trickster figure, an adversary to Adam and other heroes, rather than an eschatological adversary (e.g., *Gen. Rab.* 38.7; *Sifre Deut.* 218). ^12-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-11|11]]:** *Blood of the Lamb*, see 5.6–7n. ^12-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-12|12]]:** *Rejoice then, you heavens*, [[Isaiah 44#^44-23|Isa 44.23]]; [[Isaiah 49#^49-13|49.13]]; [[Psalms 096#^96-11|Ps 96.11]]. *Woe to the earth and the sea*, may herald the arrival of beasts, ch 13. ^12-12

### 12.13–18: The woman and the dragon

*[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-13|Text]], p. 559* ^12-13

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-14|14]]:** *Great eagle*, perhaps that of 8.13; see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-4|Ex 19.4]]. *Time, and times, and half a time*, three and a half times (see 11.1–2n.). ^12-14

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-15|15–16]]:** Leviathan was associated with floods and uncontrollable waters (see [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-8|Ex 15.8]]; [[Psalms 029#^29-10|Ps 29.10]]); waters pouring from the dragon’s mouth invert the image of childbirth in v 4b. *Earth* (Gk “Ge,” cf. “Gaia”), is here a helpful (female) force, see *2 Bar.* 6.8–9, unlike in Numbers, where the swallowing earth is punitive (16.32; 26.10). ^12-15

**[[NRSVue Revelation 12#^12-17|17]]:** *Her children*, those of whom John approves. *Commandments*, Heb “mitzvot.” John envisions those who keep Torah. ^12-17
