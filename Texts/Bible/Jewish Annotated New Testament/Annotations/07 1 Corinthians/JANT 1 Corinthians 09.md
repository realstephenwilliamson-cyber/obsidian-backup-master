### 9.1–18: Rights of an apostle

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-1|Text]], p. 336* · In this chapter, Paul explains his role as an apostle. ^9-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-1|1]]:** *Apostle*, “one sent out” (see 1.1n.), commissioned by *the Lord* to spread the gospel. *Seen Jesus*, see 15.8–10. ^9-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-2|2]]:** *Seal*, a means of ensuring a document’s authenticity by fastening it with softened wax, lead, or clay impressed with a stamp; used metaphorically: since Paul founded the community, the community authenticated his apostolic role**.** ^9-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-3|3]]:** *Defense*, Gk “apologia,” whence “apologetic.” ^9-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-4|4]]:** *Right*, apostles expected church support. ^9-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-5|5]]:** *Wife*, lit., “sister” (Gk “adelphē”), a member of the church. A “sister-wife” would provide marital companionship, although Paul chose to remain celibate (9.12). *Brothers of the Lord*, see [[Matthew 12#^12-46|Mt 12.46–49]]; [[Matthew 13#^13-55|13.55]]; [[Matthew 28#^28-10|28.10]]; [[Mark 03#^3-31|Mk 3.31–34]]; [[Luke 08#^8-19|Lk 8.19–21]]; [[John 02#^2-12|Jn 2.12]]; [[John 07#^7-3|7.3–10]]; [[John 20#^20-17|20.17]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-19|Gal 1.19]]). *Cephas*, Peter. See 1.12; 3.22; 15.5; according to [[Matthew 08#^8-14|Mt 8.14–15]]; [[Mark 01#^1-29|Mk 1.29–31]]; [[Luke 04#^4-38|Lk 4.38–39]], Jesus healed Cephas’s mother-in-law. ^9-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-6|6]]:** *Barnabas*, Aram for “son of encouragement” ([[Acts 04#^4-36|Acts 4.36]]; [[Acts 09#^9-27|9.27]]; [[Acts 11#^11-27|11.27–30]]; [[Acts 13#^13-8|13.8–13]]; [[Acts 15#^15-38|15.38–40]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-1|Gal 2.1–14]]). *Working for a living*, see 4.12n. ^9-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-8|8]]:** *Law,* of Moses (v 9). ^9-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-9|9–11]]:** *The law*, [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-4|Deut 25.4]]: the *ox* represents apostles; *plows* and *threshes* refer to evangelizing; *treading out* means the apostles’ ability to earn *material benefits* for teaching. Similarly others allegorically interpret [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-4|Deut 25.4]], e.g., to leave fallen harvest for gleaners (Josephus, *Ant.* 4.233–34). Paul uses an argument similar to the rabbinic “qal vahomer” (“a fortiori,” from the lesser to the greater): if oxen can eat, so can apostles. ^9-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-12|12]]:** *This rightful claim*, i.e., to food and a wife. *Obstacle*, Paul worried that the financial burden of his support might impede *the way of the gospel*, namely community membership*.* ^9-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-13|13]]:** *Temple,* (Gk “hieron”) can also refer to pagan shrines (e.g., [[LXX Judith 04#^4-1|Jdt 4.1]]; Bel 8, Bel \[Theodotion\] 22). Jewish and pagan temple sacrifices sustained their priests ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^18-8|Num 18.8–32]], [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-1|Deut 18.1–5]]; Josephus *Ant.* 3.224–36). ^9-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-14|14]]:** *The Lord*, Jesus ([[Matthew 10#^10-10|Mt 10.10]]; [[Luke 10#^10-7|Lk 10.7]]). ^9-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-15|15–16]]:** *Boasting*, see 5.6. ^9-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-17|17]]:** Free will and compulsion are two motivations for proclaiming the gospel (see [[Jeremiah 01#^1-5|Jer 1.5]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-15|Gal 1.15]]). ^9-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-18|18]]:** *Reward*, Paul declines apostolic rights to monetary compensation in order to teach *free of charge*, paralleling the rabbinic notion of “sekhar mitzvah mitzvah,” the performance of a divine imperative is its own reward (*m. Avot* 4.2). Rabbis were not formally compensated for their teaching until the fourteenth century, and even then the payment was construed as compensation for lost wages rather than remuneration (*Magen Avot* on *m. Avot* 4.5). ^9-18

### 9.19–23: Slave to all

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|Text]], p. 337* · Paul asserts that he is not pretending to be something he is not in order to persuade people under false pretenses to join the community. Rather, as the clause in v 21 suggests, he addresses his audiences in terms that they can understand. ^9-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|19]]:** *Free with respect to all*, belonging to no one. *Slave* (Gk “doulos”), devoted to the well-being of others in the service of God (LXX [[LXX Jeremiah 07#^7-25|Jer 7.25]]; [[Jeremiah 25#^25-4|25.4]]; [[Ezekiel 38#^38-17|Ezek 38.17]]; [[Joel 3#^3-2|Joel 2.29]]; Am 3.7; [[Zechariah 01#^1-6|Zech 1.6]]). ^9-19-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-20|20]]:** *As a Jew*, Paul argued from the perspective of Jewish interlocutors. *Under the law*, Paul argued from the perspective of following Mosaic Law. ^9-20

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-21|21]]:** *Outside the law*, the perspective of Gentiles neither bound nor protected by Torah. *Under Christ’s law*, the Gk preposition “en” is better translated “in” rather than *under* (see [[Galatians 06#^6-2|Gal 6.2]]). Paul never prohibits Jews in the church from following Torah. See “Freedom from the Law,” p. 331. ^9-21

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-22|22]]:** *Weak*, see 1.27; 2.3; 4.10; 8.7–12; 12.22. ^9-22

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-23|23]]:** *Blessings*, fulfilled promises of the kingdom. ^9-23

### 9.24–27: The good race

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-24|Text]], p. 338* ^9-24

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-24|24–25]]:** *Only one . . . perishable wreath*, winners of Greek athletic competitions were crowned with a wreath; the race for the kingdom has many victors who receive an *imperishable* prize, a resurrected body; the athletic race as a metaphor for the moral course of life is rare in Second Temple literature (see Philo, *Migr.* 133). ^9-24-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 09#^9-27|27]]:** *Punish my body and enslave it*, continuing the athletic metaphor: self-discipline required to maintain bodily purity in order to win the race for the kingdom. *Disqualified,* from the kingdom. ^9-27
