For how the replays enter, I'd use the DJ mix instead of the *Ocean's* montage. The past scene's ambient rhythm (footsteps, a dryer, the leak from the old woman's headphones) gets beatmatched under whatever room Cole is standing in, then crossfaded until the memory plays on the floor's tempo. That renders clarity as audio fidelity. The first time we hear her headphones, they're a tinny smear. On replay they're full-range and locked to the DnB room's kick. Pair that with a visual rule: sentries sit in soft focus the first time and get pulled sharp on replay, which means shooting prologue coverage for both versions. The audience learns that when the sound locks, Cole is seeing.

One discipline I'd hold firmly: replays should reveal only what was already in frame. *Ocean's* shows you footage you never got, which is fine for a heist and would be cheating here. Your theology gives the reason. Enlightenment in this film is recognition, not acquisition, the Pratyabhijñā point that nothing new is added and what was always there becomes visible. A replay that invents facts turns awakening into a plot twist. A replay that only changes what we notice (the old man was two stools down the whole time) puts the audience through a small version of Cole's experience. They had it all along too.

I'd also push back on "clearer." Drug clarity doesn't rise in a straight line, and the film is stronger if it doesn't either. Coming up clarifies. The peak can overshoot into seeing too much: every background figure lit, patterns everywhere, some of them not real. That's where your *Hellraiser* register lives, and it's where the mentor earns his place. The chemicals give Cole perception, and the mentor gives him the frame that separates insight from noise. That's your "lacking guidance" thesis, dramatized.

The drug curve also hands you a second clock. The camps need Cole to choose at midnight, at peak, while he's wide open. The mentor might want the opposite: wait for the comedown and see whether the recognition holds at dawn on 1/1/00, sober. That makes the third option partly a matter of timing, refusing to choose on their schedule.
