**1.** It is a positive commandment of the Torah[^1] to relate[^2] the miracles and wonders wrought for our ancestors in Egypt on the night[^3] of the fifteenth of Nisan,[^4] as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-3|Exodus 13:3]]] states: "Remember this day,[^5] on which you left Egypt,"[^6] just as \[[[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-8|Exodus 20:8]]] states: "Remember the Sabbath[^7] day."  
From where [is it derived that this mitzvah is to be fulfilled on] the night of the fifteenth? The Torah teaches \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-8|Exodus 13:8]]]: "And you shall tell your son on that day,[^8] saying: 'It is because of this...'[^9] [implying that the mitzvah[^10] is to be fulfilled] when matzah and maror are placed before you.[^11]  
[The mitzvah applies] even though one does not have a son.[^12] Even great Sages are obligated to tell about the Exodus from Egypt.[^13] Whoever elaborates concerning the events which occurred and took place is worthy of praise.[^14] ^7-1

**2.** It is a mitzvah to inform one's sons even though they do not ask, as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-8|Exodus 13:8]]] states: "You shall tell your son."  
A[^15] father should teach his son according to the son's knowledge:[^16] How is this applied? If the son is young or foolish, he should tell him: "My son, in Egypt, we were all slaves like this maidservant or this slave. On this night, the Holy One, Blessed be He, redeemed us and took us out to freedom."  
If[^17] the son is older and wise, he should inform him what happened to us in Egypt and the miracles wrought for us by Moses, our teacher;[^18] everything according to the son's knowledge.[^19] ^7-2

**3.** He[^20] should make changes on this night so that the children will see[^21] and will[^22] [be motivated to] ask: "Why is this night different from all other nights?"[^23] until he replies to them: "This and this occurred; this and this took[^24] place."  
What changes should be made? He should give them roasted seeds and nuts;[^25] the table should be taken away before they eat;[^26] matzot should be snatched from each other[^27] and the like.[^28]  
When a person does not have a son, his wife should ask him.[^29] If he does not have a wife, [he and a colleague] should ask each other: "Why is this night different?" This applies even if they are all wise.[^30] A person who is alone should ask himself: "Why is this night different?"[^31] ^7-3

**4.** One must begin [the narrative describing our ancestors'] base [roots] and conclude with [their] praise.[^32] What does this imply? One begins relating how originally, in the age of Terach,[^33] our ancestors denied [God's existence][^34] and strayed after vanity, pursuing idol worship.[^35] One concludes with the true faith:[^36] how the Omnipresent has drawn us close to Him, separated us from the gentiles, and drawn us near to His Oneness.[^37]  
Similarly, one begins by stating that we were slaves to Pharaoh in Egypt and [describing] all the evil done to us,[^38] and concludes with the miracles and wonders that were wrought upon us, and our freedom.[^39]  
This [implies] that one should extrapolate[^40] [the passage beginning] from \[[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-5|Deuteronomy 26:5]]]: "An Aramean sought to destroy my ancestor..."[^41] until one concludes the entire passage.[^42] Whoever adds and extends his extrapolation[^43] of this passage is praiseworthy. ^7-4

**5.** Whoever does not mention these three matters on the night of the fifteenth has not fulfilled his obligation. They are: the Paschal sacrifice, matzah, and maror.  
The Paschal sacrifice: [It is eaten] because the Omnipresent passed over the houses of our ancestors in Egypt as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-27|Exodus 12:27]]] states: "And you shall say: 'It is the Paschal sacrifice to God.'  
The bitter herbs: [They are eaten] because the Egyptians embittered the lives of our ancestors in Egypt.  
The matzah: [It is eaten] because of the redemption. These statements are all referred to as the Haggadah. ^7-5

**6.** In each and every generation, a person must present himself[^44] as if he, himself, has now[^45] left the slavery of Egypt,[^46] as \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-23|Deuteronomy 6:23]]] states: "He took[^47] us out from there."[^48] Regarding this manner, God commanded in the Torah: "Remember that you were a slave \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-15|Deuteronomy 5:15]]]"[^49] - i.e., as if you, yourself,[^50] were a slave and went out to freedom and were redeemed. ^7-6

**7.** Therefore, when a person feasts on this night,[^51] he must eat and drink[^52] while he is reclining[^53] in the manner of free men.[^54] Each and every one,[^55] both men and women,[^56] must drink four[^57] cups of wine[^58] on this night. [This number] should not be reduced.[^59] Even a poor person who is sustained by charity should not have fewer than four cups.[^60] The size of each of these cups[^61] should be a quarter [of a *log*].[^62] ^7-7

**8.** Even one of Israel's poor should not eat until he [can] recline.[^63] A woman need not recline.[^64] If she is an important woman, she must recline.[^65] [Even] a son in the presence of his father or an attendant in the presence of his master must recline.[^66] However, a student before his teacher[^67] should not recline[^68] unless his teacher grants him permission.[^69]  
Reclining on one's right side is not considered reclining.[^70] Neither is reclining on one's back or forwards.[^71][^72][^73]  
When must one recline? when eating the כזית of matzah and when drinking these four cups of wine.[^74] While eating and drinking at other times:[^75] if one reclines, it is praiseworthy;[^76] if not, there is no requirement.[^77] ^7-8

**9.** These four cups [of wine] should be mixed with water so that drinking them will be pleasant.[^78] [The degree to which they are mixed] all depends on[^79] the wine and the preference of the person drinking.[^80] [Together,] these four [cups] should contain at least a quarter [of a *log*] of pure wine.[^81]  
A person who drank these four cups from wine which was not mixed [with water][^82] has fulfilled the obligation to drink four cups of wine, but has not fulfilled the obligation to do so in a manner expressive of freedom.[^83]  
A person who drank these four cups of wine mixed [with water] at one time[^84] has fulfilled the obligation to drink wine in a manner expressive of freedom, but has not fulfilled the obligation of four cups of wine.[^85]  
A person who drank the majority [of the cup] from each of these [four] cups has fulfilled his obligation.[^86] ^7-9

**10.** On each of these four cups, one recites a blessing of its own.[^87] In addition:[^88]  
On the first cup, one recites the *kiddush* pertaining to the day;  
On[^89] the second cup, one reads the Haggadah;  
On[^90] the third cup, one recites the grace after meals;  
On[^91] the fourth cup, one concludes the Hallel[^92] and recites the blessing for songs [of praise].[^93]  
Between these cups, should one desire to drink, one may.[^94] Between the third and the fourth cup, one should not drink. ^7-10

**11.** The *charoset* is a mitzvah ordained by the words of the Sages,[^95] to commemorate the clay[^96] with which [our forefathers] worked in Egypt.[^97] How is it made?  
We take dates, dried figs, or raisins and the like, and crush them,[^98] add vinegar to them,[^99] and mix them with spices,[^100] as clay is mixed into straw.[^101] This is placed on the table[^102] on [the first two] nights of Pesach. ^7-11

**12.** According to the Torah, the eating of bitter herbs is not a mitzvah in its own right,[^103] but rather is dependent on the consumption of the Paschal sacrifice.[^104] It is one positive commandment to eat the meat of the Paschal sacrifice together with matzah and bitter herbs.[^105] According to the words of the Sages, [it is a mitzvah][^106] to eat the bitter herbs alone[^107] on this night even if there is no Paschal sacrifice. ^7-12

**13.** The bitter herbs referred to by the Torah are Romaine lettuce,[^108] endives, horseradish, date ivy,[^109] wormwood.[^110] All of these five species of vegetable are called maror. If a person ate a כזית[^111] of any one of these [species] or of all five [species] combined, he has fulfilled his obligation.[^112]  
This applies while they[^113] are still moist. One may fulfill one's obligation with their stem[^114] even if it is dry. One cannot fulfill one's obligation if they are boiled,[^115] pickled,[^116] or cooked. ^7-13
