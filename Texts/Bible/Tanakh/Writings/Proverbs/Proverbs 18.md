# Proverbs 18

← [[Proverbs 17]] · [[Tanakh]] · [[Proverbs 19]] →

Compare: [[NRSVue Proverbs 18|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 18

**18:1** He who isolates himself pursues his desires;  
He disdains all competence.[^1] ^18-1

**18:2** The fool does not desire understanding,  
But only to air his thoughts. ^18-2

**18:3** Comes the wicked man comes derision,  
And with the rogue, contempt. ^18-3

**18:4** The words a man speaks are deep waters,  
A flowing stream, a fountain of wisdom. ^18-4

**18:5** It is not right to be partial to the guilty  
And subvert the innocent in judgment. ^18-5

**18:6** The words of a fool lead to strife;  
His speech invites blows. ^18-6

**18:7** The fool’s speech is his ruin;  
His words are a trap for him. ^18-7

**18:8** The words of a querulous man are bruising;[^2]  
They penetrate one’s inmost parts. ^18-8

**18:9** One who is slack in his work  
Is a brother to a vandal. ^18-9

**18:10** The name of the LORD is a tower of strength  
To which the righteous man runs and is safe. ^18-10

**18:11** The wealth of a rich man is his fortress;  
In his fancy[^3] it is a protective wall. ^18-11

**18:12** Before ruin a man’s heart is proud;  
Humility goes before honor. ^18-12

**18:13** To answer a man before hearing him out  
Is foolish and disgraceful. ^18-13

**18:14** A man’s spirit can sustain him through illness;  
But low spirits—who can bear them? ^18-14

**18:15** The mind of an intelligent man acquires knowledge;  
The ears of the wise seek out knowledge. ^18-15

**18:16** A man’s gift eases his way  
And gives him access to the great. ^18-16

**18:17** The first to plead his case seems right  
Till the other party examines him. ^18-17

**18:18** The lot puts an end to strife  
And separates those locked in dispute. ^18-18

**18:19** A brother offended is more formidable than a stronghold;  
Such strife is like the bars of a fortress. ^18-19

**18:20** A man’s belly is filled by the fruit of his mouth;  
He will be filled by the produce of his lips. ^18-20

**18:21** Death and life are in the power of the tongue;  
Those who love it will eat its fruit. ^18-21

**18:22** He who finds a wife has found happiness  
And has won the favor of the LORD. ^18-22

**18:23** The poor man speaks beseechingly;  
The rich man’s answer is harsh. ^18-23

**18:24** There are companions to keep one company,  
And there is a friend more devoted than a brother. ^18-24

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^2]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
