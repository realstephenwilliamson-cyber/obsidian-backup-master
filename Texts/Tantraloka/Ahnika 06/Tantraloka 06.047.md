## Mūla

ओतप्रोतात्मकः प्राणस्तथापीत्थं न सुस्फुटः ।
यत्नो जीवनमात्रात्मा तत्परश्च द्विधा मतः ॥ ^mula

otaprotātmakaḥ prāṇastathāpītthaṃ na susphuṭaḥ |
yatno jīvanamātrātmā tatparaśca dvidhā mataḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IV
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 04 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v04 09 - Chapter Six, part 2#^pg274|p. 259]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 04.pdf#page=274>))
>
> Even though the vital breath is present in every way, woven warp and woof within this body up to the End of the Twelve, it is not very clearly evident that (it is such) in this way. (with 6.46cd–47ab)
>
> Exertion (yatna) (that is, the movement of the vital breath) is considered to be of two kinds. A1) (The first is) just vitality itself (jīvanamātra). A2) (The second kind) is intent on (impelling) that (fatpara). Again, it is divided into two (other) kinds, according to B1) whether (the vital breath and its movement) is perceptible (sarivedya) or B2) imperceptible (asarivedya). (Moreover,) each one (of these four) is discerned to be of two kinds, according to whether it is clearly evident (sphuṭa) or not (sphuṭa). (with 6.47cd–48)
