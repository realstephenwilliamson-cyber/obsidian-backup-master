# Tertiary Retention

> [!abstract] Definition
> Bernard Stiegler's extension of Husserlian phenomenology, denoting exteriorized, prosthetic memory supports (tools, writing, digital databases, code) that record and transmit experience across generations beyond the biological lifespan.

## Theoretical Foundations
In Edmund Husserl’s analysis of time-consciousness (*On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time*):
1. **Primary Retention:** The immediate perception of the passing present (the trailing note of a melody as it sounds).
2. **Secondary Retention:** The psychic recollection of a past present in memory (remembering the melody after it has stopped).

Stiegler intervenes in [[Stiegler - Technics and Time (vols 1-3)|Technics and Time, Vol. 1]] by establishing that primary and secondary retentions are always already conditioned by **tertiary retention**—prosthetic, technical memory supports. Tools and alphabets are not external additions to a pre-existing human consciousness; they are the condition of possibility for human temporality (*epiphylogenesis*).

## Diagnostic in the Digital Epoch
In computational capitalism, tertiary retentions are no longer inert tools (scrolls, books) but dynamic, machine-speed algorithmic systems. This automates the selection criteria of collective memory, leading to [[Temporal Short-Circuits]] and [[The Crisis of Curation]].
