## Mūla

कुरुवर्षस्योत्तरेऽथ वायव्येऽब्धौ क्रमाच्छराः ।
दश चेति सहस्राणि द्वीपौ चन्द्रोऽथ भद्रकः ॥ ^mula

kuruvarṣasyottare'tha vāyavye'bdhau kramāccharāḥ |
daśa ceti sahasrāṇi dvīpau candro'tha bhadrakaḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 04 - Chapter Eight, part 2#^pg139|p. 121]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=139>))
>
> Outside that (to the north of) Śṛṅgavat (Peak), which is to the north of Meru, is the land of Kuru. Shaped like a bow, it extends for nine thousand leagues. The creatures there live for thirteen (thousand years). To the north and northwest of the land of Kuru, in the ocean (of salt), there are two islands, 1) Candra (Moon) and 2) Bhadra (Auspicious). They measure, respectively, five thousand and ten thousand leagues. (with 8.72–73)
