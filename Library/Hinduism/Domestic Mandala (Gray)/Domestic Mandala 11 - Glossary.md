## Glossary

Note: This glossary only includes Nepali words that appear more than once in the book. The first time a Nepali word appears in the text, it is in italics with diacritical marks; subsequently it is spelled in transliterated form. 

*achhetā,* achheta Unbroken rice grains suitable for offering to deities. 

*agena,* agena Area for preparing snacks. 

*argha,* argha Yoni-shaped vessel for liquid offerings to deities as part of worship rite (puja). 

*ashuddha*, ashuddha Impurity. 

*astadal,* astadal Auspicious eight-pointed geometric design. 

*Baṇgśawāli,* Written genealogy tracing the origins, location, and 

Bangshawali membership in a clan. 

*Bartaman,* Bartaman Rite of passage in which boys receive religious instruction and the sacred thread indicative of their twice born status. 

*bāto,* bato Road or path. 

*bhāt*, bhat Boiled rice. 

*bhūmi,* bhumi The earth or ground; also the name of the deity associated with it. 

*Bhut-pret-pichās,* Evil spirits or ghosts of those who died unnaturally 

Bhut-pret-pichas and cannot become an ancestor; these spirits remain in the human realm where they possess humans causing illness and death. 

*bibāhā,* bibaha, Rite of marriage. 

*bidya*, bidya Knowledge or learning; is used to refer to the knowledge that witches acquire to curse and cause illness. 

*bindhu,* bindhu Centre point of a mandala representing the fundamental unity of the cosmos. 

*[p. 148]* ^pg165

*birakta,* birakta Detachment, ascetism. 

*boksi,* boksi Witch. 

*Brahma,* Brahma The deity associated with the fundamental unity of the cosmos. 

*Brahmachārya,* The first stage of life when a boy becomes a celebate 

Brahmacharya student receiving religious instruction from a guru. 

*chāmal*, chamal Milled rice used for cooking. 

*chelibeti,* chelibeti Sisters and daughters who are considered sacred. 

*Chhetri,* Chhetri The second ranking Parbatiya caste associated with warriors and kings. 

*chokho,* choko Clean or pure. 

*chula,* chula Hearth; area where main meals are prepared and eaten. 

*chyura*, chyura Rice that has been fried and beaten into flakes; use for snacks with tea or alcohol. 

*Damāi,* Damai Tailor caste. 

*dān*, dan An unreciprocated gift to gain merit. 

*deota,* deota Deity. 

*dhārma,* dharma Religious or ethical duty associated with one’s caste or stage of life. 

*dhog,* dhog Touching one’s forehead to the feet of another as a display of respect to a person of superordinate status. 

*dhup,* dhup Incense. 

*Dio,* Dio Established as a deity associated with light for preliminary worship in a puja. 

*dobāto,* dobato The place where two roads or paths cross. 

*dos,* dos Blame, inauspiciousness. 

*dubo,* dubo Sacred grass associated with longevity. 

*gaudān,* gaudan Literally the gift (dan) of the cow (gau); given to a Brahmin priest during major rituals. 

*gāun,* gaun Village or hamlet. 

*ghar,* ghar House including surrounding courtyard. 

*graha,* graha Planet or astral body. 

*[p. 149]* ^pg166

*Gṛhastha,* Grihastha The Householder stage of life. The second stage of life during which a person marries, raised children and worships the deities. 

*gyān,* gyan Religious knowledge. 

*hom,* hom Rite offering grains and ghee to a fire officiated by a Brahmin priest. 

*jag,* jag Foundation of a house. 

*jagya,* jagya Sacred enclosure for weddings and the boys rites of passage in the middle of which is a fire to which hom offerings are made. 

*jamin,* jamin Land. 

*janāi,* janai Sacred thread worn by male of twice-born castes. 

*janti,* janti Wedding procession. 

*jāt,* jat Caste. 

*juṭho,* jutho Impurity, pollution. 

*kalaś,* kalash Sacred water vessel. 

*Kāmi,* Kami Blacksmith caste. 

*kāmya,* kamya Desirable or object of desire; a kamya puja is performed for worldly benefits. 

*kanya,* Kanya Virgin. 

*khus,* khus Sacred grass. 

*kuṇḍali,* kundali A horoscope based upon the position of the planets at the time of a child’s birth. 

*Mahādev,* Mahadev Lit. the great deity; Shiva. 

*maṇḍala,* mandala Mystic diagram of the cosmos. 

*mandap,* mandap Lit. sacred pavilion; in many rituals, a mandap is drawn on the ground or a ritual plate as the place into which the deity is invoked. 

*maṭān,* matan Small lobby on the second floor of a traditional house. 

*māya,* maya Illusion of mundane experience. 

*mul,* mul Source, origin main or chief as in main road or origin house of a descent group. 

*[p. 150]* ^pg167

*murti,* murti Statue or image of and for a deity. 

*nāg,* nag Serpent, snake; associated with water and wealth; subterranean serpents pose a danger to house because their movement and wriggling can cause cracking in houses. 

*nau-graha,* Nau graha The nine astral bodies. 

*Nwāran,* Nwaran Rite of passage in which a child is given its formal. name by a Brahmin priest based upon the time of birth. 

*Pārbātiya,* Parbatiya Lit. people of the hills; used to refer to the castes who migrated from northern India into the hill regions of Nepal. 

*pariwār,* Pariwar Household group. 

*pitṛ,* pitri Spirits of the ancestors. 

*prasād,* prasad Blessing given by deity reciprocating the offering made by a worshipper in puja. 

*pūjā,* Puja Rite of worship and devotion to a deity. 

*Puruṣa,* Purusha The primeval being whose self-sacrifice is the origin of all beings and things of the world. 

*Rakṣā bandhan,* A cotton string coloured yellow with turmeric tied 

Raksha bandhan around the wrist after worshiping a deitiy. The raksa-bandhan (literally a bond \[bandhan\] of protection \[raksa\]) is the manifestation and mechanism of the beneficent and harmonious bond the worshipper has established with the deity as a result the worship. It protects him or her from dangerous beings, such as disembodied ghosts. 

*rekhi,* rekhi Diagrams composed of lines and geometric shapes drawn with coloured powder on the ground by household priests for important rituals such as hom sacrifices performed in rites of passage and in rites of house consecration and inauguration. 

*samṛddhi,* samriddhi Prosperity or wealth; abundance of valued things including material wealth as well as children, well-being and happiness. 

*samsāra,* samsara The continual cycle of death and rebirth. 

*[p. 151]* ^pg168

*samskāra*, samskara Rites marking points in the life cycle from conception to marriage. 

*sankrānti,* sankranti The first day of the month. 

*santān,* santan Descendents, progeny; the two to three generation patrilineal descent group of a man, i.e. a man, his sons and grandsons. 

*Sanyāsin,* Sanyasin The final stage of life when a person renounces all worldly relations and possessions. 

*Sārki,* Sarki Leatherworker caste. 

*sāyat,* sayat Auspicious time. 

*śākti,* shakta/shakti Active, creative energy of the cosmos; manifest in female deities and humans 

*shuddha*, shuddha Pure. 

*Śiva,* Shiva A major god of the Hindu pantheon. 

*silā,* sila Stone, sometimes with sacred qualities. 

*stambha,* stambha Supporting pillar in a house. 

*Subha,* subha Auspicious. 

*tham,* tham Main pillar in a house. 

*ṭikā,* tika A mark of worship towards a divine being; also a mark of blessing put on the forehead. 

*toran,* toran A long festoon of flowers used on festive occasions, such as on the branches forming the marriage jagya or around a house for the inauguration of a new house. 

*Vanaprastha,* Third stage of life when Householders retreat to the 

Vanaprastha forest. 

*vāstu puruṣa maṇḍala,* A mandala used in designing a building. It consists of 

vastu purusha mandala a image of Purusha, the primeval being, superimposed on a grid. 

*vāstu vidya,* vastu vidya Hindu knowledge of architecture. 

*yantra,* yantra Mystic diagram using geometric symbols to represent the cosmos.
