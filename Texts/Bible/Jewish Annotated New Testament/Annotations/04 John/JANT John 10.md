### 10.1–42: Good shepherd discourse

*[[NRSVue John 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 198* ^10-1

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-1|1–6]]:** A *figure of speech* (v 6), comparing Jesus to a good shepherd. ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Thief . . . bandit* may refer to the Jewish leadership (v 8). ^10-1-3

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Shepherd*, an allusion to biblical shepherds who led Israel, e.g., Moses ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-1|Ex 3.1]]), David ([[II Samuel 05#^5-2|2 Sam 5.2]]), and God (Ps 23). ^10-2

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-3|3]]:** *Hear . . . calls . . . leads*, the language recalls 5.5 and anticipates 11.43. This may be an allusion to the “harrowing of hell,” the idea that Jesus spent the days between his crucifixion and resurrection in Hades bringing the dead to faith ([[1 Peter 03#^3-19|1 Pet 3.19]], as interpreted in the *Apostles’ Creed* and *Gos. Nic.* ). If so, it promises resurrection not only of those who are alive at the time of Jesus but to those who died before, an issue that Paul also addressed (see [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-13|1 Thess 4.13–18]]). ^10-3

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-5|5]]:** *Stranger*, the identity is not specified. ^10-5

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-6|6]]:** *They did not understand*, prompting Jesus’ more explicit explanation in vv 7–21. ^10-6

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-7|7]]:** *I am the gate*, the figure changes from shepherd to the one who is the means by which sheep are brought in. ^10-7

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-8|8]]:** *Thieves . . . bandits*, see 10.1n. ^10-8

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-10|10]]:** *Life . . . abundantly*, eternal life. ^10-10

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-12|12]]:** *Hired hand*, may refer to the Jewish leadership (see [[Ezekiel 34#^34-1|Ezek 34.1–10]]). *Wolf*, evil; a more specific referent cannot be determined. ^10-12

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-16|16]]:** *Other sheep*, perhaps Gentile followers, or Christ-followers who are not part of John’s community. ^10-16

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-18|18]]:** *No one takes it from me*, the textual variant in the aorist (a type of past) tense, present in P45, may be original as it is the harder reading. In either case, Jesus is here asserting that he is not a passive victim but that he will lay down his life willingly. *Command*, his willing death is Jesus’ response to God’s will. ^10-18

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-22|22]]:** *Festival of the Dedication*, Hanukkah (beginning 25 Chislev, late November/December), commemorating the rededication of the Temple (164 BCE), after its desecration by the Seleucid king Antiochus IV ([[LXX 1 Maccabees 04#^4-52|1 Macc 4.52–59]]). It is unclear clear how this feast was observed in the first century. ^10-22

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-30|30]]:** *Father and I . . . one*, Jesus reiterates his unity with God, which the Jews see as blasphemy (v 33). The Gospel’s Jews apparently take this statement as an indication that Jesus is making himself equal to God (as in 5.17–18). In the context of the Gospel, this unity may also, or instead, refer to the singleness of purpose; since Jesus does the Father’s will, they work as one through Jesus’ activity in the world. ^10-30

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-31|31]]:** *Stones*, stoning is the prescribed penalty for blasphemy ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-16|Lev 24.16]]; *m. Sanh*. 7.4). It is difficult to know, however, what constituted blasphemy in the first century. The context suggests that Jesus’ utterances, here, and elsewhere (e.g., 5.17) are seen by the Johannine Jews as a violation of the fundamental belief in the one, unique God. *M. Sanh*. 7.5 states that the death sentence by stoning applies only to cases in which someone has uttered the Tetragrammaton and in which that person has been warned by two witnesses prior to the transgression. In the Talmud, [[Sanhedrin 56#56a|*b. Sanh.* 56a]], R. Meir extends this punishment to cases where the blasphemer had used one of the attributes, i.e., substitute names, of God. There is no evidence that anyone was *given them to me is greater than all, and no one can snatch them out of the Father’s hand* executed for blasphemy. ^10-31

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-32|32]]:** *Good works*, perhaps a reference to healings. ^10-32

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-33|33]]:** *Making yourself God*, see v 30. The Johannine Jesus would insist that it is God and not Jesus himself who has made him anything at all. ^10-33

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-34|34]]:** *Your law*, see 8.17n. “Law” can refer not only to the Torah but also to other writings, such as Psalms, that became part of the Jewish canon. *I said, you are gods*, [[Psalms 082#^82-6|Ps 82.6]]. [[Psalms 082#^82-6|Ps 82.6–7]] reads: “I say, ‘You are gods, children of the Most High, all of you; nevertheless, you shall die like mortals, and fall like any prince.’ ” While the Psalm is referring to judges as “gods,” Jesus uses this verse to counter the charge that he has blasphemed: if mortals such as judges can be called gods, why is it blasphemy for Jesus, or others, to call him God? ^10-34

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-39|39]]:** *Arrest him again*, previous attempts were in 7.30,32,44. ^10-39

**[[NRSVue John 10#^10-41|41–42]]:** *John*, the Baptist’s testimony cited as corroborating Jesus’ claims (see 1.19–28; 3.27–30). ^10-41
