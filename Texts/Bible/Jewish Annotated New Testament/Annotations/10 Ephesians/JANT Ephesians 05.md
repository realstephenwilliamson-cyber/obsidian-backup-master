# Ephesians 5 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Ephesians 04|← Ephesians 4]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Ephesians 06|Ephesians 6 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Ephesians 05|NRSVue]] · [[Ephesians 05|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Ephesians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-1|5.1]]:** *Imitators of God*, see also [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-2|Lev 19.2]]. The Talmud teaches: “Just as He clothes the naked . . . so, too, should you clothe the naked; just as He visited the sick . . . so, too, should you visit the sick, etc.” (*b. Sot*. 14a). ^5-1

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-2|2]]:** *A fragrant offering and sacrifice*, lit., “an offering and sacrifice . . . into an aroma of sweet smell”; “the minḥah” (fragrant grain offering) and “ ʿolah” (burnt offering) were burnt on the altar to create a fragrant offering; see [[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^1-9|Lev 1.9]] and 2.9. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-3|3–4]]:** *Vulgar talk*, on the power of the tongue, see [[Psalms 034#^34-14|Ps 34.13]]**–**15 and [[Matthew 12#^12-36|Mt 12.36]]**–**7; the fear may be that mentioning something leads to thinking about it, which may lead to doing it. ^5-3

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-5|5]]:** *That is, an idolater*, it is not clear whether the reference is to “one who is greedy” or also to fornicators and impure persons. The sinful “other” is no longer the generic Gentile but, rather, the idolater in particular. The rabbis have no Heb word for idolater but speak instead of “those who worship strange/foreign worship”; see, e.g., *m. Sanh.* 7.6. ^5-5

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-8|8]]:** *Darkness . . . children of light*, a common dichotomy; see, e.g., [[Luke 16#^16-8|Lk 16.8]]; [[John 12#^12-36|Jn 12.36]]; 1QM (the “War Scroll,” depicting the battle of the Sons of Light and Sons of Darkness), and 1QS 3.13**–**4.14 (which associates light with truth and darkness with deceit). ^5-8

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-12|12]]:** *Shameful even to mention what such people do secretly*, *b. Yoma* 86b questions whether it is proper to confess publically sins done in private, as all transgressions are evidence of the sinner’s rebellion against God. ^5-12

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Awake! Rise*, perhaps from an early Christian hymn, based upon [[Isaiah 60#^60-1|Isa 60.1]]. ^5-14

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-15|15]]:** *How you live*, lit., “how you walk,” conceptually the same as “halakhah.” *Not as unwise people but as wise*, unwise and wise associated with darkness and light in [[Ecclesiastes 02#^2-13|Eccl 2.13]]**–**14 and elsewhere. ^5-15

> [!note] The Household Code *(p. 395)*
> Ephesians supplements a fairly standard discussion of the management of a household (*oikonomia*, lit., “law of the household”; see Aristotle, *Pol*. 1259a-b) by means of a literal application of the ancient biblical metaphor—God is to Israel as husband is to wife or as father is to child, replacing God with Christ. In each relationship, the hierarchically superior figure (husband, father, master) is in the position of Christ, to whom the inferior figure should be subject or obedient. In each case the superior is called upon to care for the inferior (husbands should love their wives as Christ loves the church), but this does not eliminate the hierarchy. Detailed regulations, reflecting and reinforcing similar hierarchies, are found in early rabbinic literature (for husband-wife, see *m. Ketub*. 5.5**–**9; for father-child, see *m. Qidd*. 1.7 and *t. Qidd*. 1.11; for master-slave, see *Sifra*, *behar*, ch 7 and par. 6), but nowhere is such a theological justification offered. The author’s urging that a husband must love his wife (vv 25 and 28) is unparalleled in the writings of the early rabbis, who do, however, value tenderness and care in the marriage relationship; see, e.g., *b. Ned.* 20b.

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-16|16]]:** *Because the days are evil*, see [[Torah 11 - Vayigash#^47-9|Gen 47.9]]; [[Job 14#^14-1|Job 14.1]]. ^5-16

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-18|18]]:** *Do not get drunk with wine*, condemning not wine—which would have been difficult given the centrality of wine to the diet in most of the Roman world, including Judea and Galilee—but drunkenness. [[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-9|Lev 10.9]] forbids alcohol to priests approaching the sanctum for service. The rabbis express their concern for drunkenness but, on balance, consider the benefits of wine to outweigh its liabilities; see *b. Eruv*. 64a**–**65b. ^5-18

### 5.21–6.9: The household code

*[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-21|Text]], p. 396* · Prescribing relations between husbands and wives, parents and children, and masters and slaves; see “The Household Code,” p. 395. ^5-21

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-22|22]]:** *Wives, be subject to your husbands*, in biblical Heb, the same word (“baʿal”) serves for both “husband” and “master.” [[Hosea 02#^2-18|Hos 2.18]] speaks of the day when Israel will no longer call God “my master/my baʿal” but “my man.” ^5-22

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-23|23]]:** *The husband is the head . . . as Christ is the head*, the relationship between husband and wife is a common metaphor for the relationship between God and the covenantal community in the Tanakh; see, e.g., Hos 1**–**2. ^5-23

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-25|25]]:** *Husbands, love your wives*, by contrast to wives, who are to be “subject to” their husbands. Despite the balance suggested by v 21, the prescribed relationship is not egalitarian. ^5-25

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-27|27]]:** *Without a spot or wrinkle . . . holy and without blemish*, physical perfection is analogized to spiritual perfection. ^5-27

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-28|28]]:** *Husbands should love their wives as they do their own bodies*, the rabbis similarly teach that “a man’s wife is like his own body” (*b. Ber*. 24a). *He who loves his wife loves himself*, in fulfillment of [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]. The rabbis also apply this verse to one’s wife and understand it as a command to protect one’s wife’s dignity; see *b. Qidd*. 41a and b*. Nidd.* 17a. ^5-28

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 05#^5-31|31–32]]:** Construing [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-24|Gen 2.24]], describing a man abandoning his parents and becoming one flesh with his wife, as referring to Christ’s uniting with the church. See [[Mark 10#^10-8|Mk 10.8]]; [[Matthew 19#^19-5|Mt 19.5]]**–**6 where [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-24|Gen 2.24]] relates to human marriage; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-16|1 Cor 6.16]] applies [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-24|Gen 2.24]] to sexual relations with a prostitute. *Great mystery*, see 3.3n.; 3.5n. ^5-31
