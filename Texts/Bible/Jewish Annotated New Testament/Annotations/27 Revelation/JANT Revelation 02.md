### 2.1–3.22: Letters dictated to seven congregations

### 2.1–7: Ephesus

*[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 542* ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Ephesus*, a major port city in Asia Minor, known especially for a temple to the local form of the goddess Artemis. Evidence points to a thriving Jewish community there (see Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.439; *Ant.* 14.10.25,263–65; Acts 19). *Seven stars*, see 1.16n. ^2-1-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Apostles*, “ones sent out,” representatives for the community. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-6|6]]:** The author approves this congregation’s efforts to discern true and false apostles and to resist the *Nicolaitans*, an unknown group. ^2-6

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-7|7]]:** The repeated phrase, *Let anyone who has an ear . . .* , probably referred to the traditions of Jesus’ own words (see [[Mark 04#^4-9|Mk 4.9]],[[Mark 04#^4-23|23]], etc.), although here it serves to sanction the voice of the risen Christ’s spirit. *Tree of life that is in the paradise of God*, the tree of Gen 2 here located in heaven (also depicted in *1 En.* 24–25; 32). ^2-7

### 2.8–11: Smyrna

*[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-8|Text]], p. 542* ^2-8

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Smyrna*, another seaport in Asia Minor, about whose Jewish community we know little. *First and the last*, see 1.17; 1.8n. ^2-8-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-9|9–11]]:** *Slander*, see “So-Called Jews and Their Synagogues of Satan,” p. 543. *Synagogue* (i.e., assembly), common Greek term for religious or social assembly. *Of Satan*, in context, Gentile believers in Jesus (see Introduction); the traditional view, that this verse condemns Jews who do not believe in Jesus, is not supported if Revelation is read as a Jewish text. It was, of course, possible for Jews to criticize other Jews as demonically inspired; 1QH 2.22 labels apostate (from the Scrolls’ perspective) Jews a “congregation of Belial.” ^2-9

> [!note] The Letters to the Seven Congregations *(p. 542)*
> Each letter in chs 2–3 is addressed in standard Greek epistolary form, from one of the divine epithets of the “Son of Man” figure John has just encountered, to a congregation or assembly (*ekklēsia* in this era is best not translated “church”) of Christ-devotees in a city in Asia Minor. That there are seven letters owes more to Revelation’s principal numerology (see “The Numerology of Revelation,” p. 550) than to the specific importance of these congregations in John’s world. Each letter consists of obscure allusions as well as cryptic threats and promises, from none of which is it possible to reconstruct a convincing background scenario. Nor can we know John’s precise relationship to these congregations. What is apparent however, is a general crisis of authority over the strict observance of Jewish purity, with the prophet John vilifying those who would dilute halakhah as he sees it (2.9,14; 3.9). The evidence we have of Jewish life in first-century Asia Minor shows much intermingling with local Gentile culture, even as these communities seem to have maintained basic Jewish institutions like Sabbath, prayer, and some dietary practices. John is more conservative than this fluid Jewish world; he establishes himself as a kind of reformer, seeking a rigorous *priestly* purity for the end-time congregations. Thus while we cannot reconstruct the precise historical situations that the letters presume, we can glean a more general historical sense of the contexts in which a first-century Jewish prophet of the Jesus movement in Asia Minor might have found himself in conflict with other congregations over issues of practice and purity.

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Devil*, seen as the author of testing. See Job, where “the adversary” (Heb “ha-satan”) tests Job (chs 1–2). *Ten days*, a limited period ([[Daniel 01#^1-12|Dan 1.12]]). *Crown of life*, [[James 01#^1-12|Jas 1.12]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-2|1 Pet 5.2]]. The *crown* was given to a victor in a contest ([[2 Timothy 02#^2-5|2 Tim 2.5]]); here it means those who triumph through death. ^2-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Second death*, the final condemnation of the unrighteous at the end of the Judgment period (see 20.14). ^2-11

### 2.12–17: Pergamum

*[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-12|Text]], p. 543* ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Pergamum*, major city in Asia Minor, notable for its elaborate acropolis with a temple to the Roman emperor and its enormous library. Although there is scant documentation of Jews in Pergamum, the ecumenical nature of the city (with its library rivaling Alexandria’s) would make a Jewish presence likely. *Two-edged sword*, see 1.16n. ^2-12-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Satan’s throne*, often identified as the imperial altar on the acropolis. *Holding . . . my name*, commitment to the central tenets of Christ according to this author. *Antipas my witness*, Revelation’s only named martyr, about whom we know nothing. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Balaam*, seer who led Israel to intermarriage and idolatry ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-15|Num 22.15–24.25]]); used here as a pseudonym for a rival prophet who permits the eating of food ritually dedicated to idols (Gk “eidolothuta”), and unregulated sexuality (Gk “porneuo”), both likely caricatures of the Pauline mission (see [[Acts 15#^15-29|Acts 15.29]]; 1 Cor 7–8). Both would be seen as polluting to a halakhically concerned prophet like John. John identifies the true community as faithful to Jewish Law, so the reference to the biblical Balaam casts the rival prophet and his false teachings of John’s day in mythical terms. ^2-14

> [!note] So-Called Jews and Their Synagogues of Satan *(p. 543)*
> Who are “those who say that they are Jews and are not,” who are rather a “synagogue of Satan” (2.9)? Interpreters customarily assume that John, a “Christian,” would have viewed true Judaism as Christ-devoted, and thus these so-called Jews must be Jews who denied Christ. John would then, in effect, be pitting himself against the (non-Christ-devoted) Jewish community as radically as against the Roman Empire (chs 17–18). Some Christian exegetes have proposed, in fact, a collusion between Roman authorities and Jews in persecuting Jesus-believers, for which there is no reliable evidence.
>
> John nowhere in this text juxtaposes himself against Judaism or Jewish traditions. Indeed, his opponents in this part of the book appear to es-pouse, not Jewish teachings, but the diluted interpretations of dietary and sexual purity we associate with Paul of Tarsus (2.14,20; cf. 1 Cor 7–8); even John’s major enemy, Rome, is described in terms of female sexual pollution (17.4–6). Elsewhere John adheres to the strictest concepts of Jewish purity, those associated with the Temple priesthood and comparable to the laws of the Qumran community (12.17; 14.4; 21.27; 22.14). We must consider John’s profound commitment to Jewish purity in combination with the increasing popularity of Pauline teachings among Gentile “God-fearers” (that is, Gentiles who had embraced forms of Jewish devotion before the appearance of the Jesus movement) in Asia Minor over the later first century. Thus, it begins to make more sense to take John’s polemic against so-called Jews literally—as against those who are not Jews but claim, in some way, this identity. That is, he declares that those Gentile believers in Jesus who claim an affiliation with Judaism as a basis for Christ’s salvation (cf. [[Romans 02#^2-17|Rom 2.17–24]],[[Romans 02#^2-28|28–29]]) are in fact not Jews at all. “Synagogue of Satan” in this case refers generally to an assembly (Heb ʿ*edah*) rather than a building or institution, and it was often used to denote a collective opponent, as at Qumran (1QS 5.1–2,10–20; CD 1.12; 1QM 1.1). Notwithstanding the phrase’s anti-Semitic history as a condemnation of Judaism, John means “synagogue of Satan” only as a rejection of those pretending to be Jews. The real Jews are the one who, like John, follow a strict, priestly interpretation of halakhah: in the areas of dietary law (2.14,20) and sexual impurity (2.20–22; 14.4; 17; 22.14–15), taken in the context of John’s sustained embrace of priestly identity (1.6; 5.10; 20.6b,9).

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Nicolaitans*, see 2.6n. ^2-15

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-16|16]]:** *Sword*, see 1.16n., also recalling [[Isaiah 11#^11-4|Isa 11.4]] (“rod of his mouth”). ^2-16

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-17|17]]:** *Hidden manna*, heavenly food ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-31|Ex 16.31]]), to which only the risen Christ figure speaking through John has access, to bestow on the righteous. *White stone*, amulet with a secret divine name, not unlike the magical gems purveyed around the Roman world at this time. Millennialist movements often develop new forms of magical protection. ^2-17

### 2.18–29: Thyatira

*[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-18|Text]], p. 544* ^2-18

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-18|18]]:** *Thyatira*, known in Roman times for its many influential trade-guilds and its religious diversity. [[Acts 16#^16-14|Acts 16.14]] describes its Jewish population as welcoming God-fearers. *Eyes like a flame*, [[Daniel 10#^10-6|Dan 10.6]]. ^2-18-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-20|20–21]]:** *Jezebel*, King Ahab’s queen, who patronized the Baal-cult ([[I Kings 16#^16-31|1 Kings 16.31]]; [[I Kings 19#^19-1|19.1–2]]); like Balaam (2.14), a pseudonym for a prophet who allows food associated with “idols” and some loos-ening of restrictions that John deems essential for sexual purity. There is increasing evidence over the second and third centuries for the leadership of women prophets in parts of the Jesus movement. John sees *Jezebel*\[’s\] gender and her sexual teachings (which recall Paul’s own liberal views on legitimate sexuality, [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-5|1 Cor 7.5–10]]) as inextricably polluting (see “Woman and the Symbolism of Pollution,” p. 565). *Fornication*, less likely a literal charge than a metaphor describing her positive relations with Greco-Roman society. ^2-20

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-22|22]]:** *Bed* (Gk “kline”) suggests illness, not rape (recalling [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-18|Ex 21.18]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-5|1 Macc 1.5]]; [[LXX Judith 08#^8-3|Jdt 8.3]]), here with the force of a curse: she and her followers will die. *Adultery*, metaphor for idolatry (see [[Jeremiah 03#^3-6|Jer 3.6–11]]). ^2-22

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-23|23]]:** In the Hebrew Bible God is the one who searches minds and hearts (see [[Jeremiah 17#^17-10|Jer 17.10]]; [[Psalms 139#^139-23|Ps 139.23]]; [[I Chronicles 28#^28-9|1 Chr 28.9]]). ^2-23

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-24|24]]:** *Deep things of Satan*, likely an ironic reference to misguided teaching that presents itself as profound. ^2-24

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-27|27]]:** Here [[Psalms 002#^2-8|Ps 2.8–9]] is broadened from the Davidic king to all who follow Christ. ^2-27

**[[NRSVue Revelation 02#^2-28|28]]:** *Morning star*, Jesus, see 22.16; [[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-17|Num 24.17]]. In many ancient religions stars (or star iconography) endowed a god or hero with divine authority through identification with celestial elements: e.g., the second-century Jewish messianic figure Simeon Bar Kochba (“son of the star”). Chs 2–3: The seven churches. ^2-28
