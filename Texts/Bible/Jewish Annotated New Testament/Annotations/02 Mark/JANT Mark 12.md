### 12.1–12: Parable of the vineyard

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 95* · ([[Matthew 21#^21-33|Mt 21.33–46]]; [[Luke 20#^20-9|Lk 20.9–19]]). The parable, more about judgment and less about the kingdom of God than the other parables, builds upon the allegory found in [[Isaiah 05#^5-1|Isa 5.1–7]], but in Mark the tenants are Israel’s leaders, the servants are God’s prophets, and the heir is Jesus. In Isaiah the vineyard is condemned for not producing edible grapes (idolatry and injustice are compared to wild grapes), and its destruction is predicted. Some Christian traditions sees Jesus’ parable as announcing the replacement of Judaism by the church, but the parable can also be understood as Jesus’ condemnation of Jewish officials in collusion with Rome. ^12-1

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-10|10]]:** [[Psalms 118#^118-22|Ps 118.22–23]]. *Cornerstone*, Gk “kephalēn gonias”; Heb “rosh pinna,” “head of the corner.” A “keystone” (see translators’ note *a*) is the top stone in an arch that holds the arch in place; a *cornerstone* is the stone on which everything else is based. The generic statement in the psalm is applied to Jesus. ^12-10

### 12.13–17: Taxes for Caesar

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-13|Text]], p. 95* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-15|Mt 22.15–22]]; [[Luke 20#^20-20|Lk 20.20–26]]). As in 11.27–33, opponents try to elicit a politically dangerous pronouncement. The background for this passage may be events of 6–7 CE, when a prophetic leader named Judas (mentioned in [[Acts 05#^5-37|Acts 5.37]]) organized a movement to worship God alone and to refuse to pay the tax to Caesar (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.117–18). ^12-13

### 12.18–27: Dispute with the Sadducees

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-18|Text]], p. 95* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-23|Mt 22.23–33]]; [[Luke 20#^20-27|Lk 20.27–40]]). In early Israel, life was believed to continue after death in the form of children and lineage (e.g., [[Psalms 025#^25-13|Ps 25.13]]). “Sheol,” a place of shadowy existence ([[Psalms 006#^6-6|Ps 6.5]]), is a realm of death similar to the Greek Hades; in 1 Sam 28, Saul uses a medium, a “woman of spirits,” to raise Samuel from the realm of the dead (“the ground”) to prophesy for him (Sheol is not mentioned in this passage, but the shadowy existence is clear). Some notion of the resurrection of the dead appears first in the “Isaiah Apocalypse” ([[Isaiah 26#^26-19|Isa 26.19]]) and in [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2–3]] (cf. Wis 2–5; 2 Macc 7; 4 Macc; 1 Cor 15). ^12-18

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-18|18]]:** The *Sadducees*, an aristocratic party who observed the written laws of the Torah but not the Pharisees’ traditions of the elders (see 7.3–4n.). They also differed in denying resurrection; the Pharisees, like Jesus’ followers, believed in resurrection ([[Acts 23#^23-6|Acts 23.6–10]]), and rabbinic tradition followed Pharisaic belief ([[Sanhedrin 90#90b|*b. Sanh.* 90b]]–92b). ^12-18-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-19|19–23]]:** The absurdity of the test case is meant to disprove the idea of resurrection. ^12-19

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-25|25]]:** *Like angels*, describes beliefs similar to those found in contemporary Jewish apocalyptic texts (*2 Bar.* 51.5). ^12-25

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-26|26–27]]:** *Story about the bush*, see [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-1|Ex 3.1–6]]. The argument surprisingly does not hinge on predictions of Jesus’ resurrection, but rather on evidence from Scripture about the living presence of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (see 10.40n.; cf. [[Matthew 08#^8-11|Mt 8.11]]; [[Luke 13#^13-28|Lk 13.28]]). ^12-26

### 12.28–34: The greatest commandment

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-28|Text]], p. 96* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-34|Mt 22.34–40]]; [[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25–28]]). ^12-28

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-29|29]]:** Jesus quotes [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4–5]] and [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]] (in [[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25–28]], Jesus elicits the quotation from a lawyer; the combination of the two verses is not unique to Jesus); Hillel, faced with a similar question, summed up the law with the dictum: “Do not do to anyone else what is hateful to you” ([[Shabbat 31#31a|*b. Shabb.* 31a]]; cf. Tob 4.15; [[Matthew 07#^7-12|Mt 7.12]]). *Hear*, Heb “shema”; the latter became the name of the prayer, composed of [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4–9]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-13|11.13–21]]; see [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-37|Num 15.37–41]], recited according to Jewish practice twice daily. ^12-29

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-32|32–34]]:** Despite the context of the episodes before and after, the *scribe* answers Jesus positively and Jesus responds in kind. Matthew and Luke omit these lines. ^12-32

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-33|33]]:** Based on [[Hosea 06#^6-6|Hos 6.6]]; [[Micah 6#^6-6|Mic 6.6–8]]. ^12-33

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-34|34]]:** *Not far from the kingdom*, is presented as a warm response from Jesus. ^12-34

### 12.35–37: The messiah and David’s son?

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-35|Text]], p. 96* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-41|Mt 22.41–46]]; [[Luke 20#^20-41|Lk 20.41–44]]). Interjected here is a short scriptural argument to the effect that the messiah is not just the Son of David (i.e., a king of the Davidic line) but something greater (see 10.47n.). Elsewhere Mark affirms Jesus’ connection with David (2.25–26; 10.47). ^12-35

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-36|36]]:** Ps 110 opens “Of David. A Psalm: The LORD says to my lord \[the messiah, not David\] . . . .” The argument assumes that David, the purported speaker of [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]], called the messiah “my Lord,” indicating that the messiah was David’s superior. ^12-36

### 12.38–44: Rich scribes and a poor widow

*[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-38|Text]], p. 96* · ([[Matthew 23#^23-6|Mt 23.6]]; [[Luke 20#^20-46|Lk 20.46–47]]; [[Luke 21#^21-1|21.1–4]]). Matthew 23 develops this critique. Mark’s version does not mention Pharisees and is more explicitly economic: the pretensions of the scribes lead to a comparison with a poor widow (omitted in Matthew). ^12-38

**[[NRSVue Mark 12#^12-43|43–44]]:** Some scholars suggest that Mark is critical of the woman’s generosity, but the text does not suggest that. The Temple is a place where both rich and poor can contribute. ^12-43
