 But the diffi cultest rigor is forthwith, On the image of what we see, to catch from that 

 Irrational moment its unreasoning, As when the sun comes rising, when the sea Clears deeply, when the moon hangs on the wall 

 Of heaven-haven. These are not things transformed. Yet we are shaken by them as if they were. 

 We reason about them with a later reason.

 And we make of what we see, what we see clearly And have seen, a place dependent on ourselves.
