**1.** Torah should be taught only to a proper student[^1] - one whose deeds[^2] are attractive[^3] - or to a person whose behavior is unknown. However, [a potential student] who follows bad ways should[^4] be influenced to correct his behavior and trained to follow a straight path.[^5] [After he repents, his deeds] are examined and[^6] he is allowed to enter the house of study to be instructed.  
Our Sages said:[^7] Whoever teaches an improper student is considered as if he throws a stone to Mercury,[^8] as \[[[Proverbs 26#^26-8|Proverbs 26:8]]] states: "As one who winds a stone in a sling,[^9] so is he who gives honor to a fool." There is no "honor" other than Torah, as \[[[Proverbs 03#^3-35|Proverbs 3:35]]] states: "The wise shall inherit honor."  
Similarly, one should not study from a teacher who does not follow a proper path, even though he[^10] is a very wise man and his [instruction] is required by the entire nation, until he returns to a good path,[^11] as [implied by [[Malachi 2#^2-7|Malachi 2:7]]]: "For the priest's lips shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek Torah from his mouth, because he is a messenger from the Lord of Hosts."  
Our Sages said:[^12] If a teacher resembles "a messenger[^13] of the Lord of Hosts," seek Torah from his mouth.[^14] If he does not, do not seek Torah from his mouth. ^4-1

**2.** How is [Torah] taught? The teacher sits at the head and the students sit around him, so that all can see the teacher and hear his words.[^15]  
The teacher should not sit on a chair, [while] his students [sit] on the ground. Rather, either everyone should sit on the ground[^16] or everyone should sit on chairs.[^17]  
Originally, the teacher would sit and the students would stand. [However,] before the destruction of the Second Temple, everyone followed the practice of teaching while both they and the students were seated.[^18] ^4-2

**3.** If the teacher [desires] to instruct the students personally, he is entitled to do so. If he [desires to] teach through the medium of a spokesman,[^19] the spokesman should stand between him and the students. The teacher speaks to the spokesman[^20] and he announces the teaching to all the students.[^21]  
When they ask the spokesman a question, he asks the teacher.[^22] The teacher, [in turn,] replies to the spokesman[^23] and the spokesman replies to the questioner.[^24]  
The teacher should not raise his voice above that of the spokesman.[^25] Similarly, when the spokesman asks a question of the teacher, he should not raise his voice above that of the teacher.[^26]  
The spokesman is not allowed to detract from, add to, or change [the teacher's words],[^27] unless he is the teacher's father or teacher.[^28] If the teacher tells the spokesman: "My teacher told me the following..." or "My father and teacher told me the following...," when the spokesman relates the teaching to the people, he should quote the statement in the name of the sage [who authored it],[^29] mentioning the name of the teacher's father or teacher, saying: "So and so, the sage, said the following..."[^30] [This is allowed] despite the fact the teacher did not mention the sage's name, because it is forbidden to refer to one's teacher or father by name.[^31] ^4-3

**4.** If the teacher taught [a concept] and it was not grasped by the students, he should not become upset with them[^32] and display anger.[^33] Rather, he should repeat and review the matter, even if he must do so many times, until they appreciate the depth of the halachah.[^34]  
Similarly, the student should not say "I understood" when he did not understand.[^35] Rather, he should ask again and again, even if he requires several repetitions.[^36] If his teacher becomes upset with him and displays anger, he should tell him: "My teacher, this is Torah. It is necessary that I study, and my powers of comprehension are weak."[^37] ^4-4

**5.** A student should not be embarrassed[^38] because his colleagues grasped the subject matter the first or second time, while he did not understand it until it was repeated a number of times.[^39] If he becomes embarrassed because of such matters, he will find himself going in and out of the house of study without learning anything.[^40]  
Accordingly,[^41] the Sages of the previous generations stated:[^42] "A bashful person will not learn, nor should the short-tempered[^43] teach."  
When does the above[^44] apply? When the students did not understand the subject matter because of its depth or because of their limited powers of comprehension. However, if it appears to the teacher that they are not applying themselves to the words of Torah and are lax about them, and, therefore, do not understand,[^45] he is obligated to display anger[^46] towards them and shame them with his words, to sharpen their powers of concentration.[^47] In this context, our Sages said:[^48] "Cast fear into the[^49] students."  
Therefore,[^50] it is not fitting for a teacher to act frivolously in the presence of his students.[^51] He should not amuse himself in their presence, nor should he eat and drink with them.[^52] [These restrictions are intended] so that they fear him and study under him at a fast pace. ^4-5

**6.** When the teacher enters the house of study, questions should not be posed to him until he gathers his powers of concentration.[^53] [Thus,] a student should not ask until he sits[^54] and rests.[^55]  
Two should not ask at once.[^56] A teacher should not be asked about an outside matter, but rather, [only] regarding the subject which they[^57] are involved with, lest he[^58] become embarrassed.[^59]  
The teacher may [deliberately] lead the students astray with his questions[^60] and the deeds he carries out in their presence to sharpen their powers of concentration[^61] and to test whether they remember what he taught them or not. Needless to say, he may ask them regarding another matter, which they are not presently involved with,[^62] to spur them on.[^63] ^4-6

**7.** A person should not ask a question while standing, nor should a person respond while standing.[^64] One should not ask from a high place, from far away, or from behind the elders.[^65]  
A question should be asked of the teacher regarding only the subject which they are reading.[^66] A question should be asked only with an attitude of awe.[^67] A person should ask concerning only three halachot [while studying] the same subject.[^68] ^4-7

**8.** [The following rules apply when] two people ask questions simultaneously:[^69] If one question is to the point[^70] and the other is not to the point, attention is paid to the question which is to the point.[^71] [If one question concerns] a matter to be applied in practice, and the other is an abstract question, attention is paid to the question which concerns the matter to be applied.[^72]  
[If one question concerns] Torah law, and the other the interpretation of the verses of the Torah, attention is paid to the question which concerns Torah law.[^73] [If one question concerns] the interpretation of the verses of the Torah, and the other *aggadah*,[^74] attention is paid to the question which concerns the interpretation of the verses of the Torah.[^75]  
[If one question concerns] *aggadah*, and the other the inference drawn from a minor premise to a major one,[^76] attention is paid to the question which concerns the inference drawn from a minor premise to a major one.[^77] [If one question concerns] the inference drawn from a minor premise to a major one, and the other an analogy established on the basis of similar words,[^78] attention is paid to the inference drawn from a minor premise to a major one.[^79]  
If of the two questioners, one was a sage and one was a student, attention is paid to the sage;[^80] one a student and one a commoner, attention is paid to the student.[^81] However, if they are both sages, both students, or both commoners[^82] - similarly, if two people both ask questions concerning Torah law, or they both wish to reply to what has been previously stated, or both questions concern actual deeds[^83] - from this point on, the spokesman has the option [of giving precedence to whichever question he desires]. ^4-8

**9.** One should not sleep in a house of study. When any person dozes in the house of study, his Torah will be torn in shreds. This is alluded to in Solomon's words of wisdom \[[[Proverbs 23#^23-21|Proverbs 23:21]]]: "Drowsiness will clothe a man in[^84] rags."  
Conversation in the house of study should[^85] concern only the words of Torah.[^86] Even if someone sneezes, one should not wish him: "[To your] recovery" in the house of study. Needless to say,[^87] the discussion of other matters is prohibited.  
The sanctity of a house of study exceeds that of a synagogue.[^88] ^4-9
