**1.** These are the required measurements of a sukkah: Its height[^1] should not be less than ten handbreadths[^2] nor more than twenty cubits.[^3] Its area should not be less than seven handbreadths by seven handbreadths.[^4] [There is no maximum limit to] its area,[^5] and one may increase it [to include] a number of *millim*.[^6]  
A sukkah which[^7] is less than ten handbreadths high, smaller than seven handbreadths by seven handbreadths [in area], or taller than twenty cubits[^8] - even [if the increase or decrease] is of the slightest amount[^9] - is invalid. ^4-1

**2.** A sukkah which does not possess three walls is invalid.[^10] However, if it has two complete walls[^11] perpendicular to each other in the shape of [the Greek letter][^12] *gamma*, it is sufficient to construct a third wall that is [only] slightly more than a handbreadth wide and place it within three handbreadths[^13] of one of the two walls.[^14] Also, one must construct the likeness of an entrance,[^15] since it[^16] does not possess three complete walls.[^17]  
We have already explained in *Hilchot Shabbat*[^18] that wherever the term "a likeness of an entrance" is used, it may be a rod on one side, another rod on the opposite side, and a third above,[^19] even though it does not touch them.[^20] ^4-2

**3.** If the two walls[^21] were parallel to each other and there was an open space between them, one should construct a wall slightly more than four handbreadths wide[^22] and place it within three handbreadths[^23] of one of the two walls; then, [the sukkah] is kosher.[^24] However, it is necessary to construct "the likeness of an entrance."[^25]  
If the rods of the *s'chach* of the sukkah extend beyond the sukkah and one wall extends with them,[^26] they are considered to be [part of] the sukkah.[^27] ^4-3

**4.** Walls[^28] which are connected to the roof of the sukkah, but do not reach the earth:[^29] If they are more than three handbreadths above the earth, they are invalid;[^30] if the distance is less than that,[^31] they are kosher.[^32]  
[The following rules apply] should the walls be connected to the earth, without reaching the *s'chach*:[^33] If they are ten handbreadths high, they are kosher even[^34] though are removed several cubits from the roof,[^35] provided they[^36] are positioned below the end of the roof.[^37] If the roof[^38] was separated from the wall by more than three handbreadths, it is invalid;[^39] less than this amount is kosher.[^40]  
If one suspended a partition which is slightly more than four handbreadths high at a distance of less than three handbreadths from the earth[^41] and a distance of less than three handbreadths from the roof,[^42] it is kosher.[^43] ^4-4

**5.** When a person constructs his sukkah among the trees,[^44] using the trees as walls, it is kosher if:  
a)[^45] they are strong enough[^46] - or he tied them and reinforced them so that they would be strong enough[^47] - that they would not be shaken by the wind at all times; and  
b) he filled [the space] between the branches[^48] with hay and straw,[^49] tying them[^50] so that they will not be shaken by the wind.[^51]  
[This is necessary,] for any partition that cannot stand before a normal[^52] land wind[^53] is not considered to be a partition.[^54] ^4-5

**6.** If a person constructs his sukkah on top of a wagon[^55] or on the deck of a ship,[^56] it is kosher, and one may ascend to it on the festival.[^57] If one constructs it on the treetops or on a camel's back, it is kosher,[^58] but one may not ascend to it on the festival,[^59] because climbing on a tree or animal is forbidden on a festival.[^60]  
If some of the walls were the result of human activity and some were trees, we consider [its structure]. We may ascend to any [sukkah] where, if the trees were taken away, it would be able to stand with the walls that were built by man alone.[^61] ^4-6

**7.** A sukkah that does not possess a roof is invalid.[^62] To what does this refer? A sukkah whose walls are joined to each other like a hut;[^63] alternatively, when the side of the sukkah is placed against the wall.[^64] However, if it[^65] has a roof, even only a handbreadth in width,[^66] or if one lifted the side of the sukkah close to the wall a handbreadth above the ground,[^67] it is kosher.[^68]  
A round sukkah[^69] - if its circumference is large enough to contain a square seven handbreadths by seven handbreadths, it is kosher even though it has no corners. ^4-7

**8.** Should one place *s'chach* over an *exedrah*[^70] which has projections [extending from its pillars],[^71] it is kosher,[^72] regardless of whether the projections can be seen from the inside[^73] - although they cannot be seen from the outside[^74] - or whether they can be seen from the outside[^75] - although they cannot be seen from the inside.[^76] ^4-8

**9.** If it[^77] does not have projections [extending from its pillars][^78] it is not valid, because it resembles a sukkah constructed in an alley,[^79] because it has [walls on] only the two sides of the *exedrah*. The middle of the *exedrah* does not have a wall and there are no projections opposite it.[^80] ^4-9

**10.** Should a person place *s'chach* over an alleyway which possesses a *lechi*[^81] or a well which possesses *pasim*,[^82] it is considered a kosher sukkah only on the Sabbath of the festival.[^83] Since this *lechi* and these *pasim* are considered to be partitions with regard to the Sabbath laws,[^84] they are also considered to be partitions with regard to the laws of sukkah.[^85] ^4-10

**11.** Should a person implant four poles in the four corners of the roof and place *s'chach* upon them,[^86] it is kosher. Since he placed the *s'chach* [above] the edge of the roof, we consider that the lower walls ascend to the edge of the *s'chach*.[^87] ^4-11

**12.** A sukkah which has many entrances and many windows in its walls is kosher even though the open portion[^88] exceeds the closed portion, provided there is no opening larger than ten cubits.[^89]  
If there is an opening larger than ten cubits, it is necessary that the closed portion exceed the open portion, even though [the opening] is constructed in the form of an entrance.[^90] ^4-12

**13.** A sukkah whose inner space[^91] exceeds twenty cubits [is not acceptable].[^92] Should one reduce it[^93] [by placing] pillows and coverings [on the floor], it is not considered to be reduced.[^94] [This applies even if] one considered them a permanent[^95] part of the sukkah.[^96] If one reduced the space using straw and considered it as a permanent part of the sukkah, [the space] is considered to be reduced.[^97]  
Needless to say, the above applies if one used earth and considered it to be a permanent part of the sukkah. However, if one [merely brought in] earth with no specific intention, [its space] is not considered to be reduced.[^98]  
If it[^99] was twenty cubits high,[^100] but branches [from the *s'chach* descend within the twenty cubits, [the following principle applies:] If its shade would be greater than its open portion[^101] because of these branches alone,[^102] it is considered as having thick *s'chach*[^103] and is kosher.[^104] ^4-13

**14.** When [a sukkah is more than twenty cubits high, but] one builds a bench next to the middle wall extending across its entire span[^105] - if the width of the bench is equal to the minimum width of a sukkah,[^106] it is kosher.[^107]  
Should one build the bench next to the middle wall along [one] side, if there are four cubits between the bench and the [opposite] wall, it is unacceptable.[^108] If there are fewer than four cubits, it is kosher.[^109]  
Should one build the bench in the middle [of the sukkah], if there are more than four cubits from the edge of the bench to any of the sides [of the sukkah],[^110] it[^111] is not acceptable.[^112] If there are fewer than four cubits,[^113] it is kosher. It is considered as if the walls touch the bench,[^114] and[^115] the distance from the bench to the *s'chach* is less than twenty cubits.[^116]  
If one constructs a pillar[^117] [within a sukkah[^118] whose *s'chach* is more than twenty cubits high, the following rule applies]: Even though it is of the minimum size required of a sukkah,[^119] it is unacceptable,[^120] because its walls are not discernible. Thus, it is as if there is kosher *s'chach* above the pillar without any walls. ^4-14

**15.** [The following rule applies] when [the inner space of the sukkah] was less than ten [handbreadths high][^121] and one dug [into the ground of the sukkah] to create[^122] an [inner space] of ten [handbreadths]:[^123] If there are three handbreadths from the edge of the pit until the wall [of the sukkah], it is not acceptable.[^124] If there is less than that [amount], it is kosher,[^125] because any [distance] less than three [handbreadths] is considered to be [insignificant, and the two entities are considered to be] adjacent [to each other], as explained in *Hilchot Shabbat*.[^126] ^4-15

**16.** The walls of the sukkah are kosher [although made] from all [substances].[^127] All that is necessary is a barrier of any kind.[^128] Even living beings [may serve that purpose.[^129] Thus,] a person can create a wall [of the sukkah] by using a colleague so that he can eat, drink, and sleep in the sukkah, for which his colleague is serving as a wall[^130] [even] on the holiday.[^131]  
The above applies when one employs the person as a wall without his conscious knowledge. However, it is forbidden to create [a wall by using a person][^132] when the latter is conscious of the fact on the holiday.[^133] Nevertheless, it is permitted during the other days of the festival.[^134]  
Similarly, a person may create a fourth wall[^135] from utensils on the holiday. However, he should not create a third wall using utensils[^136] on the holiday, because, [by doing so], he is making the sukkah fit for use, and it is forbidden to create [even] a temporary tent on the holiday. ^4-16
