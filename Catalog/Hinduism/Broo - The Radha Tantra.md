## About

An anonymous Sanskrit Tantra from Bengal, also called the *Vāsudevarahasya*. It retells the love of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana from a Śākta point of view, making Rādhā the supreme goddess and Kṛṣṇa's guru.

- **Date:** uncertain. Wikipedia puts it between the 17th and 18th centuries; Routledge's description of Broo's edition calls it a 17th-century text. The oldest extant manuscript (Sanskrit College, Kolkata, No. 13894) is dated 1778 CE and appears to copy an earlier one; Wikipedia says most others date from the early 18th century.
- **Form:** 37 chapters (*paṭala*s) and 1,745 verses, framed as a dialogue in which Śiva tells Pārvatī the story of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and their true forms.
- **Contents:** Rādhā is a form of the Mahāvidyā Tripurasundarī; Kṛṣṇa is a masculine form of Kālī and becomes Rādhā's disciple. Wikipedia notes a formal likeness to the Mahāvidyā genre and a contextual likeness to the Vaiṣṇava Sahajiyā genre.
- **Manuscripts:** mostly from Bengal, with a fair number from Odisha, North India, Bangladesh and Nepal.
- **Edition:** Måns Broo, *The Rādhā Tantra: A Critical Edition and Annotated Translation* (Routledge Studies in Tantric Traditions), edited from manuscripts from India, Nepal and Bangladesh, with an introduction on context, metre, grammar, doctrine and intertexts. Dates differ: Wikipedia gives 2017; Routledge's listing gives copyright 2017 and a release date of June 2019; the New Books Network gives 2019. Routledge says it won the Donner Research Prize in 2017; Broo is Senior Lecturer in Comparative Religion at Åbo Akademi University. It is the only edition and translation Wikipedia names.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia treats it as a case study of exchange between Vaiṣṇavism and Śāktism in precolonial Bengal. Routledge says it shows the meeting of religious traditions in 17th-century Bengal.
