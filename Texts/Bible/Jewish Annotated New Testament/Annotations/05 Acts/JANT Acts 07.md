### 7.1–53: Stephen’s speech

*[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-1|Text]], p. 234* · Stephen rehearses major events in Israel’s past, with significant discrepancies from the accounts in the Tanakh. Ezra’s speech in [[Nehemiah 07#^7-6|Neh 7.6–37]] offers a similar rehearsal of Israel’s acts of disobedience, but presents it as an intra-communal critique, whereas Stephen ends his rebuke on a note of hostility toward Jews as a separate and recalcitrant people (e.g., “your ancestors”). ^7-1

> [!note] Stephen’s Speech *(p. 234)*
> One of the men selected to wait on tables is Stephen, a devout believer with charismatic and rhetorical skills. A group of Diaspora Jews enters into a dispute with Stephen and claims that he has spoken blasphemy. Working with other Jewish leaders, they bring him before the council where they present false witnesses who accuse him of plotting to destroy the Temple and advocating for practices contrary to Jewish law (6.8–15; see [[Mark 14#^14-56|Mk 14.56]] for a parallel to Jesus’ trial). Rather than responding to the charges, Stephen launches into a speech, the longest in Acts, which rehearses major events in Israel’s sacred story. The speech develops two themes that become a major part of the larger Lukan narrative, particularly in its representation of Jews. First, it highlights Jewish disobedience. After mentioning Abraham, Joseph, and other early ancestors, the focus shifts to Moses and the continual disobedience of Israel. The speech identifies the present generation as persisting in the same spirit. Similar rehearsals of Israel’s past, such as Neh 9 and Ps 78, also combine historical review with rebuke of the people’s rebellious nature, yet these texts, unlike Stephen’s speech, ultimately assert that God is merciful and faithful to the covenant with the people of Israel. Stephen’s speech, in contrast, ends on a note of condemnation, without any hope for Israel’s future. Second, the speech presents the Temple not as the house of God, but as a rejection of God’s true nature and universal presence.

### 7.2–8: Abraham

*[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-2|Text]], p. 235* · See Gen 12–21. ^7-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-2|2]]:** *Glory*, Gk “doxa,” in LXX for Heb “kavod,” the divine attribute of honor or worth. ^7-2-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-3|3]]:** [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-1|Gen 12.1]]. ^7-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-4|4]]:** *Settled in Haran*, contrary to [[Torah 02 - Noach#^11-31|Gen 11.31–12.3]], Stephen reports that Abraham received divine instructions before arriving in Haran. The displacement may reflect a Jewish tradition that Abraham came to believe in God while still in Chaldea (*Jub*. 11.14–12.15; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.154–57). ^7-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-5|5]]:** On the promise of land, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^13-15|Gen 13.15]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-8|17.8]]; [[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^48-4|48.4]]. ^7-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-6|6–7]]:** *Country belonging to others*, Egypt ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-13|Gen 15.13–14]]); the rest of the promise, the punishment of the Egyptians, and the possessions the Israelites gained (v 14), go unmentioned. ^7-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-8|8]]:** See [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-10|Gen 17.10–13]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-4|21.4]]. ^7-8

### 7.44–50: Places of worship

*[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-44|Text]], p. 238* ^7-44

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-44|44]]:** See [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-8|Ex 25.8–9]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-7|33.7]]. ^7-44-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-45|45]]:** See [[Joshua 03#^3-14|Josh 3.14–17]]. ^7-45

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-46|46]]:** *Found favor*, see [[II Samuel 15#^15-25|2 Sam 15.25]]. *Find a dwelling place*, see [[II Samuel 07#^7-2|2 Sam 7.2]]. ^7-46

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-47|47]]:** See [[II Samuel 07#^7-13|2 Sam 7.13]]; 1 Kings 6. ^7-47

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-48|48–50]]:** According to Stephen, the Israelites acted against God’s wishes by building the Temple, because God is universal. Most Jewish traditions accepted that God is both universal and can be worshipped in a single location ([[LXX 2 Maccabees 14#^14-35|2 Macc 14.35]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 8.107). ^7-48

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-48|48]]:** *Most High*, common term for God ([[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-8|Deut 32.8]]; [[Psalms 091#^91-1|Ps 91.1]],[[Psalms 091#^91-9|9]]). *Does not dwell in houses made with human hands*, while the general sentiment accords with Jewish tradition ([[I Kings 08#^8-27|1 Kings 8.27]]), the phrase implies that the building of the Temple was an idolatrous act ([[Isaiah 02#^2-8|Isa 2.8]]; [[Isaiah 37#^37-19|37.19]]; [[Micah 5#^5-12|Mic 5.13]]\[MT 5.12\]; [[Psalms 115#^115-4|Ps 115.4]]; [[Psalms 135#^135-15|135.15]]). ^7-48-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-49|49–50]]:** See [[Isaiah 66#^66-1|Isa 66.1–2]]. ^7-49

### 7.51–53: Final condemnation

*[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-51|Text]], p. 238* ^7-51

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-51|51]]:** *Stiff-necked*, see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-9|Ex 32.9]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-3|33.3]],[[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-5|5]]. *Uncircumcised in heart and ears*, [[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-41|Lev 26.41]]; [[Jeremiah 06#^6-10|Jer 6.10]]. *Your ancestors*, until this point, Stephen spoke of *our ancestors* (vv 2,11,12,15,19,38,39,44,45); he now separates himself from his Jewish audience (“your ancestors”) and associates them with their rebellious and murderous ancestors (also v 52). ^7-51-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-52|52]]:** *Persecuting the prophets*, possible reference to the killing of Zechariah son of Jehoiada ([[II Chronicles 24#^24-20|2 Chr 24.20–21]]), and a common motif in the ancient Jewish work *Lives of the Prophets*. See also [[Matthew 23#^23-31|Matt 23.31]]. *Murderers*, just as previous generations rejected and killed the prophets, so now Jews have rejected and killed Jesus (see 2.23). ^7-52

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-53|53]]:** Stephen answers the original question (6.13–7.1) by accusing his accusers. He is not guilty of saying things against the Torah (6.13); they are the ones who continually violate it. *Ordained by angels*, see v 38. ^7-53

### 7.54–8.1: The stoning of Stephen

*[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-54|Text]], p. 238* · The story is modeled on the death of Jesus. Acts portrays Jews as persisting in their persecution that begins with Jesus and continues to his followers. Christian tradition understands Stephen as the first martyr. ^7-54

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-54|54]]:** *Ground their teeth*, image used of God’s enemies ([[Luke 13#^13-28|Lk 13.28]]; [[Job 16#^16-9|Job 16.9]]; [[Psalms 035#^35-16|Ps 35.16]]; [[Psalms 112#^112-10|112.10]]). ^7-54-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-55|55]]:** *Right hand*, position of authority; see [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]. ^7-55

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-56|56]]:** *Son of Man*, see [[Luke 22#^22-69|Lk 22.69]]. Outside the Gospels, the term appears only here and in [[Revelation 01#^1-13|Rev 1.13]]; [[Revelation 14#^14-14|14.14]]. ^7-56

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-58|58–59]]:** Stephen’s final words echo those of Jesus ([[Luke 23#^23-34|Lk 23.34]],[[Luke 23#^23-46|46]]). ^7-58

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-58|58]]:** Stoning, see 5.26. While an authorized form of punishment, here it represents a mob action, like a lynching (see [[John 18#^18-31|Jn 18.31]]). *Saul*, Paul’s first appearance. ^7-58-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 07#^7-60|60]]:** *Do not hold*, Stephen intercedes on behalf of his killers, possibly modeled on Jesus’ final words ([[Luke 23#^23-34|Lk 23.34]]) or the servant of [[Isaiah 53#^53-12|Isa 53.12]]. ^7-60
