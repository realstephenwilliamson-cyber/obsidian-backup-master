# Colossians 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Colossians 02|Colossians 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Colossians 01|NRSVue]] · [[Colossians 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Colossians - Introduction]]

### 1.1–2: Salutation

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 408* · Colossians mimics Paul’s salutation formula. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Apostle*, lit., “one sent” (cf. Heb “shaliaḥ”). *Timothy*, all of Paul’s own letters except Romans have multiple senders; Timothy, listed as co-sender of all except Galatians, is mentioned by Paul more frequently than any other person except Jesus. See Acts 16. Timothy was so prominent in the post-Pauline churches that the Pastoral Epistles of 1 and 2 Timothy are addressed to him. *Brother*, an address for unrelated members of several religious communities: Josephus, *J.W*. 2.122, refers to the Essenes as “brothers” (Gk “adelphoi”); followers of the Greco-Egyptian god Serapis (*Paris Papyrus* \[second century bce\] 20). ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Saints*, lit., “holy ones,” Paul’s most common designation for Jesus’ followers; the term is more frequent in Ephesians and Colossians than in Paul’s own letters. *Brothers and sisters*, the plural of the Gk for “brother” (“adelphos/adelphoi”) may refer to “brothers and sisters” (see translators’ note *a*). *Colossae*, city in modern Turkey in the Lycus valley, east of Ephesus, substantially destroyed by an earthquake in 60 CE. *Grace . . . peace*, Paul adapted the standard Greek letter greeting (“chairein,” “greetings”) to the similar-sounding theological term “charis,” “grace,” and included the Jewish epistolary greeting “shalom,” “peace” (Gk “eirēnē”) (e.g., [[Ezra 05#^5-7|Ezra 5.7]]). Ephesians and the Pastoral Epistles follow the same practice. *From God our Father*, the Tanakh rarely addresses God as father, preferring “God of our ancestors” \[“Fathers”\], although see [[Isaiah 63#^63-16|Isa 63.16]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-27|Ps 89.26]]. The usage is known, but not common, at Qumran (see 4Q460, Heb “ʾavi vaʾadoni,” “my father and my lord”) and becomes increasingly common in rabbinic literature: most frequent is the address of God as both “father and king” (Heb “avinu malkeinu,” e.g., *b. Taʾan*. 25b). God is only addressed twice as “father” in the LXX, both times in the Book of Odes ch 14; much more common is the phrase “God of \[our\] ancestors/fathers” (Gk “theos paterōn,” i.e., [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-13|Ex 3.13]]). The phrase occurs in all of Paul’s own letters, the Deutero-Pauline letters, and the Pastoral Epistles. ^1-2

### 1.3–8: Thanksgiving

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-3|Text]], p. 408* · Ancient Greek letters conventionally included a thanksgiving that offered recipients good wishes. In Colossians and Ephesians the author thanks the recipients for their faith, love, and hope, standard virtues that indicate no particular epistolary situation. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Lord* (Gk “kyrios”), Paul himself only once uses the term in a secular sense, as the master of a slave ([[Galatians 04#^4-1|Gal 4.1]]), and once ironically ([[1 Corinthians 08#^8-5|1 Cor 8.5]]). All other 208 occasions refer to Jesus. In the household codes the word applies both to Jesus and to slave-owners ([[Colossians 03#^3-22|Col 3.22]]). **4–5**: *Faith . . . love . . . hope*, imitating Paul’s triumvirate of virtues. The three terms appear together only in [[1 Corinthians 13#^13-13|1 Cor 13.13]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-3|1 Thess 1.3]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-8|5.8]]. *Faith* (Gk “pistis”), both trust and trustworthiness, like the Heb “ʾemunah” (e.g., [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-4|Deut. 32.4]]; [[I Samuel 26#^26-23|1 Sam 26.23]]; [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]]; 4Q418)**;** the term occurs nearly a hundred times in Paul’s own letters. *Love* (Gk “agape”), see 2.2n. ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Laid up*, a phrase from commerce, held in trust or for safekeeping, non-metaphorically in [[Luke 19#^19-20|Lk 19.20]], metaphorically here and in [[2 Timothy 04#^4-8|2 Tim 4.8]] and [[Hebrews 09#^9-27|Heb 9.27]], but never in Paul’s own letters. *Gospel* (Gk “euangelion,” “good news”), teachings concerning as well as the narrative about Jesus. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Bearing fruit*, perhaps in imitation of [[Romans 07#^7-4|Rom 7.4–5]]. The expression is classical, but the metaphorical use is not much older than the NT, and most of the examples are biblical or Patristic (see [[Matthew 13#^13-23|Mt 13.23]]; [[Mark 04#^4-20|Mk 4.20]],[[Mark 04#^4-28|28]]; [[Luke 08#^8-15|Lk 8.15]]; [[Romans 07#^7-4|Rom 7.4–5]]). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Epaphras*, first apostle to Colossae according to this letter (4.12–13; see Philem 23). *Fellow servant* (Gk “syndoulos,” “fellow-slave”), a term appearing in NT letters here and 4.7. *Minister*, Gk “diakonos,” “server” or “waiter”; these were the first officers in the church ([[Romans 16#^16-1|Rom 16.1]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]; [[1 Timothy 03#^3-8|1 Tim 3.8]],[[1 Timothy 03#^3-12|12]] cf. [[Ephesians 03#^3-7|Eph 3.7]]; [[Ephesians 06#^6-21|6.21]]; [[Colossians 01#^1-23|Col 1.23]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Has made known to us*, as Paul was not in Colossae, the author merges the thanksgiving with the typical section in Paul’s letters where he remembers, usually fondly, his experiences while visiting the church. ^1-8

### 1.9–14: Prayer for spiritual wisdom

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-9|Text]], p. 409* ^1-9

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-9|9]]:** *God’s will*, this phrase, used nineteen times by Paul and four times in the Deutero-Pauline letters, appears in the LXX only in 1 Esd 8.16. In the DSS, the phrase “ret-zon ʾel,” “will of God,” conventionally contrasts divine with human will (e.g., 1QM 2.5; 1QS 9.13,24). *Spiritual wisdom* (Gk “sophia,” LXX for Heb “hokhmah,” “wisdom,” e.g., [[Proverbs 01#^1-2|Prov 1.2]]) and *understanding* (Gk “synesis,” LXX for Heb “binah,” “understanding, discernment,” e.g., [[Proverbs 01#^1-2|Prov 1.2]], where it is also used with “hokhmah”), a major theme of the letter ([[Colossians 01#^1-25|Col 1.25–28]]; [[Colossians 02#^2-2|2.2–4]],[[Colossians 02#^2-9|9–15]]). Paul himself uses the noun “sophia” or the adjective thirty-four times, but only twice as a positive attribute ([[Romans 11#^11-33|Rom 11.33]] and 16.27, describing God). The author of Colossians, however, follows the Jewish wisdom tradition, using the term five times, always positively. In 1 Cor 1–4, Paul contrasts the negative attributes of wisdom and understanding—which he associates with cleverness in argumentation—with “pneumatika,” “spiritual things.” The author of Colossians reverses Paul’s moral vocabulary: wisdom, here positive, involves knowledge (v 9), right living (v 10), and patience (v 11). ^1-9-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Pleasing* (Gk “ariskeia,” “desire to please”), only here in the NT. ^1-10

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Endure* (Gk “hypomonē,” “endurance”), a common theme of Paul’s letters (e.g., [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|1 Cor 4.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-12|9.12]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-6|2 Cor 1.6]]) and NT letters generally (e.g., [[Hebrews 12#^12-7|Heb 12.7]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-19|1 Pet 2.19–20]]). The paired virtues “endurance” and “patience” occur in [[2 Timothy 03#^3-10|2 Tim 3.10]]. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-12|12–13]]:** *Light . . . darkness*, a common image for moral dualism; some scholars see an origin in Persian thought. The second-century *Didache* calls it “the Two Ways”; see also [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-19|Deut 30.19]]; [[Jeremiah 21#^21-8|Jer 21.8]]; John (many occurrences, e.g., 3.19); [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-5|1 Thess 5.5]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-9|1 Pet 2.9]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-8|Eph 5.8]]. The trope is found in Greek texts (*Prodicus Judgment of Heracles* \[*Mem*. 2.1.21\]) and fully developed in the Dead Sea Scrolls (see 1QM 13 and 1QS 3.13–4.26) and the *Ep. Barn*. 18–20. ^1-12

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Giving thanks*, the thanksgiving section continues, probably to the end of the hymn (vv 15–20). *Inheritance*, cf. 3.24; [[Ephesians 01#^1-14|Eph 1.14]],[[Ephesians 01#^1-18|18]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-5|5.5]]. Paul never uses the term, but it appears in his speech in [[Acts 26#^26-18|Acts 26.18]]. The basic meaning of the Gk “klēros” is “lot”; in the LXX it translates Heb “pur” (e.g., Jon 1.7; [[Esther 03#^3-7|Esth 3.7]]). ^1-12-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Power of darkness*, continuing the theme of v 12. The phrase echoes [[Ephesians 06#^6-12|Eph 6.12]] and occurs in [[Luke 22#^22-53|Lk 22.53]]. The contrast between light (v 12) and darkness (v 13) connects Colossians with several dualist thinkers, from Persian and Hellenistic Greek moralists to the Dead Sea Scrolls and the Fourth Gospel. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Redemption . . . forgiveness*, the extent to which these theological terms reflect Jewish ideas is questionable. Although Second Temple Jewish texts mention redemption from sin (cf. 1Q13 2.13; 1QS 2.8; *T. Jos*. 18.2), in the Tanakh redemption is from oppressors (e.g., [[Psalms 119#^119-134|Ps 119.134]]), from death (e.g., [[Job 05#^5-20|Job 5.20]]), and from Egyptian slavery (e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-13|Ex 15.13]]); the Hebrew prophets extended it to refer to a future redemption from exile (e.g., [[Jeremiah 31#^31-11|Jer 31.11]]; [[Micah 4#^4-10|Mic 4.10]]). Judaism rarely spoke of inborn or original sin, from which rescue is needed (though see [[Psalms 051#^51-3|Ps 51.1–5]] \[Heb vv 3–7\]) and Is 44.22. Gk “apolutrosis,” “ransoming,” translated in the NRSV as “redemption,” appears in the LXX only in [[Daniel 04#^4-31|Dan 4.34]]; but see, e.g., “lutrōtēs,” “redeemer,” in [[Psalms 019#^19-15|Ps 19.14]] \[HB v 15, “goʾel”; LXX [[LXX Psalms 18#^18-15|Ps 18.15]]\]); the Gk verb “lutroō” (“to ransom, redeem”) is frequent in the LXX. The term is rare in Paul ([[Romans 03#^3-24|Rom 3.24]]; [[Romans 08#^8-23|8.23]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-30|1 Cor 1.30]]) but common in the Deutero-Pauline letters ([[Ephesians 01#^1-7|Eph 1.7]],[[Ephesians 01#^1-14|14]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-30|4.30]], and here). *Forgiveness* (Gk “aphesis,”) never appears in Paul but is found in both Colossians and Ephesians. ^1-14

### 1.15–20: Hymn to the Christ

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-15|Text]], p. 410* · The developed Christology borrows themes from both the LXX and Paul. The Christ more than rules the world: he is both the first created and the principle of creation (vv 15–17), the head of the world and the church (vv 18–20). This Christology is perhaps the most exalted in the NT (cf. [[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1–8]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6–11]]). It resembles but surpasses the description of personified Wisdom in [[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22–31]] (see also [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-22|Wis 7.22b–8.1]]). ^1-15

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Image of the invisible God*, echoes [[Romans 01#^1-20|Rom 1.20]], but there Paul alludes to LXX [[LXX Genesis 01#^1-2|Gen 1.2]], “invisible and disorganized,” translating Heb “tohu va-vohu,” “formless void” (NRSV). For [[Colossians 01#^1-15|Col 1.15–16]] an invisible God creates invisible things. Cf. [[1 Timothy 01#^1-17|1 Tim 1.17]]; [[Hebrews 11#^11-27|Heb 11.27]], a description of God also found in Philo (*Cher.* 101; *Leg. Gai.* 318; *QE* 1 2.237). Colossians’ meditation on the preexistence of the Christ connects its Christology to the preamble to the Gospel of John, where Christ is God’s creative word ([[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1]]; cf. [[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22]]). The sages speculated about the preexistence of the Torah, the identity of the Messiah, and a number of other cornerstones of Jewish belief (*Gen. Rab.* 1.1.4, “Six things preexisted the creation of the world”). *Firstborn*, used only once in Paul ([[Romans 08#^8-29|Rom 8.29]]). In Hebrews, it refers to the Christ ([[Hebrews 01#^1-6|Heb 1.6]]), to the firstborn sons of Egypt killed by the angel of death ([[Hebrews 11#^11-28|Heb 11.28]]), to the Israelites in Egypt who were not slain (also 11.28), and to the believers in the author’s own generation (12.23). See also [[Acts 26#^26-2|Acts 26.2]]; [[Revelation 01#^1-5|Rev 1.5]]. Biblical law (e.g., [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-15|Deut 21.15–17]]) affirms primogeniture, though some rabbinic legislators argued for a more equal distribution of estates (cf. *b. Shabb*. 10b.) ^1-15-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Thrones . . . powers*, spiritual forces that rule the world. The author adds the non-Pauline “thronoi” (thrones), indicating concentrations of power, secular, demonic, and divine (cf. [[Ephesians 01#^1-21|Eph 1.21]]; [[Ephesians 03#^3-10|3.10]]). ^1-16

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-17|17–20]]:** The Christ is the organizing principle of creation as in [[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1-4]]. **17**: *In him all things hold together*, Christ maintains the universal order. The verb “sunistēmi,” “hold together” is common; the idea of a divine force organizing the principles of the universe is found in Philo (cf. *Heir* 58; *Planting* 6 \[“For it was appropriate that the most perfect and greatest works should be made by the greatest designer”\]) but is not much older. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-18|18]]:** *Head*, the Christ is the head of the body, the church, echoing [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-18|1 Cor 12.18–31]]. Paul almost always restricts *church* (“ekklēsia,” see [[Colossians 01#^1-24|Col 1.24]]) to refer to the believers in a particular location (the exception is [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]), but in Col 1 the term encompasses the whole of the Jesus movement. *Firstborn from the dead*, first to be resurrected. Colossians imitates [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-20|1 Cor 15.20]], where Christ is the first fruits of the dead. ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Fullness of God was pleased to dwell*, *reconciled* c Gk *in the body of his flesh* the NRSV supplies “of God”; the nature of the fullness (Gk plērōma) dwelling in the Messiah remains debated. The verse may echo Ps 24 (LXX Ps 23), “The earth is the Lord’s and its fullness,” where the LXX “plērōma” translates the Heb “meloʿah” (“fullness”) ^1-19

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Reconcile* (Gk “apokatallasso”), appears in the NT only in the Deutero-Pauline letters ([[Ephesians 02#^2-16|Eph 2.16]]; [[Colossians 01#^1-20|Col 1.20]],[[Colossians 01#^1-22|22]]) and in literature only in Christian texts. Paul uses “katallagē/katallasso,” “reconciliation/reconcile” (cf. [[Romans 05#^5-10|Rom 5.10]]) with some frequency; the basic notion is recreating a harmonious relationship (see [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-11|1 Cor 7.11]] on the reconciliation of an estranged married couple; [[Romans 05#^5-10|Rom 5.10]] on restoring the peaceful relationship between God and humanity, the meaning it bears in [[LXX 2 Maccabees 01#^1-5|2 Macc 1.5]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-33|7.33]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 08#^8-29|8.29]]). In Colossians, the Christ brings the disparate elements of the universe into harmony. *Blood*, Jesus’ death serves as an atoning and reconciling sacrifice. ^1-20

### 1.21–23a: Assurance and exhortation

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-21|Text]], p. 411* ^1-21

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Estranged*, Gentiles separated from Israel’s God before they heard the good news about the Christ. The Gk word occurs in the NT elsewhere only in [[Ephesians 02#^2-12|Eph 2.12]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-18|4.18]]. ^1-21-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Fleshly body*, see [[John 01#^1-14|Jn 1.14]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-35|1 Cor. 15.35ff.]] *To present you . . . before him*, reconciliation here has a juridical context that entails judgment (v 28). ^1-22

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-23|23a]]:** *Provided that you continue,* while some Christians, following Calvin, hold that salvation, once granted, is permanent (see e.g., Calvin, *Institutes* 3.21.7), Colossians holds that reconciliation requires the enduring obedience of the faithful. *The faith*, for the Deutero-Pauline letters, the connotation of “pistis” shifts from “trust” or “trustworthiness” to “belief” in the sense of doctrine. *Proclaimed to every creature*, hyperbole indicating that the author knows about the spread of the gospel; “creature” indicates all the elements of creation, not only human beings. In the great chorus of Ps 148, all of God’s creatures are enjoined to praise him; here the image is reversed; all of the creatures of God have been evangelized. *Servant*, v 25; cf. 1.7n. ^1-23

### 1.23b–29: Witness and mission

*[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-23|Text]], p. 411* ^1-23-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-23|23b]]:** *Paul*, Outside of salutations and conclusions, Paul himself only once uses his name in first-person discourse ([[Galatians 05#^5-2|Gal 5.2]]), and there he is being emphatic. The authors of Colossians and Ephesians insert an extra mention of his name to establish Pauline authorship (cf. [[Ephesians 03#^3-1|Eph 3.1]]). ^1-23-3

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-24|24]]:** *Sufferings*, for Paul, to be an apostle is to imitate the Messiah in his suffering (e.g., [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-7|2 Cor 4.7–12]]). The authors of Ephesians and Colossians emphasize this theme (cf. [[Colossians 04#^4-3|Col 4.3]],[[Colossians 04#^4-10|10]],[[Colossians 04#^4-18|18]]; [[Ephesians 03#^3-1|Eph 3.1]],[[Ephesians 03#^3-13|13]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-1|4.1]]). *Completing what is lacking in Christ’s afflictions*, the author borrows from Paul the image of the suffering apostle (e.g., [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-9|1 Cor 4.9–13]]) but adds that this suffering is necessary to complete the Messiah’s mission. A clearer translation might be “completing the sufferings that remain on behalf of the Messiah.” *His body, that is, the church*, continuing the metaphor from 1.18. In [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-15|1 Cor 6.15–20]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-12|12.12–31]], Paul develops the metaphor that the members of the church are parts of the Messiah’s body; the Gk words for “body” and “Messiah” appear together frequently (cf. [[Ephesians 04#^4-12|Eph 4.12]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-23|5.23]]; [[Colossians 01#^1-24|Col 1.24]]; [[Colossians 02#^2-11|2.11]],[[Colossians 02#^2-17|17]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-15|3.15]]) but in the NT only in Colossians and Ephesians do the words for “body” and “church” appear together ([[Ephesians 05#^5-23|Eph 5.23]]; [[Colossians 01#^1-18|Col 1.18]],[[Colossians 01#^1-24|24]]). ^1-24

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-25|25]]:** *Commission* (Gk “oikonomia”), occurs here and in [[Ephesians 01#^1-10|Eph 1.10]]; [[Ephesians 03#^3-2|3.2]] and 3.9, but only once in Paul’s own letters ([[1 Corinthians 09#^9-17|1 Cor 9.17]]). A term from household management, it more accurately means “stewardship” (see [[Luke 12#^12-42|Lk 12.42]]; [[Luke 16#^16-1|16.1]],[[Luke 16#^16-3|3]],[[Luke 16#^16-8|8]]). ^1-25

**[[NRSVue Colossians 01#^1-26|26–27]]:** *Mystery*, lit., “secret revealed to initiates.” The term is more than twice as frequent in the Deutero-Pauline letters as in other NT books. In apocalyptic Judaism, God’s plan for Israel is one of the secrets revealed to the seer (e.g., LXX [[LXX Daniel (Greek) 02#^2-28|Dan 2.28]]; *1 En*. 63.3). Here the secret is God’s election of the Gentiles (2.2; 4.3; cf. 3.1–3n). *Saints*, see 1.2n. *Gentiles*, Colossians, like Paul’s own letters, addresses Gentile audiences. ^1-26
