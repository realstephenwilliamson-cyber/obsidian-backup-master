# John 11 (JANT notes)

### 11.1–57: Sixth sign: raising of Lazarus

*[[NRSVue John 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 200* ^11-1

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-1|1]]:** *Lazarus*, Greek form of Eleazar (see [[Luke 16#^16-20|Lk 16.20–25]]). *Bethany*, village near Jerusalem. *Mary* and *Martha*, see [[Luke 10#^10-38|Lk 10.38–42]]. ^11-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-2|2]]:** *Anointed*, anticipates 12.1–3. ^11-2

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Lord*, as elsewhere depending on context could be translated “Sir.” ^11-3

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-4|4]]:** *For God’s glory*, see 9.3; Lazarus’s illness allows Jesus to demonstrate his filial relationship to God. ^11-4

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-5|5]]:** *Loved Martha . . . Lazarus*, Jesus’ love for the Bethany siblings is mentioned to make his delay in going to them all the more remarkable. ^11-5

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-6|6]]:** *Two days longer*, deliberately ignoring the sisters’ hope that he will heal their brother, Jesus insures that Lazarus is dead and buried. ^11-6

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-9|9]]:** *Walk during the day*, that is, while the light (Jesus) is with them; see 8.12; 12.35. ^11-9

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-11|11]]:** *Asleep . . . awaken*, metaphors for death and resurrection (see [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]). ^11-11

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-16|16]]:** *Thomas . . . Twin*, while the others read *and believe* c Gk *him* d Gk *he* e Gk *Didymus* other disciples fear for Jesus’ life should he return to Judea, Thomas demonstrates his loyalty and faithfulness by urging that the disciples both respect his decision to go and stand by him even if it means dying. “Thomas” may be a transliteration of the Heb word for twin (“tʾōmâ”). ^11-16

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-17|17]]:** *Four days*, according to some rabbinic sources (e.g., *Gen. Rab.* 50.10*)* and Zoroastrianism, the spirit hovers near the body for three days. ^11-17

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-19|19]]:** *Many of the Jews . . . to console,* implying a somewhat ritualized mourning observance, though it is unclear whether the formal practice of sitting “shiva” (observing a week of home-based mourning during which time others console mourners) was already in existence. The Jews’ presence here is dissonant with the fear of exclusion from the synagogue ascribed to the parents in 9.22. ^11-19

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-24|24]]:** *Resurrection on the last day*, Pharisaic and later general Jewish belief (Josephus, *Ant*. 18.1.4; see [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1–3]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-9|2 Macc 7.9]]). See “Afterlife and Resurrection,” p. 691. ^11-24

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-25|25]]:** *Resurrection and the life*, God’s gift of new existence (see 20.30–31). *Believe . . . will live* . . . *never die*, the meaning here may pertain to the distinction between physical and spiritual life or death: those who die physically will live spiritually. ^11-25

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-27|27]]:** *You are the Messiah*, similar to Peter’s confession in [[Mark 08#^8-29|Mk 8.29]] and [[Matthew 16#^16-16|Mt 16.16]]. Peter’s confession in [[John 06#^6-69|Jn 6.69]], however, does not use the term Messiah: “We have come to believe and know that you are the Holy One of God.” ^11-27

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-31|31]]:** May refer to a ritualized weeping at a tomb (see, e.g., [[LXX Sirach 38#^38-16|Sir 38.16–18]]), though this is easily explained on the grounds of emotion separate from any mourning ritual. ^11-31

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-33|33–35]]:** *Greatly disturbed* (repeated v 38) and 13.21, the only Gospel passages that ascribe an acute emotional reaction to Jesus. ^11-33

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-35|35]]:** *Weep*, this may be a ritual, or express Jesus’ genuine grief at his friend’s death. The latter seems unlikely in view of Jesus’ prior intention to use Lazarus’s death to glorify God and himself as God’s son (11.4). ^11-35

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-39|39]]:** *Stench*, emphasize that Lazarus is truly dead, and therefore stresses the marvel of his return to life. ^11-39

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-40|40]]:** *Glory*, see 11.4n. ^11-40

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-41|41]]:** *Looked upward*, Jesus appears to pray to show that Lazarus’s resurrection manifests God’s power, not his own. ^11-41

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-43|43]]:** *Lazarus*, Jesus calls him by name (see 5.25; 10.4). ^11-43

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-44|44]]:** *Cloth*, in the first century, Jews were buried in linen shrouds; corpses were laid in a sealed tomb so that the flesh would decompose. After 11 months, the tomb would be unsealed, and the bones would be placed in an ossuary (bone box) and stored on a shelf in the tomb. ^11-44

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-47|47]]:** *Chief priests and the Pharisees* . . . *the council*, perhaps the Sanhedrin. If so, the reference to Pharisees is puzzling as they had no authority to convene a meeting of the Sanhedrin. Another indication of John’s lack of interest, or perhaps lack of knowledge, in differentiating precisely among Jewish groups. ^11-47

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-48|48]]:** *Romans will come*, the leaders fear that Rome will see Jesus’ activities as tantamount to claiming kingship. *Holy place*, Jerusalem Temple. ^11-48

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-49|49]]:** *High priest that year*, see 11.51; 18.13. Another possible example of John’s lack of precise knowledge of pre-70 Jewish society; although some high priests had relatively short tenure, Caiaphas was high priest for approximately 18 years, between 18–36 CE. (Josephus, *Ant*. 18.90–95). ^11-49

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-50|50]]:** *Nation*, the Jewish people, especially those resident in Judea. [[LXX 2 Maccabees 05#^5-19|2 Macc 5.19]] also mentions place and nation together, suggesting that the combination was not invented by the author of John. ^11-50

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-51|51]]:** *Prophesied*, the evangelist emphasizes that Caiaphas’s statement, ironically, is truer than he knows. ^11-51

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-52|52]]:** *Dispersed children*, either Jews of the Diaspora (“dispersion”), those who live outside Judea, or non-Jewish followers of Jesus. ^11-52

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-54|54]]:** *Ephraim*, 12 miles north-northeast of Jerusalem. ^11-54

**[[NRSVue John 11#^11-55|55]]:** The Gospel’s third *Passover*. *To purify themselves*, pilgrims allowed extra time for purification rites. Participation in the Passover Temple rites were undertaken in a state of ritual purity ([[II Chronicles 30#^30-17|2 Chr 30.17–18]]), often involving bathing ([[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-12|Num 19.12]]). See also Josephus, *J.W.* 1.229, which refers to the period of purification prior to the pilgrimage festival of Pentecost (Shavuot). ^11-55
