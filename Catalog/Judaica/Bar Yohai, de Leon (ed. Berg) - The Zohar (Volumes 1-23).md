## About

The central work of Kabbalah: a group of books, mostly in Aramaic, that read the Torah for its mystical meaning and treat the nature of God, the structure of the universe, the soul and redemption. It presents itself as a Tannaitic work recording the teaching of Shimon bar Yochai and his circle; modern scholars place its writing in 13th-century Spain.

- **Authorship:** Moses de León first circulated it in Castile. Most modern scholars hold that he wrote it, most often dated 1280–86, though some see several authors or older material. Adolf Jellinek made the first systematic case (1851); Gershom Scholem's in 1938 was carried on by Yehuda Liebes and Daniel Matt. Wikipedia gives de León's dates as c. 1240–1305; Britannica gives 1250–1305.
- **Parts:** an untitled Torah commentary makes up the bulk (Britannica adds Ruth and the Song of Songs); shorter units include the *Sifra di-Tzni'uta* on Genesis 1–6, the *Idra Rabba* and *Idra Zuta*, and the *Midrash ha-Ne'elam*. Scholem gave the *Ra'aya Meheimna* and *Tikkunei ha-Zohar* to a later imitator. The *Zohar Hadash* gathers manuscript material found after the first printings.
- **Teachings:** Britannica names the ten *sefirot*, the problem of evil and the cosmic weight of prayer and good deeds.
- **First printings:** *Tikkunei ha-Zohar*, Mantua 1557; the main body at Cremona 1558 (one volume) and Mantua 1558–60 (three volumes).
- **English translations:** Michael Berg's complete translation with commentary, 23 volumes (Kabbalah Centre International, 2003). The Pritzker Edition, 12 volumes (Stanford University Press), by Daniel C. Matt with Nathan Wolski and Joel Hecker, translates a new critical Aramaic text Matt built from early manuscripts. Wikipedia dates it 2004–2017; Stanford's press release gives November 2003 for volume 1.
- **Why it matters:** Britannica says many Kabbalists gave it a sanctity normally reserved for the Torah and Talmud, and that its wide influence came after the expulsion from Spain in 1492. Critics from Elia del Medigo, who argued that it could not be Shimon bar Yochai's work, to Jacob Emden, who called it a forgery, rejected its antiquity.
