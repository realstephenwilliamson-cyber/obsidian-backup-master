## Chapter 10

**10:1** Ha!  
Those who write out evil writs  
And compose iniquitous documents, ^10-1

**10:2** To subvert the cause of the poor,  
To rob of their rights the needy of My people;  
That widows may be their spoil,  
And fatherless children their booty! ^10-2

**10:3** What will you do on the day of punishment,  
When the calamity comes from afar?  
To whom will you flee for help,  
And how will you save your carcasses[^1] ^10-3

**10:4** From collapsing under [fellow] prisoners,  
From falling beneath the slain?  
Yet His anger has not turned back,  
And his arm is outstretched still. ^10-4

**10:5** Ha!  
Assyria, rod of My anger,  
In whose hand, as a staff, is My fury![^2] ^10-5

**10:6** I send him against an ungodly nation,  
I charge him against a people that provokes Me,  
To take its spoil and to seize its booty  
And to make it a thing trampled  
Like the mire of the streets. ^10-6

**10:7** But he has evil plans,  
His mind harbors evil designs;  
For he means to destroy,  
To wipe out nations, not a few. ^10-7

**10:8** For he thinks,  
“After all, I have kings as my captains![^3] ^10-8

**10:9** Was Calno any different from Carchemish?  
Or Hamath from Arpad?  
Or Samaria from Damascus? ^10-9

**10:10** Since I was able to seize  
The insignificant kingdoms,  
Whose images exceeded  
Jerusalem’s and Samaria’s,[^4] ^10-10

**10:11** Shall I not do to Jerusalem and her images  
What I did to Samaria and her idols?” ^10-11

**10:12** But when my Lord has carried out all his purpose on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, He[^5] will punish the majestic pride and overbearing arrogance of the king of Assyria. ^10-12

**10:13** For he thought,  
“By the might of my hand have I wrought it,  
By my skill, for I am clever:  
I have erased the borders of peoples;  
I have plundered their treasures,  
And exiled their vast populations.[^6] ^10-13

**10:14** I was able to seize, like a nest,  
The wealth of peoples;  
As one gathers abandoned eggs,  
So *I* gathered all the earth:  
Nothing so much as flapped a wing  
Or opened a mouth to peep.” ^10-14

**10:15** Does an ax boast over him who hews with it,  
Or a saw magnify itself above him who wields it?  
As though the rod raised him who lifts it,  
As though the staff lifted the man![^7] ^10-15

**10:16** Assuredly,  
The Sovereign LORD of Hosts will send  
A wasting away in its[^8] fatness;  
And under its body[^9] shall burn  
A burning like that of fire,  
Destroying frame and flesh.  
It shall be like a sick man who pines away.[^10] ^10-16

**10:17** The Light of Israel will be fire  
And its Holy One flame.  
It will burn and consume its thorns  
And its thistles in a single day, ^10-17

**10:18** And the mass of its scrub and its farm land. ^10-18

**10:19** What trees remain of its scrub  
Shall be so few that a boy may record them. ^10-19

**10:20** And in that day,  
The remnant of Israel  
And the escaped of the House of Jacob  
Shall lean no more upon him that beats it,[^11]  
But shall lean sincerely  
On the LORD, the Holy One of Israel. ^10-20

**10:21** Only a remnant shall return,  
Only a remnant of Jacob,  
To Mighty God. ^10-21

**10:22** Even if your people, O Israel,  
Should be as the sands of the sea,  
Only a remnant of it shall return.  
Destruction is decreed;  
Retribution comes like a flood! ^10-22

**10:23** For my Lord GOD of Hosts is carrying out  
A decree of destruction upon all the land. ^10-23

**10:24** Assuredly, thus said my Lord GOD of Hosts: “O My people that dwells in Zion, have no fear of Assyria, who beats you with a rod and wields his staff over you as did the Egyptians. ^10-24

**10:25** For very soon My wrath will have spent itself, and My anger that was bent on wasting them.”[^12] ^10-25

**10:26** The LORD of Hosts will brandish a scourge over him as when He beat Midian at the Rock of Oreb,[^13] and will wield His staff as He did over the Egyptians by the sea. ^10-26

**10:27** And in that day,  
His burden shall drop from your back,  
[^14]And his yoke from your neck;  
The yoke shall be destroyed because of fatness. ^10-27

**10:28** He advanced upon Aiath,  
He proceeded to Migron,  
At Michmas he deposited his baggage. ^10-28

**10:29** They made the crossing;  
“Geba is to be our night quarters!”  
Ramah was alarmed;  
Gibeah of Saul took to flight. ^10-29

**10:30** “Give a shrill cry, O Bath-gallim!  
Hearken, Laishah!  
Take up the cry, Anathoth!” ^10-30

**10:31** Madmenah ran away;  
The dwellers of Gebim sought refuge. ^10-31

**10:32** This same day at Nob  
He shall stand and wave his hand.[^15]  
O mount of Fair Zion!  
O hill of Jerusalem! ^10-32

**10:33** Lo! The Sovereign LORD of Hosts  
Will hew off the tree-crowns with an ax:  
The tall ones shall be felled,  
The lofty ones cut down: ^10-33

**10:34** The thickets of the forest shall be hacked away with iron,  
And the Lebanon trees shall fall in their majesty.[^16] ^10-34

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; for “carcasses,” compare the rendering of kabod in v. 16; 22.18.
[^2]: Emendation yields “Who is a staff in the hand of my fury.”
[^3]: Emendation yields “all the kingdoms fared alike!”
[^4]: Emendation yields “Since I was able to seize / those kingdoms and their images, / Why is Jerusalem better than Samaria?”
[^5]: Heb. “I.”
[^6]: According to vv. 6–7, Assyria was to plunder, but not to exile.
[^7]: Lit. “not-wood.”
[^8]: Presumably Israel’s. These verses would read well after 9.16.
[^9]: Cf. note at v. 3.
[^10]: Brought up from v. 18 for clarity.
[^11]: I.e., upon Assyria (see v. 24). Ahaz’s reliance on Assyria was interpreted by Isaiah as lack of faith in the Lord; see 7.13 with note.
[^12]: Presumably Assyria; meaning of Heb. uncertain. Emendation yields “My anger against the world shall cease.”
[^13]: See Judg. 7.25.
[^14]: Emendation yields “And his yoke shall leave your neck. / He came up from Jeshimon / 28By the ascent of Aiath, / He proceeded to Migron; / At Michmas he commanded his forces: / 29‘Make the crossing; / Geba is to be our night quarters!’” Jeshimon is the southeast corner of the Jordan Valley, Num. 21.20; 23.28; Aiath is elsewhere called Ai.
[^15]: I.e., the Assyrian king, arriving at Nob (close to Jerusalem), shall beckon his army onward; cf. 13.2.
[^16]: Or “by the bronze,” connecting Heb. ’addir with Akkadian urudu, “bronze.”
