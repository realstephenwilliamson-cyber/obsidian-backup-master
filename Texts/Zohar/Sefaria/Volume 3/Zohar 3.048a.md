# Zohar 3.048a

← [[Zohar 3.047b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 3.048b]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 7.06 - Parashat Tazri'a#^z3-48a|Parashat Tazri’a, at 3:48a]]

*Volume 3, folio 48a* · Tazria

*Passage Tazria 22:105 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 3.047b#^tazria-22-105|Tazria 22:105]].*

**Tazria 23:106** We have learnt: Man in the Scripture has four names- *adam, geber, enosh*, ish-and the highest of them is *adam.’* Said R. Judah: ‘But is it not written, “When a man (*adam*) shall bring from you an offering” (Lev. 1, 2), and who is it that requires to bring an offering? Is it not a sinner?’ ^tazria-23-106

**Tazria 23:107** R. Isaac replied: ‘The offering is the mainstay of the world, of upper and lower beings, the solace of the Almighty, and who is fitting to offer it? Surely this man who is called Adam.’ Said R. Judah: ‘If so, what of the verse, “When a man (*adam*) has on the skin of his flesh… and it become in the skin of his flesh the plague of leprosy”?’ He replied: ‘This one God desires to heal more than all others,’ ^tazria-23-107

**Tazria 23:108** and therefore it is written concerning him, “he shall be brought unto the priest”; it is the duty of anyone who sees him to bring him to the priest, in order that the holy image may not remain thus.’ ^tazria-23-108

**Tazria 23:109** Said R. Judah: ‘But it is written, “Now Moses the man (ish)”; why is he not called “adam”?’ He replied: ‘Because he was the “servant of the king”, and therefore he is called *ish* in relation to the supernal Adam. But,’ he said, ‘why then is it written, “The Lord is a man (*ish*) of war”?’ He replied: ‘The secret of the Lord is to them that fear him.’ Said R. Judah: ‘I too am one of them, yet I have not been privileged to hear this.’ ^tazria-23-109

**Tazria 23:110** Said the other: ‘Go to R. Abba, for I learnt it from him only on condition that I should not tell.’ So he went to R. Abba, and found him discoursing and saying: ‘When is there said to be completeness above? When the Holy One, blessed be He, sits on his throne. For so it is written: “And upon the likeness of the throne was the likeness as the appearance of a man (*adam*) upon it above” (Ezek. 1, 26); the term “*adam*” indicates wholeness and completeness.’ Said R. Judah: ‘God be blessed that I have found you discoursing thus. Tell me: is it not written, “The Lord is a man (*ish*) of war”?’ ^tazria-23-110

**Tazria 23:111** Said R. Abba: ‘Your question is a good one, and the answer is this. At the Red Sea there was not yet full consummation, and therefore God was called *ish*; but in the vision of Ezekiel there was full consummation, and therefore God was called *adam.’* ‘ “The law of thy mouth is better to me than thousands of gold and silver” (Ps. 119, 72),’ exclaimed R. Judah. ^tazria-23-111

**Tazria 23:112** But’, he continued, ‘it is also written, “O Lord, thou preservest man (*adam*) and beast” (Ps. 36, 7). Should not the term *ish* be used here?’ He said: ‘This is analogous to the expression: “From the cedar which is in Lebanon to the hyssop that springeth out of the wall” (I Kings 5, 16); it is the way of the Scripture in such cases to mention the two extremes.’ ^tazria-23-112

**Tazria 23:113** But,’ he went on, ‘is it not written: “And there was no man (*adam*) to till the ground” (Gen. 2, 5)?’ He answered: ‘Everything in the world was only made for the sake of man, and all things were kept back until he that was called Adam should appear, since his form was after the divine prototype, and when he was created all was complete.’ ^tazria-23-113

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Tazria 23:114.*

**Tazria 23:115** R. Jose said: ‘The *Hayoth* which Ezekiel saw all had the form of man.’ ‘But’, said R. Judah, ‘it is written: “And the face of an eagle… and the face of a lion”, etc.?’ He replied: ‘AII had the face of man, but in this face itself was seen the resemblance of an eagle, etc.’ ^tazria-23-115
