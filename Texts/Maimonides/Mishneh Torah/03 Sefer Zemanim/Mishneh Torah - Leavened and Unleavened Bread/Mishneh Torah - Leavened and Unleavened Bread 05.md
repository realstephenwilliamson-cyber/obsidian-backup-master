**1.** The prohibition against chametz applies only to the five species of grain. They include two species of wheat: wheat and spelt; and three species of barley: barley, oats, and rye.  
However, *kitniyot* - e.g., rice, millet, beans, lentils and the like - do not become leavened. Even if one kneads rice flour or the like with boiling water and covers it with fabric until it rises like dough that has become leavened, it is permitted to be eaten. This is not leavening, but rather the decay [of the flour]. ^5-1

**2.** With regard to these five species of grain: If [flour from these species] is kneaded with fruit juice alone[^1] without any water, it will never become leavened. Even if [flour] is placed in [these juices] the entire day until the dough rises, it is permitted to be eaten [on Pesach],[^2] for fruit juice does not cause [dough] to become leavened.[^3] It merely causes [the flour] to decay.[^4]  
The following are [included in the category] of fruit juice: wine, milk, honey,[^5] olive oil, apple juice,[^6] pomegranate juice and all other similar wines, oils, and beverages.[^7]  
This applies so long as no water whatsoever is mixed with them. If any water is mixed with them, they cause [the flour] to become leavened.[^8] ^5-2

**3.** [On Pesach,] we should not cook wheat in water[^9] - for example, cracked wheat[^10] - or flour [in water] - for example, dough balls.[^11] If one cooks [either of the above][^12] - behold, it is absolutely chametz. This applies[^13] if [the kernels] crack open within the dish.[^14]  
We may not fry dough[^15] in oil in a frying pan.[^16] However, we may cook a loaf [of matzah][^17] or roasted flour.[^18] If one boiled a lot of water and, afterwards, placed flour into it[^19] - behold, it is permitted, because it is cooked immediately, before it could become leaven.[^20] [Nevertheless,] it is accepted custom in Babylonia, Spain, and the entire western [diaspora] to forbid this practice. This has been decreed lest one not boil the water well enough.[^21] ^5-3

**4.** It is permissible to cook grain or flour in fruit juice.[^22] Thus, dough which was kneaded with fruit juice, cooked with fruit juice, or fried with oil in a frying pan is permitted, for fruit juice does not cause leavening.[^23] ^5-4

**5.** Roasted grain[^24] which is singed in fire[^25] and then ground [into flour]: That flour should not be cooked with water, lest it has not been roasted well in the fire, and thus will become leavened when cooked.[^26]  
Similarly, when preparing new pots,[^27] we should not cook in them anything other than matzah that was baked and then ground into flour.[^28] It is forbidden to do so with roasted flour, for perhaps it will not be roasted well, and thus may become leaven.[^29] ^5-5

**6.** We do not stir barley in water on Pesach [to remove its husks],[^30] because [the kernels] are soft and become leavened rapidly. If one did stir [barley] in water and [the kernels] softened to the point[^31] that if they were placed at the opening to a roasting pan[^32] upon which loaves were usually baked, they would burst open - behold, they are forbidden.[^33] If they have not reached this degree of softness, they are permitted. ^5-6

**7.** It is permissible to stir wheat [kernels] in water to remove the bran,[^34] and then immediately[^35] grind them, as is done when grinding fine flour.[^36] [Nevertheless,] all Jews in Babylonia, *Eretz Yisrael*,[^37] Spain, and the cities of the western [diaspora] have accepted the custom of not stirring wheat in water.[^38] This decree [was instituted] lest [the kernels] be left aside and become leavened.[^39] ^5-7

**8.** A dish that was cooked, and barley or wheat[^40] was discovered inside it:[^41] If the grains have cracked open,[^42] the entire dish is forbidden, for chametz has become mixed together with it.[^43] If they have not cracked open,[^44] they[^45] must be removed and burned,[^46] but the remainder of the dish may be eaten.[^47] [This ruling was given] because grain that has been stirred in water without cracking open is not actual leaven as forbidden by the Torah. It is only a Rabbinic [ordinance]. ^5-8

**9.** \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-17|Exodus 12:17]]] states: "Keep watch over the matzot"[^48] - i.e., be careful of the matzot and protect them from any possibility of becoming chametz.[^49] Therefore, our Sages declared: A person must be careful regarding the grain which he eats on Pesach[^50] and [make sure] that no water has come in contact with it[^51] after it has been harvested,[^52] so that it will not have become chametz at all.[^53]  
Grain which sunk in a river or[^54] came in contact with water,[^55] just as it is forbidden to eat from it [on Pesach], it is forbidden to keep [possession of] it.[^56] Rather, he should[^57] sell it to a Jew[^58] [before it becomes prohibited][^59] and inform him [about its nature], so that he can eat it before Pesach.[^60] If he sells it to a gentile before Pesach, he should sell a small amount to a number of individuals,[^61] so that it will be finished before Pesach, lest the gentile go and sell it to [another] Jew.[^62] ^5-9

**10.** Grain[^63] upon which [water] leaking [from the roof] has fallen: As long as [the leak] continues, drop after drop, it will not become chametz[^64] even if [the leak continues] the entire day.[^65] However, if [the leak] stops,[^66] if it remains [untouched] for the standard measure [of time][^67] - behold, it becomes chametz. ^5-10

**11.** We do not knead a large dough on Pesach, lest it become chametz.[^68] Rather, [the size of the dough] is confined to the measure for which one is obligated to separate Challah.[^69]  
We do not knead with hot water, with water heated in the sun, or with water that was drawn on the present day,[^70] only with water that has[^71] rested for a day. A person who violates [this requirement] and kneads using one of the above[^72] - behold, the [baked] loaf becomes forbidden.[^73] ^5-11

**12.** A woman should not sit in the sun and knead,[^74] nor [should she knead] under the open sky on a cloudy day, even in a place where the sun is not shining.[^75] [She should not] leave the dough[^76] and become involved in another matter.[^77] If she both kneads and bakes,[^78] she must have two containers of water,[^79] one to smooth [the matzot][^80] and one to cool off her hands.[^81]  
If she violated [these instructions] and kneaded dough in the sun,[^82] or did not cool off her hands, or made a dough larger than the measure for which one is obligated to separate Challah, the loaf is permitted.[^83] What is the measure for which one is obligated to separate Challah? Forty-three and a fifth eggs of average size[^84] - by volume and not by weight.[^85] ^5-12

**13.** As long as a person is busy with the dough, even for the entire day, it will not become chametz.[^86] If he lifts up his hand[^87] and allows the dough to rest so that [it rises to the extent that] a noise will resound when a person claps it with his hand,[^88] it has already become chametz[^89] and must be burned immediately.[^90] If a noise does not resound[^91] and[^92] the dough has lain at rest for the time it takes a man to walk a *mil*,[^93] it has become chametz and must be burned immediately.[^94]  
Similarly, if its surface has become wrinkled[^95] [to the extent that it resembles] a person whose hair stands [on end in fright][^96] - behold, it is forbidden to eat from it, but one is not liable for כרת [for eating it].[^97] ^5-13

**14.** There were two doughs which people stopped kneading at the same time and left unattended.[^98] From one, a sound [resounded when clapped].[^99] From the other, no sound [resounded].[^100] Both of them should be burned[^101] - behold, they are absolutely chametz. ^5-14

**15.** We should not make thick loaves[^102] with designs[^103] on Pesach, because a woman takes time making them.[^104] Thus, [the dough] will become leavened during that time. Hence, professional bakers are allowed to make [such designs],[^105] because they are skilled in their craft and quick in its execution.[^106]  
[In contrast,] a private person is forbidden to make such a design, even if he does so using a [pre-cut] form.[^107] [This stringency was instituted] lest others attempt to make [the designs] without using the forms, delay in their work, and cause [the dough] to become chametz.[^108] ^5-15

**16.** The water used to wash one's hands and the kneading trough after the kneading [is completed][^109] and, similarly, the water used when kneading[^110] - behold, it should be poured out in a place which slopes downward, so it will not collect in one place[^111] and become leavened.[^112] ^5-16

**17.** We should not soak bran[^113] in water and leave it in front of chickens,[^114] lest it become chametz. However, we can boil bran for them,[^115] and then place it before them. Nevertheless, the majority of people have already adopted the custom of not boiling [grain products], lest the water not be boiled thoroughly.[^116] ^5-17

**18.** It is permitted to prepare bran or flour for chickens in water[^117] if they are fed[^118] immediately, or if one places [the feed] before them and stands over them to ensure that the food will not stand[^119] more than the time it takes a person to walk a *mil*.[^120]  
As long as they peck at it or one turns it over by hand,[^121] it will not become leavened. Once they finish eating, one should wash out the utensil in water and pour it out in a place which slopes downward.[^122] ^5-18

**19.** A woman should not soak bran which she intends to take to the bathhouse in water [beforehand].[^123] However, she may rub dry bran on her skin.[^124] A person should not chew wheat[^125] and then place it on his wound,[^126] for it will become chametz.[^127]  
We should not place flour into charoset.[^128] If one did so, he should burn it[^129] immediately,[^130] because it will cause [the flour] to become leavened very fast.[^131] We should not place flour in mustard.[^132] If one did so, he should eat it immediately.[^133] ^5-19

**20.** It is permissible to place spices,[^134] sesame seeds, poppy seeds,[^135] and the like[^136] into dough.[^137] Similarly, it is permissible to knead the dough with water and oil, honey or milk, or to baste with them.[^138]  
On the first day,[^139] it is forbidden to knead or baste[^140] [the matzot] with any other substance besides water;[^141] not because of the prohibition against chametz,[^142] but rather so [the matzah] will be "poor man's bread."[^143] It is only on the first day that the "poor man's bread" must be commemorated.[^144] ^5-20

**21.** All earthenware vessels[^145] that were used for chametz while cold[^146] may[^147] be used for matzah while cold,[^148] except for the utensils[^149] in which yeast and charoset[^150] were placed, for their leavening effect is powerful.[^151] Similarly, a kneading trough in which chametz is kneaded and left to become leavened is considered as a place where yeast is soaked, and we do not use it on Pesach.[^152] ^5-21

**22.** An earthenware roasting pan on which loaves of chametz are baked during the entire year should not be used to bake matzah on Pesach.[^153] If it was filled with coals and kindled on the place[^154] where chametz was cooked, matzah it is permitted to be cooked on it. ^5-22

**23.** Metal and stone utensils[^155] in which chametz was boiled in water as a כלי ראשון[^156] - e.g., pots and stew pots[^157] - should be placed inside a large utensil.[^158] They should be covered with water, and the water should be boiled[^159] with them inside[^160] until they release [what they absorbed].[^161] Afterwards, they should be washed off with cold water,[^162] and [then] one may use them for matzah.[^163]  
Similarly, [with regard to] knives, the blade and the handle should be boiled in a כלי ראשון.[^164] Afterwards, one may use them for matzah. ^5-23

**24.** Utensils of metal, stone, and wood which were used for chametz as a כלי שני[^165] - e.g., bowls or cups[^166] - should be placed in a large utensil, and boiling water poured over them.[^167] They should be left in [the large utensil] until they release [the chametz they absorbed].[^168] Afterwards, they should be washed off.[^169] [Then,] they may be used for matzah. ^5-24

**25.** All earthenware utensils that were used for chametz in hot water,[^170] whether as a[^171] כלי ראשון - for example, pots[^172] - or as a  
כלי שני - for example, bowls[^173] - whether they were glazed and coated with lead so that they became like glass, or they were of simple earthenware:[^174] we do not use them for matzah.[^175] Rather, we put them aside until after Pesach,[^176] and then we may cook with them.[^177] ^5-25

**26.** [A person possesses a utensil which is] a כלי ראשון and desires to boil it [in order to prepare it for Passover use],[^178] however, he cannot find a larger utensil into which [to place it] to boil it:[^179] Behold, he may place a border of clay around its [top] edge from the outside and fill it[^180] with water until the water overflows its edge.[^181] [Then,] he may boil the water in it, and this is sufficient.[^182] Afterwards, he washes it off[^183] and may use it for matzah. ^5-26
