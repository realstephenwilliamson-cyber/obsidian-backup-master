# Proverbs 17

← [[Proverbs 16]] · [[Tanakh]] · [[Proverbs 18]] →

Compare: [[NRSVue Proverbs 17|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 17

**17:1** Better a dry crust with peace  
Than a house full of feasting with strife. ^17-1

**17:2** A capable servant will dominate an incompetent son  
And share the inheritance with the brothers. ^17-2

**17:3** For silver—the crucible;  
For gold—the furnace,  
And the LORD tests the mind. ^17-3

**17:4** An evildoer listens to mischievous talk;  
A liar gives ear to malicious words. ^17-4

**17:5** He who mocks the poor affronts his Maker;  
He who rejoices over another’s misfortune will not go unpunished. ^17-5

**17:6** Grandchildren are the crown of their elders,  
And the glory of children is their parents. ^17-6

**17:7** Lofty words are not fitting for a villain;  
Much less lying words for a great man. ^17-7

**17:8** A bribe seems like a charm to him who uses it;  
He succeeds at every turn. ^17-8

**17:9** He who seeks love overlooks faults,  
But he who harps on a matter alienates his friend. ^17-9

**17:10** A rebuke works on an intelligent man  
More than one hundred blows on a fool. ^17-10

**17:11** An evil man seeks only to rebel;  
A ruthless messenger will be sent against him. ^17-11

**17:12** Sooner meet a bereaved she-bear  
Than a fool with his nonsense. ^17-12

**17:13** Evil will never depart from the house  
Of him who repays good with evil. ^17-13

**17:14** To start a quarrel is to open a sluice;  
Before a dispute flares up,[^1] drop it. ^17-14

**17:15** To acquit the guilty and convict the innocent—  
Both are an abomination to the LORD. ^17-15

**17:16** What good is money in the hand of a fool  
To purchase wisdom, when he has no mind? ^17-16

**17:17** A friend is devoted at all times;  
A brother is born to share adversity. ^17-17

**17:18** Devoid of sense is he who gives his hand  
To stand surety for his fellow. ^17-18

**17:19** He who loves transgression loves strife;  
He who builds a high threshold invites broken bones. ^17-19

**17:20** Man of crooked mind comes to no good,  
And he who speaks duplicity falls into trouble. ^17-20

**17:21** One begets a dullard to one’s own grief;  
The father of a villain has no joy. ^17-21

**17:22** A joyful heart makes for good health;[^2]  
Despondency dries up the bones. ^17-22

**17:23** The wicked man draws a bribe out of his bosom  
To pervert the course of justice. ^17-23

**17:24** Wisdom lies before the intelligent man;  
The eyes of the dullard range to the ends of the earth. ^17-24

**17:25** A stupid son is vexation for his father  
And a heartache for the woman who bore him. ^17-25

**17:26** To punish the innocent is surely not right,  
Or to flog the great for their uprightness. ^17-26

**17:27** A knowledgeable man is sparing with his words;  
A man of understanding is reticent. ^17-27

**17:28** Even a fool, if he keeps silent, is deemed wise;  
Intelligent, if he seals his lips. ^17-28

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^2]: Or “a cheerful face”; meaning of Heb. uncertain.
