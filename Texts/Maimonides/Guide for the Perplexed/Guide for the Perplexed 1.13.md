# Guide for the Perplexed 1.13

← [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.12]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.14]] →

*Part 1, Chapter 13* · [[Guide for the Perplexed]]

THE term ‘amad (he stood) is a homonym signifying in the first instance “to stand upright,” as “When he stood (be-‘omdo) before Pharaoh” ([[Torah 10 - Miketz#^41-46|Gen. 41:46]]); “Though Moses and Samuel stood (ya‘amod)” ([[Jeremiah 15#^15-1|Jer. 15:1]]); “He stood by them” ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-8|Gen. 18:8]]). It further denotes “cessation and interruption,” as “but they stood still (‘amedu) and answered no more” ([[Job 32#^32-16|Job 32:16]]); “and she ceased (va-ta‘amod) to bear” ([[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^29-35|Gen. 29:35]]). Next it signifies “to be enduring and lasting,” as, “that they may continue (yo‘amedu) many days” ([[Jeremiah 32#^32-14|Jer. 32:14]]); “Then shalt thou be able to endure (‘amod)” ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^18-23|Exod. 18:23]]); “His taste remained (‘amad) in him” ([[Jeremiah 48#^48-11|Jer. 48:11]]), i.e., it has continued and remained in existence without any change: “His righteousness standeth for ever” ([[Psalms 111#^111-3|Ps. 111:3]]), i.e., it is permanent and everlasting. The verb applied to God must be understood in this latter sense, as in [[Zechariah 14#^14-4|Zechariah 14:4]], “And his feet shall stand (ve-‘amedu) in that day upon the Mount of Olives” ([[Zechariah 14#^14-4|Zech. 14:4]]), “His causes, i.e., the events of which He is the cause, will remain efficient,” etc. This will be further elucidated when we speak of the meaning of regel (foot). (Vide infra, chap. xxviii.) In the same sense is this verb employed in [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-28|Deuteronomy 5:28]], “But as for thee, stand thou here by me,” and [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-5|Deuteronomy 5:5]], “I stood between the Lord and you.”
