## Taming the tongue

**3:1** Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers and sisters, for you know that we who teach will face stricter judgment. ^3-1

**3:2** For all of us make many mistakes. Anyone who makes no mistakes in speaking is mature,^[Gk *a mature man*] able to keep the whole body in check with a bridle. ^3-2

**3:3** If we put bits into the mouths of horses to make them obey us, we guide their whole bodies. ^3-3

**3:4** Or look at ships: though they are so large and are driven by strong winds, yet they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the will of the pilot directs. ^3-4

**3:5** So also the tongue is a small member, yet it boasts of great exploits. How great a forest is set ablaze by a such a small fire! ^3-5

**3:6** And the tongue is a fire. The tongue is placed among our members as a world of iniquity; it stains the whole body, sets on fire the cycle of life, and is itself set on fire by hell.^[Gk *Gehenna*] ^3-6

**3:7** For every species of beast and bird, of reptile and sea creature, can be tamed and has been tamed by the human species, ^3-7

**3:8** but no one can tame the tongue—a restless^[Other ancient authorities read *uncontrollable*] evil, full of deadly poison. ^3-8

**3:9** With it we bless the Lord^[Other ancient authorities read *God*] and Father, and with it we curse people, made in the likeness of God. ^3-9

**3:10** From the same mouth comes a blessing and a curse. My brothers and sisters, this ought not to be so. ^3-10

**3:11** Does a spring pour forth from the same opening both fresh and brackish water? ^3-11

**3:12** Can a fig tree, my brothers and sisters, yield olives or a grapevine figs? No more can salt water yield fresh. ^3-12

## Two kinds of wisdom

**3:13** Who is wise and knowledgeable among you? Show by your good life that your works are done with gentleness born of wisdom. ^3-13

**3:14** But if you have bitter envy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not be arrogant and lie about the truth. ^3-14

**3:15** This is not wisdom that comes down from above but is earthly, unspiritual, devilish. ^3-15

**3:16** For where there is envy and selfish ambition, there will also be disorder and wickedness of every kind. ^3-16

**3:17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without a trace of partiality or hypocrisy. ^3-17

**3:18** And the fruit of righteousness^[Or *justice*] is sown in peace by those who make peace. ^3-18
