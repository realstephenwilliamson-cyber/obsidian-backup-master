### 16.1–23: Commendation and greetings

*[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-1|Text]], p. 319* · Although he has not been to Rome, Paul knows of many members of the community by name, or household, or service. ^16-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-1|1]]:** *Phoebe* likely carried and presented this letter. *Church*, or assembly. *Deacon*, Gk “diakonos,” servant, here an authoritative role ([[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]). *Cenchreae*, Corinth’s port. ^16-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-2|2]]:** *Saints*, see 1.7n. *Benefactor*, one who uses wealth to help the less fortunate. ^16-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-3|3]]:** *Prisca and Aquila*, [[Acts 18#^18-2|Acts 18.2]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-19|1 Cor 16.19]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-19|2 Tim 4.19]]. ^16-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-5|5]]:** *First convert*, lit., first fruit. ^16-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-7|7]]:** *Apostles*, “those sent out,” early ones dispersed to testify to the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus. ^16-7

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-10|10]]:** *Approved*, lit., a tested one. ^16-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-16|16]]:** *Holy kiss*, a formal greeting in the assemblies ([[1 Corinthians 16#^16-20|1 Cor 16.20]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-26|1 Thess 5.26]]). ^16-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-17|17]]:** *Offenses*, the same word used in 14.13 as well as Paul’s citation of [[Isaiah 28#^28-16|Isa 28.16]] in 9.33, [[Psalms 069#^69-23|Ps 69.22]] in 11.9. *Avoid them*, lit., “bend (turn) away from them.” ^16-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-18|18]]:** *Appetites*, lit., “belly”; *the simple-minded*, or naïve, unsuspecting. The accusation that they are slaves of their belly coupled with specific teaching that they oppose may indicate that they oppose accommodating Jewish dietary norms; i.e., that they are “gentilizers” (14.1–15.7). Vv 17–20 evoke references to the serpent of Gen 2–3. ^16-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-19|19]]:** See 1.5,8; 15.14–19; 16.26. *Guileless in what is evil,* or unsophisticated, not practiced. ^16-19

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-20|20]]:** Cf. [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-14|Gen 3.14–15]]. *Satan*, or the adversary. ^16-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-21|21]]:** *Timothy*, see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|1 Cor 4.17]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-10|16.10]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|2 Cor 1.1]],[[2 Corinthians 01#^1-19|19]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]; Philem 1; cf. [[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1–3]]; [[2 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|2 Thess 1.1]]; [[Colossians 01#^1-1|Col 1.1]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-5|2 Tim 1.5]]. *Lucius*, cf. [[Acts 13#^13-1|Acts 13.1]]; [[Colossians 04#^4-14|Col 4.14]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-11|2 Tim 4.11]]; Philem 24. *Jason*, see [[Acts 17#^17-5|Acts 17.5–9]]; *Sosipater*, see [[Acts 20#^20-4|Acts 20.4]]. ^16-21

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-22|22]]:** *Tertius*, the amanuensis who wrote Romans for Paul. ^16-22

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-23|23]]:** *Gaius*, see [[Acts 19#^19-29|Acts 19.29]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-14|1 Cor 1.14]]. *Erastus*, see [[Acts 19#^19-22|Acts 19.22]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-20|2 Tim 4.20]]. An “aedile” (a Roman government officer) named Erastus is memorialized as a benefactor for laying a pavement stone near the theater in Corinth, although the dating of this action undermines cross-identification. ^16-23

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-23|24–27]]:** These verses represent a useful summary, although they are absent from many manuscripts and are widely regarded as a later addition. ^16-24

**[[NRSVue Romans 16#^16-25|25]]:** *To God*, lit., “to the one”; *for long ages*, lit., “eternal ages.” verse then reads, *to the only wise God be the glory through Jesus Christ forever. Amen.* c Other ancient authorities lack 16.25-27 or include it after 14.23 or 15.33; others put verse 24 after verse 27 ^16-25
