## About

A dialogue by Plato in which Socrates tells Crito about a display by two sophist brothers, Euthydemus and Dionysodorus, whose eristic arguments are built so that any attempt at refutation fails. It sets Socratic conversation and education against sophistic method, to the latter's cost.

- **People and setting:** Socrates (narrator) and Crito in the frame; in the story, the brothers, the youth Clinias and Ctesippus. The scene is the dressing room of the Lyceum (Jowett). Wikipedia says the brothers, also pankratiasts, were real: Plato and Aristotle mention Euthydemus, Xenophon Dionysodorus.
- **Dramatic date:** Nails and Monoson (SEP) give 407 BC. Dušanić (1999) notes that Alcibiades is still alive in it (275b). Jowett, by contrast, argued that the scene could not be earlier than 404.
- **Date of composition:** Wikipedia gives c. 384 BC. The IEP places it in the early group, after Socrates' death and before Plato's first trip to Sicily in 387. Jowett held it could not have been composed before 390; Dušanić takes it to predate Isocrates' *Panegyricus* (c. 380). Its authenticity is not disputed in these sources.
- **Subject:** sophistic fallacies, among them the argument that falsehood is impossible because what is not cannot be spoken. Near the end Crito reports an unnamed writer of speeches who scorned both the display and philosophy. Dušanić identifies him as Isocrates; Jowett treats him as a type, naming Antiphon or Lysias as examples.
- **Place among the dialogues:** Diogenes Laertius records it first in Thrasyllus's sixth tetralogy, as *Euthydemus, or The Eristic*, classed "refutative", followed by *Protagoras*, *Gorgias* and *Meno*. Jowett linked it in plan and style to *Protagoras*, *Charmides* and *Lysis*. Stephanus pages 271a–307c.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia reads it as Plato's satire of the Sophists' logical fallacies; Dušanić reads its portrait of the critic as evidence for its date and political context.
