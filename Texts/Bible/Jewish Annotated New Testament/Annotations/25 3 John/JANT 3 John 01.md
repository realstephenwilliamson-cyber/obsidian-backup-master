# 3 John 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue 3 John 01|NRSVue]] · [[3 John 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 3 John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-1|1]]:** Greeting. *Elder*, Gk “presbyter”; the term can refer to a church office (e.g., Acts 15; [[Titus 01#^1-5|Titus 1.5]]; [[James 05#^5-14|Jas 5.14]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-1|1 Pet 5.1]]; 2 Jn 1), but it also means “old man” (e.g., [[Acts 02#^2-17|Acts 2.17]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-1|1 Tim 5.1]]). See the Introduction to 2 John, on the Elder, Heb “zaqen.” Perhaps “Elder” alludes to his old age, if indeed the author is identified as the Gospel’s “Beloved Disciple,” an eyewitness and companion of Jesus. *Beloved*, frequent epithet in Christian texts (vv 2,5,11 and e.g., [[Romans 12#^12-19|Rom 12.19]]; [[Romans 16#^16-5|16.5]],[[Romans 16#^16-8|8]],[[Romans 16#^16-9|9]],[[Romans 16#^16-12|12]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-58|1 Cor 15.58]]; [[2 Corinthians 12#^12-19|2 Cor 12.19]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-12|Phil 1.12]]). *Gaius*, the addressee, is probably not identical to the other men named Gaius mentioned in [[Acts 19#^19-29|Acts 19.29]]; [[Acts 20#^20-4|20.4]]; [[Romans 16#^16-23|Rom 16.23]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-14|1 Cor 1.14]]. Both Jews and non-Jews in this period bore this name. *Truth*, a major motif of the letter, related to a theme of John’s Gospel and perhaps a reference to Jesus (see, e.g., [[John 01#^1-14|Jn 1.14]]; [[John 04#^4-23|4.23–24]]; [[John 05#^5-33|5.33]]; [[John 08#^8-32|8.32]]; [[John 14#^14-17|14.17]]). ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-2|2–8]]:** Gaius commended for his hospitality.  ^1-2

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Soul* (Gk “psyche”), Jewish postbiblical thought adopted Greek conceptions of humans as having a dual body/soul composition; its use here is part of a stock phrase wishing the addressee good health. ^1-2-2

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Friends* (Gk “adelphoi,” lit., “brothers”), the term group members use to refer to one another (also vv 5,10). *Testified*, Gk “martyroō,” “bore witness to,” the verbal form of the Gk word on which English “martyr” is based. The theme of testifying, or bearing witness (also vv 6,12) is a Johannine motif. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-4|4]]:** *My children* may refer to believers the elder converted; it does not have the dualistic connotation it has in other Johannine literature, like the “children of God” vs. the “children of the devil” ([[1 John 03#^3-10|1 Jn 3.10]]). ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Church*, Gk “ekklēsia,” referring to the assembly of believers. *Send them on*, likely, provide financial support. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-7|7]]:** Lit., “for they went out for the sake of the Name accepting no support from the Gentiles” (see Introduction). *Support*, see [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-3|1 Cor 9.3–12]]. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-9|9–12]]:** Diotrephes and Demetrius.  ^1-9

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Diotrephes*, community member who snubbed the elder. *Does not acknowledge our authority*, lit., “does not welcome us,” the same verb used in v 10. The breach of hospitality contravened social conventions. Perhaps Diotrephes rejected the elder’s teaching (see 2 Jn 9–11). ^1-9-2

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-10|10]]:** *False charges*, rivalry between Christian leaders was not uncommon (e.g., [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-5|1 Cor 11.5]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-11|12.11]]; [[Revelation 02#^2-14|Rev 2.14]],[[Revelation 02#^2-20|20–23]]). Diotrephes refuses to welcome the friends, and his hostile behavior to the elder and his associates proves that he is not “walking in the truth” (see 2 Jn 4–6); *expels* (Gk “ekballei,” removes from the community; see [[Matthew 18#^18-17|Mt 18.17]]), it is unclear how formalized a procedure this is. The nearest parallel is Paul’s suggestion that the Corinthians ([[1 Corinthians 05#^5-1|1 Cor 5.1–5]],[[1 Corinthians 05#^5-11|11]]) remove from table fellowship “anyone who bears the name of brother or sister who is sexually immoral or greedy, or is an idolater, reviler, drunkard, or robber.” Like Paul, Diotrephes seems to have enough authority to judge who should be expelled from the community. Expulsion is also found in the Dead Sea Scrolls (e.g., 1 QS 7.1–2,16–17,22–25, and 8.21–23). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Imitate*, Gk “mimēsis,” a form of instruction (see [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-7|2 Thess 3.7]],[[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-9|9]]; [[Hebrews 13#^13-7|Heb 13.7]]). ^1-11

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Demetrius*, otherwise unknown, may be the bearer of the letter. ^1-12

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-13|13–15]]:** Closing.  ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-13|13–14]]:** *Rather not write with pen and ink* (lit., “ink and reed”), the elder is declaring his preference for face to face conversation (cf. 2 Jn 12, “that our joy may be complete”). ^1-13-2

**[[NRSVue 3 John 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Peace to you*, a Semitic rather than conventional Greek blessing, found in other Christian letters ([[Ephesians 06#^6-23|Eph 6.23]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-14|1 Pet 5.14]]; 2 Jn 3). *Friends*, a Christian address (see [[John 15#^15-15|Jn 15.15]]), likely referring to the elder’s supporters. ^1-15
