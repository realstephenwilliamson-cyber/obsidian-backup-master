To make your grammatical point, you must focus on the **\$\text{Ἐγώ εἰμι}\$ (*Ego eimi*)**.

#### 1. The Superfluous Pronoun

In Greek, verbs imply the subject. To say "I am," one could simply say \$\text{εἰμι}\$ (*eimi*). However, Jesus uses the explicit pronoun \$\text{Ἐγώ}\$ (*Ego*) alongside the verb.

- **Standard Reading:** This is for emphasis. "I, *and only I*, am the way."

- **Your Argument's Reading:** The emphasis is not on exclusivity, but on **identity**. Jesus is defining the nature of the "I." The "I" *is* the way. The grammatical subject (\$\text{Ἐγώ}\$) is fused with the predicate of existence.

#### 2. The Absolute Usage

Scholars often distinguish between the "predicate" *Ego eimi* (e.g., "I am the bread") and the "absolute" *Ego eimi* (e.g., [[John 08#^8-58|John 8:58]], "Before Abraham was, I am").

- Your argument bridges these. You are suggesting that even in the predicate form ("I am the way"), the **"I AM"** operates as a divine title. The "Way" is not a path *outside* of the self; the Way is the realization of the Divine Self (\$\text{Ἐγώ εἰμι}\$) within.
