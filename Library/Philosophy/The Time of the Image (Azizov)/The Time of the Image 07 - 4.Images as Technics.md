## 4.	Images as Technics

4.1	

The Spatialisation and the Grammatisation 


### 4.1	The Spatialisation and the Grammatisation 
of the Visible

Now we have to see how the activity of adopting, setting forth and projecting helps to make a place and ›placing‹ possible. Stiegler underlines the spatialising nature of grammatisation:

»The grammatisation \[…\] consists in a spatialisation of time, given that behavior is above all a form of time (a meaningful sequence of words, an operational sequence of gestures, a perceptual flow of sensations, and so on). Spatialising time means, for example, transforming the temporal flow of a speech such as the one I am delivering to you here and now into a textual space, a de-temporalised form of this speech: it is thus to make a spatial object« (Stiegler 2012: 7).

I will explore the possibilities of spatialising and ›placing‹ in its relation to the event and to the meaning, which plays a crucial role in the formation of images as technics. As quoted above, »an event of delivering a speech and then to a textual space« is an event of »de-temporalisation and making a spatial object« (Stiegler 2012: 7). What happens to a letter of the alphabet when it loses the connection with the speech? It turns into the dot or the dash. The spatialisation takes place by the lateral spread of the meaning. The meaning receives a shape while realising the intention and opens up the space by exteriorising memory. Recollecting conveys the role of translating memory to meaning within the space of language. Language is, of course, only one aspect of spatialisation, but it is the most known and closest to understanding. It gives way to the emergence of meaning and helps to expand the space of knowing. Accretion 

*[p. 138]* ^pg139

and expansion are accompanying elements of spatialisation. If to take into account that writing is a visible activity, and reading and visualising certain ideas is the condition of perception, then it is possible to say that the visual is the economy which derives from the historical development of philosophy in its constant attempts to expand its boundaries, to make a place for thinking. In a way philosophy has survived many centuries because it constantly questions its boundaries and constantly attempts to invest in its territory ideas that migrate from somewhere else. Philosophy itself is a space made as a result of accretion. The necessity of philosophy is inseparable from the necessity to ask questions where they proliferate. It was true from the very early ages of humanity, for example when in the late sixth century B.C. Pythagoras of Samos and his followers thought to understand the events of the world by numbers only (Singh 1998). But as numbers were multiplied and grown, they understood that there is a need to think what to do with them. Pythagoras understood that growing questions with numbers and geometrical objects cannot be dealt with by means of mathematics any longer; thus, there was a need to open up the possible space to locate its outcome. Philosophy was created as a space for questioning, for an inquiry into things that cannot be explained just by themselves. This is also the condition of the possibility of philosophy: by questioning philosophy expands its territory.

Each question as a finite being creates the condition for the infinite. In our times of the electronic, digital evolution this condition has gone up to data, and the territory of thinking has expanded from reading to watching, without rejecting any of them. As a result, philosophy cannot only be read but it can also be shown without replacing the activity of reading. For example, watching a philosophy lecture works in its difference to the reading of the same lecture as a text. The difference is the essential basis of a visual mechanics. It is the visual expansion of the real ever since the understanding of the real took place. The expansion of the visual dimension makes thinking possible differently, and it is the progress from readability as a text to visibility as a text.

The triad of text, image and sound organises an expressive dimension. There should be a new methodology of bringing these novelties into being. There is a need of a new model which should prove that, if we are becoming critical of previous models, it is despite the fast development of means and ideas. These developments make previous models to a part of the experience, and new models are extensions of older ones. The past

Like monads of Leibniz, once snipped into grid, different forms of knowledge turns into patterns (Leibniz 1991). Ideas and forms ›communicate‹ between themselves as they become programmed in the electronic space. It is becoming more difficult to distinguish between concepts and forms while the programming replaces the productivity.[^171]

To distinguish becomes an event, and in this event the temporality and the constant translation challenges the existing space radically because of the persistence of ›spatio-temporality‹. The translation of temporal objects back into the spatio-temporal dimension of images is an event of knowing. This event opens-up the space, which is not the end but the terminal, the interval for temporal objects, which provides the possibility for the further translation. It places one thing into something else. To make sure, ›placing‹ means to place something into something else, and this usually is an event in itself. But how does an event take place? An event of change starts from the place. It takes place. The Place as a Necessary Condition of Being: the Image as the Condensed Fragmentation 


### 4.2	The Place as a Necessary Condition of Being:

The place is a necessary condition of being.[^180] This means not the space or the country or any other spatial order, which constantly creates confusions; without denying their existence, the place is at question here. The place consists of a temporal duration of bringing ideas and discursive questions together spatially. The place is a position in relation to other positions.[^181] It is given birth by the projection or what is called ›projects‹. Heidegger defines the basic aspect of the project as ›it first opens a domain (Spiel-raum) where things show themselves‹ (Heidegger 1967: 92).

From this moment the projection moves not only forward but also backwards. The world becomes a space where moving backward is possible equally in the same way as moving forward. The place of the projection may be defined as the trajectory of moving forward and backward, and this motion proceeds before being grasped by thought. The point of being

Chris Maker’s work shows that, while the world cannot be perceived directly, it can be perceived as an edited version of the events in this world. Montage and narrative are assimilating to each other, as thinking becomes articulate in the object of art. Thinking appropriates technics and as such creates the distortion of pure perception. The cinema as art is not pure; it is an appropriation of different forms of art, such as painting, literature and theatre. The cinema does not only appropriate the artistic operations of literature, theatre, painting; it also ›defies these arts‹. The cinema as a temporal industrial object constantly occupies itself discovering its function as art in the mixture. The cinema is a ›mixed cinema‹ and cannot exist without the mixture (Bazin 2004: 53-75). This mixture is the condition of the conceptual impurity of the cinema. In this the point of reference is lost, the virtual and the actual become indiscernible. This makes the pure existence for the cinema impossible. It is this very impossibility that defines the cinema. The cinema becomes the unique art of the border, the impure art par excellence. The cinema is the indiscernibility of the actual and virtual to the extent that there is no longer any linkage of the real with the imagery. This creates a constant mixture of actual and virtual images. As a result, in *La Jetée* not only the ›industrial temporal object‹ is mixed with the non-industrial one, but it is also a mixing of elements of the thought and memory by appropriating events of the world.

The complexity of the film is in this mixture. This mixture consists of what we believe to be real and what we don’t believe to be real. It transcends borders of belief, yet this transcendence is isomorphic, and the power of thought gives way to an unthought in thought that is beyond the outside world but is capable of reconstructing our belief in the world. The montage is the process of reconstruction; it is the methodology of ›rückfragen‹. It helps to exteriorise the interior world of subjects. In *La Jetée* the cinematic image carries out ›the suspension of the world‹. The suspension of the world with a disturbance is far from making thought visible and is »in the contrary directed to what does not let itself be thought in thought and equally to what the vision does not let itself be seen in vision« (Jean-Louis Schefer quoted in Deleuze 2005: 163). If the shift from the thought to the image and from the image to a thought is made, it is in order to bring the default subject back to the suspended world and to restore our belief in what we see and as we see.

If we believe what we see in the cinema, it is because the lost belief is restored by the fullness given to the image. The technology that destroyed the protagonist’s memory is later used by the montage to restore the destroyed world and to reconcile the world of memory with its beginnings. The thought reconciles the unthought. The fullness given to the image creates the balance between technics and thought. »Technics is unthought« (Stiegler 1998: ix.). In *La Jetée* thought is assimilated to the technics and the latter became informed by thinking. This is because images take a new sense, determining relations of time and space in their impossibility to exist at the same time. What makes time direct and the space visible is technics after it is being inserted back into thought.
