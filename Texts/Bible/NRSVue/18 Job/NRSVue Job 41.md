# Job 41 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Job 40|← Job 40]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Job 42|Job 42 →]]

**41:1** ^[40.25 in Heb]“Can you draw out Leviathan with a fishhook or press down its tongue with a cord? ^41-1

**41:2** Can you put a rope in its nose or pierce its jaw with a hook? ^41-2

**41:3** Will it make many supplications to you? Will it speak soft words to you? ^41-3

**41:4** Will it make a covenant with you to be taken as your servant forever? ^41-4

**41:5** Will you play with it as with a bird or put it on a leash for your young women? ^41-5

**41:6** Will traders bargain over it? Will they divide it up among the merchants? ^41-6

**41:7** Can you fill its skin with harpoons or its head with fishing spears? ^41-7

**41:8** Lay hands on it; think of the battle; you will not do it again! ^41-8

**41:9** ^[41.1 in Heb]Any hope of capturing it^[Heb *of it*] will be disappointed; one is overwhelmed even at the sight of it. ^41-9

**41:10** No one is so fierce as to dare to stir it up. Who can stand before it?^[Heb *me*] ^41-10

**41:11** Who can confront it^[Heb *me*] and be safe?^[Gk: Heb *that I shall repay*]—under the whole heaven, who?^[Heb *to me*] ^41-11

**41:12** “I will not keep silent concerning its limbs or its mighty strength or its splendid frame. ^41-12

**41:13** Who can strip off its outer garment? Who can penetrate its double coat of mail?^[Gk: Heb *bridle*] ^41-13

**41:14** Who can open the doors of its face? There is terror all around its teeth. ^41-14

**41:15** Its back^[Cn Compare Gk Vg: Heb *pride*] is made of shields in rows, shut up closely as with a seal. ^41-15

**41:16** One is so near to another that no air can come between them. ^41-16

**41:17** They are joined one to another; they clasp each other and cannot be separated. ^41-17

**41:18** Its sneezes flash forth light, and its eyes are like the eyelids of the dawn. ^41-18

**41:19** From its mouth go flaming torches; sparks of fire leap out. ^41-19

**41:20** Out of its nostrils comes smoke, as from a boiling pot and burning rushes. ^41-20

**41:21** Its breath kindles coals, and a flame comes out of its mouth. ^41-21

**41:22** In its neck abides strength, and terror dances before it. ^41-22

**41:23** The folds of its flesh cling together; it is firmly cast and immovable. ^41-23

**41:24** Its heart is as hard as stone, as hard as the lower millstone. ^41-24

**41:25** When it raises itself up the gods are afraid; at the crashing they are beside themselves. ^41-25

**41:26** Though the sword reaches it, it does not avail, nor does the spear, the dart, or the javelin. ^41-26

**41:27** It counts iron as straw and bronze as rotten wood. ^41-27

**41:28** The arrow cannot make it flee; slingstones, for it, are turned to chaff. ^41-28

**41:29** Clubs are counted as chaff; it laughs at the rattle of javelins. ^41-29

**41:30** Its underparts are like sharp potsherds; it spreads itself like a threshing sledge on the mire. ^41-30

**41:31** It makes the deep boil like a pot; it makes the sea like a pot of ointment. ^41-31

**41:32** It leaves a shining wake behind it; one would think the deep to be white-haired. ^41-32

**41:33** On earth it has no equal, a creature without fear. ^41-33

**41:34** It surveys everything that is lofty; it is king over all that are proud.” ^41-34
