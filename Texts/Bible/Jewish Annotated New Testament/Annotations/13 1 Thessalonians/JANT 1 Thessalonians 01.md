### 1.1: Address and salutation

*[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 420* · *Sylvanus*, elsewhere called Silas. *Timothy*, Paul’s close associate. [[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1–3]] describes him a having a Jewish mother (see also [[2 Timothy 01#^1-15|2 Tim 1.15]]); he and Sylvanus accompanied Paul on his missionary journey. Unlike most of his letters, Paul does not here identify himself as an apostle, but see 2.7. *God the Father*, Tanakh and rabbinic references to God as “father” are usually in the limited covenantal sense of “Father of Israel.” See [[Isaiah 63#^63-16|Isa 63.16]]; [[Isaiah 64#^64-7|64.8]]; [[LXX Tobit 13#^13-4|Tob 13.4]] \[*Sinaiticus* A\]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-3|Wis 14.3]]; [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|Sir 23.1]],[[LXX Sirach 23#^23-4|4]]; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 02#^2-21|3 Macc 2.21]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 7.380; Philo, *Decalogue* 64; *Praem.* 166; *Spec. Laws* 1.41; 2.225; *b. Taʾan.* 25b). *Lord Jesus Christ*, *Lord* refers to Jesus’ divinity, *Jesus* is his proper name, and *Christ* describes his messianic nature. *Grace* (Gk “charis”), referring to God’s blessing, a possible variant of “charein,” “greetings.” *Church* (Gk *“*ekklēsia”), as synonym for “synagogue” (Gk “synagōgē”), denotes primarily an assembly of people, not a building. ^1-1

### 1.2–10: Opening thanksgiving

*[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-2|Text]], p. 420* ^1-2-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Give thanks* **(**Gk “eucharisteo”), in the letter-writing conventions of Paul’s time, an expression of concern for the well-being of the addressees followed the salutation. Paul extends the thanksgiving—all one sentence in Greek—to v 10. *Mention you in our prayers*, Moses prays for Miriam to be healed ([[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-13|Num 12.13]]), and “the people” ask Samuel to pray for them ([[I Samuel 12#^12-19|1 Sam 12.19]]); see also *m*. *Ber* 5.5; *b*. *Ned* 40b for examples of rabbis praying for individuals by name. ^1-2-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Remembering before*, i.e., expressly mentioning their devotion in his prayers. *Faith . . . love . . . hope*, standard Pauline linkage (cf. 5.8; [[1 Corinthians 13#^13-13|1 Cor 13.13]]). ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Beloved by God*, for converts to Judaism as beloved by God, see *Gen. Rab*. 70.5; *Num. Rab*. 8.2; *Midr. Tanh*. 6. *Chosen*, evoking God’s choice of Israel (e.g., [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-6|Deut 7.6]]). In rabbinic sources, the primary example of God’s choosing Israel is the gift of Torah, see *m. Avot* 3.14, “Beloved are the people Israel, for a special article \[Torah\] was given them.” *Gospel*, here the “good news” of Jesus. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Holy Spirit* (see 4.8), here perhaps indicating divine inspiration (cf. the “spirit of the Lord” in prophetic literature, e.g., [[Isaiah 61#^61-1|Isa 61.1]]; [[Ezekiel 11#^11-5|Ezek 11.5]]) but not indicating formal Trinitarian views, which developed later (see [[Romans 15#^15-19|Rom 15.19]]; cf. Jn 14–16). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Imitators of us and of the Lord*, Paul often presents himself as an example to be followed ([[1 Corinthians 04#^4-16|1 Cor 4.16]]; [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-1|11.1]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-17|Phil 3.17]]; [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-7|2 Thess 3.7]],[[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-9|9]]); he similarly lists other believers ([[Philippians 03#^3-17|Phil 3.17]]; [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-7|2 Thess 3.7]],[[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-9|9]]; [[Hebrews 06#^6-12|Heb 6.12]]; [[Hebrews 13#^13-7|13.7]]) and the Christ ([[1 Corinthians 11#^11-1|1 Cor 11.1]]) as exemplars. The rabbis were also seen as exemplars; see *b. Ber* 62a, in which R. Akiva and R. Kahana learn by watching their teachers in the privy and in bed. *Persecution* (Gk “thlipsis”), used in LXX (e.g., [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1]]) to describe the suffering of God’s people in an eschatological context; some Jewish post biblical sources describe a period of suffering or global upheaval before the coming of the Messiah, e.g., *b. Sanh* 98b: “There will be no peace because of the affliction.” *Word*, reference to their reputation as steadfast believers. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Achaia*, the southern province of Greece, location of Corinth and Athens. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Turned to God*, [[LXX Tobit 14#^14-6|Tob 14.6]] speaks of the nations (Gk “ethne”) who will turn (Gk “epistrepho”; NRSV “convert”) and “worship God in truth.” See also for Gentiles turning to the God of Israel, [[Isaiah 19#^19-22|Isa 19.22]]; [[Jeremiah 18#^18-8|Jer 18.8]],[[Jeremiah 18#^18-11|11]]; [[Psalms 022#^22-28|Ps 22.28]]. From *idols*, the converts were pagans; Jewish sources are particularly critical of idol worshippers (see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-12|Wis 14.12]] \[“For the idea of making idols was the beginning of fornication”\]; *m. Avod. Zar.* 4.7). *Living and true God*, compare to [[Jeremiah 10#^10-8|Jer 10.8–10]] where the “true” and “living” God is contrasted to idols. Turning to the “living God” is part of Paul’s missionary appeal to Gentiles. Early Jewish literature contains portrayals of Gentiles attracted to the “living God” (e.g., Deut 6.26; [[Joshua 03#^3-10|Josh 3.10]], and see [[LXX Daniel (Greek) 05#^5-23|Dan 5.23]]\[LXX\]; Bel 40–42; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 06#^6-28|3 Macc 6.28]]; *Jub*. 21.3–4; cf. [[Matthew 16#^16-16|Mt 16.16]]; [[Matthew 26#^26-63|26.63]]). ^1-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Wait for his Son*, for Paul, who never mentions a virginal conception, the son is the preexistent and eternal Christ who took on human form, died, was resurrected, and will return (see, e.g., [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6–8]]). *Heaven*, the realm of God (see 4.16n; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-1|1 Cor 12.1–3]]), where Jesus abides until his return. *He raised*, the resurrection of Jesus is the model for the eventual resurrection of all believers. Resurrection of dead, a Pharisaic notion (see Josephus, *J.W.* 2.162) is found in Second Temple and rabbinic descriptions of the messianic age (see e.g., *m. Sanh.* 10.1, which claims that resurrection is a teaching derived from Torah \[see “Afterlife and Resurrection,” p. 691\]). *Rescues*, from death. *Wrath that is coming*, end-time manifestation of God’s righteous anger (e.g., [[Ezekiel 38#^38-18|Ezek 38.18–23]]; [[Revelation 16#^16-12|Rev 16.12–16]]; *Sib. Or.* 3.545–72; 5.683, “And God will reign down on the earth dire wrath”; *1 En*. 55.3). Rabbinic sources also refer to a day of wrath, e.g., *b. B. Bat.* 10a; 116a; *b. Avod. Zar.* 18b, elaborating upon biblical predictions of the “day of the Lord” (see [[Isaiah 13#^13-6|Isa 13.6]],[[Isaiah 13#^13-9|9]]; [[Jeremiah 46#^46-10|Jer 46.10]]; [[Ezekiel 30#^30-3|Ezek 30.3]]; [[Joel 1#^1-15|Joel 1.15]]; Am 5.18–20; etc.). ^1-10
