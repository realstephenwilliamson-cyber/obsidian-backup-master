# LXX Sirach 38

← [[LXX Sirach 37]] · [[LXX]] · [[LXX Sirach 39]] →

*Sirach · [[LXX]]*

**38:1** Honour a physician with the honour due unto him for the uses which ye may have of him: for the Lord hath created him.  ^38-1

**38:2** For of the most High cometh healing, and he shall receive honour of the king.  ^38-2

**38:3** The skill of the physician shall lift up his head: and in the sight of great men he shall be in admiration.  ^38-3

**38:4** The Lord hath created medicines out of the earth; and he that is wise will not abhor them.  ^38-4

**38:5** Was not the water made sweet with wood, that the virtue thereof might be known?  ^38-5

**38:6** And he hath given men skill, that he might be honoured in his marvellous works.  ^38-6

**38:7** With such doth he heal [men,] and taketh away their pains.  ^38-7

**38:8** Of such doth the apothecary make a confection; and of his works there is no end; and from him is peace over all the earth.  ^38-8

**38:9** My son, in thy sickness be not negligent: but pray unto the Lord, and he will make thee whole.  ^38-9

**38:10** Leave off from sin, and order thine hands aright, and cleanse thy heart from all wickedness.  ^38-10

**38:11** Give a sweet savour, and a memorial of fine flour; and make a fat offering, as not being.  ^38-11

**38:12** Then give place to the physician, for the Lord hath created him: let him not go from thee, for thou hast need of him.  ^38-12

**38:13** There is a time when in their hands there is good success.  ^38-13

**38:14** For they shall also pray unto the Lord, that he would prosper that which they give for ease and remedy to prolong life.  ^38-14

**38:15** He that sinneth before his Maker, let him fall into the hand of the physician.  ^38-15

**38:16** My son, let tears fall down over the dead, and begin to lament, as if thou hadst suffered great harm thyself; and then cover his body according to the custom, and neglect not his burial.  ^38-16

**38:17** Weep bitterly, and make great moan, and use lamentation, as he is worthy, and that a day or two, lest thou be evil spoken of: and then comfort thyself for thy heaviness;  ^38-17

**38:18** for of heaviness cometh death, and the heaviness of the heart breaketh strength.  ^38-18

**38:19** In affliction also sorrow remaineth: and the life of the poor is the curse of the heart.  ^38-19

**38:20** Take no heaviness to heart: drive it away, and remember the last end.  ^38-20

**38:21** Forget him not, for there is no turning again: thou shalt not do him good, but hurt thyself.  ^38-21

**38:22** Remember his judgment: for thine also shall be so; yesterday for me, and to day for thee.  ^38-22

**38:23** When the dead is at rest, let his remembrance rest; and be comforted for him when his spirit is departed from him.  ^38-23

**38:24** The wisdom of a learned man cometh by opportunity of leisure: and he that hath little business shall become wise.  ^38-24

**38:25** How can he get wisdom that holdeth the plough, and that glorieth in the goad, that driveth oxen, and is occupied in their labours, and whose talk is of bullocks?  ^38-25

**38:26** He giveth his mind to make furrows; and is diligent to give the kine fodder.  ^38-26

**38:27** So every carpenter and workmaster, that laboureth night and day: and they that cut and grave seals, and are diligent to make great variety, and give themselves to counterfeit imagery, and watch to finish a work:  ^38-27

**38:28** The smith also sitting by the anvil, and considering the iron work, the vapour of the fire wasteth his flesh, and he fighteth with the heat of the furnace: the noise of the hammer and the anvil is ever in his ears, and his eyes look still upon the pattern of the thing that he maketh; he setteth his mind to finish his work, and watcheth to polish it perfectly:  ^38-28

**38:29** So doth the potter sitting at his work, and turning the wheel about with his feet, who is alway carefully set at his work, and maketh all his work by number;  ^38-29

**38:30** he fashioneth the clay with his arm, and boweth down his strength before his feet; he applieth himself to lead it over; and he is diligent to make clean the furnace:  ^38-30

**38:31** all these trust to their hands; and every one is wise in his work.  ^38-31

**38:32** Without these cannot a city be inhabited: and they shall not dwell where they will, nor go up and down:  ^38-32

**38:33** they shall not be sought for in public counsel, nor sit high in the congregation: they shall not sit on the judges' seat, nor understand the sentence of judgment: they cannot declare justice and judgment; and they shall not be found where parables are spoken.  ^38-33

**38:34** But they will maintain the state of the world, and [all] their desire is in the work of their craft. But he that giveth his mind to the law of the most High, and is occupied in the meditation thereof,  ^38-34
