# John 5 (JANT notes)

### 5.1–47: Third sign: healing of the lame man

*[[NRSVue John 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 186* ^5-1

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-1|1]]:** *Festival of the Jews*, the festival is unspecified. ^5-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-2|2]]:** *Sheep Gate*, gate to the Temple precinct (Neh 3.l). Remains of a second-century healing sanctuary (Ascle-peion) and pool with five porticoes have been discovered near the church of St. Anne in Jerusalem. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-7|7]]:** *Water is stirred*, some manuscripts (e.g., Codex Alexandrinus) insert a verse referring to the legend that an angel would come and stir up the water. This would explain why the pool attracted those in need of healing. ^5-7

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-8|8]]:** *Stand up*, in [[Mark 02#^2-11|Mk 2.11]], proof of Jesus’ authority to forgive sins (but see below, v 14). ^5-8

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-9|9–10]]:** *Sabbath* . . . *not lawful*, the healing story becomes a Sabbath conflict: carrying any object outside one’s household was prohibited ([[Jeremiah 17#^17-21|Jer 17.21–22]]). Sabbath carrying laws were elaborated upon in great detail in the Mishnah (*Eruv.*) and the Babylonian Talmud (*Eruv. Shabb.* ) but it is not known to what degree the laws in these tractates reflect first-century Jewish law or practice. Rabbinic law, which postdates the Gospel of John, permits, even mandates, healing of a life-threatening condition on the Sabbath, but the healing of a chronic illness was not, since that treatment could easily take place before or after the Sabbath (see *b. Yoma* 84b). ^5-9

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Do not sin*, Jesus implies that illness is punishment for sin; see 9.2–3 (see also [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-9|Num 12.9–10]]). ^5-14

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-16|16]]:** *Persecuting*, no specific persecution is mentioned ([[Matthew 05#^5-10|Mt 5.10]]; [[Acts 09#^9-4|Acts 9.4]]). ^5-16

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-17|17]]:** *Still working*, [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-2|Gen 2.2–3]] states that God rested on the seventh day. Philo (*Cher*. 86–90; *Leg. all.* 1.5–6) conceded that God did not rest on subsequent Sabbaths, given that vegetation grew, children were born, and other natural processes continued. Jesus declares his filial relationship to and imitation of God as a creative force (1.1–4); no explicit statement in the Gospel abrogates Jewish Sabbath observance ([[Mark 02#^2-27|Mk 2.27]]). ^5-17

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-18|18]]:** *Kill him*, [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-14|Ex 31.14–15]]; [[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-2|35.2]] specify that violating the Sabbath warrants death. The punishment by death of a man who gathered sticks on the Sabbath is recounted in [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-32|Num 15.32–36]]. The historicity of this account is often questioned, and there is no evidence that the penalty for desecration of the Sabbath was ever carried out. *Making himself equal to God*, in stating that he is working just like God is, Jesus is taking himself out of the human category to which Sabbath observance applies, and placing himself in the category of the divine that is equal to God, at least in the sense that the Sabbath laws with regard to healing do not apply to him. Elsewhere, Jesus clarifies that his authority is derived *Bethsaida* c Other ancient authorities add, wholly or in part, *waiting for the stirring of the water; 4for an angel of the Lord went down at certain seasons into the pool, and stirred up the water; whoever stepped in first after the stirring of the water was made well from whatever disease that person had.* d Or *had left because of* from and therefore dependent on God, implying a hierarchical relationship in which he is subordinate to God (5.30). ^5-18

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-19|19]]:** *The Son does likewise*, Jesus’ actions are like what *the Father* does, not in the sense of slavish imitation, but rather within a specific context according to divine will. ^5-19

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-21|21]]:** *Gives life*, the use of the present tense to express what otherwise reads like an anticipatory statement regarding the resurrection of Christians brings to the fore the tension in the Gospel of John with regard to a present salvation (realized eschatology; see 5.24n.) or future salvation (future eschatology). The Gospel does not explicitly resolve this tension but creates the impression that the believer experiences both. ^5-21

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-22|22]]:** *Judges no one*, apocalyptic texts such as *1 En.* and *2 Bar.* express the belief in a final judgment after the messianic age, that is, after the coming of a personal Messiah; see *1 En.* 22; *2 Bar.* 7; this view is present in rabbinic literature as well, e.g., *m. Ed*. 2.10, which lists the length of time that specific groups are punished after judgment (see “Afterlife and Resurrection,” p. 691). ^5-22

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-24|24]]:** *Has eternal life*, God’s goals, expressed in Jesus’ ministry, are already accomplished (a position known as “realized eschatology,” meaning that the conditions of the “last days” \[Gk “eschata”\] are already present \[“realized”\]). ^5-24

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-25|25]]:** *Dead will hear*, Jesus’ voice reaches the dead, who will be called to final judgment (see 11.1–44; see also [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1–3]]; [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-13|1 Thess 4.13–18]]). ^5-25

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-29|29]]:** *Resurrection of life . . . of condemnation*, references to eternal life or eternal death. The language is similar to [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]. ^5-29

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-31|31–47]]:** [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-6|Deut 17.6]] specifies that in capital cases, two or three witnesses are required. Continuing the juridical motif, Jesus acts as his own lawyer and calls *John* the Baptist (v 33), his *works* and God’s works (v 36), *the Father* (v 37) and the *scriptures* (vv 39–47) as witnesses. Thereby he accuses his Jewish opponents of misunderstanding their scriptures and alienating God. This passage, as in others that describe Jesus as doing God’s works (5.20; 7.21; 9.3; 10.32,37; 14.10–12; 15.24), hint at the belief that Jesus is the prophet-like-Moses predicted by [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15]], as expected by both Jews and Samaritans. ^5-31

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-39|39]]:** *In them you have eternal life*, the Gospel asserts that Jews seek salvation in the scriptures. [[Proverbs 03#^3-18|Prov 3.18]] refers to the Torah as “a tree of life to those who lay hold of her” and states that “those who hold her fast are called happy.” The meaning does not however appear to be eschatological; the idea that Jews search the scriptures for eternal life does not have support in sources contemporaneous with the Gospel of John. ^5-39

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-41|41]]:** *Glory from human beings*, Jesus is not seeking validation from other people, as he receives his glory from God. See 8.54; 11.4; 12.16,23; 13.31; 16.14; 17.1,4,10. ^5-41

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-45|45]]:** *Accuser is Moses,* a references to the scriptures, which, the Gospel asserts here, testify to Jesus’ identity as Messiah and God’s son; those who do not interpret the scriptures in this way, insists the Gospel, stand accused of misunderstanding their own scriptures. ^5-45

**[[NRSVue John 05#^5-46|46]]:** *He wrote about me*, an inclusion with v 39, referring to the christological interpretation of the Torah as prophesying Jesus’ coming. Many passages are in view here, e.g., Gen 18, the visit of the Lord (18.1) or three angels (18.2) to Abraham, alluded to throughout [[John 08#^8-37|Jn 8.37–47]], and the giving of the manna, Ex 16, which is the subject of the discourse in [[John 06#^6-25|Jn 6.25–65]]. ^5-46
