### 11.1–4: The “Our Father” or “Lord’s Prayer”

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 138* · ([[Matthew 06#^6-9|Mt 6.9–13]]). ^11-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-1|1]]:** *Praying*, see 3.21n. *As John taught*, showing both continuity with John as well as distinction. ^11-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-2|2]]:** *Father*, Jewish address for God, especially in postbiblical prayers ([[LXX Tobit 13#^13-4|Tob 13.4]]; [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|Sir 23.1]]; [[LXX Sirach 51#^51-10|51.10]]). *Hallowed be your name*, the sanctity of God’s name is a biblical theme: [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-3|Lev 20.3]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-2|22.2]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-20|Ezek 36.20]]; [[Psalms 030#^30-5|Ps 30.4]]; [[I Chronicles 16#^16-10|1 Chr 16.10]],[[I Chronicles 16#^16-35|35]]; see also the early Aram “Kaddish” prayer “Yitgad dal veyitqaddash shmei rabba,” “Magnified and sanctified be \[God’s\] great name.” *Your kingdom come*, yearning for the messianic age. *B*. *Ber.* 40b records, “Rav said that any benediction in which the Divine Name is not mentioned is no benediction. R. Johanan, however, said: Any benediction in which \[God’s\] Kingship \[Heb “malkut”\] is not mentioned is no benediction.” ^11-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Daily bread*, the obscure Gk “epiousion,” translated by Jerome as “daily” (Lat “cotidianum”) may derive from Gk “epi” + “einai” (“to come”) and thus suggest a future aspect, e.g., “bread for tomorrow” and so the messianic banquet (13.29; 14.15; see also [[Isaiah 25#^25-6|Isa 25.6–8]]; 1QSa 2.11–22; *1 En*. 25.4–6; 62.13–16; *T. Levi* 18.10–11. *M. Avot* 3.16 \[“And the judgment is a true judgment, and everything is ready for the banquet”\]; *Num. Rab*. 13.2 \[“The Holy One, blessed be he, will therefore in the hereafter give them to drink of the wine that is preserved in grapes since the six days of creation”\]). References to bread may also suggest manna ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-15|Ex 16.15]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-24|Ps 78.24]]; [[Nehemiah 09#^9-15|Neh 9.15]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 16#^16-20|Wis 16.20]]) and so divine provision. ^11-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-4|4]]:** *Sins . . . indebted*, Aram “ḥov” can be translated “sin” or “debt”; in rabbinic, but not biblical literature, sins were considered debts against God. *We ourselves forgive*, see *m. Yoma* 8.9 (“For transgressions between man and man, the Day of Atonement atones, only if the man will regain the good will of his friend”); *b. Shabb*. 151b; *b. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 17a; *b. Meg*. 28a; *Midr. Tanh. , Hukkat* 19.1–22.1. *Time of trial*, test or temptation, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-1|Gen 22.1]], Abraham, Job, and for Israel, [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-4|Ex 16.4]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^8-2|Deut 8.2]]. Because God controls events, God can also test; see 22.40,46 (“pray that you will not come into the time of trial”). ^11-4

### 11.9–13: Assurance of God’s attention

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-9|Text]], p. 138* · ([[Matthew 07#^7-7|Mt 7.7–11]]). ^11-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-9|9]]:** *Ask, and it will be given*, see [[Psalms 145#^145-18|Ps 145.18]] (“The LORD is near to all who call on him, to all who call on him in truth”). ^11-9-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-11|11]]:** *Anyone* (Gk “pater”), “father”; see *necessary, or only one* b Other ancient authorities read *Our Father in heaven* c A few ancient authorities read *Your Holy Spirit come upon us and cleanse us.* Other ancient authorities add *Your will be done, on earth as in heaven* d Or *our bread for tomorrow* e Or *us into temptation*. Other ancient authorities add *but rescue us from the evil one* (or *from evil*) f Other ancient authorities add *bread, will give a stone; or if your child asks for* 8.42n. ^11-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-13|13]]:** *How much more*, a “qal vahomer” (Heb for “light and heavy”; a rhetorical form that argues from the lesser to the greater \[a fortiori\]) argument (e.g., *m. Makk*. 3.15: “R. Hananiah ben Gamaliel said: ‘If he that commits one transgression thereby forfeits his life, how much more, if he performs one religious duty, shall his life be given to him!’ ”). *Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n. ^11-13

### 11.14–23: Beelzebul controversy

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-14|Text]], p. 139* · ([[Matthew 12#^12-22|Mt 12.22–30]]; [[Mark 03#^3-22|Mk 3.22–27]]). ^11-14

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-15|15]]:** *Beelzebul*, Satan (see v 18); Beelzebul, “exalted Baal,” originally a title of the Canaanite Baal ([[II Kings 01#^1-2|2 Kings 1.2]]; Baal-zebub, a parody of the name, means “lord of the flies”). *B. Sanh*. 43a, 107b attributes Jesus’ wonder-workings to demonic powers. ^11-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-16|16]]:** *Sign*, see [[Matthew 16#^16-1|Mt 16.1]] and [[Mark 08#^8-11|Mk 8.11]]. Jewish tradition recognized mighty works; the question was whether they were miracle or magic, facilitated by God or Satan (see “Jewish Miracle Workers,” p. 680). ^11-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-18|18]]:** *Satan*, see 4.3n. ^11-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-19|19]]:** *Your exorcists*, Jewish miracle workers (see Josephus, *Ant*. 8.45 on Solomon’s power to exorcise). **20**: *Finger of God*, see [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^8-15|Ex 8.19]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^9-10|Deut 9.10]]. ^11-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-21|21–22]]:** *One stronger*, here Jesus’ self-reference. ^11-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-23|23]]:** See 9.50; one either follows Jesus or is his enemy. ^11-23

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-24|11.24–26]]:** Accounting for relapse after exorcism, see also [[Matthew 12#^12-43|Mt 12.43–45]]. ^11-24

### 11.27–28: True family

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-27|Text]], p. 139* ^11-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-27|27]]:** *Woman in the crowd*, there is nothing unusual about her presence or her voice. *Blessed*, blessings of both individuals (e.g., [[Judges 05#^5-24|Judg 5.24]] for Jael) and groups are common in the Tanakh. ^11-27-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-28|28]]:** *Blessed rather*, see 8.21. Luke continues to emphasize the family determined by belief or trust in Jesus rather than by marriage or biology. ^11-28

### 11.29–32: Sign of Jonah

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-29|Text]], p. 139* · ([[Matthew 12#^12-38|Mt 12.38–42]]; [[Mark 08#^8-11|Mk 8.11–12]]). ^11-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-29|29]]:** *Sign*, see 11.16. *Sign of Jonah*, Jonah’s successful preaching to the (Gentile) Ninevites but also his rescue from the fish (analogous to resurrection *Holy Spirit from heaven* b Gk *sons* \[see [[Matthew 12#^12-39|Mt 12.39]]\]). Jonah functions as a prototype for Jesus, and just as his presence and preaching moved the Ninevites to repentance, so Jesus will move his followers. ^11-29-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-30|30]]:** *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. ^11-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-31|31]]:** *Queen of the South*, of Sheba ([[I Kings 10#^10-1|1 Kings 10.1–10]]; [[II Chronicles 09#^9-1|2 Chr 9.1–9]]; Josephus \[*Ant*. 8.165\] identifies her as “queen of Egypt and Ethiopia”), a Gentile. *Will rise*, at the resurrection; see [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2–3]]. ^11-31

### 11.33–36: Internal light

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-33|Text]], p. 140* ^11-33

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-33|33]]:** *Lamp*, see 8.16; [[Matthew 05#^5-15|Mt 5.15]]; [[Mark 04#^4-21|Mk 4.21]]. ^11-33-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-34|34–36]]:** *Eye*, see [[Matthew 06#^6-22|Mt 6.22–23]]. ^11-34

### 11.37–52: Invectives against Pharisees and lawyers

*[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-37|Text]], p. 140* ^11-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-37|37]]:** *Pharisee invited him to dine*, see 7.36n. ^11-37-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-38|38]]:** *Did not first wash* ([[Matthew 15#^15-1|Mt 15.1–9]]; [[Mark 07#^7-1|Mk 7.1–5]]), “Netilat yadayim,” washing hands before eating, a ritual of sanctification known from early rabbinic texts (see *m. Yad*. passim). Josephus describes the Essenes as bathing before eating (*J.W*. 2.129). ^11-38

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-39|39]]:** *Lord*, see 1.17n. ^11-39

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-41|41]]:** *Alms* (Heb “tzedaqah”), see 6.30n. ^11-41

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-42|42]]:** *Woe to you Pharisees*, see [[Matthew 23#^23-13|Mt 23.13–33]]. *Tithe*, see [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-22|Deut 14.22]], etc.; with the proceeds used to support the widow, the orphan, the resident alien, and the Levite ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-29|Deut 14.29]]). *Mint and rue* . . . , *m. Shev*. 9.1 actually states, “Rue, goosefoot, wild coriander, water parsley, and eruca of the field are exempt from \[separation of\] tithes.” ^11-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-44|44]]:** *Unmarked graves*, graves were whitewashed ([[Matthew 23#^23-27|Mt 23.27]]) to prevent accidental contraction of corpse impurity (see “The Law,” p. 655), not to conceal. ^11-44

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-46|46]]:** *Burdens hard to bear*, see [[Matthew 23#^23-4|Mt 23.4]]. ^11-46

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-47|47]]:** *You build the tombs,* logically, this should indicate repudiation of rather than complicity in the murders, so the line implies hypocrisy along with violence. *Your ancestors killed*, Jesus casts lawyers and Pharisees as murderers, distinguishes himself from them (see 7.30n.), and foreshadows his death. ^11-47

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-49|49]]:** *Wisdom of God*, see 7.35n. on wisdom. The quotation cannot be located, but see [[Jeremiah 07#^7-25|Jer 7.25–26]]; if the line is dependent on Jeremiah, the *apostles* has been added. ^11-49

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-50|50]]:** *Charged with the blood*, see [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]]. ^11-50

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-51|51]]:** *Abel*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-8|Gen 4.8]]; Jewish tradition does not regard Abel as a “prophet.” *Zechariah*, probably referring to [[II Chronicles 24#^24-20|2 Chr 24.20–22]] (and so naming victims from Genesis through Chronicles, which according to *b. B. Bat.* 14b concludes the canon); alternately, a Zechariah killed in the Temple during the first revolt against Rome (mentioned by Josephus, *J.W*. 4.335–44). ^11-51

**[[NRSVue Luke 11#^11-52|52]]:** *Did not enter*, see [[Matthew 23#^23-13|Mt 23.13]]. *basket* ^11-52
