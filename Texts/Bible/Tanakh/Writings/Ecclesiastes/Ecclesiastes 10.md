## Chapter 10

**10:1** Dead flies turn the perfumer’s ointment fetid and putrid;[^1] so a little folly outweighs massive wisdom. ^10-1

**10:2** A wise man’s mind tends toward the right hand, a fool’s toward the left.[^2] ^10-2

**10:3** A fool’s mind is also wanting when he travels, and he lets everybody know he is a fool. ^10-3

**10:4** If the wrath of a lord flares up against you, don’t give up your post;[^3] for when wrath abates, grave offenses are pardoned.[^4] ^10-4

**10:5** Here is an evil I have seen under the sun as great as an error committed by a ruler: ^10-5

**10:6** Folly was placed on lofty heights, while rich men sat in low estate. ^10-6

**10:7** I have seen slaves on horseback, and nobles walking on the ground like slaves. ^10-7

**10:8** He who digs a pit will fall into it; he who breaches a stone fence will be bitten by a snake. ^10-8

**10:9** He who quarries stones will be hurt by them; he who splits wood will be harmed by[^5] it. ^10-9

**10:10** [^6]If the ax has become dull and he has not whetted the edge, he must exert more strength. Thus the advantage of a skill [depends on the exercise of] prudence. ^10-10

**10:11** If the snake bites because no spell was uttered, no advantage is gained by the trained charmer. ^10-11

**10:12** A wise man’s talk brings him favor, but a fool’s lips are his undoing. ^10-12

**10:13** His talk begins as silliness and ends as disastrous madness. ^10-13

**10:14** Yet the fool talks and talks!  
[^7]A man cannot know what will happen; who can tell him what the future holds? ^10-14

**10:15** [^8]A fool’s exertions tire him out, for he doesn’t know how to get to a town. ^10-15

**10:16** Alas for you, O land whose king is a lackey and whose ministers dine in the morning! ^10-16

**10:17** Happy are you, O land whose king is a master and whose ministers dine at the proper time—with restraint, not with guzzling! ^10-17

**10:18** Through slothfulness the ceiling sags,  
Through lazy hands the house caves in. ^10-18

**10:19** They[^9] make a banquet for revelry; wine makes life merry, and money answers every need. ^10-19

**10:20** Don’t revile a king even among your intimates.[^10]  
Don’t revile a rich man even in your bedchamber;  
For a bird of the air may carry the utterance,  
And a winged creature may report the word. ^10-20

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^2]: I.e., a wise man’s mind brings him good luck; a fool’s brings him bad luck.
[^3]: Emendation yields “hope.”
[^4]: Lit. “abatement (2 Chron. 36.16) remits grave offenses.” For hinniaḥ, “to remit,” cf. Abodah Zarah 13a; cf. hanaḥah, “remission of taxes,” Esth. 2.18.
[^5]: Emendation yields “profit…shall make use of.”
[^6]: Meaning of verse uncertain.
[^7]: The thought of this sentence is resumed at v. 20.
[^8]: This verse continues the thought of v. 3.
[^9]: I.e., the ministers of v. 16.
[^10]: Others “thoughts”; meaning of Heb. uncertain.
