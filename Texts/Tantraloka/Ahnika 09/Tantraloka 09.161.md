## Mūla

धरादिवत् तथात्यन्ताभावोऽप्येवं विविच्यताम् ।
यत्सङ्कल्प्यं तथा तस्य बहिर्देहोऽस्ति चेतनः ॥ ^mula

dharādivat tathātyantābhāvo'pyevaṃ vivicyatām |
yatsaṅkalpyaṃ tathā tasya bahirdeho'sti cetanaḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 06 - Chapter Nine, part 4#^pg158|p. 141]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=158>))
>
> Consider (the notion) of absolute non-existence (antyantābhāva) in the same way. (with 9.160–161ab)
>
> The non-existence (or otherwise, of anything, including a) sky-flower, like that of the Earth (principle) and the rest, depends upon the subject conditioned by time and space. (This is clear from the fact that it is he who says): “it does not exist”. (with 9.160cd–161a)
>
> Whatever can be imagined (sarrkalpya) (to exist on the authority of the scriptures) in this way is sentient (cetana), and possesses an external body like (any common man, such as) Caitra. Every (creature) up to Suśiva has a body like that. As is its body, so is the external world of the same type. Thus the series of worlds up to Suśiva are proved to exist. (with 9.161cd–163ab)
