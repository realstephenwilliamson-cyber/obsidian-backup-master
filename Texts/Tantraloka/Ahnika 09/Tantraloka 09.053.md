## Mūla

निःसंख्यैर्बहुभी रूपैर्भात्यवच्छेदवर्जनात् ।
शांभवाः शक्तिजा मन्त्रमहेशा मन्त्रनायकाः ॥ ^mula

niḥsaṃkhyairbahubhī rūpairbhātyavacchedavarjanāt |
śāṃbhavāḥ śaktijā mantramaheśā mantranāyakāḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 04 - Chapter Nine, part 2#^pg76|p. 59]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=76>))
>
> The All-pervasive Lord, who is perfectly full by virtue of His inherent freedom, manifests, devoid as He is of limitation, in countless forms. (with 9.52cd–53ab)
>
> The Divine (śāmbhava) (subjects), those Born of Power (śaktija), the Great Lords of Mantra, the Lords of Mantra and the Mantras – these are the five groups of pure (subjects), in due order. Whatever be the inherent nature (rūpa) that shines in its own respective group in conjunction (with it) is said to be the (particular) tattva amongst these ones. As is (for example) the Earth (principle, that extends through all its worlds), starting with Kālāgni. (with 9.53cd–55ab)
