### 3.1–21: Nicodemus

*[[NRSVue John 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 181* ^3-1

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Pharisee*, Pharisees normally function as Jesus’ opponents (but see 12.42). *Nicodemus*, a Greek name used among Jews as Naqdimon (*b. Taʾan*. 20a). *Leader*, Nicodemus’s leadership role is not specified (see [[Luke 14#^14-1|Lk 14.1]]). ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-2|2]]:** *By night*, secretly. Night and darkness are associated with those—such as Jewish authorities—unable to recognize Jesus as the divine Son (9.39–41). ^3-2

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-3|3]]:** *Very truly*, see 1.51n. *Kingdom of God*, used by John only in 3.3,5, but prominent in the other Gospels, referring to the future divine domain whether in “this world” ([[Mark 09#^9-1|Mk 9.1]]), in some other place where the “righteous” will dwell ([[Luke 13#^13-29|Lk 13.29]]), or an altered state of existence in the present ([[Luke 17#^17-21|Lk 17.21]]). *Born from above*, or “born anew,” the origin of the term “born-again Christian.” ^3-3

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-4|4]]:** Puns and double entendres are frequent in John, as they are in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Daniel 05#^5-25|Dan 5.25–28]]), classical Greek literature (e.g., Ovid, *Metam.* ), and, indeed, in numerous texts from all cultures. ^3-4

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-5|5]]:** *Water and Spirit*, suggesting baptism (see “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674) as a rebirth permitting one to enter, or see (v 3) the kingdom. The combination may suggest both baptism and the gift of the Spirit ([[Acts 01#^1-5|Acts 1.5]]). ^3-5

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-6|6]]:** *Born of the Spirit*, see 8.12–59; John dismisses genealogy as identifying a child of God. ^3-6

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Wind*, Gk “pneuma,” like Heb “ruaḥ,” can mean wind, breath, or spirit. Each is unpredictable and cannot be seen or grasped, but is essential for life and can express great power. ^3-8

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-11|11]]:** *We . . . you*, Jesus views Nicodemus as a representative of his opponents. The Greek varies between the singular and plural of “you” throughout this discourse, suggesting that Jesus is addressing Nicodemus both as an individual and as a representative of those who do not understand. ^3-11

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-13|13]]:** *Ascended . . . descended*, allusions to 1.51 and Gen 28 (Jacob’s ladder). The statement ignores Enoch ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-22|Gen 5.22]],[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-24|24]]) and Elijah ([[II Kings 02#^2-11|2 Kings 2.11]]), who both ascended to heaven instead of undergoing death. ^3-13

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-14|14]]:** *Lifted up*, a double entendre referring to the lifting up of the serpent in [[Torah 39 - Chukat#^21-4|Num 21.4–9]] and to the crucifixion. *Serpent*, held up by Moses to save Israel from a plague of snakes ([[Torah 39 - Chukat#^21-4|Num 21.4–9]]; see also [[II Kings 18#^18-4|2 Kings 18.4]]). ^3-14

> [!note] “Born again” (JN 3.3–5) *(p. 181)*
> The Greek term *anōthen* can mean “again” or “from above.” There is no need however to choose between these alternative meanings in this verse. The Gospel often employs wordplays that contribute to its technique of misunderstanding (on the part of the disciples or other dialogue partners) and correction (on Jesus’ part). In contrast to 3.8, which features a word-play (spirit/wind) that works in both Greek and
>
> Hebrew/Aramaic, this one works only in Greek; indeed, the entire Gospel reflects a strong familiarity with Greek language rather than Hebrew or Aramaic. The idea of rebirth from above is hinted at in the Prologue, which distinguishes between those born of blood, human flesh, and will, and those born of God. Some modern Evangelical Christians refer to themselves as being “born again” into a personal relationship with Christ.

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Eternal life*, the first usage in this Gospel of a phrase that, whatever its precise meaning, is a marker for salvation and all the positive consequences that faith in Jesus as the Messiah and Son of God will bring about. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-16|16–21]]:** Because the Greek manuscripts lack punctuation, identifying the speaker in long discourses is difficult. These verses are often attributed to the narrator rather than Jesus. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-16|16]]:** Jesus is God’s *Son*; Jesus’ death testifies to God’s love for *the world*, and is necessary for salvation. *Perish . . . eternal life*, the contrast implies that eternal life is not intrinsic but is given or withheld according to a person’s relationship to the Son or to God. ^3-16-2

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Be saved through him*, to receive eternal life by faith in Jesus as Messiah and Son of God. ^3-17

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-19|19–20]]:** *Evil*, despite the reference to “deeds,” the “doing” refers back to 3.18, “believing.” For that reason good and evil here are moral rather than theological categories. ^3-19

### 3.22–36: Baptizing

*[[NRSVue John 03#^3-22|Text]], p. 182* ^3-22

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-22|22]]:** *Baptized*, the Synoptics do not mention that Jesus baptized. ^3-22-2

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Aenon*, from the Aram plural of “spring”; *Salim*, comes from the Semitic root for “peace.” Tradition locates this event variously in Transjordan, in the northern Jordan valley, and in Samaria. In contrast to the Synoptic Gospels, where Jesus’ main ministry takes place after John’s imprisonment, John suggests that the missions overlapped. ^3-23

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-24|24]]:** *Prison*, the Gospel presumes but does not recount John’s imprisonment (see [[Mark 06#^6-14|Mk 6.14–29]]). ^3-24

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-25|25]]:** *Purification*, implying that baptism is related to Jewish practices concerning ritual purity (see e.g., [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^14-8|Lev 14.8]]), rather than the “Christian” baptism as such. ^3-25

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-27|27]]:** *Receive . . . except what has been given*, the Gospel refers several times to the idea that believers come to Jesus by divine will. This idea does not denote a predetermination of who will be saved and who will not, but rather indicates that by choosing to believe, individuals demonstrate their affinity with the divine will. **[[NRSVue John 03#^3-29|29]]:** John is the *friend* to the *bridegroom*. See 2.9, in which the steward apparently mistakes the bridegroom for the one who provided the wine, but in doing so also suggests the symbolic identification of Jesus as the bridegroom. *Bridegroom*, image of one who rejoices ([[Isaiah 62#^62-5|Isa 62.5]]; [[Jeremiah 16#^16-9|Jer 16.9]]); in the other Gospels the bridegroom is the symbol of one who is to arrive, after which festivities begin ([[Matthew 25#^25-1|Mt 25.1–13]]), or the one in whose presence rejoicing takes place ([[Matthew 09#^9-15|Mt 9.15]]; [[Mark 02#^2-9|Mk 2.9]]; [[Luke 05#^5-34|Lk 5.34]]). In [[Revelation 18#^18-23|Rev 18.23]] he is the symbol of the Messiah united with God’s people at the eschatological wedding banquet. This saying (see [[John 02#^2-1|Jn 2.1–11]]) may allude to the Jewish idea of the eschatological banquet as a metaphor for the messianic age’s joy and abundance ([[Isaiah 25#^25-6|Isa 25.6–8]]; [[Ezekiel 34#^34-17|Ezek 34.17–30]]; see also 4Q521 2.ii.5–13; *b. Sanh.* 96–99). ^3-29

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-30|30]]:** *Increase . . . decrease*, John the Baptist diminishes his own importance. The verse may reflect a historical competition between the followers of Jesus and the Baptist, and the ascendancy of Jesus’ group. ^3-30

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-32|32–33]]:** *No one accepts . . . Whoever has accepted*, a contradictory formulation indicating that those who do not accept the testimony doubt not only the one who testifies, but also God’s trustworthiness. ^3-32

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-34|34–35]]:** *Spirit . . . all things in his hands*, the Son’s authority from God includes the granting of God’s spirit (see 3.5–6). ^3-34

**[[NRSVue John 03#^3-36|36]]:** See 3.16n. ^3-36
