**1.** When a person checks and searches on the night of the fourteenth [of Nisan], he should remove [all] chametz from holes, hidden places, and corners,[^1] and gather the entire amount together, putting it in one place until the beginning of the sixth hour[^2] and [then,] destroy it.[^3] If he desires to destroy it on the night[^4] of the fourteenth, he may.[^5] ^3-1

**2.** The chametz which was put aside on the night of the fourteenth,[^6] so that it can be eaten on the next day until [the end of] the fourth hour,[^7] should not be spread out and scattered in every place. Rather, it should be put away in a utensil or in a known corner, and care should be taken concerning it.[^8] Otherwise, should some be found lacking, he would have to search for it and check [the house] a second time, for mice[^9] might have dragged it away.[^10] ^3-2

**3.** When the fourteenth falls on the Sabbath,[^11] we search for chametz on the night before Sabbath eve,[^12] the night of the thirteenth.[^13] We set aside [enough] chametz to eat[^14] until [the end of] the fourth hour on the Sabbath day.[^15] The remainder should be destroyed before[^16] the Sabbath.  
If some of the chametz remains on the Sabbath[^17] day after the fourth hour, he should nullify it[^18] and cover it with a utensil[^19] until the conclusion of the first day of the festival, and then destroy it.[^20] ^3-3

**4.** A person who has many loaves of bread that were *Terumah* and must burn them on the Sabbath eve;[^21] he should not mix the pure loaves together with the impure loaves and burn them.[^22] Rather, he should burn the pure loaves alone, the impure ones alone,[^23] and the ones [whose status[^24] is] left pending[^25] alone.[^26]  
He should leave a sufficient quantity, but no more than necessary,[^27] of the pure loaves to eat until [the conclusion of] the fourth hour on the Sabbath day.[^28] ^3-4

**5.** A person who either inadvertently or intentionally did not search on the night of the fourteenth should search on the fourteenth in the morning.[^29] If he did not search on the fourteenth in the morning,[^30] he should search at the time for destroying [the chametz].[^31] If he did not search at the time for destroying the chametz, he should search in the midst of the festival.[^32] If the festival passed without his having searched, he should search after the festival to destroy whatever chametz he might find which [he possessed] during Pesach,[^33] since we are prohibited against benefiting [from such chametz].[^34] ^3-5

**6.** When a person checks for chametz on the night of the fourteenth, on the day of the fourteenth,[^35] and during the festival,[^36] he should recite the [following] blessing before he begins to search:  
Blessed[^37] are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning the destruction of chametz.[^38]  
He must check and search in all the places where chametz is brought in, as was explained.[^39] If he searches after the holiday, he does not recite a blessing.[^40] ^3-6

**7.** When he concludes searching, if he searched on the fourteenth at night, or on the fourteenth during the day before the sixth hour, he should nullify all the chametz[^41] that remains in his possession[^42] that he does not see.[^43]  
He should say: "All chametz which is in my possession that I have not seen,[^44] behold, it is nullified and must be considered as[^45] dust."  
However, if he searched after the beginning of the sixth hour and onward,[^46] he can no longer nullify it, for it is not in his possession, since benefiting from it is forbidden. ^3-7

**8.** Thus, a person who does not nullify [his chametz] before the sixth hour[^47] and discovers chametz[^48] which he:  
considered important and [valued] in his[^49] heart,  
then forgot at the time of the destruction of chametz,  
and hence did not destroy,  
transgresses [the prohibitions]: "[leaven] shall not be seen" and "[leaven] shall not be found."  
Behold, he has neither destroyed nor nullified [his chametz], and nullification at this time would not be effective, for [the chametz] is no longer in his possession.[^50] Even so, the Torah considers it as if it were in his possession, to obligate him for [transgression of the commandments:] "[leaven] shall not be seen" and "[leaven] shall not be[^51] found."  
[Therefore,] he is obligated to destroy it whenever he finds it. If he finds it on the day of a festival,[^52] he should cover it with a utensil until the evening, and then destroy it.[^53] [If the chametz] was consecrated property,[^54] there is no need to cover it with a utensil, for regardless, everyone shies away from its use.[^55] ^3-8

**9.** A person who left his house before the time for destroying chametz in order to fulfill a mitzvah[^56] or in order to partake of a feast associated with a mitzvah[^57] - e.g., a feast associated with betrothal or marriage[^58] - and recalls that he possesses chametz at home. If it is possible for him to go back, destroy it, and then return to the fulfillment of the mitzvah, he should return.[^59] If not,[^60] he should nullify [ownership over the chametz] in his heart.[^61]  
Should he go out to save[^62] from a troop of attackers, from a [flooding] river, from a fire, from [being buried] under fallen objects, all that is necessary is for him to nullify it in his heart.[^63] Should he go out for his own purposes[^64] and remember that he possesses chametz at home, he must return immediately.[^65]  
How much [chametz] must be present [to require] him to return? the size of an egg.[^66] If there is less than the size of an egg, it is sufficient for him to nullify it in his heart.[^67] ^3-9

**10.** A person who put aside a rolled dough[^68] at home,[^69] [forgot about it,] went out and remembered after he had left [home]: Should he be sitting before his teacher[^70] and fear that the dough will become leavened before he can come [home],[^71] behold, he may nullify [ownership over the dough] in his heart before it becomes leaven.[^72]  
However, if [the dough] has already become leavened, his nullification is not at all effective,[^73] for he has already violated [the prohibitions]: "[leaven] shall not be seen" and "[leaven] shall not be found." He must destroy it immediately[^74] when he returns home.[^75] ^3-10

**11.** How must chametz be destroyed?[^76] It may be burned; crumbled and tossed to the wind; or thrown into the sea.[^77] If the chametz is hard and the sea will not cause it to dissolve speedily, one should crumble it and then throw it into the sea.[^78]  
If other substances fell upon chametz and it was covered by three handbreadths or more of earth,[^79] it is considered as having been destroyed. [Nevertheless,] one must nullify [ownership over] it in one's heart if the sixth hour has not arrived.[^80]  
A person who gave[^81] it to a gentile before the sixth hour need not destroy it.[^82]  
If one burns it before the sixth hour, he is permitted to benefit from the charcoal that remains during Pesach.[^83] However, if he burns it from [the beginning of] the sixth hour and onward, since benefit may not be derived from it, it should not be used as fuel[^84] for an oven or range.[^85] One may not bake or cook with it.[^86]  
If one did bake or cook [using the chametz as fuel], it is forbidden to derive benefit from that loaf or that dish.[^87] Similarly, it is forbidden to derive benefit from the charcoal that remains from it, because it was burned after benefit from it became forbidden.[^88] ^3-11
