CHAPTER TWO (vol. 2, notes 02-03): what is different from the brief (28 Oct 2026)

1. The OCR lost the footnote numbers in this chapter, so a new pre-pass made the definitions from the page images: it found
   each footnote's start on the image (the line beginning with a superscript number in the footnote zone), numbered the
   starts through the chapter (the superscript OCR'd where it could be read, else by sequence), and made `[^n]: ` at the
   matching place in the note, often splitting a line where the converter had run a page's footnotes together.
   pages.txt shows, under each page, what the pre-pass did ("pre-pass:" lines) and its flags.
2. CHECK EVERY DEFINITION ON YOUR PAGES against the image: its label must be the printed superscript number at the start of
   that footnote, and the definition must begin where that footnote begins. Fix a wrong label or a wrong start with def
   ops (brief, op 2). Where the pre-pass missed a footnote (the flags name 44, 56, 67, 131 and 142; a page "with no
   footnote defined" may still hold one), make it: old = its first words (with any garble mark just before them),
   new = `[^n]: ` + the same words, or "\n\n[^n]: " + the words when it starts in the middle of a line.
   Some definitions still begin with a garble mark the pre-pass left (e.g. `[^95]: " Above`, `[^127]: 1” The`):
   remove the mark with a def op.
3. LABELS = PRINTED NUMBERS. The print numbers this chapter's footnotes 1-155 or so through the chapter; the note must use
   exactly the printed numbers. Do not renumber anything else. 146-151 are already real.
4. CARRY-OVERS. Chapter Two's footnotes are long: many run over one or more pages, and some pages hold nothing but
   footnote text. A carried-over part is plain text at the top of the next page's block (before that page's first
   definition, or the page's only text). Join each paragraph of it with a move op (brief, op 4): `after` = the last line of
   that footnote's text before it (its definition line, its last same-page continuation, or the line of the previous
   carried paragraph when it runs over several pages: chains are allowed; several paragraphs moved after the same line
   keep their order). Body text (the translation and commentary) that continues on the next page is NOT moved.
5. Same-page further paragraphs of a footnote (verse lines, quotations, new paragraphs) that are not indented: indent them
   (op 3). Body text the OCR put among the footnotes, or footnote text inside a body line: report, don't fix.
6. YOUR PAGES ONLY. Another reader has the other pages of the same note.txt. Write ops only for lines that lie on your
   pages (from your first page's marker line to the line before the marker of the page after your last). A move op's
   `after` may point to a line before your range (a footnote carried over from the page before yours).
