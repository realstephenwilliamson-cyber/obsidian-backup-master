# Rashi 47 - Re'eh

← [[Rashi 46 - Eikev]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 48 - Shoftim]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-26|Deuteronomy 11:26–16:17]]* · Text: [[Torah 47 - Re'eh]]

## Chapter 11

### 11:26

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-26|Deuteronomy 11:26]]

**Look, I place a blessing and a curse.** To be pronounced at Mount Gerizim and Mount Eival.

### 11:27

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-27|Deuteronomy 11:27]]

**The blessing.** On condition that you listen.

### 11:28

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-28|Deuteronomy 11:28]]

**From the course which I command you this day, to follow.** This teaches that anyone who worships idols has strayed from the entire course commanded to Yisroel. From here it is derived that anyone who admits to idolatry denies the entire Torah.

### 11:29

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-29|Deuteronomy 11:29]]

**You shall place the blessers.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, יָת מְבָרְכַיּאָ, “The blessers.”

**On Mount Gerizim.** Towards Mount Gerizim they turned their faces, and opened with the blessing, “Blessed is the man who does not make a sculptured or molten, etc.” All the curses in that chapter were first recited with the word “blessed.” Then they turned their faces towards Mount Eival, and opened with the curse.

### 11:30

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-30|Deuteronomy 11:30]]

**Are they not.** This provides the landmarks.

**Far beyond.** Far after the crossing of the Yardein, in the distance beyond, as indicated by the word “beyond.” Wherever אַחֲרֵי — “beyond” is mentioned, the reference is to great distance.

**Along the route of the sunset.** Beyond the Yardein, toward the west. The cantillation of the passage proves that they are two matters, since they are punctuated with two cantillation symbols; אַחֲרֵי, “far beyond,” is punctuated with the *pashta* symbol, while דֶרֶךְ, “the route,” is punctuated with the *mishpal* symbol, and is dotted. If אַחֲרֵי דֶּרֶךְ, “far beyond the route,” were a single phrase, then אַחֲרֵי, “far beyond,” would be punctuated with the *meshares* symbol — the reverse *shofar* — and דֶּרֶךְ, “route,” would be punctuated with the *pashta* symbol, and would be undotted.

**Opposite Gilgal.** At a distance from Gilgal.

**Moreh Plain.** This was Shechem, as it is said, “Until the territory of Shechem — until Moreh Plain.”

### 11:31

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-31|Deuteronomy 11:31]]

**For you shall pass across the Yardein, etc.** The miracles at the Yardein will portend, for you, that you will enter the land and take possession of it.

## Chapter 12

### 12:2

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-2|Deuteronomy 12:2]]

**You must obliterate.** Destroy, then destroy afterward. This teaches that, when destroying idolatry, one must tear out the roots.

**All of the places where the nations worshipped, etc.** What must you obliterate from them? ‘Their gods on the mountains.’

### 12:3

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-3|Deuteronomy 12:3]]

**Altar.** Composed of a number of stones.

**Pillar.** Single-stoned. This is the *‘bimus’* mentioned in the Mishnah, ‘A stone quarried originally as a *bimus.*’

**Asherah.** A tree that was worshipped.

**Obliterate their name.** Give them degrading nicknames. ”The house of exaltation,” entitle “The house of ruin.” “Universal vision” — “Thorny vision.”

### 12:4

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-4|Deuteronomy 12:4]]

**Do not act this way.** By bringing burnt-offerings for the sake of Heaven anywhere except for the place [God] will select. Another interpretation. ‘Smash their altars, obliterate their names — do not act this way. . .’ This prohibits erasing the sacred name, and breaking an altar stone, or a Temple courtyard stone. Rebbi Yishmael said, ‘Is it conceivable that an Israelite would smash altars? Rather, do not act as they do, whereby your iniquity would cause the ruin of the Temple of your forefathers.’

### 12:5

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-5|Deuteronomy 12:5]]

**Seek His Presence.** This refers to the Tabernacle at Shiloh.

### 12:6

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-6|Deuteronomy 12:6]]

**Your sacred offerings.** Obligatory *shelamim*-offerings.

**Your tithes.** The animal tithe and the second tithe, to be eaten within the walls [of Jerusalem].

**The elevation of your hands.** This refers to the first *bikurim*- fruits, concerning which it is said, “The *kohein* shall take the basket from your hand.”

**The first born of your cattle.** To give to the *kohein,* so that he may offer them up there.

### 12:7

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-7|Deuteronomy 12:7]]

**As Adonoy has blessed you.** According to the blessing, bring.

### 12:8

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-8|Deuteronomy 12:8]]

**You may not do everything we do, etc.** This continues from above, from “For you shall pass across the Yardein, etc.” When you cross the Yardein, you will be immediately permitted to bring up offerings on the personal *bamah*-altar, thoughout all fourteen years of conquest and apportionment. However, on the personal *bamah*-altar, you may not offer everything you offer here, this day, at the Mishkan, which remains with you and has been anointed. This is an appropriate place for bringing sin-and guilt-offerings, and promissory and contributory-offerings. However, on the personal *bamah*-altar, nothing may be offered except for the promissory and contributory, And this is the meaning of “each what is upright in his eyes.” Promissory-and contributory offerings, That you donate because you feel it is proper to bring them and not because you *must.* These you may offer on a personal *bamah*-altar.

### 12:9

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-9|Deuteronomy 12:9]]

**For you will not have come.** Throughout those fourteen years.

**As yet.** Like “yet.”

**To the peace.** This refers to Shiloh.

**The territory.** This refers to Jerusalem.

### 12:10

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-10|Deuteronomy 12:10]]

**When you cross the Yardein and settle in the land.** When you have apportioned it, and everyone distinguishes his portion and his tribe.

**And He has granted you peace.** After the conquest and apportionment, and peace with the nations whom Adonoy allowed to remain in order to test Yisroel — this did not occur until the time of Dovid — then:

### 12:11

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-11|Deuteronomy 12:11]]

**Let it be that the place, etc.** Build, for yourselves, the chosen Sanctuary in Jerusalem. This is as it is said concerning Dovid: “It was, when the king had settled in his palace, and Adonoy had granted him peace on every side, from all his enemies; then the king said to the prophet Noson, ‘Look here, I am settled in a palace of cedarwood, while the ark of God languishes amidst curtains.”’

**It is there that you shall bring.** The discussion above concerns Shiloh, while the discussion here concerns Jerusalem. This is why Scripture separates them, by granting permission between them. From the time that Shiloh was laid waste and they arrived at Nov, and Nov was laid waste and they arrived at Giveon, personal *bamah*-altars were permitted, until they arrived at Jerusalem.

**Your choice promissory-offerings.** This teaches that one must offer the choicest.

### 12:13

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-13|Deuteronomy 12:13]]

**Take heed.** This places a negative commandment over the matter.

**Anywhere you envision as proper.** Which occurs to you. However, you may bring an offering by prophetic instruction, as with Eliyahu at Mount Carmel.

### 12:14

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-14|Deuteronomy 12:14]]

**Of one of your tribes.** In Binyamin’s portion. Above, however, it is said, “of all your tribes.” How can this be? When Dovid purchased the threshing site from Aravna the Yevusite, he gathered the gold from all the tribes; still, the threshing site was in Binyamin’s territory.

### 12:15

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-15|Deuteronomy 12:15]]

**Except, with the fullness of your appetite.** What is Scripture’s subject? Is it “appetite meat,” permitting it to them without offering the sacrificial portions? But it is stated elsewhere, “When Adonoy, your God, expands your borders, etc., and you assert, ‘Let me eat meat, etc.”’ What, then, is the subject here? Sacred animals which were afflicted with a blemish which may be redeemed and eaten anywhere. Perhaps they may be redeemed even with a transient blemish? The Torah therefore teaches, “except.”

**You may slaughter and eat.** You are not permitted to shear or milk, but only to eat by slaughtering.

**The ritually unclean and the clean.** Because this was converted from a sacred sacrifice, concerning which it is said, “Meat which came into contact with anything unclean may not be eaten,” it is necessary to permit the unclean and the clean to eat it in a single plate.

**Like the deer and the gazelle** which are not eligible as sacrifices.

**Like the deer and the gazelle.** This exempts them from the gifts of the foreleg, the jaw, and the maw.

### 12:16

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-16|Deuteronomy 12:16]]

**However, you may not eat the blood.** Although I have stated that its blood is not to be sprinkled on the altar, you may not eat it.

**Spill it like water.** This teaches you that it does not require covering. Another interpretation. It is like water with regard to the conditioning (*hechsher*) of edible seeds [for ritual contamination].

### 12:17

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-17|Deuteronomy 12:17]]

**You are not permitted.** This passage places a negative commandment over the matter.

**You are not permitted [lit. not able].** R’ Yehoshua ben Korchah said, ‘You are able, but are not permitted.” Similarly, “The Yevusites, who dwelt in Jerusalem, the sons of Yehudah were not permitted [lit. not able] to drive out.” They were able, but were not permitted, because Avrohom had made a covenant with them, when he bought the double tiered Machpelah-cave from them. They were not Yevusites, but Chitites, but, in recognition of the city entitled Yevus (they were called Yevusites). This is stated clearly in Pirkei dR’ Eliezer. This is why it is said, “...unless you cast aside the blind and the lame” — the statues on which the oath was inscribed.

**The first born of your cattle.** This prohibition applies to the *kohanim.*

**The terumah-separation of your hand.** This refers to the first fruits.

### 12:18

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-18|Deuteronomy 12:18]]

**Before Adonoy.** Within the walls.

**And the Levite in your cities.** If you do not have his portion for him — for example, the first tithe — give him the tithe of the indigent. If you do not have the tithe of the indigent, invite him to partake of your *shelamim*-offering.

### 12:19

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-19|Deuteronomy 12:19]]

**Watch yourself.** This places a negative commandment over the matter.

**On your land.** In the diaspora, however, you are not admonished to concern yourself with him more than with the indigent Israelites.

### 12:20

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-20|Deuteronomy 12:20]]

**When . . . expands, etc.** The Torah teaches proper behavior — a man should not crave eating meat unless he enjoys expansion and affluence.

**To the full extent of your appetite, etc.** However, in the wilderness, non-sacrificial meat was forbidden to them, unless it was sanctified and brought as a *shelamim*-offering.

### 12:21

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-21|Deuteronomy 12:21]]

**When the place is distant from you.** So that you are unable to come every day to offer the *shelamim* sacrifice, as you are now, when the Mishkan travels with you,

**you may slaughter, as I have commanded you.** We learn that there is a command concerning the proper method of slaughtering. This refers to the slaughtering laws told to Moshe at Sinai.

### 12:22

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-22|Deuteronomy 12:22]]

**Only, as the deer, etc, may be eaten.** You are not admonished to eat them in ritual purity. Perhaps, just as the fat of the deer and the gazelle are permitted, so is the fat of the non-sacrificial animal permitted? The Torah therefore teaches, “only.”

### 12:23

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-23|Deuteronomy 12:23]]

**Only exercise strength in not eating the blood.** From the statement “exercise strength,” you learn that they wallowed in the eating of blood. It was therefore necessary to say “exercise strength.” This is R’ Yehudah’s opinion. R’ Shimon ben Azai said, Scripture merely admonishes and teaches you the extent to which you must exercise strength with *mitzvos.* If blood, which is easily avoided, as a man has no craving for it, yet it is necessary to strengthen you in admonishing against it; then certainly the other commandments.

**Do not eat the spirit with the meat.** This prohibits the limb of a living animal.

### 12:24

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-24|Deuteronomy 12:24]]

**Do not eat it.** This prohibits the dripping *tamtzis* blood.

### 12:25

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-25|Deuteronomy 12:25]]

**Do not eat it.** This prohibits the blood absorbed in the limbs.

**So that you may enjoy beneficence, etc.** Go forth, learn the reward for *mitzvos.* Take blood, which a man finds repugnant; yet, one who refrains from it gains merit for himself and his children after him. Certainly with larceny and promiscuousness, which a man craves.

### 12:26

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-26|Deuteronomy 12:26]]

**However, your sacred offerings.** Although you may slaughter non-sacrificial animals, I did not permit you to slaughter sacrificial offerings and eat them in your cities without offering them up. Rather, bring them to the chosen Sanctuary.

### 12:27

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-27|Deuteronomy 12:27]]

**Execute your burnt-offerings.** If they are burnt-*olah*- offerings, place the flesh and the blood on the altar. If they are *shelamim*-peace-offerings, “the blood of your sacrifices shall be poured on the altar” first, and then “the flesh may be eaten.” The Rabbis expounded additionally. “However, your sacrificial-offerings . . .”: This teaches concerning sacrificial-offerings outside the land. And it teaches that exchange-offerings and the offspring of sacrifices must be offered up.

### 12:28

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-28|Deuteronomy 12:28]]

**Preserve.** This refers to the study of the law, which must be preserved in one’s gut, not to be forgotten, as it is said, “It is pleasurable that you preserve them in your gut.” If you have studied the law, then it is possible for you to heed and to perform; but anyone who is not involved in study can not be involved in performance.

**All these words.** A minor commandment should be as precious to you as one of great consequence.

**That which is good.** In the eyes of Heaven.

**And that which is upright.** In the eyes of mortals.

### 12:30

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-30|Deuteronomy 12:30]]

**Lest you are torn apart.** Onkelos’ interpretation is that the reference is to a pitfall. In my opinion, he was not exacting in following the literal meaning, for we never find the letter ‘ נ in the word יוֹקֵשׁ, “pitfall,” even as a root letter which is sometimes dropped. However, in reference to tearing apart and violent shaking, we find the ‘ נ, “His knees knocked against each other.” Here, too, my rendition is, “Lest you are torn apart with them,” by emulating their activities. Similarly, “Let the debtor jostle after all his possessions.” He curses the villain, wishing him numerous debtors who will jostle one another in pursuit of his money.

**After they have been annihilated before you.** After you observe when I have annihilated them before you, focus your attention as to why I annihilated them — because of their decadent behavior. So you refrain from emulating them so that no one come to annihilate you.

**How did . . . worship?** Since no punishment was specified for idol worship except for slaughtering, burning, pouring libations, or prostrating oneself, as it is written, “For the sake of none but Adonoy alone” — activities generally performed for the sake of the Supreme Being. This passage proceeds to teach that, if it is customary to worship an idol with some other ritual for example, exposing oneself for the sake of Peor, or casting a stone for the sake of *Markolis* — then that is its recognized worship form, and [the worshiper] is liable. However, for slaughtering, burning, pouring libations, and prostrating oneself, even when not customary, one is liable.

### 12:31

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-31|Deuteronomy 12:31]]

**For, even their sons.** “Even” includes their fathers and mothers. R’ Akiva said, ‘I saw a Gentile tie his father before his dog, who then ate him.’

## Chapter 13

### 13:1

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-1|Deuteronomy 13:1]]

**Everything.** The less significant as well as the greatly consequential.

**Be careful to fulfill.** This adds a negative command to the positive commands stated in this chapter, as “be careful” always denotes a negative command, except that one is not punished with lashes for violating “be careful” when it is added to a positive command.

**Do not add to it.** Five *totafos* with the *tefilin,* five species with the *lulav,* four benedictions with the priestly benediction.

### 13:2

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-2|Deuteronomy 13:2]]

**And he gives you an omen.** In the heavens, as it is said with Gideon, “Produce an omen for me,” and it is said, “Please, let the fleece be dry, etc.”

**Or a miracle.** On earth. In spite of this, pay no heed to him. And should you ask, ‘Why has the Holy One, may He be blessed, empowered him to produce an omen’? “For Adonoy your God is testing you.”

### 13:5

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-5|Deuteronomy 13:5]]

**Keep his commandments.** The Torah given through Moshe.

**Heed his voice.** The voice of the prophets.

**Serve Him.** In his Sanctuary.

**Cleave to Him.** Embrace His ways; engage in kindness, bury the dead, and visit the sick, just as the Holy One, may He be blessed, did.

### 13:6

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-6|Deuteronomy 13:6]]

**A fabrication.** Something cast out of the universe, non-existent, never created. I never told him to say it. *Destolude,* “perverted” in Old French.

**And redeemed you from the house of bondage.** Even if He had done nothing for you but redeemed you, this is sufficient.

### 13:7

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-7|Deuteronomy 13:7]]

**If . . . should incite you.** The word הֲסָתָה always denotes inciting, as it is said, “If Adonoy has incited you against me.” *Ametra,* “seduce” in Old French. He seduces him into performing the act.

**Your brother.** Paternally, or —

**Your mother’s son.** Maternally.

**Your bosom.** Who reclines in your bosom, and is wrapped up with you. *Afichiede* in Old French. Similarly, “And from the area wrapped in the earth” — from the foundation embedded in the earth.

**Who is your soul mate.** This refers to your father. Scripture specifies those whom you cherish — certainly with others.

**Clandestinely.** Scripture depicts ordinary circumstances. The seducer will ordinarily only deliver his message clandestinely. Similarly, Solomon says, “In the evening, when day has darkened into night, in the black of night and murkiness.”

**Whom you never knew, neither you nor your forefathers.** This is something extremely degrading to you, for even the Gentile nations do not abandon their forefathers’ tradition; yet, he tells you ‘Abandon your forefathers’ tradition’!

### 13:8

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-8|Deuteronomy 13:8]]

**Who are near you or at a distance.** Why specify “near” and “distant”? Rather, Scripture states the following: By the nature of those nearby, you may learn the nature of those at distance. Just as those nearby are empty of substance, so are those distant insubstantial.

**From the ends of the earth.** This refers to the sun, the moon, and the heavenly hosts, which traverse the world from end to end.

### 13:9

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-9|Deuteronomy 13:9]]

**Do not be favorably inclined towards him.** Do not be inclined towards him, do not love him. Because it is said, “Love your fellow as yourself” — here, do not love him.

**Do not listen to him.** When he begs for his life, for forgiveness. Because it is said, “Lay aside [your hatred of him] and assist him” — here, do not lay aside [your hatred].

**Do not view him compassionately.** Because it is said, “Do not stand by at the spilling of your brother’s blood” — here, have no compassion.

**Do not take pity.** Do not extend yourself to exonerate him.

**Do not cover for him.** If you have incriminating information, you are not permitted to keep silent.

### 13:10

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-10|Deuteronomy 13:10]]

**For you must surely execute him.** If the court found him innocent, retry him to incriminate him; but if the court found him guilty, do not retry him to exonerate him.

**Let your hand strike him first.** It is a *mitzvah* for the object of his seduction to execute him. If he did not succeed in killing him, then others must perform the execution, as it is said, “The hand of all the people, etc.”

### 13:13

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-13|Deuteronomy 13:13]]

**In which to settle.** This excludes Jerusalem, which was not given as a dwelling place.

**If you hear, etc., saying.** Saying the following:

### 13:14

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-14|Deuteronomy 13:14]]

**Men have gone out** — unscrupulous. Without a yoke, as they have cast off the yoke of Heaven.

**Men.** But not women.

**The inhabitants of their city.** But not the inhabitants of another city. From here it is derived that an apostate city cannot be so designated unless suborned by men, and by its own constituents.

### 13:15

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-15|Deuteronomy 13:15]]

**You must investigate, inquire, and interrogate thoroughly.** From here it is derived that there are seven inquiries, from the added passages. Here there are three: “investigation,” “inquiry,” and “thoroughness.” “Interrogate” is not counted, since the examining interrogation is derived from there. Elsewhere, it is said, “The magistrates must investigate thoroughly.” Still elsewhere, it is said, “You must investigate thoroughly.” ”Thoroughly” and “Thoroughly” are extrapolated by the principle of textual comparison, so that the Torah’s specifications with each may be applied to the other.

### 13:16

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-16|Deuteronomy 13:16]]

**You must surely strike down.** If you are unable to execute them by the method specified by Scripture for them, execute them by another method.

### 13:17

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-17|Deuteronomy 13:17]]

**For Adonoy your God.** For His Name and for His sake.

### 13:18

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-18|Deuteronomy 13:18]]

**So that Adonoy withdraws His raising fury.** For, so long as idolatry exists in the world, fury rages in the world.

## Chapter 14

### 14:1

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deuteronomy 14:1]]

**Do not lacerate yourselves.** Do not inflict lacerations and gashes on your flesh over the dead, as is the custom among the Emorites for you are God’s children, and must therefore appear suitably becoming, rather than lacerated and hairless.

**Between your eyes.** Near the forehead. Elsewhere it is said, “They may not tear bald spots in their heads.” This equates the entire head with the area between the eyes.

### 14:2

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-2|Deuteronomy 14:2]]

**For you are a sacred people.** Your own sanctity extends from your forefathers; in addition, “Adonoy has chosen you.”

### 14:3

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-3|Deuteronomy 14:3]]

**Any abomination.** Anything I made abominable to you. For example, puncturing the ear of a first-born animal in order to slaughter it outside the Sanctuary — this is something I made abominable to you, from “There shall not be any blemish on it.” Here the Torah proceeds to teach that it is forbidden to slaughter it and then eat it on the basis of that blemish. If one cooked meat with milk — this is something I made abominable to you, so eating it is prohibited here.

### 14:4

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-4|Deuteronomy 14:4]]

**These are the animals . . . the gazelle, the deer, the antelope.** We learn that wild animals are included in the word בְּהֵמָה, “animal”; and we learn that the unclean domestic and wild animals are more numerous than the clean ones, since the less numerous are specified everywhere.

### 14:5

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-5|Deuteronomy 14:5]]

**The ibex.** Targum renders “wild goat,” “Mountain goats.” This is the *esteinbuc* [in Old French].

**And the wild ox.** *Torbala,* ‘ox of the forest.’ *Bala,* ‘forest’ in Aramaic.

### 14:6

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-6|Deuteronomy 14:6]]

**Cloven.** Split, as Targum [Onkelos] renders.

**Hoof.** *Plante* [in Old French].

**Truly split.** Divided into two nails. Some are cloven, but not divided into nails. They are unclean.

**Within the animals.** The inference is, ‘what is found within the animal, you may eat.’ From here it is derived that the fetus is permitted through the slaughter of its mother.

### 14:7

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-7|Deuteronomy 14:7]]

**The dromedary.** A creature with two humps and two spinal columns. The Rabbis remark: Why were they repeated? The animals — because of the dromedary, and the fowl — because of the *ra’ah,* which were not listed in Toras Kohanim.

### 14:8

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-8|Deuteronomy 14:8]]

**And do not come in contact with their carcasses.** The Rabbis explain that this applies during the festivals, for a man is required to purify himself during the festival. Perhaps the admonition applies throughout the year? The Torah therefore teaches, “Say to the *kohanim,* etc.” If uncleanliness emanating from a human corpse is stringent, restricting only the *kohanim,* but not the Israelites — then the lenient uncleanliness emanating from an animal carcass is — certainly.

### 14:11

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-11|Deuteronomy 14:11]]

**You may eat any pure bird.** This permits the bird released by the *metzora.*

### 14:12

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-12|Deuteronomy 14:12]]

**This is what you may not eat.** This prohibits the slaughtered bird.

### 14:13

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-13|Deuteronomy 14:13]]

**The ra’ah, ayah, etc.** The *ra’ah, ayah,* and *dayah* are one and the same. Why is it entitled “*ra’ah*”? Because its vision is exceedingly powerful. Why does our prohibition specify all its names? In order to refute those who would take exception; so that one does not prohibit it as “*ra’ah,*” while another permits it with the claim that its name is “*dayah,*” or its name is “*ayah,*” and it was thus never prohibited by Scripture. With the birds, the impure ones are specified. This teaches that the pure birds are more numerous than the impure ones. Therefore, those less numerous are specified.

### 14:16

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-16|Deuteronomy 14:16]]

**And the *tinshames.*** *Chalve soriz,* [in Old French].

### 14:17

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-17|Deuteronomy 14:17]]

**The *shalach.*** Which catches fish in the ocean.

### 14:18

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-18|Deuteronomy 14:18]]

**The *duchifas.*** This refers to the wild turkey, in Old French, *herupe,* with a double crest.

### 14:19

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-19|Deuteronomy 14:19]]

**Flying creeping creatures.** This refers to tiny insects that creep along the ground. Flies, hornets, and impure locusts, are called שֶׁרֶץ .

### 14:20

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-20|Deuteronomy 14:20]]

**Every ritually clean bird, you may eat.** But not the impure ones. This adds a positive command to the negative. Similarly with the animals: “That, you may eat” — but not impure animals. A negative command derived from a positive statement is regarded as a positive command, so that the violator transgresses a positive as well as a negative command.

### 14:21

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-21|Deuteronomy 14:21]]

**To the alien in your cities.** The resident alien, who has resolved to refrain from idolatry, but eats meat without ritual slaughter.

**For you are a people sanctified to Adonoy.** Sanctify yourselves by abstaining from that which is permitted. Things which are permitted, but some abstain from them as if they were prohibited; do not permit them in their presence.

**Do not cook a kid.** Three times; this excludes wild animals, fowl, and impure animals.

### 14:22

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-22|Deuteronomy 14:22]]

**Do not cook a kid, etc. You must surely tithe.** What is the relationship between the two subjects? The Holy One, may He be blessed, said to Yisroel, ‘Do not cause Me to cook the young shoots of your crops while still in their mothers’ innards, for, if you do not tithe properly, just before their ripening I shall summon the east wind and batter them, as it is said, “battered before becoming standing grain.” Similarly, with regard to the *bikurim*-gift.

**On a yearly basis.** From here it is derived that one may not use the new crop as tithe for the old.

### 14:23

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-23|Deuteronomy 14:23]]

**You shall eat, etc.** This refers to the second tithe, for we have already been taught to give the first tithe to the Levites, as it is said, “When you receive, from the Israelites, etc.” And they are permitted to eat it anywhere, as it is said, “You may eat it anywhere.” Obviously, then, this refers to a different tithe.

### 14:24

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-24|Deuteronomy 14:24]]

**For . . . has blessed you.** So that the grain is too abundant to carry.

### 14:26

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-26|Deuteronomy 14:26]]

**For anything you desire.** This is a general statement.

**For cattle, flocks, wine, intoxicating liquor.** This is specific.

**And for anything that you wish.** This is a return to the general. Just as the specific statement refers explicitly to the earth’s progeny which is suitable for human consumption, etc.

### 14:27

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-27|Deuteronomy 14:27]]

**And the Levite, etc., you must not abandon.** By neglecting to give him the first tithe.

**Since he has no portion or inheritance with you.** This excludes the gleaning, forgotten sheaves, and edge of the field gifts, and abandoned produce, since his share in them is equivalent to yours. Hence, they are exempt from tithing.

### 14:28

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-28|Deuteronomy 14:28]]

**At the end of three years.** This comes to teach that if one withheld his tithes during the first and second years of the *shmittah*-cycle, he must divest his home of them during the third year.

### 14:29

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-29|Deuteronomy 14:29]]

**The Levite shall come.** And take the first tithe.

**And the proselyte and the orphan.** They take the second tithe, which belongs to the indigent this year. You are not required to eat it in Jerusalem, the way you are required to eat the second tithe during the first two years.

**Let them eat their fill.** Give them enough to satiate them. From here we derive, “One may not give the indigent in the threshing area less than, etc.” You must bring to Jerusalem the first and second year’s tithes which you withheld, and confess, ‘I have divested my home of the sacred produce’ as specified in “When you have finished tithing.”

## Chapter 15

### 15:1

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-1|Deuteronomy 15:1]]

**At the end of seven years.** Perhaps seven years for each and every loan? The Torah therefore teaches, “The seventh year is approaching.” If you say that there are seven years for each and every loan? for everyone’s loan, how is it that it is approaching? This teaches that the seven years follow the count of the *shemitah* cycle.

### 15:2

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-2|Deuteronomy 15:2]]

**Suspend every creditor’s hand.** Suspend the hand of every creditor.

### 15:3

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-3|Deuteronomy 15:3]]

**You should demand payment from the stranger.** This is a positive commandment.

### 15:4

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-4|Deuteronomy 15:4]]

**End it [poverty], so that there will be no one destitute among you.** Yet elsewhere it is said, “For the destitute will never cease.” But, whenever you fulfill the will of God, there will be destitute among others, but not among you; whenever you fail to fulfill the will of God, there will be destitute among you.

**Destitute.** [This refers to someone] poorer than עָנִי, “indigent.” The word אֶבְיוֹן denotes someone desirous of everything.

### 15:5

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-5|Deuteronomy 15:5]]

**Only if you listen.** Then, there will be no one destitute among you.

**You listen [lit., “listen you listen”].** Listen a little, and you will be given the opportunity to listen much.

### 15:6

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-6|Deuteronomy 15:6]]

**Just as He told you.** Where did He tell you? “You are blessed in the city.”

**You will grant loans.** Whenever a word denoting lending is used in reference to the lender, the usage is causative, “you will grant loans.” But if it had said וְעָבַעְתָּ, then it would refer to the borrower, as in “you will borrow.”

**You will grant loans to nations.** Perhaps you will borrow from one and lend to another? The Torah therefore teaches, “but you will not borrow.”

**You will rule over many nations.** Perhaps other nations will rule over you? The Torah therefore teaches, “but they will not rule over you.”

### 15:7

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-7|Deuteronomy 15:7]]

**If there should be someone destitute in your midst.** The neediest should be given preference.

**Among one of your brothers.** Your paternal brother should be given preference over your maternal brother.

**Your cities.** The indgent of your city should be given preference over the indigent of a different city.

**Do not harden.** Some people agonize over whether to give or not; the Torah therefore says, “Do not harden.” Some people open the hand, then close it; the Torah therefore says, “Do not close.”

**Against your destitute brother.** If you do not give him, you will eventually become a brother of the destitute.

### 15:8

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-8|Deuteronomy 15:8]]

**You must surely open.** [lit. “Open, you must open] Even repeatedly.

**Rather, you must surely open.** כִּי here is understood as “rather.”

**You must surely extend a loan to him.** If he refuses charity, extend a loan to him.

**To cover the wants.** However, you are not required to make him wealthy.

**Which he lacks.** Even a horse to ride, or a servant to announce his arrival.

**He [lit. “him.”]** This refers to a wife. Similarly, it is said, “I shall make ‘him’ a helpmate alongside him.”

### 15:9

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-9|Deuteronomy 15:9]]

**And if he cries out, concerning you.** Perhaps this is a *mitzvah?* The Torah therefore teaches “So that he does not cry out.”

**You will be regarded as sinful.** In any case, even if he does not cry out. Then why does it say, “And if he cries out concerning you”? I punish more swiftly with an outcry than without an outcry.

### 15:10

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-10|Deuteronomy 15:10]]

**You must surely give him.** Even a hundred times.

**Him.** Between you and him [in total privacy].

**For, as a consequence of this thing [lit. this word].** Even if you merely said that you would give. You will be rewarded for saying as well as for giving,

### 15:11

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-11|Deuteronomy 15:11]]

**Therefore.** For this reason.

**Saying.** I am counselling you for your own good.

**To your brother, to your indigent.** To which brother? To the indigent.

**To your indigent.** With one *yud* meaning a single indigent. However, “your indigent” with two *yuds* refers to two of the indigents.

### 15:12

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-12|Deuteronomy 15:12]]

**If . . . is sold to you.** Through others. When the courts sold him for his larceny, is Scripture’s subject here. But it is already said, “If you purchase a Hebrew slave,” and Scripture there refers to a sale by the courts. Rather, because of two novel teachings here. One, it is written “Or a Hebrew woman” — she must also be liberated after six years. But not a woman sold by the courts, since women are not sold for larceny, as it is said, “For ‘his’ larceny” — not for her larceny. Rather, a minor sold by her father. This teaches that if six years elapsed before she reached puberty, she must be liberated. Another novel teaching here is “you must surely present him with a severance bonus.”

### 15:14

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-14|Deuteronomy 15:14]]

**You must surely present him with a severance bonus.** The literal meaning of this is “adornment,” to be held high and viewed. An object which indicates that you treated him benevolently. Some explain that the word refers to a necklace worn around the throat.

**From your flocks, your threshing area, and your wine cellar.** Perhaps only from these alone? The Torah therefore teaches, “with which . . . blessed you.” From everything with which your Creator blessed you. Why are [only] these mentioned? Just as these are distinguished by their blessed status, so do we include everything categorized as blessed. This excludes mules. Also, the Rabbis derive in tractate Kidushin, through the *gezerah shava*-word comparison principle, the amount which must be given to him from each species.

### 15:15

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-15|Deuteronomy 15:15]]

**Remember that you were a slave.** And I bestowed a parting gift and repeated it with the spoils of Egypt and [then] the spoils of the sea. You, as well, should bestow a severance gift and then repeat it.

### 15:17

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-17|Deuteronomy 15:17]]

**Perpetual slave.** Perhaps this follows the plain meaning? The Torah therefore teaches, “Return, everyone, to his ancestral property, everyone shall return to his family.” This teaches that the reference here is merely in the perpetuity of *Yovel.*

**Do the same to your maid-servant as well.** Bestow the severance bonus upon her. Perhaps even with the piercing [of the ear] Scripture equates her? The Torah therefore teaches. “If the slave should say.” The slave undergoes the piercing, but not the maid-servant.

### 15:18

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-18|Deuteronomy 15:18]]

**For, double the profit produced by the wage earner.** From here it is derived that a Hebrew slave works day and night, thus producing double the work of the wage earner hired on a daily basis. What work does he do at night? His master gives him a Gentile maid-servant, and the offspring are the property of the owner.

### 15:19

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-19|Deuteronomy 15:19]]

**Every firstborn . . . you must consecrate.** Elsewhere it is said that one should not consecrate him. How is this resolved? He may not consecrate him as a different sacrifice. Here, however, it teaches that it is a *mitzvah* to declare ‘You are hereby consecrated as a firstborn.’ Another interpretation. One cannot say “You must consecrate”! since it has already been said. “Do not consecrate”! And one cannot say “Do not consecrate”! since it has already been said, “You must consecrate”! How is this resolved? You may consecrate it whereby its monetary value becomes sacred, then donate, to the sacred *hekdesh* domain, the value of its beneficiary payment.

**You may not work with your first-born ox, or shear, etc.** The converse, as well, is prohibited by Rabbinic derivation, but Scripture depicts what is commonly practiced.

### 15:20

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-20|Deuteronomy 15:20]]

**Before Adonoy your God, you must eat it.** This is addressed to the *kohein*, as we already find that this is one of the priestly gifts, whether sound or blemished, as it is said, “Their flesh shall be yours, etc.”

**Each year.** This teaches that it should not be detained for more than a year. Perhaps it becomes disqualified once the year had passed? It has already been compared to the tithe, as it is said, “You must eat, before Adonoy your God, etc., the tithe of your grain, wine, and oil, and the first-born of your cattle and flocks.” Just as the second tithe is not disqualified from one year to the next, so is the first-born not disqualified. Rather, it is a *mitzvah* within the year.

**Each year [lit. year by year].** If it was slaughtered at the end of the year, it may be eaten that day, and another day into the following year. This teaches that it may be eaten for two days and a night.

### 15:21

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-21|Deuteronomy 15:21]]

**A blemish.** This is a general statement.

**If it is crippled or blind.** This is specific.

**Any severe blemish.** This returns to the general. Just as the specific statement refers explicitly to a visible permanent blemish, so must every disqualifying blemish be visible and permanent.

### 15:23

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-23|Deuteronomy 15:23]]

**Only, do not eat its blood.** In order that you do not say that, as the entire animal is permitted by exemption from a generally prohibited category — since it is sacred, yet may be slaughtered outside the sanctuary without redemption and eaten — then perhaps even its blood is permitted. The Torah therefore teaches “Only do not eat its blood.”

## Chapter 16

### 16:1

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-1|Deuteronomy 16:1]]

**Take heed of the month of spring.** Before it arrives. Take heed that it is compatible with springtime, so that you may bring the *omer-mincha*- offering. If not, add a month to the year.

**From Egypt at night.** But didn’t they leave by day, as it is said, “On the day after the pascal-offering, the Israelites went out, etc.”? But, because it was at night that Pharoah granted them permission to leave, as it is said, “He called for Moshe and Aharon at night, etc.”

### 16:2

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-2|Deuteronomy 16:2]]

**You shall slaughter the pesach-offering to Adonoy your God; flocks.** As it is said, “You shall take it from the sheep and goats.”

**And cattle.** You shall slaughter, as the *chagigah*-offering. If those engaged as partners in the *pesach* were a large group, they must bring, with it, a *chagigah*-offering, so that it is eaten when they have filled themselves. In addition, the Rabbis derive many teachings from this passage.

### 16:3

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-3|Deuteronomy 16:3]]

**Bread of anguish.** Bread which memorializes the anguish they suffered in Egypt.

**Since in haste you left.** Without sufficient time for the dough to leaven. This will serve as a memorial. The haste was not yours, but Egypt’s, as it is said, “The Egyptians pressed the people, etc.”

**So that you remember.** By eating the *pesach* and *matzah,* the day of your departure.

### 16:4

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-4|Deuteronomy 16:4]]

**None of the flesh may remain —** which you slaughter towards evening of the first day — until morning. This prohibits retaining the flesh of the *pesach* offered during future generations, since this was previously stated only with regard to the *pesach* brought in Egypt. The “first day” mentioned here refers to the fourteenth of Nisan, as it is said, “But before the first day you must remove all leaven from your homes.” Because Scripture has digressed from matters concerning the *pesach,* and entered a discussion of the laws of the seven days — for example, “For seven days, are you to eat on it *matzos*,” “No sourdough of yours may be seen in all of your boundary” — it is now necessary to identify the slaughter to which this admonition is directed. For, if it had written, “None of the flesh which you slaughter towards evening may remain until morning,” one might have concluded that *shelamim*-offerings slaughtered throughout the entire seven day period are included in “do not allow to remain,” and thus may be eaten for only a day and a night. Scripture, therefore, specifies “towards evening ‘of the first day.”’ Another interpretation. To the *chagigah*-offering of the fourteenth, is Scripture’s reference, teaching that it may be eaten for two days. And “the first” mentioned here is Scripture’s reference to the first day of the festival. This is the sense of the passage: ‘The flesh of the *chagigah* which you slaughter towards evening may not remain overnight during the first day of the festival until the morning of the second; however, it may be eaten on the fourteenth and fifteenth.’ This is the way it is taught in tractate Pesachim.

### 16:6

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-6|Deuteronomy 16:6]]

**Towards evening, at sunset, at the time you left Egypt.** These are three separate times. Towards evening — after six hours — slaughter it. At sunset — eat it. At the time you left [Egypt] — you burn it, meaning that it is disqualified as *nosar,* and must be removed to the burning site.

### 16:7

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-7|Deuteronomy 16:7]]

**You shall cook.** The reference is to roasting over an open fire. This, too, is described as cooking.

**You may depart in the morning.** The morning of the second day. This teaches that one is required to remain overnight, to include the night after the festival.

### 16:8

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-8|Deuteronomy 16:8]]

**For six days you shall eat matzos.** Elsewhere it is said seven days. Seven from the old crop, six from the new. Another interpretation. This teaches that eating *matzoh* on the seventh day is not obligatory. You learn from this, and apply it to all six days; For, the seventh was included in a general group, and then isolated, with the teaching that eating *matzah* then is not obligatory, but arbitrary. It was not isolated strictly for itself, but as an example for the entire group. Just as the seventh day is arbitrary, so are all the days arbitrary, except for the first evening, which Scripture designates as obligatory, as it is said, “In the evening you shall eat matzos.”

**[Is one of] withdrawal for the sake of Adonoy your God.** Withdraw from working. Another interpretation. A gathering for celebrating with food and drink, as in “Please, let us gather you up.”

### 16:9

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-9|Deuteronomy 16:9]]

**From the time the sickle begins felling the standing grain.** From the time the *omer* is cut, which begins the harvest.

### 16:10

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-10|Deuteronomy 16:10]]

**The fullness of your open-handed gift.** The fullness of the gift of your hand, wholly commensurate with the blessing. Bring *shelamim*-offerings of rejoicing, and invite guests for feasting.

### 16:11

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-11|Deuteronomy 16:11]]

**The Levite, proselyte, orphan, and widow.** My four correspond with your four — your son, daughter, servant, and maid-servant. If you bring joy to Mine, I shall bring joy to yours.

### 16:12

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-12|Deuteronomy 16:12]]

**Remember that you were a slave, etc.** I redeemed you with thr stipulation that you observe and fulfill these statutes.

### 16:13

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-13|Deuteronomy 16:13]]

**When you harvest.** At harvest time, when you place the summer produce in storage. Another interpretation of when you harvest your threshing area and wine cellar. This teaches that the *sukkah* should be covered with the husks and skins left in the threshing area and wine cellar.

### 16:15

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-15|Deuteronomy 16:15]]

**You shall experience pure joy.** According to its plain meaning, this is not a command, but an assurance. According to the Talmudic teaching, however, it is derived from here that the last evening of the festival is included in the *mitzvah* to rejoice.

### 16:16

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-16|Deuteronomy 16:16]]

**He shall not appear empty handed in the presence of Adonoy.** Rather, bring *olah*-pilgrimage-offerings and festive *shelamim*-offerings.

### 16:17

[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-17|Deuteronomy 16:17]]

**Everyone according to the gift appropriate to his means.** Someone who has numerous guests and great affluence should bring many *olah-* and *shelamim*-offerings.
