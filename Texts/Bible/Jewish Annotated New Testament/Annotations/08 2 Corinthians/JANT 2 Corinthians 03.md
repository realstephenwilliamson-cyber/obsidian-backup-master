### 3.1–12: Ministers of the new covenant

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 357* ^3-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Letters of recommendation*, introductions to the writer’s friends; see [[Acts 18#^18-27|Acts 18.27]]. Paul’s lack of such letters is seen as a weakness; see 5.12. ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-2|2–3]]:** *Spirit* recalls the Corinthians’ conversion; see [[Galatians 03#^3-2|Gal 3.2–3]]. *Tablets of stone* . . . *tablets of human hearts*, contrasts the Decalogue or Ten Commandments ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-1|Ex 20.1–17]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-12|24.12]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-18|31.18]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-1|34.1]]) with a “new covenant” through which God says, “I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts” ([[Jeremiah 31#^31-31|Jer 31.31–33]]); [[Ezekiel 36#^36-26|Ezek 36.26–27]] contrasts “heart of stone” with “heart of flesh” and the spirit of God to be poured into the people. Talmudic rabbis similarly found a powerful metaphor in the idea of Torah being in people’s hearts (e.g., “Study with all your heart and with all your soul to know my ways. . . . Keep my Torah in your heart and have awe of me before your eyes,” [[Berakhot 17#17a|*b. Ber*. 17a]]). ^3-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-3|3]]:** *Spirit of the living God*, the manifest presence of God on earth, combining two biblical and later Jewish conceptions: God’s “Spirit” (Heb “ruaḥ”), which fills those to whom God imparts wisdom (e.g., Bezalel, [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-2|Ex 31.2-5]]; see [[Berakhot 55#55a|*b. Ber.* 55a]]), and “living,” i.e., manifestly active in human affairs (e.g., [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-23|Deut 5.26]]; [[Joshua 03#^3-10|Josh 3.10]]; [[Psalms 042#^42-3|Ps 42.2]]; [[Shabbat 137#137b|*b. Shabb.* 137b]]; [[Eruvin 13#13b|*b. Eruv.* 13b]]; *b. [[Pesachim 87#87b|Pesah. 87b]]*). ^3-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-4|4]]:** *Confidence*, see v 12 and 1.12–14n. ^3-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-5|5–6]]:** *Competence*, see 2.15–16n. In these verses the adjective, noun, and verb forms of Gk “hikanos” (“fitting,” “qualified”) indicate that God equips *us* for serving the *new covenant*; see 3.2–3n. The \[Holy\] *Spirit* recalls v 3 and anticipates vv 17–18. In Paul’s binary thinking, since *the Spirit gives life*, i.e., resurrects ([[Romans 04#^4-17|Rom 4.17]]; [[Romans 08#^8-11|8.11]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-22|1 Cor 15.22]],[[1 Corinthians 15#^15-36|36]],[[1 Corinthians 15#^15-45|45]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-21|Gal 3.21]]; cf. [[John 05#^5-21|Jn 5.21]]; [[John 06#^6-63|6.63]]; [[1 Peter 03#^3-18|1 Pet 3.18]]), its opposite, the *letter*, i.e., the written code, must mean death. ^3-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-7|7–11]]:** Evoking the image of Moses’ radiant countenance ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-29|Ex 34.29–35]]), Paul uses a rabbinic argument, from the lesser to the greater (“qal vahomer”): *if the ministry of* (that which brought) *death* \[i.e., the lesser covenant written in stone\] . . . *came in glory* \[reflected in Moses’ radiant face\] . . . *how much more will the* \[greater\] *ministry of the Spirit come in glory*. The Talmud (*y. Sanh*. 7.11) lists this interpretative technique as the first of Hillel’s seven exegetical principles. ^3-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-7|7]]:** *Ministry of death*, the old covenant’s fading glory. *Chiseled*, at [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-12|Ex 24.12]] God gives Moses “the tablets of stone, with the law and the commandment, which I have written for their instruction” (cf. [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-18|Ex 31.18]]). [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-16|Ex 32.16]] adds that God’s writing was “engraved upon the tablets.” ^3-7-2

> [!note] Moses’ Radiant Face (2 Cor 3.12–18; Ex 34.29–35) *(p. 357)*
> According to Ex 34, when Moses descended Mt. Sinai with the tablets of the Torah, “the skin of his face was shining” (34.30) because “he had been talking with God” (34.29). Because this transfiguration made the people afraid, Moses veiled his face while he spoke with them, but in direct conversation with God he removed the veil. Paul reads this text midrashically, attributing to Moses a purpose that is not explicit in the original text but that uses that text’s language to support Paul’s own unique perspective. Thus Paul asserts that Moses “put a veil over his face to keep the people of Israel from gazing at the end of the glory”—Paul here refers to the Torah—“that was being set aside” (3.13).
>
> Rabbinic commentators offer differing readings. According to [[Berakhot 7#7a|*b. Ber*. 7a]], the emanating glow was Moses’ reward for hiding his face from the divine presence at the burning bush ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-7|Ex 3.7]]). The rabbis also took practical lessons from Moses’ experience: that Moses did not know his face was glowing ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-29|Ex 34.29]]) proves that one need not inform a gift’s recipient of the gift ([[Shabbat 10#10b|*b. Shabb*. 10b]]); that Moses wrote the covenant and God repaid him with radiance teaches that the bridegroom pays the cost of preparing the marriage contract (*Deut. Rab*. 3.12). *Ex. Rab*. 33.1 states that individuals can determine the worth of merchandise from the value of the commission paid the broker. In this way, Moses’ glowing face reveals the value of Torah.

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-9|9]]:** *Ministry of condemnation*, that is, life under the law, which once brought *glory*. *Ministry of justification*, life under the new covenant, which depends solely upon God’s grace. ^3-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-11|11]]:** *What was set aside*, the law. ^3-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-12|12]]:** *Boldness*, fearlessness of appearing before God in the end-time (v 4; 1.12–14n.). ^3-12

### 3.13–18: Moses’ veil

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-13|Text]], p. 358* · Paul interprets Moses’ *veil* ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-33|Ex 34.33–35]]) as covering the transitory *glory. . . set aside*, lit., “abolished.” ^3-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-14|14–15]]:** Paul reads Ex 34 as a rabbinic-style parable (“mashal”). The *veil* represents lack of enlightenment. *Old covenant* is the Mosaic (Sinaitic) Torah. ^3-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Moses* signifies the Torah (see [[II Chronicles 25#^25-4|2 Chr 25.4]]; [[Mark 12#^12-26|Mk 12.26]]; [[Acts 15#^15-21|Acts 15.21]]). ^3-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-16|16]]:** [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-34|Ex 34.34]] states that when Moses stood in God’s presence, he (Moses or God—in the Heb the subject of the verb is ambiguous) removed the veil. Paul represents the verb as passive—“is removed”—which supports Paul’s point: truly knowing the *Lord* removes the *veil*, in contrast to veiled “minds” (vv 14–15). ^3-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Spirit*, as in 3.6 and 3.8, refers to the New Covenant, which offers freedom from the Law. Paul here may be referring to the risen Jesus, in whom similarly Paul sees freedom from the Law. [[Isaiah 61#^61-1|Isa 61.1]] offers parallel language: the Spirit of the Sovereign Lord is upon Isaiah, who thereby proclaims freedom to captives. ^3-17

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 03#^3-18|18]]:** *Glory of the Lord* parallels the *Lord, the Spirit* (vv 16–17). *Being transformed*, a fundamental change in those without veils. *Image*, see [[Romans 08#^8-29|Rom 8.29]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-49|1 Cor 15.49]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-10|Col 3.10]]. *One degree of glory to another* reflects the Greco-Roman belief that an encounter with the divine transforms the beholder into its image. See “Moses’ Radiant Face,” p. 357. ^3-18
