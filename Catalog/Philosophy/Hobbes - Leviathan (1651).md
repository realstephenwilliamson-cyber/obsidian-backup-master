## About

Thomas Hobbes's (1588–1679) treatise on the structure of society and legitimate government, written during the English Civil War. It argues for a social contract and rule by an absolute sovereign.

- **1651:** first published in London, printed for Andrew Crooke. Hobbes dated the dedication to Francis Godolphin from Paris, April 1651. Wikipedia credits the engraved frontispiece to Abraham Bosse, working with Hobbes; the Wellcome Collection catalogue calls the engraver anonymous, sometimes attributed to Bosse.
- **Three "1651" editions:** three editions carry the same imprint and date. They are named for their title-page ornaments: the "Head" (the true first edition), the "Bear" and the "Ornaments". Sources date the later two differently:
  - Wikisource says both appeared in Hobbes's lifetime.
  - The Wellcome catalogue, following Macdonald and Hargreaves's bibliography, places the "Bear" in Holland and the "Ornaments" around 1680.
  - Noel Malcolm's research, reported by the Bodleian and followed by the ESTC, finds the "Bear" to be a piracy: London sheets printed in 1670 combined with sheets printed in the Netherlands, out before the end of 1678. He dates the "Ornaments" to the late 1690s, no later than 1702, after Hobbes's death.
- **1668:** Hobbes's revised Latin version, *Leviathan, sive De materia, forma, & potestate civitatis ecclesiasticae et civilis*, printed in Amsterdam by Joan Blaeu in the collected Latin works (*Opera philosophica*).
- **2012:** Noel Malcolm's critical edition of the English and Latin texts, in three volumes, in the Clarendon Edition of the Works of Thomas Hobbes (Oxford).
- **The vault's text:** Project Gutenberg #3207 says it was prepared from the Pelican Classics edition, which was prepared from the first edition, that is, the "Head".
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia counts it among the earliest and most influential examples of social contract theory. It adds that John Mearsheimer's realist theory of international relations draws largely on it.
