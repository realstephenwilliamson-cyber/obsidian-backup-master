## Translation

>
> **5) The Lower (Apara) Procedure Deposition of the Parts of Mantra in relation to the Categories of the Six- fold Path**
>
> [^21]The Parts (of the Vidyā) (pada) (of Parāparā) here (in this case) are of two kinds according to (whether they are) sorted[^22] into the categories (varga) (of the cosmic and phonemic order) or the Vidyā (itself).[^23] *Now listen to how they pervade (the spaces they occupy) like their Mantra.*[^24] (with 6.18cd–19ab)

> [!source] Dyczkowski, *Mālinī*, January 2024 – April 2024
> p. 29 ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Malini (journal), January-April 2024.pdf#page=31>))
>
> The first (occupies) four finger (spaces), two others eight finger (spaces) each, three, ten each, one, fifteen finger (spaces) and another again (of fifteen fingers) and another of four which is the ninth. (The tenth), which is immense, pervades (them all).[^26] (with 6.19cd–20)

[^21]: Next the Tantra goes on to present the core and mainstay of the depositions, that is, the three standard measures of the body in which they are deposited. As Abhinava explains in the Tantrāloka: 'There are 1) eighty-four (finger breadths from the left toe) up to the forehead; 2) there are ninety-six up to the Cavity (of Brahmā), and 3) one hundred and eight up to the End of the Twelve (finger space above the head) (*dvādaśānta*). This threefold procedure that (I have) seen in the scripture called Śrīpūrva (i.e., the *Mālinīvijayottara*), will be explained (step by step,) in due order.' TĀ 16/100-101ab These three measures are those of the three depositions that follow. They are the Lower – Apara – one which is described in MV /18cd-24, the Intermediate one (Parāpara) described in MV 6/25—26ab and the Supreme one (Para) described in MV 6/26cd-27ab. It is quite clear that these three are three basic measurements of the body. However, Abhinava further relates them to the three homonymous Trika goddesses. In that case they are named Parā, Parāparā and Aparā, in the feminine. This equation is not found in the Mālinīvijayottara and Abhinava does not tell us where he got it from, perhaps from his Trika teacher Śambhunātha, if not from his own inspiration (svataḥ). It is a little confusing because, as we shall see, Parāparā Vidyā can be projected onto the body in all three formats. We observe that all four depositions described up to here are of the lower (Apara) type measuring 84 fingers. They are those of 1) the thirty-six principles (MV 6/2-6ab). 2) the Five Elements (MV 6/6cd-9ab), 3) the three principles (MV 6/10), and 4) the Path of the Worlds (MV 6/11-18ab). After Abhinava has described the first one of these (in TĀ 16/101cd-105) he goes on to describe the other two formats briefly with reference to the deposition of the thirty-six principles it in just two verses. These three depositions measure 84, 96 and 108 fingers respectively. The 118 worlds are similarly projected into an 84-finger space. Abhinava finds occasion to note that the first of this projection of the 36 principles, is a Lower – Apara – procedure in which they are projected into a space of 84 finger breadths. He goes on to supplement the Mālinīvijayottara by briefly stating how the other two procedures in relation to the worlds would be, saying: 'There are thus one hundred and eighteen worlds deposited here in the body, which measures eighty-four (finger-breadths). The rest (of the body) is pervaded by the Śiva principle. Such is said to be the lower (apara) procedure. In the one called intermediate, the number (of finger-breadths for each tattva from Water to Nature) is fifty-eight (fingers) instead of forty-six. One should know that there are seventy in the supreme (procedure). Here, in these two procedures, one should increase (the size of each) part progressively for the group of (fifty-six) worlds, beginning with Lakula (in the Water principle) up to the eight Yogas (in the Nature principle), in accord with the calculations.' TĀ 16/129-131.

Here again Abhinava renders the meaning of the *Mālinīvijayottara* accessible to us by paraphrasing the text adding the few necessary details to complete the sense as he does so. He informs us, in his own subtle way, what the *Mālinīviyajottara* does not tell us. In this case it is that the Mantra of which these are the parts are the two alphabet goddesses, namely. Mātṛkā and Mālinī. The former is the common order of the alphabet, and the latter the Trika alphabet. In the *Tantrāloka* he says: '(The teacher) should conjoin the nine Parts (of Mantra) associated with (the phonemes of) Mātāka and Mālinī, beginning with the Part (corresponding to) Earth, into (nine spaces, consisting of) four, eight, eight, ten, ten, ten, fifteen, four, and fifteen (finger-spaces, respectively). The tenth Part, which is called 'Śiva', pervades (all the preceding ones).' TĀ 16/135cd-137ab The finger spaces are thus distributed as follows: 4 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 15 + 15 + 4 = 84. For example, the *pada* of Earth is KṢ, according to the Mātṛkā alphabet, and PH, according to Mālinī, and takes up four fingerspaces. In TĀ 11/51-53, drawning from MV 2/51-57, Abhinava enumerates the mantras, parts, phonemes, worlds and principles as they are accommodated into the spheres of the five Forces, thus listing the contents of the six Paths in relation to one another. There the parts of mantra are ten. These correspondences are tabulated in appendix >>>. The corresponding measurements with respect to the body are not listed by in the *Tantrāloka* 11/51-53. We get them from here and have been added to table. Here we observe an example of how Abhinava's exegesis is supplemented by his source. One wonders how much we have missed by the loss of the Tantras to which he refers that have been lost. Initiation can be imparted by just depositing the Parts of Mantra, without reference to the Paths. The *Mālinīvijayottara* does not say this directly, Abhinava understood this to be implied, or else was told by his Trika teacher Śambhunātha who read the Tantra with him. Thus, Abhinava writes in the *Tantrāloka*: 'If the ten Parts (*pada*) (of the Mantra) mentioned previously (i.e., Parāparā Vidyā) are deposited independently, and (if the teacher wishes that) the initiation be performed by means of them alone, then this is the (manner in which) the deposition is conceived. When the main factor in the initiation is the principles (*tattva*) and the rest (of the Six Paths), the series of Parts is considered to be included there in that way, in accord with (each of) the principles and the rest. When their own predominance applies, (the teacher) should deposit the series of Parts successively (in due order) (krameṇa) (independently) in (the course of) initiation, without referring them to the (path of the) principles and the rest.' TĀ 16/132-135ab This is the lower (Apara) procedure, that is, the projection onto 84 fingers' spaces on the body. Again completing the Mālinīvijaya for us, Abhinava goes on to explain the other two procedures are in relation to it: 'The two procedures, (intermediate and supreme, are derived from this one) by adding to the five Parts, omitting that of Earth, (a space of) twelve fingers (for the intermediate one), and twelve (more for the supreme one).'
