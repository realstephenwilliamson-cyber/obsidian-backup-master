### 13.1–13: Love

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 345* · Paul’s great evocation of love first recalls his discounting of rhetorical abilities (1.17; 2.1) and wisdom (1.20–25), before turning to spiritual gifts, which he warns will amount to nothing if not used for the community’s best interest and to nurture love. ^13-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Love,* (Gk “agape”) of God and neighbor, the *more excellent way* introduced in 12.31 (LXX [[LXX Leviticus 19#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; LXX [[LXX Deuteronomy 06#^6-5|Deut 6.5]], which Jesus summarizes as the “Greatest Commandment” in [[Matthew 22#^22-36|Mt 22.36–40]]; [[Mark 12#^12-31|Mk 12.31]]; see also [[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25–28]]), associated with righteous worship (LXX Ps 30.23; [[LXX Psalms 39#^39-16|39.16]]; [[LXX Psalms 68#^68-35|68.35]]; [[LXX Psalms 96#^96-10|96.10]]; [[LXX Psalms 144#^144-20|144.20]]). *Tongues . . . of angels*, see *T. Job* 48.3; 49.2; 50.1–2; *1 En.* 71.11. *Clanging cymbal,* used in both pagan and Jewish worship (LXX [[LXX Psalms 150#^150-5|Ps 150.5]]; Josephus *Ant.* 7.306; Ovid, *Metam.* 4.38, Strabo, *Geogr.* 10.3.7,13,15). ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-2|2]]:** *Knowledge . . . faith*, see 12.8–9. *Mysteries*, see 15.51–52n. ^13-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Give away*, extreme generosity was a hallmark of the “ekklēsia*”* according to [[Acts 02#^2-45|Acts 2.45]] and was perhaps part of Jesus’ program ([[Matthew 19#^19-21|Mt 19.21]]; [[Mark 10#^10-21|Mk 10.21]]; [[Luke 12#^12-23|Lk 12.23]]; [[Luke 18#^18-22|18.22]]). *Boast,* about hardships, since enduring them demonstrated virtue and validated authority (see [[Romans 05#^5-3|Rom 5.3–4]]; [[Romans 08#^8-35|8.35–36]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-3|2 Cor 1.3–7]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-7|4.7–12]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-16|4.16–5.5]]; 6.3–10; 7.8–11; 12.21–13.2; Plutarch, *De laude* 544C). Other manuscripts have “burned,” punished (see 4.9–13). ^13-3

> [!note] Jewish Views of Love *(p. 345)*
> Love of God and neighbor have been central to Judaism since the Bible, as affirmed by Jesus and Rabbi Akiba, who considered love of neighbor to be “a great rule in the Torah” ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]]; [[Matthew 22#^22-36|Mt 22.36-40]]; *Sifre Deut*. 32 on [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]]; [[Mark 12#^12-28|Mk 12.28–34]]; *m. Avot* 1.12; *y. Ber.* 67a; *y. Ned.* 30b; *S. Eli. Rab.* 15). The Bible extends love to include the stranger ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-34|Lev 19.34]]), which commentators like Moses Luzzatto \[eighteenth century\] construed as referring to all human beings. The majority of rabbinic texts about love concern love between God and Israel. The modern Eastern European Jewish ethical movement known as Musar considers selfless love the most important attribute to cultivate (Eliyahu Eliezer Dessler \[twentieth century\], *Michtav me-Eliyahu*, “Discourse on Lovingkindness”). Hebrew has only one word, *ʾahava*, for all types of love, unlike Greek, which distinguishes *agapē* (spiritual and romantic love) from *philia* (fondness), *epithumia* (longing or desire), *storgē* (affection), and *eros* (sensual love, sexual attraction or lust); the terms are not mutually exclusive, and only the first three are found in the New Testament. *Agapē* is the Greek translation of Heb *ʾahava* in LXX [[LXX Leviticus 19#^19-18|Lev 19.18]] and [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]], but also in the Song of Songs for romantic love (LXX [[LXX Song of Songs 01#^1-4|Song 1.4]]; [[Song of Songs 3#^3-5|3.5]]). Similarly, *philia* expresses favor (LXX [[II Chronicles 19#^19-2|2 Chr 19.2]]) and fondness (LXX [[LXX Proverbs 08#^8-17|Prov 8.17]]; [[Proverbs 17#^17-17|17.17]]; LXX [[LXX Ecclesiastes 03#^3-8|Eccl 3.8]]) as well as human appetites for food and sleep (LXX [[LXX Genesis 27#^27-4|Gen 27.4]]; LXX [[LXX Isaiah 56#^56-10|Isa 56.10]]). *Epithumia* can refer to desire of a romantic or non-romantic nature (LXX [[LXX Deuteronomy 14#^14-26|Deut 14.26]]; LXX [[LXX Proverbs 23#^23-3|Prov 23.3]]; LXX [[LXX Song of Songs 02#^2-3|Song 2.3]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-11|Wis 6.11]]), while *eros* is used, rarely, for passion, whether sexual or not (LXX [[LXX Esther (Greek) 02#^2-17|Esth 2.17]]; LXX [[LXX Proverbs 04#^4-6|Prov 4.6]]; [[Proverbs 30#^30-16|30.16]]). Many Jewish scholars assert that love as a feeling is not adequate, and it is demonstrated properly through action (e.g., Abraham Isaac Kook \[1865–1935\], *Middot ha-Rayah* \[“The Moral Principles”\], “Love” 2). This view may already be found in [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5–9]], where v 5 mandates loving God, and vv 6–9 illustrate various actions that express such love.

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-10|10]]:** *Complete*, eschatological fulfillment (15.24). ^13-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-11|11]]:** *Child*, see 2.6n., 3.1n. ^13-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Face to face*, like Moses’ encounter with God ([[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-8|Num 12.8]]; [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-10|Deut 34.10]]; Philo, *Heir* 262, uses the same contrast). ^13-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Faith, hope, and love,* will outlast spiritual gifts, but *love* is eternal. In the fulfillment of the kingdom (the end time, see 6.10), *love* will embrace all (see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-9|Wis 3.9]]; *T. Gad* 4.7 on love as a gift of the Spirit). Rabbinic tradition ties faith in God to hope for the world-to-come (*Mek. Beshallah* 7 on [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-31|Ex 14.31]]). ^13-13
