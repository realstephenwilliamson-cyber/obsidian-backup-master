# 2 Corinthians 7 (JANT notes)

[[JANT 2 Corinthians 06|← 2 Corinthians 6]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 2 Corinthians 08|2 Corinthians 8 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07|NRSVue]] · [[2 Corinthians 07|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 2 Corinthians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-1|7.1]]:** *Beloved*, see 2.4n. *Cleanse ourselves . . . defilement*, the Gk term *defilement* (“molysmoú”), reflecting the Heb “tumaʾah,” appears only here in the NT. Biblical and postbiblical Judaism understood the body to require periodic purification from ritual impurities (e.g., [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^15-11|Lev 15.11]], on the impurity that results from bodily emissions). But already in the Tanakh the concept of defilement was extended metaphorically to moral failings; see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-30|Lev 16.30]], referring to the cleansing power of the Day of Atonement: “For on this day atonement shall be made for you, to cleanse (lit., purify) you; from all your sins you shall be clean (lit., “purified”) before the Lord.” *Body and spirit* are not in opposition here; contrast [[Galatians 05#^5-16|Gal 5.16–26]]. Like Paul, both the Tanakh and early rabbinic Judaism distinguish the physical body from the “soul” or “spirit,” viewed as the life force. Associated with respiration, the “soul” was understood to derive from God’s own “breath” ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]]). The rabbis see body and soul as closely connected and equally responsible for a person’s choices. At the eschaton, the reunited body and soul will be judged together (*b. Sanh*. 90b–91a). *Fear of God* (see 5.11), common Jewish conception (e.g., [[Proverbs 01#^1-7|Prov 1.7]]), understood as underpinning piety. ^7-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-2|2–4]]:** Resumption of appeal in 6.11–13. ^7-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-3|3]]:** *Die . . . live*, Paul reverses an expression of deep affection—“live . . . die together”—to emphasize his bond with the Corinthians through identification with Christ. ^7-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-4|4]]:** *Boast*, see 1.12–14n. *Consolation . . . affliction*, see 1.3–7n. ^7-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-5|7.5–16]]:** **Resumption of travelogue (2.12–13).** ^7-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-6|6–8]]:** *God, who consoles the downcast*, *b. Ketub*. 8b refers to God as “Lord of consolation,” who comforts the bereaved. *Titus* (see 2.13n.) brought news that Paul’s *letter* (see 2.3–4n.) was received favorably; see 2.5–11. ^7-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-8|8]]:** *My letter*, see 2.3–4n. ^7-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-10|10]]:** *Godly grief* . . . *repentance*, this idea of repentance, turning from sin, parallels the central theme of Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement. For Paul, unlike in Judaism, the result is *salvation*, not just continued earthly life. ^7-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-12|12]]:** *One who did the wrong*, the offender of 2.6. *Who was wronged*, Paul. ^7-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 07#^7-16|16]]:** *Confidence* concludes the narrative of vv 5–16 and anticipates the collection (chs 8–9). ^7-16
