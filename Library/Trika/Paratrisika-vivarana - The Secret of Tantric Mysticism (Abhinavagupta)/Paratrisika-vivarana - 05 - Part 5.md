EXPOSITION 

In this relationship of Maālini to the para or transcendental state, Abhinavagupta brings out the following points: 

1\. ‘Sarvam sarvātmakam-— Everything consists of everything else’. This is the basic principle. Siva is immanent in manifestation. Since Siva is not confined to any particular element, but is the all-of-Reality, therefore, there is nothing in the universe 

*[p. 137]* ^pg163

which does not contain the all-of-Reality. When it is said for instance, that prthivi is solidity and has the attribute of gandha or smell, it does not mean that solidity is the only thing that is represented by prthivi. It only means that solidity is predominant in 1100727, but it contains the other elements also in miniature. 

Trika philosophy maintains that the entire manifestation is an expression of parā ŝakti or parā vāk or transcendental logos. This 0410 vāk is creative energy. Every letter of the alphabet represents energy in some form. The letters of the alphabet are arranged in two schemes in Trika, viz., Mātrkā and Mālini. Mātrkā means the little mother or phonematic creative energy. Mālini literally means the Devi who wears a mālā or garland of fifty letters of the Sanskrit alphabet. Mātrkā also wears a garland of fifty letters. That is why sometimes Mātrkā is called as pūrvamālinī and Mālinē is called as uttara-mālini. But the word Maālini is rarely used for -Mātrkā. Another etymology of Maālini is ‘malate viśvam iti Mālini’, 1.6. the śakti of letters which holds the entire universe within itself (the root ‘mal meaning ‘to hold’). 

The main difference between Maātrkā and 11410 consists in the arrangement of letters. In Mātrkā, they are arranged in a regular order, i.e. the vowels come first, and the consonants come next in a serial order. In Maālini, they are arranged in an irregular way i.e. the vowels and consonants are mixed and no serial order is observed. 

On the principle of ‘sarvam sarvātmakan?, Abhinavagupta has tried to show that the first sixteen letters of the Mālini order represent Śiva ¿attva just as the first sixteen vowels of the Mātrkā order do. Out of the sixteen letters of the Mālini order seven, viz., D

R, 7, ¢, Í, are called amrta bija, immortal germs or vowels, because they are the resting place of I-consciousness; they do not produce anything; they do not change. I connotes iśfānā bija or the power of mastery. U connotes ‘unmesa’ or introvertive ellloresence of knowledge, and & connotes ārdhvāśrayaṇa or the tendency to extroversion. All these vowels are concerned only with the inner life of Śiva.

In the previous description, Abhinavagupta has shown how the various phases of 74217140 I-consciousness represented by the first sixteen letters of Mālini are represented in the 41421140 order with reference to para or supreme state of 612 tattva. 

In this, he has shown how the various phases of aham-vimarśa of Siva tattva represented by the first sixteen letters of Mālini are represented in the Mātrkā order with reference to parāpara-samvitti or supreme-cum-non supreme state of Śiva tattva. 

Of the sixteen letters, seven viz. 2, F, ¢ l, 2, u and dare 276 bija letters or vowels. As such, they are aspects of Siva both in Mālini and Maātrkā and therefore undergo no change. They have the same function in parāpara as in para. 

The phonemes, according to Trika philosophy, have three aspects : sarvāgrarūpatā, sarvamadhyarūpatāand sarvāntagāmitā. Sarvā-grarūpatā refers to a phoneme as it is in itself. Sarvamadhyatā refers to phonemes undergoing transformation, and sarvāntagāmitā refers to phonemes reaching finally the state of Suddhavi«yā, svara, Sadāśiva and Śakti. 

Six consonants of Maālini, viz. na, tha, ca, dha, na and ba are reflected in Maātrkā in different phonemes in parāpara state under the law of sarvamadhya-rūpatā as shown in the chart.

Three consonants of Malini, , viz. ka, kha and ga after undergoing the changes into ksa, ha, and sa respectively under the law of sarva-madhya-rūpatā point to the final destiny, viz., of passing into suddhavidyā, Īśvara, Sadāśiva, and Śakti. They, therefore, refer to sarvāntagamitā state. Ka which represents prthivi in Mātrkā is reflected into ksa which symbolizes Śakti, but in the parāpara state, it is not simply Śakti but sadāśivamaya-Sakti. The parāmarśa or comprehension of 5202692 is aham idam (I am this, the unity of subject and object); the parāmarśsa of Śakti is only aham or 1, for in para state, there is no idam or objectivity separately; everything is only I or aham. Since this is a description of parāpara state, therefore, ksa or Śakti is described as Sadāśivamaya-Ṣakti. Similarly, ‘ha’ which represents Sadāśiva is in parāpara state Ďśvara-maya-sadāśiva. Similarly ‘sa’ which represents Īśvara is in parāpara state uddhavidyāmaya-lśvara. 

Thus the pervasion of Śiva full of invariable and infinite akti is described through the first sixteen letters in the order of the letters of the Mālini group from the point of view of sarvāgrarūpatā (parāsaṇvitti) and sarvamadhyarūpatā and sarvāntyarūpata (parāparasamvitti).
