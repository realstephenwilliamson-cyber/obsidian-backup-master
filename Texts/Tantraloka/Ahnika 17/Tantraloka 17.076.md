## Mūla

शिखां ग्रन्थियुतां छित्त्वा मलमाणवकं दहेत् ।
यतोऽधिकारभोगाख्यौ द्वौ पाशौ तु सदाशिवे ॥ ^mula

śikhāṃ granthiyutāṃ chittvā malamāṇavakaṃ dahet |
yato'dhikārabhogākhyau dvau pāśau tu sadāśive || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 08 - Chapter Seventeen#^pg191|p. 171]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=191>))
>
> When (everything) up to the end of Māyā has been purified, (the Goddess) Vāgīśīi, who was previously the power of Māyā, is (now) the power of Pure Knowledge (śuddhavidyā). Having invoked this Pure Knowledge, (the teacher) should offer it a libation. Once the plane of Sadāśiva has been purified sufficiently in this way by (the same) process, having severed the topknot along with the knot (on the thread), he should burn the fundamental Impurity of the individual soul (ā&navamala). (with 17.74–76ab)
>
> The fetter which is the Impurity of the individual soul is present (in a subtle form) within Sadāśiva, for it is said that there are two fetters within it, namely, (the residual trace of) worldly experience (bhoga) and the aptitude (adhikāra) (for it). However, (the Impurity of) Māyā extends here (up to) the Night (of Māyā) (niśā). When the Impurity called that of the individual soul, which is in the topknot (śikhā) that flows downwards, has been burnt (away), the disciple, as is proper, should bathe, and the teacher should rinse his mouth himself. (with 17.76cd–78ab)
