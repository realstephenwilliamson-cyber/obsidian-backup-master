Introduction

Prodicus came from the small island of Ceos, just off the tip of the Attic peninsula. He came to Athens as an embassador of his country, and made his fortune from his skills as a sophist, He seems to have traveled about Greece like other sophists, teaching for instance in Boeotia. He belongs to the second generation of sophists, who were born around 470-460 (see Plato Protagoras 3170). δ Like most sophists he made his reputation by exhibiting and teaching the skills of public speaking. In the realm of oratory, he was particularly known for making fine verbal distinctions between synonyms. We might think of him as the originator of lexicography. Plato portrays him as using this skill somewhat pedantically (15), but this may be a parody of a more subtle use. In any case, a refined sense of verbal nuance is an important element of rhetoric, and Prodicus seems to have been especially adept in instilling that sense in his

students.

\_ Besides his interests in rhetoric, Prodicus apparently wrote a treatise about natural philosophy. We have, however, no good information about his specific doctrines of natural philosophy. The one notable theory we are told about is an account of how religion arose from the worship of useful arts and their inventors (28-31 \[F3\]). This quasi-anthropological account is reminiscent of others of the time; it does not entail that Prodicus was an atheist (at least in the modern sense), for he might have subscribed to some different conception of the divine such as Xenophanes offered; but it did call into question the Greek pantheon. It is not ‘lear that his views caused a public stir in his lifetime, and the claim in <sup>1</sup>that he ‘was put to death in Athens seems to be a confusion.

The one long fragment we have of Prodicus’ work, the Choice of Heracles, feaches a moral lesson on the value of hard work. It would be useful both to €stablish Prodicus as an advocate of conventional virtues, and to inspire his tudents to apply themselves in their education.

Although Prodicus does not seem to have been very original in his methods or Mis theory, he had a broad range of interests and was successful in developing a Riche in sophistic education as an expert on style and diction.
