**1.** How many candles should one light on Chanukah? The[^1] mitzvah is that a single candle should be lit[^2] in each and every house,[^3] regardless of whether there are many members of the household, or merely one person [lives] there.[^4]  
A person who performs the mitzvah in a beautiful and conscientious manner should light candles for every member of the household,[^5] whether male or female.[^6]  
A person who is even more conscientious in his performance of the mitzvah than this and observes the mitzvah in the most desirable manner[^7] should light candles for every member of his household, a candle for each individual, whether male or female, on the first night.[^8] On each subsequent night, he should add a candle [for each of the members of the household].[^9] ^4-1

**2.** What does the above imply? When there are ten members of a household, on the first night one lights ten candles, on the second night - twenty, on the third night - thirty, until on the eighth night, one lights eighty candles. ^4-2

**3.** It is common custom in all of our cities in Spain that a single candle is lit for all the members of the household on the first night. We proceed to add a new candle on each and every night, until on the eighth night eight candles are lit. [This practice is followed] regardless of whether there are many members of the household or only one man [is lighting candles].[^10] ^4-3

**4.** When a candleholder has two openings,[^11] it can be counted for two individuals.[^12]  
[The following rules apply when] one fills a bowl with oil and surrounds it with wicks: If one covers it with a utensil,[^13] each of the wicks is considered to be a separate candle.[^14] If one does not cover it with a utensil,[^15] it is considered to be a large fire, and is not counted even as a single candle.[^16] ^4-4

**5.** The Chanukah candles should not be kindled before sunset.[^17] Instead, [they should be kindled] at sunset.[^18] One should not light later[^19] or earlier.[^20]  
Should one forget, or even if one purposely did not light at sunset,[^21] one may light afterwards until there are no longer any passersby in the marketplace.[^22]  
How long a duration of time is this? Approximately half an hour or slightly more than that.[^23] Should this time pass, one should not kindle the lights.[^24]  
One should place enough oil in the lamp[^25] so that it will continue burning until there are no longer any passersby in the marketplace.[^26] If one lit it and it became extinguished, one need not light it a second time.[^27] If it remained burning until there are no longer passersby in the marketplace,[^28] one may extinguish it or remove it[^29] if one desires.[^30] ^4-5

**6.** All oils and all wicks are acceptable for use in the Chanukah lamps,[^31] even those oils that are not drawn after the wick[^32] and even those wicks that do not hold the light well.[^33] Even on the Sabbath nights of Chanukah, it is permitted to light[^34] with oils and wicks that are forbidden to be used for the Sabbath lights.[^35]  
[The reason for this leniency is that][^36] it is forbidden to use the Chanukah candles [for one's own purposes][^37] whether on the Sabbath or on a weekday.[^38] It is even forbidden to use their light to inspect or count coins.[^39] ^4-6

**7.** It is a mitzvah to place[^40] the Chanukah lamp at the outside of the entrance to one's home,[^41] within the handbreadth that is closest to the doorway[^42] on the left side as one enters the home,[^43] so that the mezuzah will be on the right side[^44] and the Chanukah lamp on the left side.[^45]  
When a person lives in a second storey apartment,[^46] he should place [the Chanukah lamp] in a window close to the public domain.[^47] If [a person] places a Chanukah lamp more than twenty cubits[^48] [above the ground], his actions are of no consequence,[^49] because [the lamp] does not attract attention [at that height].[^50] ^4-7

**8.** In a time of danger, a person may place a Chanukah lamp inside his house;[^51] even if he lit it on his table, it is sufficient.[^52]  
[Therefore,] another lamp must be burning in the house to provide light for one's [mundane] activities.[^53] If a fire is burning in the house,[^54] an additional candle is not necessary. For a prestigious person who does not normally use the light of a fire, an additional candle is required.[^55] ^4-8

**9.** A Chanukah lamp that was kindled by a deaf-mute, a mentally incapable person, a minor, or a gentile is of no consequence. It must be kindled by a person who is obligated to light it.  
Should the [Chanukah lamp] be kindled inside and then taken and placed at the entrance of one's home while it is still burning, it is of no consequence. One must light it in its place.  
If one held a candle and stood in one place, it is of no consequence, since an observer will say, "He is standing there for his own purposes."  
When a lamp was burning through the entire [Sabbath] day, one may extinguish the light, recite the blessings [for the mitzvah], and relight the lamp. Kindling the lamp fulfills the mitzvah and not placing it down.  
It is permissible to light one Chanukah candle from another Chanukah candle. ^4-9

**10.** When a courtyard has two entrances from two different directions,[^56] it requires two [Chanukah] lamps.[^57] [Were one to light at only one entrance,] the passersby from the other direction might say, "A Chanukah light had not been placed down."[^58] If, however, [two entrances to a courtyard][^59] are located on the same side, [it is sufficient] to light at only one of them.[^60] ^4-10

**11.** A guest[^61] [at another person's home, whose family] kindles [the Chanukah lights] for him at his home need not kindle [Chanukah lights] in the home where he is [temporarily] lodging.[^62] If, however, he has no home in which [Chanukah lights] are being kindled,[^63] he is required to light in the place where he is lodging.[^64] He should share[^65] in the oil [used by the owner of his lodgings].[^66]  
If he is staying in a private dwelling,[^67] he is required to light in the place where he is staying,[^68] even though [Chanukah lights] are being kindled for him at home,[^69] because [of the impression created in the minds] of the passersby.[^70] ^4-11

**12.** The mitzvah of kindling Chanukah lamps is very dear. A person should be very careful in its observance to publicize the miracle and thus increase our praise of God and our expression of thanks for the miracles which He wrought on our behalf. Even if a person has no resources for food except [what he receives] from charity, he should pawn or sell his garments and purchase oil and lamps to kindle them [in fulfillment of the mitzvah].[^71] ^4-12

**13.** When a person has only a single *prutah*[^72] and he [is required to fulfill both the mitzvot of] sanctifying the [Sabbath] day[^73] and lighting the Chanukah lamp, he should give precedence to purchasing oil to kindle the Chanukah lamp over [purchasing] wine to recite *kiddush*.[^74] Since both [of these mitzvot] are Rabbinic in origin,[^75] it is preferable to give precedence to the kindling of the Chanukah lamp, for it commemorates the miracle.[^76] ^4-13

**14.** If [a person has the opportunity to fulfill only one of two mitzvot,] lighting a lamp for one's home [i.e., Sabbath candles][^77] or lighting a Chanukah lamp[^78] - or, alternatively, lighting a lamp for one's home or reciting *kiddush* - the lamp for one's home receives priority, since it generates peace within the home.[^79]  
[Peace is of primary importance, as reflected by the mitzvah requiring] God's name to be blotted out[^80] to create peace between a husband and his wife.[^81] Peace is great, for the entire Torah was given[^82] to bring about peace within the world, as \[[[Proverbs 03#^3-17|Proverbs 3:17]]] states: "Its ways are pleasant ways and all its paths are peace."  
Blessed[^83] be the Merciful One who grants assistance. This concludes the third book.[^84] ^4-14
