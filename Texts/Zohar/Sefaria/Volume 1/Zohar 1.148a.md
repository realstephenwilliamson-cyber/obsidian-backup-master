*Volume 1, folio 148a* · Vayetzei

*Passage Vayetzei 1:11 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.147b#^vayetzei-1-11|Vayetzei 1:11]].*

**Vayetzei 1:12** THEREAFTER HE LAY DOWN IN THAT PLACE TO SLEEP , for now that the couch was properly arranged, he, namely the sun, lay down on it. Thus the words “and he lay down in that place to sleep” are parallel to the text: “the sun ariseth and the sun comes in”.’ ^vayetzei-1-12

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayetzei 4:23–4:29.*

**Vayetzei 5:32** Whilst R. Isaac was one day sitting at the entrance of the cave of Apikutha, a man passed by with his two sons. Said one of them to the other: ‘The sun is most powerful when it is in the South, and were it not for the wind which tempers the heat, the world could not exist.’ ^vayetzei-5-32

**Vayetzei 5:33** Said the younger brother: ‘If not for Jacob, the world could not subsist. For when the unity of God is proclaimed by his sons with the verse, “Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one” (Deut. 6, 4), which is an expression of perfect and absolute oneness, then Jacob their father joins them, and takes possession of his house, where he abides in close association with his fathers, so that male and female become united.’ ^vayetzei-5-33

**Vayetzei 6:34** Said R. Isaac to himself: ‘I will join them and listen to what they have to say.’ He accordingly went along with them. The man then commenced to discourse on the verse: *Arise, O Lord, unto thy resting place, thoa and the ark of thy strength* (Ps. 132, 8). ‘David,’ he said, ‘when he uttered these words, was like a man saying to a king, “Let your Highness arise and proceed to his abode of rest.”’ ^vayetzei-6-34

**Vayetzei 6:35** Moses also addressed God similarly when he said: “Arise, O Lord, and let thine enemies be scattered” (Num. 10, 35). The difference between the two is this. Moses spoke like a man giving orders in his own household, and so, as it were, bade the Lord to make war against His enemies; whereas David solicited Him to retire to His place of rest, and in accordance with the rules of etiquette included in his invitation both the King and His Consort. Hence he said: “Arise, O Lord, unto thy resting place, thou and the ark of thy strength”, so as not to separate them.’ ^vayetzei-6-35

**Vayetzei 6:36** From David’s conduct on this occasion we learn that anyone who invites a king should strive to entertain him in some novel fashion, so as to afford him special pleasure. If, for instance, it is the king’s wont to be entertained by ordinary clowns and jester, he should provide for him specially refined and courtly entertainers. ^vayetzei-6-36
