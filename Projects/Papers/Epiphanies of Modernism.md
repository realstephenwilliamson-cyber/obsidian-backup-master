Epiphanies of Modernism

Taylor writes: “All this \[here he COULD mean the book up until now\] can help explain the particular form of the modernist turn to interiority. Thinkers in the early twentieth century were exercised by a problem which is still posed today: What is the place of the Good, or the True, or the Beautiful, in a world entirely determined mechanistically?” (SoS 459)

At first glance it is easy to view this chapter as Taylor’s tangential treatise on aesthetics, that has little to do with the above quote, but this would be shortsighted. It is true that Taylor is using modern art and poetry to make his point but rather than being tangential he is simply using what he thinks (and I must agree) is the clearest expression possible of epiphanic power. This being the case we must not lose sight of the point because of the pretty words. In my opinion the point is that an epiphany or rather an “epiphanic event” goes against the idea that the *telos* of human existence is merely an inwardly created activity/process/ideal that is connected to nothing outside of itself. It is the power of epiphany to completely annihilate the mechanistic determination (mentioned in the quote above) that, as Taylor has so painstakingly illustrated, rules our modern lives/minds/existences.

This is HUGE (So much so Taylor could have started with this and saved us all many headaches! Or could he have?) What he is saying in other words is that there is some such entity as Wholeness and that humans (at least “normal” ones) seem to strive towards it BUT the amount of that Wholeness (which can be achieved) directly correlates to one’s investment in “something” beyond one’s own desires. And I would add that this “something” need not be external, that it may in fact be a component of the Freudian unconscious, but I think Taylor is saying that it is external. What do you think?

As this is the case for Taylor’s use of the epiphany it is also helpful to remember Freud’s advice to novice analysts: “giving the resistance a name could not result in its immediate cessation.
