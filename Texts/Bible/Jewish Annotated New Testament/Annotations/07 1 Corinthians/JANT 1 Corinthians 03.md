# 1 Corinthians 3 (JANT notes)

Text: [[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03|NRSVue]] · [[1 Corinthians 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 Corinthians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-1|3.1–4]]:** **Mother and infants.** ^3-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-1|1]]:** *The flesh,* the lowest spiritual state with respect to the gospel. *Infants*, see [[Galatians 04#^4-3|Gal 4.3]]. *Spiritual people,* understand and fully display eschatological traits like serenity and harmony. Later Christian writers applied this contrast of flesh vs. spirit to Jews, whose rejection of Christ and interpretations of scripture were identified with their non-spiritual, carnal nature (see Augustine’s treatment of [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|1 Cor 9.19–21]], *Op. mon.* 12, and *Glossa Ordinaria* \[fifteenth century\] prologue to Jonah). ^3-1-2

> [!note] Paul and the Trinity *(p. 326)*
> Like their Jewish contemporaries, early Christians advocated diverse understandings of the nature of God within the general framework of divine oneness expressed in Tanakh. The relationship among the three “persons” of the Trinity—Father, Son, Holy Spirit—and their individual natures divided various Christian sects. Even after Christian authorities promulgated the doctrine of the Trinity at the Council of Nicea (325 CE), concerns arose over early Christian writings that seemed to understand God differently, most notably the idea that Jesus the Christ was subordinate to God the Father, an idea called subordinationism. Despite its obscurity, [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-24|1 Cor 15.24–28]] expresses an end-time theology that seems ultimately subordinationist. The passage illustrates Paul’s understanding that Christ (the Messiah) is not “truly God” (Gk *theon alēthōs*, as articulated in the Chalcedonian
>
> Creed, 451 CE), even though Christ incarnates God’s wisdom and power (1.24), imparts the Holy Spirit (6.17), and is the conduit for all existence (8.6); ultimately, “Christ belongs to God” (3.23), who is both the source of all that exists in the universe as well as its purpose. Further evidence of subordinationism in this letter is the use of “God” (*theos*) over 100 times compared with 64 occurrences of “Christ.” Paul may be emphasizing the role of the divine Father in the eschaton to address misconceptions among believers that the gospel allocates God’s power and sovereignty to Christ (see 1.12,30); perhaps he is particularly addressing those who say, “I belong to Christ,” and who claim that the manifestation of the Father’s glory, along with a sense of salvation, are seen as already experienced fully in the present. Many Christians would reject this subordinationist characterization of Paul’s theology. (See “Christology,” p. 754.)

Yoma* 75a, *Pesiq. Rav Kah.* 12; see also [[Galatians 04#^4-19|Gal 4.19]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-7|1 Thess 2.7–8]]). ^3-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-3|3]]:** The association of baser moral instincts with *the flesh* and ethical behavior with the spirit or mind appears in Jewish philosophical literature (e.g., Philo, *Leg. all.* 2.50). *Behaving*, lit., “walking around.” ^3-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-4|4]]:** *Paul . . . Apollos*, see 1.12n. ^3-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-5|3.5–9]]:** **Farmer and plants.** Ancient writers profess a divine cause for organic processes ([[Psalms 148#^148-5|Ps 148.5–6]]; *b. Nidd.* 31a; Cicero, *Nat. d.* 1.53). ^3-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-5|5]]:** *Servants* (Gk “diakonoi,” whence “deacons”), a biblical image for those who convey God’s message ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-10|Ex 4.10]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-11|Num 11.11]]; [[Jeremiah 07#^7-25|Jer 7.25]]). ^3-5-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-6|6–8]]:** Agricultural images for God’s relationship to the community (e.g., [[Isaiah 05#^5-5|Isa 5.5–7]]; [[Isaiah 32#^32-15|32.15]]; [[Isaiah 61#^61-3|61.3]]; [[Jeremiah 32#^32-41|Jer 32.41]]; Ps 1; 18.3; CD 1.7; 1QS 8.5; Philo, *On Agriculture.* 9; *Jub.* 1.16; 16.26; see also [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-36|1 Cor 15.36–41]]). ^3-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Wages,* the reward for being righteous, a common metaphor ([[Jeremiah 17#^17-10|Jer 17.10]]; LXX [[LXX Psalms 61#^61-13|Ps 61.13]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.183; 18.309; Plutarch, *Mor.* 183D). ^3-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-9|9]]:** *Servants*, Gk “synergoi,” lit., “co-workers.” *God’s field, God’s building*, images of Israel as God’s plantings are found in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Jeremiah 24#^24-6|Jer 24.6]]; [[Ezekiel 16#^16-7|Ezek 16.7]]) and also as God’s temple in Qumran literature (1QS 8.5–9; 11.8). Parables employ agricultural and architectural terms to describe God’s relationship to Israel (e.g., [[Matthew 20#^20-1|Mt 20.1–16]]; [[Matthew 07#^7-21|7.21–28]]; *m. Avot* 2.15; *Avot de R. Natan* A 24.1; *b. Ber.* 64a; *y. Ber.* 2.8). ^3-9

### 3.10–17: Builder and buildings

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-10|Text]], p. 327* ^3-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-10|10]]:** *Skilled master builder* (Gk “sophos architecton”), literally “wise architect,” a common metaphor (see, e.g., Philo, *Dreams* 2.8). ^3-10-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-13|13]]:** *The Day*, judgment day, “Day of the Lord,” a common motif in Second Temple and rabbinic literature (1 *En.* 96.2; *Sib. Or.* 3.55,741; 4Q163 frags. 6–7; 2.11; *b. Shabb*. 118a; see also [[Romans 13#^13-12|Rom 13.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|1 Cor 5.5]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-4|1 Thess 5.4]]). In prophetic texts (e.g., [[Isaiah 13#^13-6|Isa 13.6]]; [[Joel 1#^1-15|Joel 1.15]]; Am 9.11), “the Day of the Lord” (Heb “yom YHWH”), sometimes called “the day,” “that day,” or “those days,” is a technical term referring to the time of God’s judgment, sometimes of Israel, sometimes of the nations. *Fire*, not hell-fire but testing a metal’s purity by heating it—a biblical, Second Temple, and rabbinic metaphor (e.g., [[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-22|Num 31.22–23]]; [[Ezekiel 22#^22-18|Ezek 22.18–22]]; [[Malachi 3#^3-3|Mal 3.3]]; [[Psalms 012#^12-7|Ps 12.7]]; 66.10–12; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-4|Wis 3.4–6]]; *Sib. Or.* 3.85; *L. A. B.* 25.13; *T. Abr.* 13.11–14; *b. Sanh.* 93a). ^3-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-14|14]]:** *Reward*, wages (see 3.8n.). ^3-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Through fire*, see Dan 3.50; [[1 Peter 04#^4-12|1 Pet 4.12–17]]; *L. A. B.* 6.16–18. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|16–17]]:** *You*, plural. The “ekklēsia” constitutes *God’s temple* as a corporate entity, which Paul identifies with the “body of Christ” (12.27)*.* Paul uses “naos” (lit., “sanctuary”) for the *temple*, also used for the Jerusalem Temple ([[LXX 1 Kingdoms 01#^1-9|1 Sam 1.9]]\[LXX\], identified as holy throughout the Psalms, e.g., [[Psalms 005#^5-8|Ps 5.8]]\[LXX\]); [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-19|1 Cor 6.19]] uses “temple” (without the definite article) to refer to each member of the community. The Qumran community referred to itself as a sanctuary (Heb “beit qodesh”; 1QS 8.5–9). This spiritualization of the Jerusalem Temple paved the way for anti-Jewish supersessionist interpretations of its destruction in 70 CE as punishment for rejecting Christ (*Barn.* 16; Justin, *Dial.* 22; Tertullian, *Marc.* 33; Athanasius, *Inc.* 6). ^3-16

### 3.18–23: Reversal of standards

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-18|Text]], p. 327* ^3-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-19|19]]:** [[Job 05#^5-12|Job 5.12–13]]. ^3-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-20|20]]:** [[Psalms 094#^94-11|Ps 94.11]]. Paul modifies the biblical verses by compressing the two verses from Job and substituting “wise” for “human beings” in the Psalm to fit his rhetorical purposes. See also 1.19n. Juxtaposing biblical passages is a common rabbinic practice. ^3-20

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-21|21]]:** *Boast*, see 1.29n. ^3-21

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-22|22]]:** see 1.12n. *All belong to you*, lit., “all are yours,” a Stoic slogan. ^3-22

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Belongs to*, is subject to (See “Paul and the Trinity,” p. 326; and 15.28). ^3-23
