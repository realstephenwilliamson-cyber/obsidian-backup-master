CHECKING FOOTNOTES AGAINST THE PRINTED PAGE (28 Oct 2026). Reading task; you change nothing but your own output file.

Each case is a pair of files with the same id: vNN.jpg is one page of a printed book (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 1888, as
reset by CCEL), and vNN.txt is the Markdown text an Obsidian note holds for that page. In the text, a footnote CALL in the body is
written <sup>N</sup>; the FOOTNOTES themselves are written as plain paragraphs near the end, "N text", several often run together
on one line ("2799 Matt. xix. 24. 2800 Matt. xix. 25."). Scripture references may be wrapped as links: [[Matthew 19#^19-24|Matt. xix. 24]]
reads as "Matt. xix. 24". Square brackets may be escaped as \[ and \]. A bare number before the footnotes (e.g. "368") may be the
1888 edition's page number: ignore it. The text may start with "…" (cut for length).

For each case, compare the footnotes printed at the foot of the page image with the footnote paragraphs in the text:
- every footnote printed on the page should be in the text, with its own number directly before its own text;
- no number should sit before the wrong note's text, and no footnote text should lack its number;
- ignore italics, line breaks, spacing, link markup, and small OCR differences in Greek accents or letters.

Output: a JSON Lines file at the path given in your task, one line per case, in id order:
{"id": "v03", "verdict": "ok", "problems": ""}
or {"id": "v03", "verdict": "problem", "problems": "footnote 2805 'Ps. li.' is on the page but its number is missing in the text"}
Verdicts: ok / problem / unclear (say why). Check that there is one line per case and each parses as JSON.
Reply with one short line of counts (ok, problem, unclear). Do not paste the cases into the reply.
