When the Absolute (A) has assumed the form of a couple in union (*saṁghaṭṭa*) by virtue of its (two-fold) state as (both) the object of awareness and the subject who reflects upon it, (it becomes) an object of enjoyment (that is, Bliss, Ā). The same is the case with the will (I) and the other (vowels). (188) (188cd-189ab) अनुत्तरानन्दभुवामिच्छाद्ये भोग्यतां गते ॥ १८९ ॥
संध्यक्षराणामुदयो भोक्तृरूपं च कथ्यते । 

anuttarānandabhuvāmicchādye bhogyatāṁ gate || 189 ||
saṁdhyakṣarāṇāmudayo bhoktṛrūpaṁ ca kathyate | 

The emergence of the diphthongs, which is said to be the enjoyer, takes place when the will and the rest become objects of enjoyment of the states of the Absolute (A) and Bliss (Ā). (189) (189cd-190ab) 

अनुत्तरानन्दमयो देवो भोक्तैव कथ्यते ॥ १९० ॥
इच्छादिकं भोग्यमेव तत एवास्य शक्तिता । 

anuttarānandamayo devo bhoktaiva kathyate || 190 ||
icchādikaṁ bhogyameva tata evāsya śaktitā | 

It is said that the enjoyer himself is (in the highest sense) God Whose nature is the Absolute (A) and Bliss (Ā). The will and the rest are just the object of enjoyment and so (for that very reason is) His empowered state (*“saktitaa*) (also). (190) (190cd-191ab) 

भोग्यं भोक्तरि लीनं चेद् भोक्ता तद्वस्तुतः स्फुटः ॥ १९१ ॥
अतः षण्णां त्रिकं सारं चिदिष्युन्मेषणात्मकम् । 

bhogyaṁ bhoktari līnaṁ ced bhoktā tadvastutaḥ sphuṭaḥ || 191 ||
ataḥ ṣaṇṇāṁ trikaṁ sāraṁ cidiṣyunmeṣaṇātmakam | 

If the object of enjoyment merges into the enjoyer, (as it must in order to be experienced and so is not independent of him), it is in reality (nothing but) the enjoyer which, clearly evident, (alone remains). Thus, the essence of the six (vowels) is the triad (*trika*) consisting of consciousness (A), desire (*i.si*) (I) and expansion (U). (191) (191cd-192ab) 

तदेव त्रितयं प्राहुर्भैरवस्य परं महः ॥ १९२ ॥
तत्त्रिकं परमेशस्य पूर्णा शक्तिः प्रगीयते । 

tadeva tritayaṁ prāhurbhairavasya paraṁ mahaḥ || 192 ||
tat trikaṁ parameśasya pūrṇā śaktiḥ pragīyate | 

(The wise) say that that same group of three is Bhairava’s supreme glory. That Triad (Trika) is proclaimed to be the Supreme Lord’s power in all its fullness. (192) (192cd-193ab) 

*[p. 42]* ^pg42

तेनाक्षिप्तं यतो विश्वमतोऽस्मिन्समुपासिते ॥ १९३ ॥
विश्वशक्ताववच्छेदवन्ध्ये जातमुपासनम् । 

tenākṣiptaṁ yato viśvamato ‘sminsamupāsite || 193 ||
viśvaśaktāvavacchedavandhye jātamupāsanam | 

When this is properly worshipped, encompassing as it does the entire universe, He who is the possessor of all powers and free of limitation, is (truly) worshipped. (193) (193cd-194ab) 

इत्येष महिमैतावानिति तावन्न शक्यते ॥ १९४ ॥
ityeṣa mahimaitāvāniti tāvanna śakyate || 194 || 

(In fact) it is not possible (to say) that (even) this is the full extent of His glory for who can delimit the powers of the (Lord) whose power is unlimited? (194) (194cd) 

तस्मादनुत्तरो देवः स्वाच्छन्द्यानुत्तरत्वतः ॥ १९५ ॥
विसर्गशक्तियुक्तत्वात्सम्पन्नो विश्वरूपकः । 

tasmādanuttaro devaḥ svācchandyānuttaratvataḥ || 195 ||
visargaśaktiyuktatvāt sampanno viśvarūpakaḥ | 

Therefore, God Who is the Absolute (*anuttaradeva*), by virtue of His absolute freedom and because he is united with the power of emission, has a cosmic form (*viśvarūpaka*) (and is all things). (195) (195cd-196a) 

एवं पञ्चाशदामर्शपूर्णशक्तिर्महेश्वरः ॥ १९६ ॥
विमर्शात्मैक एवान्याः शक्तयोऽत्रैव निष्ठिताः । 

evaṁ pañcāśadāmarśapūrṇaśaktirmaheśvaraḥ || 196 ||
vimarśātmaika evānyāḥ śaktayo ‘traiva niṣṭhitāḥ | 

In this way, the Great Lord Whose power is full because of (these) fifty (forms of) reflective awareness is only one and His nature is reflective awareness. (All) other powers are included here (within them). (196) (196cd-197ab) एकाशीतिपदा देवी ह्यत्रान्तर्भावयिष्यते ॥ १९७ ॥
ekāśītipadā devī hyatrāntarbhāvayiṣyate || 197 || 

(Thus,) the goddess consisting of eighty-one parts (*pada*) will be included here (in the fifty letters). (197ab) (197cd) 

एकामर्शस्वभावत्वे शब्दराशिः स भैरवः ।
आमृश्यच्छायया योगात्सैव शक्तिश्च मातृका ॥ १९८ ॥
सा शब्दराशिसङ्घट्टाद् भिन्नयोनिस्तु मालिनी ।
प्राग्वन्नवतयामर्शात्पृथग्वर्गस्वरूपिणी ॥ १९९ ॥
एकैकामर्शरूढौ तु सैव पञ्चाशदात्मिका । 

*[p. 43]* ^pg43

ekāmarśasvabhāvatve śabdarāśiḥ sa bhairavaḥ |
āmṛśyacchāyayā yogāt saiva śaktiśca mātṛkā || 198 ||
sā śabdarāśisaṅghaṭṭād bhinnayonistu mālinī |
prāgvannavatayāmarśāt pṛthagvargasvarūpiṇī || 199 ||
ekaikāmarśarūḍhau tu saiva pañcāśadātmikā | 

Bhairava Who is the Assembly of Sounds (*“sabdarāśi*) (abides) within (the ‘I’ consciousness which is) the state of the essential nature (*svabhaavatva*) of the one reflective awareness (of pure ‘I’ consciousness). And (His) power, that is, Mātṛkā, is (the state which arises) by (His) association with (just) the shade (that is, a subtle trace) of the object of awareness. This (power) becomes Mālinī in which the (seed vowels) are mixed in with the matrixes (of the consonants) by coupling with the Assembly of Sounds. (Then again) she (assumes) the nature (of the nine) individual classes of letters by the application, as before, of a nine-fold act of reflective awareness. When a reflective awareness of each individual (letter finally) affirms itself, this same (power assumes) the nature of the fifty (forms of reflective awareness). (197cd-199) (198-200ab) 

इत्थं नादानुवेधेन परामर्शस्वभावकः ॥ २०० ॥
शिवो मातापितृत्वेन कर्ता विश्वत्र संस्थितः । 

itthaṁ nādānuvedhena parāmarśasvabhāvakaḥ || 200 ||
śivo mātāpitṛtvena kartā viśvatra saṁsthitaḥ | 

Śiva’s nature, pervaded (*anuvedhena*) in this way by (the divine) Sound (*nāda*) (of consciousness), is reflective awareness. As both the Mother and Father, He is the (one universal) agent present here everywhere. (200) (200cd-201ab) विसर्ग एव शाक्तोऽयं शिवबिन्दुतया पुनः ॥ २०१ ॥
गर्भीकृतानन्तविश्वः श्रयतेऽनुत्तरात्मताम् । 

visarga eva śākto ‘yaṁ śivabindutayā punaḥ || 201 ||
garbhīkṛtānantaviśvaḥ śrayate ‘nuttarātmatām | 

When, as Śiva’s Point (*śivabindu*), this, the emission of power (*śāktavisarga*) has again encompassed within itself the infinite universe, it assumes the nature of the Absolute. (201) (201cd-202ab) 

अपरिच्छिन्नविश्वान्तःसारे स्वात्मनि यः प्रभोः ॥ २०२ ॥
परामर्शः स एवोक्तो द्वयसंपत्तिलक्षणः । 

aparicchinnaviśvāntaḥsāre svātmani yaḥ prabhoḥ || 202 ||
parāmarśaḥ sa evokto dvayasaṁpattilakṣaṇaḥ | 

The characterizing feature of the reflective awareness of the Lord within His own nature, which is the inner essence of the undifferentiated universe (*viśva*), is said to be the apprehension of duality (*dvayasaṁpatti*). (202) (202cd-203ab) 

*[p. 44]* ^pg44

अनुत्तरविसर्गात्मशिवशक्त्यद्वयात्मनि ॥ २०३ ॥
परामर्शो निर्भरत्वादहमित्युच्यते विभोः । 

anuttaravisargātmaśivaśaktyadvayātmani || 203 ||
parāmarśo nirbharatvādahamityucyate vibhoḥ | 

The reflective awareness inherent in the non-dual nature of Śiva and (His) power, that is, the Absolute and emission is, by virtue of (its) overflowing plenitude (*nirbhara*), said to be the ‘I’ of the All-pervasive Lord. (203) (203cd-204ab) अनुत्तराद्या प्रसृतिर्हान्ता शक्तिस्वरूपिणी ॥ २०४ ॥
प्रत्याहृताशेषविश्वानुत्तरे सा निलीयते । 

anuttarādyā prasṛtiṁāntā śaktisvarūpiṇī || 204 ||
pratyāhṛtāśeṣaviśvānuttare sā nilīyate | 

The flow (of emanation) (*prasṛti*) that begins with the Absolute (A*)* and ends with H is (the Lord’s) power (*śakti*) which, once it has encompassed the entire universe (and withdrawn it back into itself), merges into the Absolute. (204) (204cd-205ab) तदिदं विश्वमन्तःस्थं शक्तौ सानुत्तरे परे ॥ २०५ ॥
तत्तस्यामिति यत्सत्यं विभुना सम्पुटीकृतिः ।
तेन श्रत्रीशिकाशासखरे शक्तेः सम्पुटिताकृति || २०६ ॥ 

tadidaṁ viśvamantaḥsthaṁ śaktau sānuttare pare || 205 ||
tat tasyāmiti yat satyaṁ vibhunā sampuṭīkṛtiḥ |
tena śrītrīśikāśāstre śakteḥ sampuṭitākṛtiḥ || 206 || 

This universe (rests) within power (*śakti*) and that within the Absolute which is supreme and that (again) within that (power). Thus verily (in this way) the All-pervasive Lord encapsulates (*saṁpuṭiikṛti*) (the universe of power). (Thus the Lord has taught) the encapsulated form of power in the venerable *Trīśikā* scripture. (205- 206ab) (205cd-206ab) (205cd-206) 

संवित्तौ भाति यद्विश्वं तत्रापि खलु संविदा ।
तदेतत्त्रितयं द्वन्द्वयोगात्सङ्घाततां गतम् ॥ २०७ ॥
एकमेव परं रूपं भैरवस्याहमात्मकम् । 

saṁvittau bhāti yad viśvaṁ tatrāpi khalu saṁvidā |
tadetat tritayaṁ dvandvayogāt saṅghātatāṁ gatam || 207 ||
ekameva paraṁ rūpaṁ bhairavasyāhamātmakam | 

The universe manifests within (cognitive) consciousness (*sa.mvitti*) and, indeed, as (pure) consciousness (*sa.mvidaa*), (manifests) there also. These three, merged (into each other) by uniting in pairs, are Bhairava’s one, supreme nature, which is (pure) ‘I’ (consciousness). (206cd-207) (207-208ab) 

*[p. 45]* ^pg45

विसर्गशक्तिर्या शम्भोः सेत्थं सर्वत्र वर्तते ॥ २०८ ॥
तत एव समस्तोऽयमानन्दरसविभ्रमः । 

visargaśaktiryā śambhoḥ setthaṁ sarvatra vartate || 208 ||
tata eva samasto ‘yamānandarasavibhramaḥ | 

This, Śiva’s power of emission, is present everywhere in this way. All this play of the aesthetic savour of bliss (*ānandarasa*) (comes) from that. (208) (208cd-209ab) तथाहि मधुरे गीते स्पर्शे वा चन्दनादिके ॥ २०९ ॥
माध्यस्थ्यविगमे यासौ हृदये स्पन्दमानता ।
आनन्दशक्तिः सैवोक्ता यतः सहृदयो जनः ॥ २१० ॥ 

tathāhi madhure gīte sparśe vā candanādike || 209 ||
mādhyasthyavigame yāsau hṛdaye spandamānatā |
ānandaśaktiḥ saivoktā yataḥ sahṛdayo janaḥ || 210 || 

The vibration in the Heart (that one experiences) while (hearing) a sweet song, (feeling a gentle) caress or (smelling fragrant) incense and the like, when the state of indifference (*mādhyasthya*) (towards them) ceases, is said to be the power of bliss due to which a person is sensitive to beauty (*sah.rdaya*). (209-210ab) (209-210) 

The Three Forms of Emission पर्व विसृज्यसकलटं कर्तव्यं शुन्यतानरे ।
चित्तविश्रान्तिसंज्ञोऽयमाणवस्तदनन्तरम् ॥ २११ ॥
दृष्टश्रुतादितद्वस्तुप्रोन्मुखत्वं स्वसंविदि ।
चित्तसंबोधनामोक्तः शाक्तोल्लासभरात्मकः ॥ २१२ ॥
तत्रोन्मुखत्वतद्वस्तुसङ्घट्टाद्वस्तुनो हृदि ।
रूढेः पूर्णतयावेशान्मितचित्तलयाच्छिवे ॥ २१३ ॥
प्राग्वद्भविष्यदौन्मुख्यसंभाव्यमिततालयात् ।
चित्तप्रलयनामासौ विसर्गः शाम्भवः परः ॥ २१४ ॥ 

pūrvaṁ visṛjya sakalaṁ kartavyaṁ śūnyatānale |
cittaviśrāntisaṁjño ‘yamāṇavastadanantaram || 211 ||
dṛṣṭaśrutāditadvastupronmukhatvaṁ svasaṁvidi |
cittasaṁbodhanāmoktaḥ śāktollāsabharātmakaḥ || 212 ||
tatronmukhatvatadvastusaṅghaṭṭād vastuno hṛdi |
rūḍheḥ pūrṇatayāveśānmitacittalayācchive || 213 ||
prāgvadbhaviṣyadaunmukhyasaṁbhāvyamitatālayāt |
cittapralayanāmāsau visargaḥ śāmbhavaḥ paraḥ || 214 || 

1\) Initially (the form of emission) is that of the individual soul (*āṇavavisarga*) and is called the ‘Repose of the Mind’ (*cittaviśrānti*) (within duality). (It takes place) once (consciousness) has emitted its entire (*sakala*) product into the Fire (that consumes) (the transcendental) Voidness (of consciousness). 

2\) After that (the second, empowered emission - *śāktavisarga* is) the overflowing plenitude (*bhara*) of the exuberance of empowered (consciousness which 

*[p. 46]* ^pg46

is experienced) when things that are heard or seen etc. are propense (*pronmukha*) (to merge) into one’s own consciousness. It is called the ‘Awakening of the Mind’ (*cittasaṁbodha*). 

3\) The supreme emission, which is that of Śiva (*śāmbhavavisarga*), is called the ‘Dissolving Away of the Mind’ (*cittapralaya*). It comes about by (A) the union (*saṁghaṭṭa*) of (outer) reality (*vastu*) (with consciousness) by its intent there (towards consciousness) that takes place by (B), (its) establishment in the Heart (of pure self-reflective consciousness) brought about by complete (*pūrṇatayā*) penetration (*āveśa*) (into “Siva consciousness). (Thus this emission takes place by) (C) the merger of the limited mind into Śiva (that this union with consciousness brings about), along with (D) the dissolving away of all possible limitation that could be brought about by some future intent (towards contracted states of consciousness) as was the case before (this state of emission is attained). (210cd-214ab) (211-214) 

तत्त्वरक्षाविधानेऽतो विसर्गत्रैधमुच्यते ।
हृत्पद्मकोशमध्यस्थस्तयोः सङ्घट्ट इष्यते ॥ २१५ ॥
विसर्गोऽन्तः स च प्रोक्तश्चित्तविश्रान्तिलक्षणः ।
द्वितीयः स विसर्गस्तु चित्तसंबोधलक्षणः ॥ २१६ ॥
एकीभूतं विभात्यत्र जगदेतच्चराचरम् ।
ग्राह्यग्राहकभेदो वै किञ्चिदत्रेष्यते यदा ॥ २१७ ॥
तदासौ सकलः प्रोक्तो निष्कलः शिवयोगतः ।
ग्राह्यग्राहकविच्छित्तिसम्पूर्णग्रहणात्मकः ॥ २१८ ॥
तृतीयः स विसर्गस्तु चित्तप्रलयलक्षणः । एकीभावात्मकः सूक्ष्मो विज्ञानात्मात्मनिर्वृतः ॥ २१९ ॥ 

tattvarakṣāvidhāne ‘to visargatraidhamucyate |
hṛtpadmakośamadhyasthastayoḥ saṅghaṭṭa iṣyate || 215 ||
visargo ‘ntaḥ sa ca proktaścittaviśrāntilakṣaṇaḥ |
dvitīyaḥ sa visargastu cittasaṁbodhalakṣaṇaḥ || 216 ||
ekībhūtaṁ vibhātyatra jagadetaccarācaram |
grāhyagrāhakabhedo vai kiñcidatreṣyate yadā || 217 ||
tadāsau sakalaḥ prokto niṣkalaḥ śivayogataḥ |
grāhyagrāhakavicchittisampūrṇagrahaṇātmakaḥ || 218 ||
tṛtīyaḥ sa visargastu cittapralayalakṣaṇaḥ |
ekībhāvātmakaḥ sūkṣmo vijñānātmātmanirvṛtaḥ || 219 || 

“It is said that the union (*saṁghaṭṭa*) of the two (namely, mind and thought) takes place in the centre of the calix of the lotus of the Heart. That is said to be the inner emission, and its sign (*lakṣaṇa*) is the repose of the mind. The sign of the second emission is the awakening of the mind. This entire universe, mobile (living) and immobile (insentient), shines here as one (*ekībhūta*). (This emission is of two types). When there is a subtle division here between subject and object, it is said to be (immanent and so is) ‘with aspects’ (*sakala*). (The second type) is united with (transcendental) Śiva and so is ‘without aspects’ (*niṣkala*). The sign of the third emission is the dissolving away of the mind. It is full (and perfect) cognitive consciousness (*saṁpūrṇagrahaṇa*) that corresponds to the cessation (*vicchitti*) (of the distinction between) subject (*grāhaka*) and object (*grāhya*). In a state of oneness (*ekībhāva*), it is subtle, complete knowledge (*vijñāna*), satisfied in its own nature. (214cd-219ab) (215-219) 

*[p. 47]* ^pg47

निरूपितोऽयमर्थः श्रीसिद्धयोगीश्वरीमते ।
सात्र कुण्डलिनी बीजं जीवभूता चिदात्मिका ॥ २२० ॥
तज्जं ध्रुवेच्छोन्मेषाख्यं त्रिकं वर्णास्ततः पुनः । 

nirūpito ‘yamarthaḥ śrīsiddhayogīśvarīmate |
sātra kuṇḍalinī bījaṁ jīvabhūtā cidātmikā || 220 ||
tajjaṁ dhruvecchonmeṣākhyaṁ trikaṁ varṇāstataḥ punaḥ | 

The same reality (*artha*) has been described in the venerable *Siddhayogiiśvarīmata* (where we read that) the seed here (of all things) is Kuṇḍalinī. (She is) life and her nature is consciousness. The triad (*trika*) called the Stable One (A), Will (I) and Expansion (U) is born from that and then (from this triad all the other) letters. (219cd-220) (220-221ab) 

आ इत्यवर्णादित्यादियावद्वैसर्गिकी कला ॥ २२१ ॥
ककारादिसकारान्ता विसर्गात्पञ्चधा स च ।
बहिश्चान्तश्च हृदये नादेऽथ परमे पदे ॥ २२२ ॥
बिन्दुरात्मनि मूर्धान्तं हृदयाद्व्यापको हि सः । 

ā ityavarṇādityādiyāvadvaisargikī kalā || 221 ||
kakārādisakārāntā visargātpañcadhā sa ca |
bahiścāntaśca hṛdaye nāde ‘tha parame pade || 222 ||
bindurātmani mūrdhāntaṁ hṛdayādvyāpako hi saḥ | 

Ā arises from the letter A and so on up to the power of emission. (The consonants) from K to S are born of emission and this, (as) the Point (*bindu*), has five aspects, namely, 1) (as the Point itself then) 2) externally within the Heart (as Nara - the individual soul) and 3) internally (within the Heart as the state of rest). (Then) 4) (externally within) Sound (as Śakti) and 5) (internally) within the Supreme Abode (that is, within the End of the Twelve as Śiva). (Residing within them the Point) pervades (the body) from the heart to the extremity of the head (*muurdhaanta*). (221-222) (221cd-223ab) 

आदिमान्त्यविहीनास्तु मन्त्राः स्युः शरदभ्रवत् ॥ २२३ ॥
ādimāntyavihīnāstu mantrāḥ syuḥ śaradabhravat || 223 || 

Mantras devoid of the first (letter) (A) and the last (H) would indeed be (barren) like autumn clouds. (223ab) (223cd) 

गुरोर्लक्षणमेतावदादिमान्त्यं च वेदयेत् ।
पूज्यः सोऽहमिव ज्ञानी भैरवो देवतात्मकः ॥ २२४ ॥ 

gurorlakṣaṇametāvadādimāntyaṁ ca vedayet |
pūjyaḥ so ‘hamiva jñānī bhairavo devatātmakaḥ || 224 || 

The characteristic of a teacher is just this, namely, that he should know the first and last (letters of the alphabet — A and H). He who knows this is as worthy of devotion as I am (*so’ham iva*). He is Bhairava Himself, the deity. (223cd-224ab) (224) 

*[p. 48]* ^pg48

श्लोकगाथादि यत्किंचिदादिमान्त्ययुतं ततः ।
तस्माद्विदंस्तथा सर्वं मन्त्रत्वेनैव पश्यति ॥ २२५ ॥ 

ślokagāthādi yatkiṁcidādimāntyayutaṁ tataḥ |
tasmādvidaṁstathā sarvaṁ mantratvenaiva paśyati || 225 || 

Therefore, everything, whatever verse or prose (he experiences) united with the first and last (letter) in this way, he sees as Mantra. (224cd-225ab) (225) विसर्गशक्तिर्विश्वस्य कारणं च निरूपिता ।
एतरेयाख्यवेदान्ते परमेशेन विस्तरात् ॥ २२६ ॥ 

visargaśaktirviśvasya kāraṇaṁ ca nirūpitā |
etareyākhyavedānte parameśena vistarāt || 226 || 

The Supreme Lord has declared at length in the *Aara.nyaka* (*vedānta*) called *Aitareya* that the power of emission is the cause of the universe. (225cd-226ab) (226) यल्लोहितं तदग्निर्यद्वीर्यं सूर्येन्दुविग्रहम् ।
अ इति ब्रह्म परमं तत्सङ्घट्टोदयात्मकम् ॥ २२७ ॥ 

yallohitaṁ tadagniryad vīryaṁ sūryenduvigraham |
a iti brahma paramaṁ tatsaṅghaṭṭodayātmakam || 227 || 

The red (flow of menses) is Fire, while (its) vitality is in the form of the Sun and Moon. The Supreme Brahman is (the emission) ‘AḤ’ that arises from their union. (226cd-227ab) (227) 

तस्यापि च परं वीर्य पञ्चभूतकलात्मकम् ।
भोग्यत्वेनान्नरूपं च शब्दस्पर्शरसात्मकम् ॥ २२८ ॥ 

tasyāpi ca paraṁ vīrya pañcabhūtakalātmakam |
bhogyatvenānnarūpaṁ ca śabdasparśarasātmakam || 228 || 

His supreme vitality is the energy (*kalā*) of the five gross elements. As the object of enjoyment, it is food which consists (of the sensations of) sound, touch, taste (and the rest). (227cd-228ab) (228) 

शब्दोऽपि मधुरो यस्माद्वीर्योपचयकारकः ।
तद्धि वीर्यं परं शुद्धं विसिसृक्षात्मकं मतम् ॥ २२९ ॥ 

śabdo ‘pi madhuro yasmād vīryopacayakārakaḥ |
taddhi vīryaṁ paraṁ śuddhaṁ visisṛkṣātmakaṁ matam || 229 || 

Even a sweet sound intensifies the vitality (*vīrya*) (of consciousness) and so it is that that very vitality is considered to be pure and supreme and its nature the desire to emit (*visisṛkṣā*). (228cd-229ab) (229) 

*[p. 49]* ^pg49

तद्बलं च तदोजश्च ते प्राणाः सा च कान्तता ।
tad balaṁ ca tadojaśca te prāṇāḥ sā ca kāntatā | 

That (vitality) is strength (*bala*), vigour (*ojas*), the vital breaths and beauty (*kantatā*). (229cd) (230ab) 

तस्माद्वीर्यात्प्रजास्ताश्च वीर्य कर्मसु कथ्यते ॥ २३० ॥
यज्ञादिकेषु तद्वृष्टौ सौषधीष्वथ ताः पुनः ।
वीर्ये तच्च प्रजास्वेवं विसर्गे विश्वरूपता ॥ २३१ ॥ 

tasmād vīryāt prajāstāśca vīrya karmasu kathyate || 230 ||
yajñādikeṣu tadvṛṣṭau sauṣadhīṣvatha tāḥ punaḥ |
vīrye tacca prajāsvevaṁ visarge viśvarūpatā || 231 || 

From that vitality are born the creatures and these are said to be the vitality (and vigour) in (all) rites including (Vedic) sacrifices and the rest, these of rain, and this of the herbs and these again of the vitality (of the male and female seeds) and this of the creatures. In this way, emission is of the nature of all things. (230-231ab) (230cd-231) शब्दराशिः स एवोक्तो मातृका सा च कीर्तिता ।
क्षोभ्यक्षोभकतावेशान्मालिनीं तां प्रचक्षते ॥ २३२ ॥ 

śabdarāśiḥ sa evokto mātṛkā sā ca kīrtitā |
kṣobhyakṣobhakatāveśānmālinīṁ tāṁ pracakṣate || 232 || 

This same (emission) is called the Assembly of Sounds (Śabdarāśi) and also Mātṛkā and by the penetration (*āveśa*) (of the consonants which are the) objects of arousal and (the vowels) that arouse them, it is said to be Mālinī. (231cd-232ab) (232) बीजयोनिसमापत्तिविसर्गोदयसुन्दरा ।
मालिनी हि परा शक्तिर्निर्णीता विश्वरूपिणी ॥ २३३ ॥ 

bījayonisamāpattivisargodayasundarā |
mālinī hi parā śaktirnirṇītā viśvarūpiṇī || 233 || 

Mālinī is (the goddess Paraa) the supreme power, beautiful with the emergence of the emission (brought about) by the (mutual) contemplation (*samaapatti*) of the seed (vowels) and the matrix (consonants), is said to be all things (*viśvarūpiṇī*). (232cd-233ab) (233) 

The Levels of Speech एषा वस्तुत एकैव परा कालस्य कर्षिणी ।
शक्तिमद्भेदयोगेन यामलत्वं प्रपद्यते ॥ २३४ ॥ 

eṣā vastuta ekaiva parā kālasya karṣiṇī |
śaktimadbhedayogena yāmalatvaṁ prapadyate || 234 || 

*[p. 50]* ^pg50

This (power), which is in reality the only one and supreme (*paraa*) (goddess Paraa) is (Kālī), she who attracts (and absorbs) time. By union (*yoga*) with the power-holder aspect (of absolute consciousness) (She) assumes the state of the (united) couple (*yāmala*) (“Siva and “Sakti). (233cd-234ab) (234) 

तस्य प्रत्यवमर्शो यः परिपूर्णोऽहमात्मकः ।
स स्वात्मनि स्वतन्त्रत्वाद्विभागमवभासयेत् ॥ २३५ ॥ 

tasya pratyavamarśo yaḥ paripūrṇo ‘hamātmakaḥ |
sa svātmani svatantratvādvibhāgamavabhāsayet || 235 || 

The reflective awareness of this (couple) is perfectly full ‘I’ (consciousness) which, by virtue of its freedom, makes division (*vibhāga*) manifest within its own nature. (234cd-235ab) (235) 

विभागाभासने चास्य त्रिधा वपुरुदाहृतम् ।
पश्यन्ती मध्यमा स्थूला वैखरीत्यभिशब्दितम् ॥ २३६ ॥ 

vibhāgābhāsane cāsya tridhā vapurudāhṛtam |
paśyantī madhyamā sthūlā vaikharītyabhiśabditam || 236 || 

When division manifests, its form is said to be of three kinds, namely, (the Speech of) Vision (Paśyantī), the Middle One (Madhyamā) and gross, Corporeal Speech (Vaikharī). (235cd-236ab) (236) 

The Speech of Vision (Paśyantī) 

तासामपि त्रिधा रूपं स्थूलसूक्ष्मपरत्वतः ।
तत्र या स्वरसन्दर्भसुभगा नादरूपिणी ॥ २३७ ॥
सा स्थूला खलु पश्यन्ती वर्णाद्यप्रविभागतः । 

tāsāmapi tridhā rūpaṁ sthūlasūkṣmaparatvataḥ |
tatra yā svarasandarbhasubhagā nādarūpiṇī || 237 ||
sā sthūlā khalu paśyantī varṇādyapravibhāgataḥ | 

Again each of these has three forms as gross, subtle and supreme. The gross form of the Speech of Vision (Paśyantī) is, in that context, that of (musical) sound composed of a pleasing arrangement of notes (*svara*) (which although sung) are not differentiated into distinct letters (or words). (236cd-237) (237-238ab) 

अविभागैकरूपत्वं माधुर्यं शक्तिरुच्यते ॥ २३८ ॥
स्थानवाय्वादिघर्षोत्था स्फुटतैव च पारुषी । 

avibhāgaikarūpatvaṁ mādhuryaṁ śaktirucyate || 238 ||
sthānavāyvādigharṣotthā sphuṭataiva ca pāruṣī | 

The (delightful) sweetness (*mādhurya*) (of music) which consists of (the harmoniousness of) its undifferentiated unity is called (its) ‘power’ (*śakti*). The fully evident (articulation of words) produced by the impact of the breath on the locus (of articulation) is (by comparison) harsh. (238) (238cd-239ab) 

*[p. 51]* ^pg51

तदस्यां नादरूपायां संवित्सविधवृत्तितः ॥ २३९ ॥
साजात्यान्तर्म(त्तन्म-)यीभूतिर्झगित्येवोपलभ्यते । 

tadasyāṁ nādarūpāyāṁ saṁvitsavidhavṛttitaḥ || 239 ||
sājātyāntarma (ttanma-)yībhūtirjhagityevopalabhyate | 

He who is (immersed) in this (musical) sound, (close to) consciousness and similar to it, experiences in an instant a state of oneness with it (*tanmayībhūti*) by virtue of the character it shares in common (with consciousness) (*sājātya*). (239) (239cd-240ab) 

येषां न तन्मयीभूतिस्ते देहादिनिमज्जनम् ॥ २४० ॥
अविदन्तो मग्नसंविन्मानास्त्वहृदया इति । 

yeṣāṁ na tanmayībhūtiste dehādinimajjanam || 240 ||
avidanto magnasaṁvinmānāstvahṛdayā iti | 

Those who do not experience (this) state of oneness (with the beautiful object) and the submergence of (their identification with the) body etc. (into universal consciousness), (whereas, on the contrary, their) consciousness is submerged are considered to be insensitive to beauty (*ahṛdaya*). (240) (240cd-241ab) 

यत्तु चर्मावनद्धादि किञ्चित्तत्रैष यो ध्वनिः ॥ २४१ ॥
स स्फुटास्फुटरूपत्वान्मध्यमा स्थूलरूपिणी । 

yat tu carmāvanaddhādi kiñcit tatraiṣa yo dhvaniḥ || 241 ||
sa sphuṭāsphuṭarūpatvānmadhyamā sthūlarūpiṇī | 

The sound that comes from an instrument (such as drums) made of stretched skin is both indistinct (insofar as it cannot produce words set to music) and also at the same time distinct and so corresponds to the gross form of the Middle Speech. (241) (241cd-242ab) 

मध्यायाश्चाविभागांशसद्भाव इति रक्तता ॥ २४२ ॥
अविभागस्वरमयी यत्र स्यात्तत्सुरञ्जकम् । 

madhyāyāścāvibhāgāṁśasadbhāva iti raktatā || 242 ||
avibhāgasvaramayī yatra syāt tatsurañjakam | 

The attractive quality (*raktatā*) of the Middle (form of Speech) essentially stems from the aspect which is undivided (into articulated speech). (A sound) which consists of (a single, continuous) undivided note is very pleasing. (242) (242cd-243ab) अविभागो हि निर्वृत्यै दृश्यतां तालपाठतः ॥ २४३ ॥
किलाव्यक्तध्वनौ तस्मिन्वादने परितुष्यति । 

avibhāgo hi nirvṛtyai dṛśyatāṁ tālapāṭhataḥ || 243 ||
kilāvyaktadhvanau tasminvādane parituṣyati | 

*[p. 52]* ^pg52

The absence of division (in this sense) is pleasing; witness (for example,) the effect of playing a regularly recurrent rhythm (*tālapāṭha*). Indeed, (everybody) is completely satisfied by (a musical) sound (*dhvani*) (which is just one note and hence is, one could say) unmanifest (music). (243) (243cd-244ab) 

या तु स्फुटानां वर्णानामुत्पत्तौ कारणं भवेत् ॥ २४४ ॥
सा स्थूला वैखरी यस्याः कार्यं वाक्यादि भूयसा । 

yā tu sphuṭānāṁ varṇānāmutpattau kāraṇaṁ bhavet || 244 ||
sā sthūlā vaikharī yasyāḥ kāryaṁ vākyādi bhūyasā | 

Gross Corporeal Speech (Vaikharī) is the cause that generates clearly (articulated) phonemes and its products are the many words and the like. (244) (244cd-245ab) 

अस्मिन्स्थूलत्रये यत्तदनुसन्धानमादिवत् ॥ २४५ ॥
पृथक्पृथक्तत्त्रितयं सूक्ष्ममित्यभिशब्द्यते । 

asmin sthūlatraye yat tadanusandhānamādivat || 245 ||
pṛthak pṛthak tat tritayaṁ sūkṣmamityabhiśabdyate | 

Each of these three, in their gross aspect, is preceded by a specific (well determined) intention (*anusandhāna*) (to produce a particular sound). These three (forms of intention) are called the subtle (form of the three levels of Speech). (245) (245cd-256ab) 

षड्जं करोमि मधुरं वादयामि ब्रुवे वचः ॥ २४६ ॥
ṣaḍjaṁ karomi madhuraṁ vādayāmi bruve vacaḥ || 246 || 

‘I produce (the note) Saa’, ‘I play (the drum) sweetly’ and ‘I speak’. (246ab) (246cd) पृथगेवानुसन्धानत्रयं संवेद्यते किल ।
pṛthagevānusandhānatrayaṁ saṁvedyate kila | 

Indeed, one can (clearly) perceive (in this way) that there are three individual (specific) intentions (corresponding to the subtle forms of the three levels of Speech). (246cd) (247ab) 

एतस्यापि त्रयस्याद्यं यद्रूपमनुपाधिमत् ॥ २४७ ॥
तत्परं त्रितयं तत्र शिवः परचिदात्मकः । 

etasyāpi trayasyādyaṁ yad rūpamanupādhimat || 247 ||
tat paraṁ tritayaṁ tatra śivaḥ paracidātmakaḥ | 

The triad that precedes these three, which is free of limiting adjuncts, is the supreme triad. Śiva, who is supreme consciousness, (manifests) there (at that level). (247) (247cd-248ab) 

*[p. 53]* ^pg53

विभागाभासनायां च मुख्यास्तिस्रोऽत्र शक्तयः ॥ २४८ ॥
अनुत्तरा परेच्छा च परापरतया स्थिता ।
उन्मेषशक्तिर्ज्ञानाख्या त्वपरेति निगद्यते ॥ २४९ ॥ 

vibhāgābhāsanāyāṁ ca mukhyāstisro ‘tra śaktayaḥ || 248 ||
anuttarā parecchā ca parāparatayā sthitā |
unmeṣaśaktirjñānākhyā tvapareti nigadyate || 249 || 

There are three principal powers here that are responsible for the manifestation of (these) divisions. They are the Absolute (Anuttarā), which is the Supreme (Parā power), the will, which abides as the Middle One (Parāparā), and the power of expansion called knowledge, which is said to be the Lower (Aparā power). (248-249ab) (248cd-249) 

क्षोभरूपात्पुनस्तासामुक्ताः षट् संविदोऽमलाः ।
kṣobharūpāt punastāsāmuktāḥ ṣaṭ saṁvido ‘malāḥ | 

The aforementioned six pure states of consciousness, (symbolized by the letters A, Ā, I, Ī, U and Ū), are generated by the arousal of these (powers). (249cd) (250ab) आसामेव समावेशात्क्रियाशक्तितयोदितात् ॥ २५० ॥
संविदो द्वादश प्रोक्ता यासु सर्वं समाप्यते । 

āsāmeva samāveśāt kriyāśaktitayoditāt || 250 ||
saṁvido dvādaśa proktā yāsu sarvaṁ samāpyate | 

These (six forms of consciousness) emerge as the power of action and so, by penetrating (*samāveśa*) (into one another), are said to be twelve forms of consciousness. Within them everything is concluded (and contained). (250cd-251ab) (250cd-251ab) 

एतावद्देवदेवस्य मुख्यं तच्छक्तिचक्रकम् ॥ २५१ ॥
एतावता देवदेवः पूर्णशक्तिः स भैरवः । 

etāvad devadevasya mukhyaṁ tacchakticakrakam || 251 ||
etāvatā devadevaḥ pūrṇaśaktiḥ sa bhairavaḥ | 

Such is the God of the god’s principle Wheel of Energies by virtue of which the God of the gods is Bhairava Who is endowed with all power (*pūrṇaśakti*). (251) (251cd-252ab) 

परामर्शात्मकत्वेन विसर्गाक्षेपयोगतः ॥ २५२ ॥
इयत्ताकलनाज्ज्ञानात्ताः प्रोक्ताः कालिकाः क्वचित् । 

parāmarśātmakatvena visargākṣepayogataḥ || 252 ||
iyattākalanājjñānāt tāḥ proktāḥ kālikāḥ kvacit | 

*[p. 54]* ^pg54

These (powers) are, in some places (in the scriptures), called Kālikās because their nature is reflective awareness and because they project, emit, enumerate and know (all things). (252) (252cd-253ab) 

श्रीसारशास्त्रे चाप्युक्तं मध्य एकाक्षरां पराम् ॥ २५३ ॥
पूजयेद्भैरवात्माख्यां योगिनीद्वादशावृताम् । 

śrīsāraśāstre cāpyuktaṁ madhya ekākṣarāṁ parām || 253 ||
pūjayedbhairavātmākhyāṁ yoginīdvādaśāvṛtām | 

It is also said in the venerable *Sāra* scripture that one should worship the (Goddess) Paraa, Who is the one syllable (SAUḤ), and is called Bhairava’s Self, surrounded by the twelve Yoginīs. (253) (253cd-254ab) 

ताभ्य एव चतुःषष्टिपर्यन्तं शक्तिचक्रकम् ॥ २५४ ॥
एकारतः समारभ्य सहस्रारं प्रवर्तते ।
तासां च कृत्यभेदेन नामानि बहुधागमे ॥ २५५ ॥
उपासाश्च द्वयाद्वैतव्यामिश्राकारयोगतः ।
श्रीमत्त्रैशिरसे तच्च कथितं विस्तराद्बहु ॥ २५६ ॥
इह नो लिखितं व्यासभयाच्चानुपयोगतः । 

tābhya eva catuḥṣaṣṭiparyantaṁ śakticakrakam || 254 ||
ekārataḥ samārabhya sahasrāraṁ pravartate |
tāsāṁ ca kṛtyabhedena nāmāni bahudhāgame || 255 ||
upāsāśca dvayādvaitavyāmiśrākārayogataḥ |
śrīmattraiśirase tacca kathitaṁ vistarādbahu || 256 ||
iha no likhitaṁ vyāsabhayāccānupayogataḥ | 

From (just) these (twelve energies) alone emerge (all) the wheels of energies, starting with (the wheel) with one spoke to that with sixty-four and one thousand spokes. They have many names in the scriptures according to their various functions. The ways they are worshipped (*upāsā*) are (also various) according to whether the form (of their worship) is dual, non-dual or mixed. This has been explained at length in the venerable *Triśirobhairavatantra* (*Traiśirasa*) but not here for fear of going into too many details and also because it would serve no purpose to do so. (254-256) (254cd-257ab)

अनुल्लासादुपाधीनां यद्वा प्रशमयोगतः । 

upādhyatītaṁ yad rūpaṁ tad dvidhā guravo jaguḥ || 259 ||
anullāsādupādhīnāṁ yadvā praśamayogataḥ | 

The teachers have said that the form which transcends (these) limiting adjuncts is of two types, according to whether limiting adjuncts do not emerge or due to (their) cessation (*praśama*). (259) (259cd-260ab) 

प्रशमश्च द्विधा शान्त्या हठपाकक्रमेण तु ॥ २६० ॥
अलंग्रासरसाख्येन सततं ज्वलनात्मना । 

praśamaśca dvidhā śāntyā haṭhapākakrameṇa tu || 260 ||
alaṁgrāsarasākhyena satataṁ jvalanātmanā | 

Again, (their) cessation (*praśama*) is of two kinds according to whether it takes place peacefully (*śāntyā*) (by the normal procedures of initiation etc) or by a process of violent digestion (*haṭhapāka*) brought about (in an instant by Bhairava) who is called the One Who (Delights) in the Aesthetic Savour of Devouring Completely (*alaṁgrāsarasa*) and whose nature burns perpetually. (260) (260cd-261ab) हठपाकप्रशमनं यत्तृतीयं तदेव च ।
उपदेशाय युज्येत भेदेन्धनविदाहकम् ॥ २६१ ॥ 

haṭhapākapraśamanaṁ yat tṛtīyaṁ tadeva ca |
upadeśāya yujyeta bhedendhanavidāhakam || 261 || 

This cessation brought about by violent digestion, the third type, which burns the fuel of differentiation (*bheda*), is (especially) worthy of being taught. (261) (261cd-261ef) 

निजबोधजठरहुतभुजि भावाः सर्वे समर्पिता हठतः ।

nijabodhajaṭharahutabhuji bhāvāḥ sarve samarpitā haṭhataḥ |
vijahati bhedavibhāgaṁ nijaśaktyā taṁ samindhānāḥ || 262 || 

All existing things offered forcefully (*haṭhataḥ*) (in an instant) to the fire that burns in the stomach of one’s own consciousness, abandon the division of relative distinctions and by their own power, they are (its) fuel. (262)

अश्नन्त्यमृतसाद्भूतं विश्वं संवित्तिदेवताः ॥ २६३ ॥

When, by (this process of) violent digestion the differentiated nature of things is destroyed, the deities of consciousness feed upon the universe (thus) turned to nectar. (263) 

तास्तृप्ताः स्वात्मनः पूर्णं हृदयैकान्तशायिनम् ।

cidvyomabhairavaṁ devamabhedenādhiśerate || 264 || 

(When) they are satisfied, they rest at one with God Bhairava, the Sky of Consciousness Who, (as) their own full (and complete) (*pūrṇa*) nature, resides solitary in the Heart. (264) 

एवं कृत्यक्रियावेशान्नामोपासाबहुत्वतः ।

evaṁ kṛtyakriyāveśānnāmopāsābahutvataḥ |
āsāṁ bahuvidhaṁ rūpamabhede ‘pyavabhāsate || 265 || 

Although their nature manifests in unity (*abheda*) in this way, it is of many kinds for many are their names and manner of worship (*upāsā*) due to their functions and operations. (265)

एकद्वित्रिचतुष्पञ्चषट्सप्ताष्टनवोत्तरैः ॥ २६६ ॥

rudrārkānyakalāsenāprabhṛtirbhedavistaraḥ | 

Again, according to the way in which (these) goddesses merge together (*aavaapa*) and unfold (*udvaapa*), they become extensively diverse (by combining in groups of) one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, sixteen, eighteen and so on (infinitely). Conclusion: the Universe is an image reflected in the mirror of consciousness*.* अलमन्येन बहुना प्रकृतेऽथ नियुज्यते ॥ २६७ ॥

Enough of all these many other (secondary details), now we shall apply (ourselves) to the main point. (267cd)

He within whose conscious nature the universe and this (whole) category of phenomena (*bhāvavarga*), freely extending (*prapañcavat*), appears as a reflection is, in truth, the Lord of the universe! In the same way, he within whose nature this kind of reflective awareness, free of thought constructs, arises constantly is indeed stamped with (the seal of) the supreme means (*śāmbhavopāya*). (Thus) for one who possesses the discernment (*pravivecita*) which is the reflective awareness of ‘I-ness’ in all its fullness (*pūrṇāhantā*) there is nothing else (which differs) from that here of any kind be it Mantra, Mudrā, ritual or worship. He who enters repeatedly into this state of penetration (*samāveśa*) (into pure Bhairava consciousness) free of thought constructs, attains Bhairava’s state (*bhairavībhāva*), otherwise called liberation in this life. (271)

यत्र सूत्रणयापीयमुपायोपेयकल्पना ॥ २७२ ॥

From this point onwards we will discuss (the nature of) this (state of) liberation in life (*jīvanmukti*) (which is such) even though this notion that there is a means and a goal (persists) there in a subtle form (*sūtraṇayā*). The Awakened One should apply the fifty aforementioned divisions to the reflective awareness free of thought constructs (here) called the Divine Means (*śāmbhavopāya*). This (same) procedure is applied (to all the other principles) also from that of Water onwards right up to the last (where the yogi reaches) the supreme principle beyond all obscuring coverings which pervades (all things), is free, omniscient and conceived to be Śiva. The (yogi) free of thought constructs beholding that also reflected in the noble and uncreated (*akalpita*) mirror of consciousness becomes by himself (spontaneously) Bhairava. (277)

In the same way, he who reflects upon his own nature through the unified emergence of each one of these (principles), starting with Earth, one after the other, it manifests (in all its) plenitude. “(All) this is born from me”, “it is reflected in me” and “is not separate from me” - three-fold is this Divine (Śāmbhava) Means. (280)

That (reflective awareness thus) forges this link (which contains implicitly within itself and connects together) creation, persistence and withdrawal. As is said in the Spanda teachings: “that in which all this creation is established and from whence it arises . . .” (281)

etāvataiva hyaiśvaryaṁ saṁvidaḥ khyāpitaṁ param |

svātmanyeva cidākāśe viśvamasmyavabhāsayan |

“I, manifesting the universe in my own Self, the Sky of Consciousness, am the creator who is all things (*viśvātmaka*)!” - by this insight one becomes Bhairava. “The entire cosmic order (*ṣaḍadhvajāta*) is reflected within me alone, I am the agent of persistence!” - (with this insight) this (one’s own) cosmic nature becomes evidently apparent. “The universe melts away into me whose nature is the scorching fire of the great, perpetually active (*satatodita*) consciousness” - seeing thus one becomes peaceful. “The entire universe with (all its) diverse (aspects) emerges out of me in many ways and is also established here in the same way within me (in my pure consciousness) from which (all duality) has fallen away and nothing else (remains).” He who sees (the cycle of creation and destruction) in this way without parts because creation, persistence and withdrawal has been unified (and so) has penetrated the Fourth state (of pure, divine consciousness), shines radiantly (as supreme consciousness). Indeed, only a few, sanctified (*bhāvita*) by the (most intense grace of the) Supreme Lord, attain faith (and perfect satisfaction) in this, the supreme means which is the repository of Śiva’s oneness. (288)

snānaṁ vrataṁ dehaśuddhirdhāraṇā mantrayojanā |

*[p. 61]* ^pg61

order (*adhvakḷpti*) (by intense meditation), sacrificial rites, oblation, repetition of Mantras and contemplation (*samādhi*), (all these, or any other means) one could conceive based on duality (*bheda*), are not applicable here (at all). (289-290ab) परानुग्रहकारित्वमत्रस्थस्य स्फुटं स्थितम् ॥ २९० ॥

तं चाराधयते भावितादृशानुग्रहेरितः ।
तदा विचित्रं दीक्षादिविधिं शिक्षेत कोविदः ॥ २९२ ॥

Now if (the disciple) is not such, (but even then), purified by devotion to Rudra (Bhava) and, impelled by (the thought that) there will be such grace in the future, worships that (teacher), then the skilful (teacher), should impart initiation and (whatever other) wonderfully diverse (practice the disciple is fit to receive). (291cd-292) भाविन्योऽपि ह्युपासास्ता अत्रैवायान्ति निष्ठितिम् ।
एतन्मयत्वं परमं प्राप्यं निर्वर्ण्यते शिवम् ॥ २९३ ॥ 

bhāvinyo ‘pi hyupāsāstā atraivāyānti niṣṭhitim |
etanmayatvaṁ paramaṁ prāpyaṁ nirvarṇyate śivam || 293 || 

(All forms of) worship, even those in the future, (ultimately develop and) become well established (and fulfilled) (*niṣṭhitata*) here (in the profound) realization that: “the supreme goal, which is such, is Śiva.” (293) 

इति कथितमिदं सुविस्तरं परमं शाम्भवमात्मवेदनम् ॥
iti kathitamidaṁ suvistaraṁ paramaṁ śāmbhavamātmavedanam || 

So we have explained at length the supreme insight into one's own nature pertaining to the supreme (Śāmbhava) means.
