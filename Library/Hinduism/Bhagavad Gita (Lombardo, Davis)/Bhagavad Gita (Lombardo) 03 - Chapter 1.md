Chapter One

Dhritarashtra spoke:

\[1\] “Tell me, Sanjaya, what my sons
and the sons of Pandu did when they met arrayed for battle
on Kurukshetra’s sacred plain.”

Sanjaya answered:

\[2\] “When King Duryodhana saw
the fighting force of Pandu’s sons
he approached his teacher, Drona,
and addressed him with these words:

\[3\] ‘Look, O master, at this army,
this great Pandava fighting force
marshaled by Drupada’s son,
by your own sagacious student.

\[4\] Here are heroes, mighty archers,
Arjuna’s and Bhima’s equals,
Yuyudhana and Virata,
and Drupada, great charioteer;

\[5\] Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana,
the heroic king of Kashi,
Purujit and Kuntibhoja,
Shaibya too, a bull of a man;

\[6\] mighty Yudhamanyu also,
and the powerful Uttamaujas,
Subhadra’s son, Draupadi’s sons,
all driving their great chariots.

[p. 4] \[7\] And all of our most distinguished,
know them, highest of the Twice-born!
All the commanders of my troops
I will now name for you to hear.

\[8\] You, lord Drona, Bhishma, Karna,
and Kripa the victorious,
Ashvatamma and Vikarna,
the son of Somadatta too,

\[9\] and many other heroes who
for my sake put their lives at risk,
both wielding and hurling weapons,
all of them veteran warriors.

\[10\] Our forces under Bhishma
do not measure up, are lacking.
But our forces under Bhima
do measure up, are sufficient.

\[11\] In all movements and positions,
in whatever places stationed,
all of you, protect old Bhishma,
do indeed protect him, lordships!’

\[12\] Making Duryodhana happy,
his grandfather, the aged Kuru,
made his conch horn roar like a lion,
full of dignity and power.

\[13\] Then all the horns and kettledrums,
the great cymbals and the trumpets
were struck and sounded all at once
with a din like rolling thunder.

\[14\] And, standing in their chariot,
mighty, yoked with silver horses,
[p. 5] Madhu’s slayer and Pandu’s son
sounded their own divine conch horns.

\[15\] Krishna blew his horn Panchajanya,
Arjuna sounded Devadatta,
and terrible, wolf-bellied Bhima
blew his great conch horn Paundra.

\[16\] Kunti’s son, King Yudhishthira,
sounded Anantavijaya;
Nakula and Sahadeva blew
Sughosha and Manipushpaka.

\[17\] The Kashi king, best of archers,
and the great warrior Shikandi,
Dhrishtadyumna and Virata,
and invincible Satyaki,

\[18\] Drupada and Draupadi’s sons,
all together, O Lord of Earth,
and Subhadra’s strong-armed son—
each of them blew his own conch horn.

\[19\] And the noise shattered the hearts
of the sons of Dhritarashtra,
the tumultuous sound echoing
throughout the heavens and the earth.

\[20\] Then Arjuna, son of Pandu,
seeing the sons of Dhritarashtra
arrayed for battle, raised his bow
amid the first clash of weapons.

\[21\] And he spoke these words to Krishna:
‘O Imperishable Lord of Earth,
draw my chariot to the middle,
station it between the armies.

[p. 6] \[22\] Keep it there until I’ve surveyed
these battle-hungry warriors
with whom I must do combat soon
in this war we’ve undertaken.

\[23\] I see men banded together
to do battle in the service
of Duryodhana, the evil-minded
son of King Dhritarashtra.’

\[24\] And so Krishna, as requested
by Arjuna, O Dhritarashtra,
drew the chariot to the middle
of the two opposing armies.

\[25\] Before the eyes of Bhishma, Drona,
and all these princes of the earth,
Arjuna, son of Pritha, spoke:
‘Look at these assembled Kurus.’

\[26\] Arjuna saw standing there
fathers first, and then grandfathers,
maternal uncles, teachers, brothers,
sons, grandsons, and friends as well,

\[27\] Fathers-in-law and companions.
All of these in both the armies
the son of Kunti contemplated,
all his kinsmen arrayed for battle.

\[28\] He was filled with boundless pity,
and in despair he spoke these words:
‘Seeing my own people, Krishna,
drawing near in battle frenzy,

\[29\] Both my legs collapse beneath me,
my mouth is dry, my tongue is cracked,
[p. 7] all my body shakes and trembles,
my hair bristles and stands on end.

\[30\] My bow Gandiva falls from my hand,
and my skin feels like it’s burning.
I cannot stand in position,
and my mind is wandering off.

\[31\] I perceive bad omens, Krishna,
inauspicious, O Handsome One!
I foresee that no good can come
from killing my own kin in battle.

\[32\] I do not desire victory,
hanker after royal pleasures.
What is kingship to us, Govinda?
What is luxury, or life itself?

\[33\] Those for whose sake we desire
kingship, delicious food, pleasures,
stand here in battle formation,
forsaking their wealth and their lives.

\[34\] They are teachers, fathers and sons,
grandfathers, maternal uncles,
fathers-in-law and grandsons too,
brothers-in-law and other kin.

\[35\] I have no wish, Slayer of Madhu,
to kill those who are going to kill,
not even to rule the triple world,
much less to be an earthly ruler.

\[36\] What joy would it give us, Krishna,
to kill Dhritarashtra’s sons?
We would be beset with evil
if we killed our attackers here.

[p. 8] \[37\] It is not just for us to kill
Dhritarashtra’s sons, our kinsmen.
If we murder our own people,
could we ever be happy, Krishna?

\[38\] There may be some people whose minds
are so overpowered with greed
they do not see that it is wrong
to betray and destroy their kin.

\[39\] Do we not know enough, Krishna,
to turn aside from such evil?
To understand how wrong it is
to destroy friends and family?

\[40\] With destruction of the family
ancient family customs vanish,
and lawlessness overpowers
the entire family structure.

\[41\] When lawlessness reigns, O Krishna,
the family’s women are corrupted,
and when they are corrupted, Krishna,
the castes become intermingled.

\[42\] This intermingling hurls families
into hell with their destroyers,
and their ancestors are deprived
of their rice and water offerings.

\[43\] Castes intermixed, families ruined—
when these crimes are committed
the duties of castes are abolished,
eternal family laws as well.

\[44\] Men whose family laws are wiped out
dwell in hell indefinitely.
[p. 9] Have we not heard this repeated,
again and again, Lord Krishna?

\[45\] Ah no! Alas! A great evil
is what we have resolved upon,
ready to kill our own people
out of greed for royal pleasures!

\[46\] If the armed sons of Dhritarashtra
should kill me unarmed in battle
and offering no resistance,
for me that would be a happier fate.’

\[47\] Saying this, Arjuna sat down
in his chariot on the field,
throwing down his bow and arrows,
his heart overcome with sorrow.”
