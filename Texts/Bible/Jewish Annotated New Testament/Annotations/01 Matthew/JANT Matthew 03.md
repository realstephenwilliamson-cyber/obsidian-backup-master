### 3.1–12: John the Baptist

*[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 14* · ([[Mark 01#^1-2|Mk 1.2–8]]; [[Luke 03#^3-1|Lk 3.1–20]]; [[John 01#^1-19|Jn 1.19–28]]). ^3-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-1|1]]:** *John the Baptist*, a popular figure admired by some Jews (Josephus, *Ant.* 18.116–19). *Baptist*, from the Greek meaning “dip” or “immerse” (see “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674). *Wilderness of Judea*, west of the Dead Sea. ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Repent*, a prophetic call (e.g., [[Ezekiel 18#^18-30|Ezek 18.30]]). *Kingdom of heaven,* rather than, as in the Gospels of Mark and Luke, “kingdom of God”; either a circumlocution designed to preserve the holiness of God’s name or an indication that Matthew views the kingdom as an actual place in the sky. Earliest usage of the phrase may go back to the Hebrew Bible (see [[Psalms 103#^103-19|Ps 103.19]], which indicates that the Lord’s throne is in heaven). See also *Pesiq. Rab.* 2, associating the “kingdom of heaven” with [[Zechariah 14#^14-9|Zech 14.9]]. ^3-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-3|3]]:** [[Isaiah 40#^40-3|Isa 40.3]], written originally to encourage Jews in Babylonian exile to return to Judea. 1QS 8.12–14 can also be understood as locating the *voice* in the *wilderness*. Masoretic cantillation marks, which also serve as punctuation, understand *in the wilderness* as belonging with what follows (“In the wilderness prepare”) rather than with what precedes. ^3-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-4|4]]:** *Hair . . . belt*, recalling Elijah ([[II Kings 01#^1-8|2 Kings 1.8]]; see [[Matthew 11#^11-14|Mt 11.14]]; [[Matthew 17#^17-11|17.11–13]]), who is also depicted in the Tanakh as a new Moses. ^3-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-6|6]]:** *River Jordan*, for water purification rites ([[Isaiah 04#^4-4|Isa 4.4]]; [[Isaiah 44#^44-3|44.3]]; [[Jeremiah 04#^4-14|Jer 4.14]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-25|Ezek 36.25–27]]; [[Zechariah 13#^13-1|Zech 13.1]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 4.205; *m. Yoma* 8.9). Confessing their sins, see *t. Yoma* 2.1; see also *y. Yoma* 8.9; *Lev. Rab.* 3.3. On the power of the Jordan, see [[II Kings 05#^5-1|2 Kings 5.1–19]], concerning Elijah’s disciple Elisha. ^3-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-7|7]]:** *Pharisees and Sadducees*, Jewish movements (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.164–65; *Ant.* 13.278–79; 17.42; 18.16–17). See “Jewish Movements of the NT Period,” p. 614. [[Luke 03#^3-7|Lk 3.7]] has John address the crowds. *Brood of vipers*, newborn vipers were believed to eat through their mother’s stomach, killing her. *Wrath to come*, eschatological judgment. ^3-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Fruit*, good deeds; baptism itself is not sufficient. ^3-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-9|9]]:** *Abraham*, the father of the Jewish people, see 1.2n.; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-7|Gen 17.7]]; [[Proverbs 17#^17-2|Prov 17.2]]; *b. Yebam*. 64b, “Look to Abraham your father.” *As our ancestor*, perhaps referring to the “merits of the fathers” (Heb “zekhut avot”), meaning that the great deeds and characters of the Hebrew patriarchs influence God’s judgment (favorably) upon the Jewish people; Moses pleads that God remember the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-11|Ex 32.11–14]]). *Stones . . . children*, a pun in Hebrew (“avanim . . . banim,” “sons . . . stones”). ^3-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Fire*, used for purification ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-23|Num 31.23]]; *b. Sanh*. 39a). E G Y P T The geography of the Gospel of Matthew. ^3-11

> [!note] Righteousness *(p. 15)*
> Matthew’s Gospel emphasizes the concept of righteousness (1.19; 3.15; 5.10–11; 6.1,33; 9.13; 10.41; 13.43; 21.31–32; 22.14; 23.35; 25.46). The term (Gk *dikaiosynē*; Heb *tzedakah* as in the term *tzaddik*, a righteous person) in Jewish texts also carries the connotation of giving generously to the poor or “almsgiving” ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-22|Dt 14.22]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-20|16.20]]), and by extension any loving act. Some rabbinic traditions view *tzedakah* as being more important than all of the other commandments combined
>
> (*b. B. Bat*. 9b), and that one who gives *tzedakah* fills the world with kindness (*b. Sukk*. 49a). In Matthew, Joseph is a “righteous” man in his resolve to divorce Mary quietly (1.19) and so not create a scandal. Jesus models this higher righteousness in insisting that John baptize him (3.15) and so submitting himself to John. Jesus’ Sermon on the Mount is very Jewish in the sense that it is punctuated by the concern for “righteousness” ([[Matthew 05#^5-6|Mt 5.6]],[[Matthew 05#^5-10|10]],[[Matthew 05#^5-20|20]]; [[Matthew 06#^6-33|6.33]]), living in a right relationship with both others and God.

### 3.13–17: Jesus’ baptism

*[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-13|Text]], p. 16* · ([[Mark 01#^1-9|Mk 1.9–11]]; [[Luke 03#^3-21|Lk 3.21–22]]; [[John 01#^1-29|Jn 1.29–34]]). ^3-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-14|14]]:** John recognizes Jesus’ superiority. ^3-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Fulfill all righteousness*, a messianic accomplishment ([[Jeremiah 23#^23-5|Jer 23.5–6]]; [[Jeremiah 33#^33-15|33.15–16]]; *T. Jud.* 24.1), see 1.19n. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-16|16]]:** *Heavens were opened,* indicating impending divine revelation ([[Isaiah 64#^64-1|Isa 64.1]]; [[Ezekiel 01#^1-1|Ezek 1.1]]). *Spirit of God,* [[Isaiah 11#^11-2|Isa 11.2]]; [[Isaiah 42#^42-1|42.1]]; [[Isaiah 61#^61-1|61.1]]; *b. Hag.* 15a. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Voice from heaven*, Jewish tradition speaks of the “bat kol” (Heb “daughter of \[the\] voice”), a heavenly voice, representing God’s proclamation or judgment, especially in an era after the cessation of prophecy (e.g., *b. Ber*. 3a; *b. Yoma* 9b; *b. Sanh*. 96b; *b. Eruv.* 13b; *Tg. Cant*. on 2.12). *This is my Son*, cf. [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1]]; [[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Isaiah 42#^42-1|Isa 42.1]]; [[Jeremiah 31#^31-9|Jer 31.9]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]; on son and beloved together, see the binding of Isaac ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-2|Gen 22.2]]) and [[Hosea 11#^11-1|Hos 11.1]].
