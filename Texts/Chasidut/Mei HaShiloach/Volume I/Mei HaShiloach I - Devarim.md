# Mei HaShiloach I - Devarim

← [[Mei HaShiloach I - Masei]] · [[Mei HaShiloach]] · [[Mei HaShiloach I - Vaetchanan]] →

*Volume I · Deuteronomy · Devarim* · Torah: [[Torah 44 - Devarim]] · [[Mei HaShiloach]]

## Teaching 1

*“These are the words .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-1|Devarim, 1:1]])  
It is written in the Midrash (Sochar Tov, 39), “The Torah was only given to Israel so they would not occupy themselves with idle words.” This is the meaning of (*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 058#^58-2|58:2]]), “will indeed the mute speak righteousness?” For all the powers that were created in man, the Holy One, blessed be He, gave a commandment with which to serve God. Man was created with the power of speech, and he was given words of Torah with which to occupy himself.

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-1|Deuteronomy 1:1]]

## Teaching 2

*“… in the plain over against Suf .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-1|Devarim, 1:1]])  
Why were the sins of Israel discreetly mentioned through the names of the places where the sins occurred rather than by mentioning the time when they occurred? (See Rashi on this verse, who teaches of the merits of Moshe that he mentioned the names of the places where the children of Israel sinned, but not the sins themselves. He did not say, “at the Aravah you sinned with the Baal Peor, at at Shittim with the Moabites, etc.” This was so as to honor them and not be overly harsh in his reproof. The Author takes this further, with a simple question. Why not mention the times instead of the places?)  
This is in order that man does not think that they sinned according to their power of choice, and that they had the choice to remove themselves from the sin. Place hints at this, for it was not possible for them to guard themselves from the sin and move to a different place (where they might not have sinned.)[^1]

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-1|Deuteronomy 1:1]]

## Teaching 3

*“Eleven days from Horev .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-2|Devarim, 1:2]])  
Upon receiving the Torah the heart of Israel was not yet refined so as to be completely drawn after God’s will. They understood in their intellect that it was good to fulfill God’s will, yet their heart was not yet purified. Only once they had entered the land was their heart refined. This is as Moshe Rabeynu said to them, “eleven days from Horev.” After you received the Torah at Horev and then you reached Kadesh Barnea in only three days, you thought that his was a sign thatyour hearts were already purified.[^2] Yet it is not so, for (the distance from Horev to Kadesh) is actually eleven days (meaning it is a longer journey, a greater effort), and (in this Moshe was telling them,) “You have not yet acquired words of Torah that they may be fixed in your heart. It is only after forty years, by means of failures and trials, that your hearts became refined,” for “one cannot uphold the Torah unless he has failed in it [*Gittin*, 43a].”  
Concerning this King David said (*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 127#^127-2|127:2]]), “It is vain for you to get up early [to think that in only three days you have sufficiently refined your hearts] … by eating the bread of sorrows, thus He gives His beloved sleep.” Before the heart is refined one must undergo toil and strife in order to fulfill God’s will. Yet after words of Torah are fixed in his heart, then the Holy One, blessed be He, bestows words of Torah upon him without any struggle, even while sleeping, and one does not need to undergo great efforts.

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-2|Deuteronomy 1:2]]

## Teaching 4

*“… I alone cannot bear you .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-9|Devarim, 1:9]])  
The principle of the portion of Moshe Rabeynu was the reception of the Torah, and the land of Israel was the portion of Yehoshua. So when it was said to them (verses 6 and 7), “It is enough of your dwelling on this mountain. You shall turn and go,” this signified the beginning of their entrance into the land. Then his strength waned.

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-6|Deuteronomy 1:6–7]], [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-9|Deuteronomy 1:9]]

## Teaching 5

*“And I said to them at that time, saying, ‘I alone cannot bear you … bring men of wisdom.…’ ”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-9|Devarim, 1:9]], 13)  
When they were close to entering the land of Israel, Moshe Rabeynu felt that it was God’s will that Yehoshua would be the one to bring them in. But still he wanted Israel to pray to God saying that they did not want another leader (than Moshe, in effect to change God’s will). This is hinted in the way he said, “I alone cannot bear you.” (Not alone, but if all of Israel prays, then perhaps.) Even though we find later (at the beginning of the next *Parsha*) that he asks God to be the one to bring them in, he says it to them here in order for them to understand that he needs their prayers now. This verse is the ninth verse from the beginning of the *Parsha*, and each time the ninth verse contains a depth much deeper than the plain meaning, as is explained above in *Parshat Balak*.[^3] However, they didn’t understand (Moshe’s hidden meaning, wanting them to pray that he should be the one to bring them in), and they said, “it is good to do what you have said [to choose other leaders]” (*Devarim*, [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-14|1:14]]). Therefore it is said, “Take men of wisdom, of deep understanding, and known (among your tribes),”[^4] (verse 13) and after, “so I took wise men, and known…” (verse 15). On this our sages say (*Nedarim*, [[Nedarim 20#20b|20b]]), “the second time it does not say, ‘of deep understanding,’ because he didn’t find anyone of deep understanding.” This means that (because they answered him in verse 14, “it is good to choose new leaders”) he didn’t find anyone with an understanding of his hidden intention (as if to say), “for if you had understood my intention and prayed, maybe your prayer would have been successful.”

**Torah:** [[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-2|Numbers 22:2–25:9]], [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-9|Deuteronomy 1:9]], [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-14|Deuteronomy 1:14]]

## Teaching 6

*“Hashem your God has increased you, and behold, you are today like the stars of the heavens for a multitude.”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-10|Devarim, 1:10]])  
It is said in the Midrash (*Devarim* Rabba, 1:14), “Moshe Rabeynu said before the Holy One, blessed be He, ‘why did you not compare them to the sun and the moon, which are greater than the stars?’ The Holy One, blessed be He, answered him, ‘in the future, shame will come from the sun and the moon, as it is said [Yesahya, [[Isaiah 24#^24-23|24:23]]], “then the moon shall be confounded, and the sun shall be ashamed, when the Lord of Hosts shall reign on mount Zion and Jerusalem.’”[^5] And God continued, ‘yet no shame shall come from the stars, as it is said ([[Joel 2#^2-27|Yoel, 2:27]]), ‘For I am in the midst of Israel, and I am the Lord your God, and there is no other, and you shall no longer be ashamed.’” The sun and the moon represent a time when one acts according to his own human wisdom and understanding, for (in the time to come) all the thoughts of man according to human wisdom will be nullified. On the other hand, the stars represent the place where the Holy One, blessed be He, illuminates the eyes of man, and he goes according to the illumination of the light of God. This will endure forever and ever.

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-10|Deuteronomy 1:10]]

## Teaching 7

*“And it was, in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first of the month, Moshe spoke .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-3|Devarim, 1:3]])  
The reason why he specifies here the exact month is because the month of Shevat (the eleventh month) teaches of the rod [*Shevet* in Hebrew] and ethical reproof, as it is written ([[Proverbs 29#^29-15|Mishlei, 29:15]]), “the rod and reproof will bring wisdom.” Here Moshe Rabeynu advised them to enter into the Torah, and God would redeem them from all evil, as it is written (*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 111#^111-9|111:9]]), “He sent redemption to his people, He commanded eternally His covenant.” The covenant [*brit* in Hebrew] is the Torah, as it is written ([[Jeremiah 33#^33-25|Yirmiya, 33:25]]), “If My covenant did not last day and night.” This is as it is said ([[Proverbs 15#^15-33|Mishlei, 15:33]]), “the fear of God instructs wisdom,” for there is ethical instruction by means of suffering, God forbid, and the study of Torah is also called ethical instruction, as it is said ([[Proverbs 08#^8-10|Mishlei, 8:10]]), “take My instruction.” From the Torah one may also come to the kind of suffering (bringing ethical understanding), for “fortunate is the man whom God punishes, and learns from His Torah [*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 094#^94-12|94:12]]].” This is as it says (*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 111#^111-10|111:10]]), “the beginning of wisdom is the fear of God, a good understanding to all who do them,” meaning that this is wisdom and good understanding to the man who enters himself into the Torah. By means of this he is saved from all evil.

**Torah:** [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-3|Deuteronomy 1:3]]

## Teaching 8

*“For only Og king of Bashan remains … Behold! His bed is a bed of iron, is it not in Rabat of the children of Ammon? Nine cubits its length and four cubits its width .…”* ([[Torah 44 - Devarim#^3-11|Devarim, 3:11]])  
Moshe Rabeynu feared Og, because he had the sign of the *brit* (covenant) given to him by our father Avraham, as it is written in the Zohar (*Bamidbar*, [[Zohar 9.07 - Parashat Huqqat#^z3-184a|184a]]), “for Og was Eliezar the servant of Avraham,” as is known. Moshe then feared that maybe God was with him, God forbid. This is why it is written, “nine cubits its length and four cubits its width,” for four, the letter *dalet*, and nine, the letter *tet*, are the letters preceding the first two letters of the name of God, *Yud Hei*. All the Divine influences to man come from these letters, for the last two letters in God’s name, *Vav Hei*, come from the side of human apprehension, as is known. Moshe feared that perhaps the name *Yud Hei* rested on Og, so God showed him that it is not so. Even though it seemed that on the surface his actions were good, still his insides were not clean. This is why it says “is it not in Rabat of the children of Ammon.” Ammon is the *klipah* (evil force) of desire and promiscuity, as is known from the Zohar (ibid.), “that [Ammon] contaminated his sign of the *brit*.”  
Concerning this the Holy One, blessed be He, promised Moshe Rabeynu (*Devarim*, [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^2-25|2:25]]), “On this day I will begin to put the dread and fear of you on the face of the nations which are under all the heavens.” “Heavens” teaches of something that seems to all on the surface to receive power and assistance from God, and God showed him that among the nations there is none of this in reality. This is why the phrasing seems to be changed. It would seem that it should have been, “to put the fear of you on *all* the nations which are under *the heavens*.” This hints that all the surface appearances that seem good among the nations really have no substance within them.[^6]
