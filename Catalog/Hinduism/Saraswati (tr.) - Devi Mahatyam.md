## About

A Sanskrit text in praise of the Goddess (Devi) as supreme reality, forming chapters 81–93 of the Markandeya Purana. It has 700 verses in 13 chapters and is also called the Durga Saptashati, Chandi Path and Shata Chandi.

- **Author and frame:** Wikipedia's infobox names Vyasa; within the Purana the narrator is the sage Markandeya. In the story, the sage Medhas teaches King Suratha and the merchant Samadhi.
- **Date:** Wikipedia says it was added in the 5th or 6th century to a Purana that Ludo Rocher dates to about the 3rd century CE, and that it is generally dated 400–600 CE; the Dadhimati Mata inscription of 608 CE quotes it. Wendy Doniger O'Flaherty gives c. 550 CE. Britannica gives about the 5th or 6th century CE.
- **Contents:** three episodes. Chapter 1: Madhu and Kaitabha, slain by Vishnu after the Goddess as Mahamaya has held him in sleep. Chapters 2–4: Durga defeats the buffalo demon Mahishasura. Chapters 5–13: the war with Shumbha and Nishumbha, including Chanda, Munda and Raktabija. Wikipedia names the Goddess's three saguna forms as Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati, and places the saguna hymns in chapters 1, 4 and 11. Britannica adds the Seven Mothers (Saptamatrika) and the forms Chandi and Ambika.
- **Ritual use:** recited at Navaratri, Durga Puja and in Durga temples, with supplementary texts (angas) such as the Devi Kavacha, Argala Stotra and Keelaka, whose number and order vary by tradition. Britannica says devotees memorize it and recite it word for word.
- **Commentaries and translations:** commentaries include Bhaskararaya's *Guptavati* and Nagoji Bhat's *Nagesi*. Wikipedia says it was translated into English in 1823, with a French analysis and excerpts in 1824.
- **Why it matters:** Britannica calls it the first text in the high Sanskrit tradition to raise the Goddess to ultimate prominence, and says it treats her forms, from Kali to Shri, as one. Thomas Coburn, cited by Wikipedia, calls it the earliest work to conceive the object of worship as Goddess. Wikipedia counts it among the most important texts of Shaktism, with the Devi-Bhagavata Purana and Devi Upanishad.
