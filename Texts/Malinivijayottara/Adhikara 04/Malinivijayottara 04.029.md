## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, *Mālinī*, April 2023 – June 2023
> p. 21 ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Malinivijayottaratantra, chapters 1-5 (2022-2023).pdf#page=62>))
>
> **Three and Four Kinds of Knowledge**
>
> It is said that knowledge is of three kinds. 1) The first one is considered to be (the one received) through hearing (that is, study) (śrauta). 2) Now, another consists of cogitation (cintā) and 3) (one) consists of imaginative contemplation (bhāvanā).
>
> 1) The one (called) 'hearing' (i.e., study) is considered to be the full knowledge of a subject (artha) (taught in) scripture which is dispersed (in various places in it).
>
> 2) Having seen all of a teaching (artha) in the scriptures, its systematic arrangement (brought about by reflecting that) “this is (said) here; (whereas) this is said here and this is (rightly) applied here,” is the knowledge consisting of cogitation. 2A and 2B) It is taught that it is of two kinds, according to whether it is (superficial and) slow (manda) or (deep and) well practiced. There, (of these two), the well-practiced is the best.
>
> 3 and 4) Then, when it is well developed, the one consisting of contemplation (bhāvanā) arises within it. It is due to that once the yogi has attained yoga, he gets the fruit of yoga. In this way, the man of knowledge (jñānin) is said to be of four kinds (according to which of these kinds of knowledge he possesses).[^21] (with 4.28–32)

[^21]: These are the three kinds of knowledge attained by study, cogitation and meditation, which is either weak or strong. MV 4/28-32 is quoted in TĀv ad 13/327, which is a concise explanation of the purport of this passage.
