## About

The first volume of *The Ante-Nicene Fathers*, the American edition of a British series of English translations of Christian writers before the Council of Nicaea (325). It gathers the Apostolic Fathers with Justin Martyr and Irenaeus.

- **Editors:** Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson edited the original series. A. Cleveland Coxe, an Episcopal bishop, revised it, arranged it chronologically and added short prefaces and notes (title page, CCEL). Coxe bracketed his own notes to keep them apart from the translators'.
- **1867–73:** the original *Ante-Nicene Christian Library* was published in Edinburgh by T. & T. Clark and sold by subscription. Wikipedia says it was meant as an alternative to the Oxford Movement's Library of the Fathers. Coxe's preface says this volume contains "the equivalent of three volumes of the Edinburgh series" (tertullian.org transcription).
- **1885:** Coxe's preface is dated 6 January 1885. The Christian Literature Company issued the series from that year, first at Buffalo, then New York, without permission; Wikipedia calls it a pirate edition, complete by 1896. Library copies show reprints: Buffalo, 1887 (Princeton Theological Seminary) and Scribner, New York, 1895, with copyrights of 1885–87 held by the Christian Literature Co. (Internet Archive).
- **Contents:** Clement of Rome's First Epistle to the Corinthians; the Epistle to Diognetus (under "Mathetes"); Polycarp to the Philippians and his Martyrdom; Ignatius (seven epistles, the Syriac version and spurious letters); the Epistle of Barnabas; fragments of Papias; Justin Martyr's two Apologies, Dialogue with Trypho and shorter works; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* I–V, with fragments.
- **The series:** ten volumes, the last of indexes. Wikipedia says T. & T. Clark published a ninth volume of new translations in 1897 to complete its own series; the Internet Archive record names Allan Menzies as its editor.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says the volumes "continue to be reprinted by publishers"; its external links point to free online texts (CCEL, Wikisource). It also notes that academic and Roman Catholic reviewers criticized Coxe's introductions and notes. Coxe himself called this first volume "the experimental one", issued under the pressure of a "virtual obligation" to appear in the first month of 1885.
