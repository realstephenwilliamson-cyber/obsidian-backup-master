# Galatians 4 (JANT notes)

### 4.1–7: Sons and heirs

*[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 382* ^4-1

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-1|1]]:** *Heirs . . . minors . . . slaves . . . owners*, all these nouns are singular in the original (see 3.23–29). ^4-1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-2|2]]:** *Guardians* (Gk “epitropoi,” sing. “epitropos”), “guardian” or “trustee,” a loan word in rabbinic literature for a guardian of an orphan (e.g., *t*. *Ter.* 5.7). ^4-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-3|3]]:** *Elemental spirits* (or: *the rudiments*), polytheistic piety previously observed by the Gentile Galatians (4.8–9). See [[Colossians 02#^2-8|Col 2.8]],[[Colossians 02#^2-20|20]]. ^4-3

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-4|4]]:** *Fullness of time*, see [[Mark 01#^1-15|Mk 1.15]]; [[Ephesians 01#^1-10|Eph 1.10]]. In the Tanakh this phrase is used, e.g., of a woman about to give birth ([[Torah 06 - Toldot#^25-24|Gen 25.24]]). Many rabbis believed that the Messiah would arrive at a time predetermined by God (*b. Ketub.* 111a; *b. Sanh.* 97b). *Born of a woman*, not necessarily implying divine paternity, cf. [[Job 14#^14-1|Job 14.1]]; [[Matthew 11#^11-11|Mt 11.11]]. *Under the law*, lit., “under law,” i.e., as a Jew. ^4-4

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-5|5]]:** *Those who were under the law*, see 4.21n. This phrase would seem to refer to Jews, but Paul goes on to say *we*, indicating “we humans,” see 3.26; 4.3. *Adoption*, Paul changes his metaphor, from becoming adults (4.1–2) to being adopted ([[Romans 08#^8-14|Rom 8.14–17]]). An alternative translation is “sonship” (as in [[Romans 09#^9-4|Rom 9.4]]) denoting the relationship between God and his special people. ^4-5

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Spirit of his Son*, Paul distinguishes between Christ and God (see 1.1n.; [[1 Corinthians 08#^8-6|1 Cor 8.6]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6]]**–**11), but seems to identify the Spirit and Christ ([[Romans 08#^8-9|Rom 8.9–10]]). In the fourth century the Nicene Creed distinguished God the Father, God the Son (Christ), and the God the Spirit. This Trinitarian conception is unknown to Paul and is barely attested in the NT ([[Matthew 28#^28-19|Mt 28.19]]; see “Christology,” p. 754). *Abba! Father!* “Abba” is Aram for “Father” or “my father.” It is glossed with Gk “pater” (father) in [[Mark 14#^14-36|Mk 14.36]] and [[Romans 08#^8-15|Rom 8.15]]. Rabbinic theology, following biblical precedent ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1]] and [[Jeremiah 31#^31-20|Jer 31.20]]), often conceived of God as father and Israel as son or sons. Rabbinic prayers were sometimes directed to “our father (“avinu*”)* in heaven” (e.g., *m. Sot.* 9.15; see also [[Matthew 06#^6-9|Mt 6.9]]) or “our father (“avinu”) our king” (e.g., *b. Taʾan.* 25b, “Our father our king, we have no king but you; our father our king, for your sake have pity on us”) but no rabbinic prayers invoke God as *Abba*, perhaps because rabbinic prayers are overwhelmingly in the plural. ^4-6

### 4.8–11: Avoiding the pagan past

*[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-8|Text]], p. 382* ^4-8

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-8|8–9]]:** *Beings* . *. . elemental spirits,* see 4.3n. Paul implicitly equates the old pagan worship of the Galatians, which they had observed before accepting Christ, with the Torah observance that they are now adopting as a result of the preaching of Paul’s opponents. ^4-8-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-10|10]]:** *Days . . . years*, usually understood as referring to Jewish calendrical observance. See [[Romans 14#^14-5|Rom 14.5–6]]; [[Colossians 02#^2-16|Col 2.16]]. The alternative explanation that these are pagan holidays, and the entire paragraph speaks of the Galatians’ return to paganism, is unlikely because nowhere else in the letter does Paul accuse the Galatians of such behavior. ^4-10

### 4.12–20: Paul as exemplar

*[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-12|Text]], p. 382* ^4-12

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-13|13]]:** *Physical infirmity*, v 15 may suggest eye trouble. *Gospel*, see 1.6n. *First*, or on the prior occasion, suggesting Paul visited Galatia twice (see [[Acts 16#^16-6|Acts 16.6]]; [[Acts 18#^18-23|18.23]]). ^4-13

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-14|14]]:** *Though my condition put you to the test, you did not scorn or despise me*, lit., “and that which was a temptation to you in my flesh, you did not reject or despise.” Because of Paul’s ailment the Galatians might have re- *through Christ* e Or *beggarly rudiments* f Gk *Brothers* jected him, see [[Psalms 022#^22-25|Ps 22.24]]. *As an angel of God, as Christ Jesus*, the Galatians showed Paul the same respect they would have shown to a heavenly representative. ^4-14

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-17|17–18]]:** *They*, Paul’s opponents, who teach Torah observance and separate from the non-Law-observant, see 2.12. *Make much*, or “seek out.” ^4-17

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-19|19]]:** *Pain of childbirth*, Paul, like Moses ([[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-12|Num 11.12]]; see also [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-7|1 Thess 2.7]]) imagines himself a mother in labor. In [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-15|1 Cor 4.15]] and [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-11|1 Thess 2.11]] Paul calls himself a father, and he regularly refers to his followers as “children” ([[1 Corinthians 04#^4-14|1 Cor 4.14]]; [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-13|2 Cor 6.13]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-11|1 Thess 2.11]]). *Until Christ is formed in you*, the Galatians too are “pregnant”! ^4-19

### 4.21–5.1: Allegory of Hagar and Sarah

*[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-21|Text]], p. 383* ^4-21

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-21|4.21]]:** The Greek distinguishes *subject to the law*, lit., “subject to law,” without the definite article, from *listen to the law*, with the definite article (see [[Romans 03#^3-21|Rom 3.21]]). Without the definite article, *law* (Gk “nomos”) usually means something general like “legal observances” as in the phrase “subject to law” or “under law” (3.23; 4.4–5; 5.18; [[Romans 06#^6-14|Rom 6.14–15]]). With the article, *the law* usually refers to the Torah (3.10,19; 5.3). Similarly in rabbinic idiom “the Torah” (Heb “ha-Torah”) usually refers to the written Torah, while “Torah” refers to something much broader, Oral Torah. See *m. Avot* 1.1, “Moses received Torah (not ‘the’ Torah) at Sinai.” *Listen to the law*, listen to what scripture says. ^4-21-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-22|22]]:** *It is written*, Gen 16; 21. ^4-22

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-24|24]]:** *Is an allegory*, literal meaning is not the real meaning. Allegories typically represent moral qualities or philosophical concepts as concrete entities such as persons or places. Allegories are present in the Tanakh (e.g., Isa 5). Philo, the Alexandrian Jewish philosopher, Paul’s older contemporary, understands many biblical stories as allegories. For example, Abram’s journey from Chaldea to Canaan (Gen 12) represents the soul’s ascent from the world of matter to that of the spirit. For Philo, allegorical meanings complement or supplement literal meanings (see “Philo of Alexandria,” p. 714); in contrast, Paul thinks that the allegorical meaning completely replaces the literal meaning. Numerous rabbinic texts (e.g., *Gen. Rab.* 65.21) understand the conflict between Esau and Jacob (Gen 25–33) as representing the conflict between Israel (Jacob) and Rome (Edom, Esau). In Paul’s allegory, Hagar, a slave who bears children into slavery, represents the Sinaitic covenant**.** ^4-24

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-25|25]]:** *Jerusalem . . . with her children*, on Zion as mother, see [[Isaiah 49#^49-14|Isa 49.14–23]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-8|66.8]]. Textual variants (which read “For Sinai is a mountain in Arabia” instead of “Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia”) show that ancient readers were not sure how to understand Paul’s identification of Hagar with Sinai. ^4-25

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-25|25–26]]:** *Jerusalem*, when speaking of Jerusalem as a symbol, Paul uses a transliteration of the Heb (“Ierousalêm”); when speaking of the actual place (1.17–18; 2.1), he uses the Gk (“Hierosoly-ma”). *Present Jerusalem . . . Jerusalem above*, various biblical verses depict God as building the Jerusalem Temple ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-17|Ex 15.17]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-69|Ps 78.69]]) or Jerusalem itself ([[Psalms 147#^147-2|Ps 147.2]]; perhaps Ps 87). This Pauline passage seems to be the earliest attestation of the idea that a heavenly Jerusalem corresponds to the earthly one, the former built by God, the latter built by humans. After the Temple’s destruction in 70 CE, this idea takes on a *mountain in Arabia* consolatory function: earthly Jerusalem is destroyed but heavenly Jerusalem endures (*4 Ezra* \[=2 Esd\] 7.26; [[Revelation 03#^3-12|Rev 3.12]]; [[Revelation 21#^21-1|21.1–3]]; *b. Taʾan.* 5a (God says, “I shall enter the heavenly Jerusalem only when I return to the earthly Jerusalem”). Paul understands the relationship between earthly and heavenly Jerusalem to be not complementary but adversarial. Contrast [[Hebrews 12#^12-22|Heb 12.22]]. ^4-25-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-27|27]]:** See [[Isaiah 54#^54-1|Isa 54.1]]. ^4-27

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-29|29]]:** That Ishmael *persecuted* Isaac is based on a midrashic reading of [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-9|Gen 21.9]] (e.g., *Gen. Rab.* 53.15). ^4-29

**[[NRSVue Galatians 04#^4-30|30]]:** See [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-10|Gen 21.10]]. ^4-30
