# Guide for the Perplexed 1.44

← [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.43]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.45]] →

*Part 1, Chapter 44* · [[Guide for the Perplexed]]

THE Hebrew ‘ayin is a homonym, signifying “fountain”; e.g., “By a fountain (‘en) of water” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^16-7|Gen. 16:7]]). It next denotes “eye”; comp. (‘ayin) “Eye for eye” ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-24|Exod. 21:24]]). Another meaning of the word is “providence,” as it is said concerning Jeremiah, “Take him and direct thine attention (eneka) to him” ([[Jeremiah 39#^39-12|Jer. 39:12]]). In this figurative sense it is to be understood when used in reference to God; e.g., “And my providence and my pleasure shall be there perpetually” ([[I Kings 09#^9-3|1 Kings 9:3]]), as we have already explained (page 140); “The eyes (‘ene), i.e., the Providence of the Lord thy God, are always upon it” ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-12|Deut. 11:12]]); “They are the eyes (‘ene) of the Lord, which run to and fro through the whole earth” ([[Zechariah 04#^4-10|Zech. 4:10]]), i.e., His providence is extended over everything that is on earth, as will be explained in the chapters in which we shall treat of Providence.

When, however, the word “eye” is connected with the verb “to see,” (raah or ḥazah) as in “Open thine eyes, and see” ([[I Kings 19#^19-16|1 Kings 19:16]]); “His eyes behold” ([[Psalms 011#^11-4|Ps. 11:4]]), the phrase denotes perception of the mind, not that of the senses: for every sensation is a passive state, as is well known to you, and God is active, never passive, as will be explained by me.
