## Chapter Six

The Blessed One continued:

\[1\] “He who performs the ritual
without attachment to its fruit
is a renunciant and *yogin*,
not he who neglects the ritual.

\[2\] What is called renunciation,
that is *Yoga*, son of Pandu.
Without renouncing all desire
no one can become a *yogin*.

\[3\] The sage who aspires to *Yoga*
attains it by activity.
One who has already attained it
maintains it by tranquility.

\[4\] Clinging neither to sense objects
nor attached to any action,
renouncing purpose and desire,
one is said to have attained *Yoga*.

\[5\] One should lift oneself by the Self
and not cause oneself to sink down.
The Self alone is oneself’s friend
and oneself’s only enemy.

\[6\] The Self is a friend to whoever
has conquered himself with the Self.
For one who hasn’t conquered oneself,
the Self remains an enemy.

[p. 38] \[7\] One’s self conquered, one’s highest Self
remains at peace, immovable
in cold or heat, pleasure or pain,
whether honored or dishonored.

\[8\] One content with knowing the Self,
unmoving, with senses conquered,
is called a *yogin*, one to whom
stones, clods, and gold are all the same.

\[9\] Unbiased toward friends and enemies,
seeing kinsman and foe as equal,
impartial to the good and base—
such a person is preeminent.

\[10\] A *yogin* should train constantly
to become at one with the Self,
in solitude, with mind controlled,
without desires or possessions.

\[11\] Having set up in a clean place
a firm seat, neither high nor low,
a seat of kusha grass covered
with deerskin and soft cloth on top,

\[12\] Sitting there and training his thought
on one point, controlling his mind
and his senses, let him practice
*Yoga* to purify his self.

\[13\] With body, neck, and head erect,
remaining steady and unmoving,
gazing at the tip of his nose,
not looking around anywhere,

\[14\] His self quieted, all fear gone,
firm in vows of celibacy,
[p. 39] with mind controlled, thoughts fixed on Me,
he sits steadfast, devoted to Me.

\[15\] Practicing constantly in this way,
a *yogin* with his mind subdued
enters into supreme *nirvana*,
supreme peace, at one with Me.

\[16\] *Yoga* means not eating too much,
but does not mean eating nothing,
not the habit of too much sleep,
nor, Arjuna, never sleeping.

\[17\] For the one who is disciplined
in eating, in activity,
in sleep, and in being awake,
*Yoga* is destruction of sorrow.

\[18\] When his mind is under control,
and he is absorbed in Self alone,
free of desire and of longing,
he is said to be a *yogin*.

\[19\] As a lantern out of the wind
does not flicker, so too the mind
of the *yogin* does not waver,
steady in *Yoga* of the Self.

\[20\] When his mind, restrained by *Yoga*,
becomes quiet in his practice,
when he himself perceives the Self
and in the Self becomes content,

\[21\] He knows infinite happiness,
grasped by mind, transcending senses,
and, established in his practice,
does not deviate from thusness.

[p. 40] \[22\] Having attained that, he cannot
imagine any greater gain.
Established there, he is not moved
even by profound suffering.

\[23\] Let this unyoking from sorrow
be called *Yoga*, known as *Yoga*,
practiced with determination,
with a mind that is undismayed.

\[24\] Having abandoned every desire
born of his will, each one of them,
the multitude of sense perceptions
restrained completely by his mind,

\[25\] Little by little becoming still,
awareness firmly in his grasp,
his mind established in the Self,
he should not think of anything.

\[26\] Whenever the unsteady mind
wanders away, moves to and fro,
he should restrain it, lead it back
to the Self to be controlled there.

\[27\] His mind at peace, the *yogin* then
approaches happiness supreme,
all of his passions pacified,
free of evil, one with *Brahman*.

\[28\] Practicing *Yoga* constantly,
the *yogin* then, free from evil,
readily encounters *Brahman*,
attaining boundless happiness.

\[29\] The person trained in *Yoga* sees
the Self present in all beings
[p. 41] and sees all beings in the Self.
He sees the same Self everywhere.

\[30\] He who sees Me in everything
and sees everything in Me,
that person is never lost to Me
and I am never lost to him.

\[31\] The *yogin*, in oneness grounded,
who reveres Me as indwelling all,
whatever else he may be doing
continues to abide in Me.

\[32\] The one who likens all beings
to his Self, sees them as equal,
whether in pleasure or in pain,
is thought of as a perfect *yogin*.”

Arjuna then said:

\[33\] “This *Yoga* of Equanimity
that you teach, O Slayer of Demons—
because of the mind’s restlessness,
I do not see how to maintain it.

\[34\] The mind, O Krishna, really is
restless, strong, and recalcitrant,
just as difficult to control,
I believe, as the wind itself.”

Krishna answered:

\[35\] “Without a doubt, mighty warrior,
the mind is restless, hard to restrain;
but through practice and dispassion
it can be brought under control.

[p. 42] \[36\] I too think *Yoga* is difficult—
for one lacking self-control;
but through self-control and effort,
and skillful means, it can be attained.”

Then Arjuna said:

\[37\] “What about someone who has faith,
but his mind wanders, and he has not
reached the pinnacle of *Yoga*,
what end, O Krishna, does he meet?

\[38\] Fallen from both paths, abandoned by
heaven and this world, O Krishna,
does he not vanish like a cloud,
deluded on the path to *Brahman*?

\[39\] Dispel this doubt of mine completely,
as you ought to, O Lord Krishna,
for no one will come forth but you
who can cut through this doubt of mine.”

And Krishna said:

\[40\] “Neither in this world, Arjuna,
nor in the next does he perish.
No one who performs good acts
will ever come to grief, my son.

\[41\] Having attained pure, righteous realms,
he will dwell there for countless years
after falling from *Yoga*’s heights,
then be reborn in a noble house.

\[42\] Or he may even be reborn
in a family of wise *yogins*,
[p. 43] a birth that is very difficult
for one to meet with in this world.

\[43\] He then connects with the knowledge
acquired in his former body
and strives harder than before
for perfection, O Arjuna.

\[44\] He cannot help being borne along
by his practice in past lives.
Even the wish to know *Yoga*
is superior to the *Vedas*.

\[45\] But it is the *yogin* who strives
for purity and perfection
assiduously through many births
who will reach the ultimate goal.

\[46\] The *yogin* is superior
to ascetics, to the learned,
and to those who practice ritual.
Therefore, Arjuna, be a *yogin*.

\[47\] And of all *yogins*, that one who
has great faith and whose inner self
is merged with Me, who worships Me,
is deemed by Me the most devout.”
