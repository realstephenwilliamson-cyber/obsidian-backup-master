### 27.1–10: Judas’ suicide

*[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-1|Text]], p. 62* ^27-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-1|1–2]]:** [[Mark 15#^15-1|Mk 15.1]]; [[Luke 23#^23-1|Lk 23.1]]. *Pilate*, Roman governor in Judea from 18–36 CE, known for cruelty (Tacitus, *Ann*. 15.44; Josephus, *J. W.* 2.166–77; *Ant*. 18.35,55–64,85–89,177; see “Jewish History, 331 BCE–135 CE”). ^27-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-3|3]]:** *He repented*, a point unique to Matthew. *Thirty pieces of silver*, see 26.15n. ^27-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-4|4]]:** *Innocent blood*, see [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-25|Deut 27.25]]; [[Jeremiah 19#^19-4|Jer 19.4]]. Judas (27.4), Jewish leaders (26.59), Pilate (27.23–25), Pilate’s wife (27.19), crowds (27.18), and the centurion (27.54) proclaim Jesus’ righteousness and innocence. ^27-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-5|5]]:** *Hanged himself*, Matthew is the only Gospel that includes this account (contrast [[Acts 01#^1-16|Acts 1.16–20]]), paralleling the death of David’s betrayer, Ahithophel ([[II Samuel 17#^17-23|2 Sam 17.23]]). ^27-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-6|6]]:** *Blood money*, money related to death or immorality cannot be used for holy purposes (see [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-19|Deut 23.18–19]]; a prostitute’s fee may not be brought into the “house of the Lord”). ^27-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-9|9–10]]:** Combining [[Zechariah 11#^11-12|Zech 11.12–13]]; [[Jeremiah 18#^18-1|Jer 18.1–19]]; [[Jeremiah 32#^32-6|32.6–15]]. **27**. ^27-9

> [!note] The Blood Cry *(p. 62)*
> Only in [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]] are “all the people” (Gk *pas ho laos*) depicted as clamoring for Jesus’ crucifixion and insisting, when Pilate demurs, “His blood be on us and on our children.” The phrase, notoriously referred to as “the blood cry,” had been interpreted by Christians throughout the centuries to mean that all Jews, at all times and places, were to be held accountable for Jesus’ death. The earliest record of this belief may date back to [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|1 Thess 2.14–16]] in the mid-first century CE, in which Paul blames “the Jews” for the death of Jesus, although the meaning of “the Jews” there is questionable, especially since Paul was a Jew himself. Paul was likely referring to the Jewish leaders, to a select few community members, or to “Judeans,” here meaning only people in Judea (see “*Ioudaios*,” p. 596). By the time of Justin Martyr in the second century, the belief that the Jews in general were accountable for Jesus’ death is more clear (see Justin, *Dial*. 16; see also *Gos. Pet*. 1,20–27, “Then the Jews and the elders and the priests, having come to know how much wrong they had done themselves . . .” implies general blame for Jesus’ death). Even in antiquity, however, some disagreed with this reading (e.g., J. Chrys., *Hom. Matt.* 86.2, in part, citing that St. Paul was a Jew himself). With the promulgation of the Vatican II document “Nostra Aetate” in 1965, the Roman Catholic Church and, subsequently, many other Christian groups, rejected this view of collective guilt.
>
> Probably drawn from earlier Scriptures ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-9|Lev 20.9]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-10|Deut 19.10]]; [[Joshua 02#^2-19|Josh 2.19]]; [[II Samuel 01#^1-16|2 Sam 1.16]]; esp. [[Jeremiah 26#^26-15|Jer 26.15]], “you will be bringing innocent blood upon yourselves”; cf [[Acts 05#^5-28|Acts 5.28]]), the verse for Matthew is more likely about prophetic fulfillment than a universal condemnation of Jews. Further, for Matthew’s own context, it was “all the people” of Jerusalem, and their children, who witnessed the destruction of the city in 70 CE. Some scholars suggest Matthew meant also to suggest an irony: the crowd, unknowingly, calls Jesus’ salvific blood upon themselves (see [[Matthew 26#^26-28|Mt 26.28]]).

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-11|11–26]]:** Jesus before Pilate ([[Mark 15#^15-2|Mk 15.2]]**–**5; 23.1**–**7,13**–**25; [[John 18#^18-29|Jn 18.29]]**–**19.16). ^27-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-11|11]]:** *King of the Jews*, Pilate would view a Jewish “king” as a threat to the Empire. ^27-11-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-14|14]]:** *Gave him no answer*, see 26.63n.; [[1 Timothy 06#^6-13|1 Tim 6.13]]. ^27-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-15|15]]:** *Release a prisoner*, Rome sometimes released prisoners but not those “plainly worthy of death” (Josephus, *Ant*. 20.215; cf. *m. Pesah.* 8.6). ^27-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-16|16]]:** See [[Mark 15#^15-7|Mk 15.7]]; [[Luke 23#^23-25|Lk 23.25]]. Rome would not release a *notorious prisoner. Jesus Barabbas*, Aramaic, lit., “son of \[the/a\] father.” Some early manuscripts lack “Jesus.” The name “Barabbas” appears in rabbinic literature, in particular, an Abba bar Abba (“father son of the father”), the father of a famous scholar named Samuel; see *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 18#18b|18b]]. The names make a theological point: the innocent “Jesus ‘son of the father’” pays the ransom for his guilty counterpart. ^27-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-19|19–20]]:** *His wife*, mentioned only by Matthew (see *Acts Pil*. 2.1). Her *dream* recollects Joseph (Mt 1**–**2). ^27-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-22|22]]:** *Crucified*, Roman execution for non-citizens and insurrectionaries, sometimes in large groups; crucifixion was usually preceded by scourging (Josephus, *J.W.* 5.11.449**–**51). The victim was nailed (through the wrists) or bound to the transverse cross beam; the punishment included public exposure (victims were often naked) and the lingering nature of the death, by asphyxiation, as the victim’s exhaustion eventually made it impossible to raise the body in order to breathe. Torah mandates that one who is punished by being impaled upon a stake must be buried on the same day ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-22|Deut 21.22]]**–**23). The wicked Haman was also impaled on a stake ([[Esther 07#^7-10|Esth 7.10]]), along with his ten sons (10.13**–**14). ^27-22

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-24|24]]:** *Washed his hands*, mentioned only by Matthew. The ritual is linked to purification or redemption ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-1|Deut 21.1]]**–**9; [[Psalms 026#^26-6|Ps 26.6]]; [[Psalms 073#^73-13|73.13]]). Pilate, failing to uphold justice, is complicit in Jesus’ execution. ^27-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-25|25]]:** *His blood be on us and our children*, this unique Matthean verse resulted in Christians, throughout the generations, blaming Jews for Jesus’ or *this righteous man’s blood* death (see 21.37-39nn.). Matthew’s first readers likely related the verse to the Jerusalem population, killed or enslaved by the Romans as result of the First Revolt against Rome in 66**–**70 CE. Early followers of Jesus may have viewed the devastation as punishment for condemning Jesus. ^27-25

### 27.27–54: The Crucifixion

*[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-27|Text]], p. 64* · ([[Mark 15#^15-16|Mk 15.16–41]]; [[Luke 23#^23-26|Lk 23.26–49]]; [[John 19#^19-17|Jn 19.17–37]]). ^27-27

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-27|27–31]]:** *Mocking* ([[Mark 15#^15-16|Mk 15.16–20]]; [[Luke 23#^23-26|Lk 23.26]]–-32; [[John 19#^19-1|Jn 19.1–2]]; *Gos. Pet*. 2.5–3.9), Matthew’s theme of prophetic fulfillment continues (20.19; see also [[Isaiah 50#^50-6|Isa 50.6]]; [[Jeremiah 48#^48-17|Jer 48.17]]; cf. [[Psalms 022#^22-8|Ps 22.7]]). ^27-27-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-27|27]]:** *Cohort*, around 600 men. ^27-27-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-28|28]]:** *Scarlet robe*, a Roman soldier’s tunic, contrast with the other Gospels’ “purple,” a royal color; see [[Mark 15#^15-17|Mk 15.17]]; [[John 19#^19-2|Jn 19.2]]. ^27-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-32|32]]:** *Cyrene*, in modern-day Libya; home to a large Jewish community in the first century. ^27-32

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-33|33]]:** *Golgotha,* Gk transliteration of the Aram “skull.” The reason for the name is unclear. It could refer to the shape of the hill. ^27-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-34|34]]:** *Offered him wine*, to dull the pain ([[Proverbs 31#^31-6|Prov 31.6]]; *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 43#43a|43a]]; see also [[Mark 15#^15-23|Mk 15.23]]). *He would not drink it*, see 26.29. ^27-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-35|35]]:** *Divided his clothes*, [[Psalms 022#^22-19|Ps 22.18]]. ^27-35

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-38|38]]:** *Two bandits*, see 20.21n. ^27-38

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-39|39]]:** *Derided him* (lit., “blasphemed him”), 9.3n.; [[Psalms 022#^22-8|Ps 22.7–8]]; 109.25. ^27-39

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-40|40]]:** *Destroy the temple*, see 1.11n., 23.38n., 24.1–2n. *If you are the Son of God*, recalls the devil’s challenge (4.3,5; see also 26.61). ^27-40

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-46|46]]:** *Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani*, see [[Psalms 022#^22-2|Ps 22.1]]. The first two words are in Hebrew, possibly to recall the familiar Hebrew psalm, followed by Aramaic, the vernacular of the period; the psalm, which informs much of the description of the crucifixion, ends on a triumphal note. ^27-46

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-47|47]]:** *Elijah* (Heb “Eli-ya-hu”), the prophet expected to announce the messianic age (11.14–15n.). According to rabbinic tradition, Elijah aids the righteous in their time of need *(b. Avod. Zar.* 17b; *b. Taʾan.* 21a). ^27-47

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-51|51–53]]:** See [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^26-31|Ex 26.31–35]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-8|Heb 9.8]]; *had been spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled, “They divided my clothes among themselves, and for my clothing they cast lots.”* c Or *blasphemed* d Or *is he unable to save himself?* e Or *earth* f Other ancient authorities add *And another took a spear and pierced his side, and out came water and blood* 10.19. ^27-51

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-48|48]]:** *Sour wine*, see [[Psalms 069#^69-22|Ps 69.21]]. ^27-48

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-51|51]]:** *Curtain of the temple was torn in two,* could symbolize the Temple’s destruction, Jesus’ role as the “forgiver of sins” and the “Divine Presence,” thereby replacing the Temple (12.6; 21.12–13n.; 23.38n.; see also 1.23; 18.20; 28.20; cf. *Acts Pil*. 11.1), or divine mourning. *Earth shook*, Gk “seismos,” lit., “earthquake” (see 8.24n.; 24.7; 28.2; see also [[Judges 05#^5-4|Judg 5.4]]; [[Isaiah 24#^24-19|Isa 24.19–22]]; [[Isaiah 29#^29-6|29.6]]; [[Psalms 018#^18-7|Ps 18.6–8]]; [[Revelation 06#^6-12|Rev 6.12]]; [[Revelation 08#^8-5|8.5]]; [[Revelation 11#^11-13|11.13]],[[Revelation 11#^11-19|19]]). No non-Christian sources record these events; the emphasis here is on theology (rather than on a geological event). ^27-51-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-52|52]]:** *Saints,* Gk “hagioi,” lit., “holy ones.” *Were raised*, many Jews expected a general resurrection as part of the messianic age ([[John 11#^11-24|Jn 11.24]]). ^27-52

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-54|54]]:** *Centurion*, see 8.8,10nn. ^27-54

### 27.55–56: Women witnesses

*[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-55|Text]], p. 65* ^27-55

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-55|55]]:** *Provided for him*, women served as patrons ([[Luke 08#^8-1|Lk 8.1–3]]). Matthew mentions women followers by name for the first time (27.61). *Mary Magdalene*, Magdala, also known as Tarichaea, village in Galilee known for processing fish. *Mary the mother of James and Joseph*, otherwise unknown. *Mother of the sons of Zebedee*, see 20.20n. **27**. ^27-55-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-57|57–61]]:** Jesus’ entombment ([[Mark 15#^15-42|Mk 15.42–47]]; [[Luke 23#^23-50|Lk 23.50–56]]; [[John 19#^19-38|Jn 19.38–42]]). ^27-57

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-57|57–58]]:** *When it was evening*, burials were not permitted on the Sabbath (*m. Shabb*. 23.5; see also [[John 19#^19-31|Jn 19.31]]). Jewish law requires that the deceased be buried quickly ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-22|Deut 21.22–23]]; see also [[LXX Tobit 01#^1-17|Tob 1.17–18]]; [[LXX Tobit 02#^2-3|2.3–7]]; *m. Ber*. 3.1; *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 14#14b|14b]]). See “The Burial of Jesus,” p. 677. No mention is made of what happens to the other crucified men. *Arimathea*, Judean town of uncertain location. *Rich*, see [[Isaiah 53#^53-9|Isa 53.9]]. Later Christian traditions, perhaps originating with the English historian William of Malmesbury in the twelfth century, have Joseph conducting missionary work in Britain. ^27-57-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-61|61]]:** The wife of Zebedee is not mentioned as going to the tomb. ^27-61

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-62|27.62–66]]:** **Pharisees ironically remember the prediction.** ^27-62

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-62|62]]:** *Day of Preparation*, Gk “paraskeuē,” “making ready,” an ambiguous term. It appears in the NT here and [[Mark 15#^15-42|Mk 15.42]]; [[Luke 23#^23-54|Lk 23.54]], where it means the day preceding the Sabbath (Passover has already begun); in [[John 19#^19-14|Jn 19.14]],[[John 19#^19-31|31]],[[John 19#^19-42|42]] it means the day preceding Passover, although [[John 19#^19-42|Jn 19.42]] implies that Passover fell on a Sabbath. Matthew is likely recalling [[Mark 15#^15-42|Mk 15.42]] here, perhaps to acknowledge that the crucifixion took place on a Friday. ^27-62-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-63|63]]:** *After three days . . .* , see [[Hosea 06#^6-2|Hos 6.2]]; cf. *Gos. Pet*. 8.28–32. ^27-63

**[[NRSVue Matthew 27#^27-64|64]]:** *Command the tomb to be made secure*, see [[Daniel 06#^6-18|Dan 6.17]]; *Apoc. Mos*. 42.1. ^27-64
