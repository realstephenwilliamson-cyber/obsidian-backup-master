### 12.1–13: Divine discipline

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 485* ^12-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-1|1]]:** *Cloud*, common metaphor for “crowd” in Greco-Roman literature (Herodotus 8.109; *Vit. Phil*. 7.16). *Witnesses*, Gk (pl.) “martyrōn,” origin of the term “martyr.” *Weight . . . sin*, an occasional comparison found in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Psalms 038#^38-5|Ps 38.4]]). *Race*, another common metaphor in Greco-Roman literature, found also in NT exhortations; see e.g., [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-24|1 Cor 9.24–27]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-7|2 Tim 4.7]]. ^12-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-2|2]]:** *Pioneer and perfecter*, see 2.10. *Right hand of the throne*, see [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]; also [[Hebrews 01#^1-3|Heb 1.3]],[[Hebrews 01#^1-13|13]]; [[Hebrews 08#^8-1|8.1]]; [[Hebrews 10#^10-12|10.12]]. ^12-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-3|3–4]]:** *Endured . . . hostility . . . resisted . . . shedding your blood*, continuing with the comparison between the audience and Jesus, the writer points out that Jesus endured mockery ([[Mark 15#^15-16|Mk 15.16–20]]; see [[Isaiah 53#^53-3|Isa 53.3]]; [[Psalms 022#^22-8|Ps 22.7]]) and was martyred by crucifixion. *Not yet resisted*, whatever hardship the audience has endured, it pales in comparison to Jesus’ suffering. ^12-3

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-5|5–11]]:** Suffering is understood as instructional *discipline* administered by God; cf. [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-12|2 Macc 6.12–17]]; *Sifre Deut.* 311; see “Perfection through Suffering,” p. 464. ^12-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-5|5–6]]:** [[LXX Proverbs 03#^3-11|Prov 3.11–12]]\[LXX\]; the MT describes the Lord instructing “as a father the son in whom he delights”; the LXX, likely reading the Hebrew differently, states that the Lord *chastises every child whom he accepts.* ^12-5-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-8|8]]:** *Illegitimate*, bastards. ^12-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-9|9]]:** *Human parents*, lit., “fathers of our flesh.” *Father of spirits*, God; father of human spirits, contrasts with “fathers of our flesh.” ^12-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-12|12]]:** Citing [[Isaiah 35#^35-3|Isa 35.3]]. ^12-12

### 12.14–29: Final warnings

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-14|Text]], p. 485* ^12-14

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-14|14]]:** *Pursue peace*, a common exhortation; see [[Psalms 034#^34-15|Ps 34.14]] (HB v 15); [[Romans 14#^14-19|Rom 14.19]]; *m. Avot* 1.12 (“Hillel would say: ‘Be of the disciples of Aaron—a lover of peace, a pursuer of peace, one who loves the creatures and draws them close to Torah’”). *See the Lord*, achieve salvation. ^12-14-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-15|15]]:** *Grace of God*, perhaps referring to God’s action through Jesus; cf. 2.9. *Root of bitterness,* possibly an allusion to [[LXX Deuteronomy 29#^29-17|Deut 29.17]]\[LXX\]. ^12-15

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-16|16]]:** *Esau* . . . *birthright*, see [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^25-29|Gen 25.29–34]]. *Immoral*, Gk “pornos,” meaning sexually *immoral*. Postbiblical traditions portray Esau as Rome, and later as Christianity, as more wicked than depicted in the Tanakh, the opposite of his brother Jacob, though no texts describe him as “pornos”; see Philo, *Virtues* 208; *b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 101#101b|101b]]; *Gen. Rab.* 37,40,43,45. ^12-16

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-17|17]]:** *Inherit the blessing*, Gen 27. *Found no chance distracts* b Or *who instead of* c Other ancient authorities read *such hostility from sinners against themselves to repent*, see 6.4–8; 10.26–31. *With tears*, Esau’s grief when he learns his brother has stolen his blessing, [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^27-38|Gen 27.38]]. ^12-17

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-18|18–21]]:** Sinaitic imagery; see Ex 19; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-10|Deut 4.10–15]]. ^12-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-19|19]]:** *Voice*, in contrast to [[Hebrews 12#^12-19|Heb 12.19]], Maimonides claimed that Israel’s experiencing of the divine voice guaranteed that the Torah was of divine origin (see *Mishneh Torah*, *Hilkhot Yesodei ha-Torah* 8.1), see also *Mek.* “Bakhodesh” 4, comment on [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-19|Ex 19.19]]. ^12-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-20|20]]:** [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-12|Ex 19.12–13]]. ^12-20

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-21|21]]:** [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^9-19|Deut 9.19]], moved to a new context; on Moses’ fear, see also *b. Shabb*. [[Shabbat 88#88b|88b]]. ^12-21

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-22|22]]:** *Mount Zion*, the mountain in Jerusalem where God dwells; site of the Jerusalem Temple; the image is in contrast to descriptions of Sinai in vv 18–21; cf. [[Galatians 04#^4-21|Gal 4.21–31]]. *Living God*, see 3.12; 9.14; 10.31. *Heavenly Jerusalem*, although *Jerusalem* is not earlier named explicitly, the heavenly city was mentioned; see 11.10,16. *Innumerable angels*, angels are often part of theophanies; see [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^33-2|Deut 33.2]] (referring specifically to Sinai); [[Daniel 07#^7-10|Dan 7.10]]; [[Revelation 05#^5-11|Rev 5.11]]; *1 En.* 13.8; in 4QShirShabba angels singing God’s praise assemble in heaven for Sabbath worship. See “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682. ^12-22

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-23|23]]:** *Firstborn*, though possibly meaning the angels, it more likely refers to humans. *Firstborn* is used of human beings as a term of endearment, expressing the special relationship God has with human beings as their creator (see 1.6n.). The term may also resonate with the story of Jacob’s usurpation of Esau’s rightful inheritance as the firstborn (vv 14–17). *Enrolled*, in Greco-Roman cities, citizens were registered shortly after birth to record their status and thus ensure their legal and social privileges; following a Mesopotamian model, Jewish tradition sometimes describes the righteous as “inscribed” in a heavenly book; see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-32|Ex 32.32]]; [[Psalms 069#^69-29|Ps 69.29]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1]]. The image is ubiquitous in the Rosh Ha-Shanah and Yom Kippur liturgy. ^12-23

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-24|24]]:** *Mediator of a new covenant*, see chs 8–9. *Sprinkled blood*, see 9.11–28; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-4|Ex 24.4–8]]. *Blood of Abel*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-10|Gen 4.10]]; [[Torah 02 - Noach#^11-4|11.4]]. ^12-24

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-25|25]]:** *One who is speaking*, most likely God (not Christ, but see 11.4); so 1.1. *They did not escape*, referring to the wilderness generation. A reader might infer that Hebrews is referring to antediluvian humanity since Abel has just been mentioned, but the imagery that pervades this section comes from the story and traditions of Sinai (see 18–21n.; 22n.). *One who warned them*, most likely Moses, so vv 18–21. ^12-25

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-26|26–27]]:** *Voice shook the earth*, God’s voice is as thunder, [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-16|Ex 19.16–19]]. *Yet once more*, [[LXX Haggai 02#^2-6|Hag 2.6]]\[LXX\] implies *removal* because Hebrews takes “once” to mean that God’s shaking of heaven and earth happens only once; see 6.4. ^12-26

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-27|27]]:** *What cannot be shaken*, the heavenly realm, what remains once the material world passes away. ^12-27

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 12#^12-29|29]]:** *Consuming fire*, citing [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-24|Deut 4.24]] and 9.3, an image that inspires the fearsome nature of God, but here the phrase also evokes sacrificial offerings. *assembly* ^12-29
