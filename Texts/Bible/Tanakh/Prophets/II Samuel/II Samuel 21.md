## Chapter 21

**21:1** There was a famine during the reign of David, year after year for three years. David inquired of the LORD, and the LORD replied, “It is because of the bloodguilt of Saul and [his] house, for he put some Gibeonites to death.” ^21-1

**21:2** The king summoned the Gibeonites and spoke to them.—Now the Gibeonites were not of Israelite stock, but a remnant of the Amorites, to whom the Israelites had given an oath; and Saul had tried to wipe them out in his zeal for the people of Israel and Judah.— ^21-2

**21:3** David asked the Gibeonites, “What shall I do for you? How shall I make expiation, so that you may bless the LORD’s own people?” ^21-3

**21:4** The Gibeonites answered him, “We have no claim for silver or gold against Saul and his household; and we have no claim on the life of any other man in Israel.” And [David] responded, “Whatever you say I will do for you.” ^21-4

**21:5** Thereupon they said to the king, “The man who massacred us and planned to exterminate us, so that we[^1] should not survive in all the territory of Israel— ^21-5

**21:6** let seven of his male issue be handed over to us, and we will impale them before the LORD in Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of the LORD.”[^2] And the king replied, “I will do so.” ^21-6

**21:7** The king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul, because of the oath before the LORD between the two, between David and Jonathan son of Saul. ^21-7

**21:8** Instead, the king took Armoni and Mephibosheth, the two sons that Rizpah daughter of Aiah bore to Saul, and the five sons that Merab[^3] daughter of Saul bore to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite, ^21-8

**21:9** and he handed them over to the Gibeonites. They impaled them on the mountain before the LORD; all seven of them perished at the same time. They were put to death in the first days of the harvest, the beginning of the barley harvest. ^21-9

**21:10** Then Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it on a rock for herself, and she stayed there from the beginning of the harvest until rain from the sky fell on the bodies;[^4] she did not let the birds of the sky settle on them by day or the wild beasts [approach] by night. ^21-10

**21:11** David was told what Saul’s concubine Rizpah daughter of Aiah had done. ^21-11

**21:12** And David went and took the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan from the citizens of Jabesh-gilead, who had made off with them from the public square of Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them up on the day the Philistines killed Saul at Gilboa. ^21-12

**21:13** He brought up the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan from there; and he gathered the bones of those who had been impaled. ^21-13

**21:14** And they buried the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan[^5] in Zela, in the territory of Benjamin, in the tomb of his father Kish. And when all that the king had commanded was done, God responded to the plea of the land thereafter. ^21-14

**21:15** Again war broke out between the Philistines and Israel, and David and the men with him went down and fought the Philistines; David grew weary, ^21-15

**21:16** and Ishbi-benob[^6] tried to kill David.—He was a descendant of the Raphah;[^7] his bronze spear weighed three hundred shekels and he wore new armor.— ^21-16

**21:17** But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his aid; he attacked the Philistine and killed him. It was then that David’s men declared to him on oath, “You shall not go with us into battle any more, lest you extinguish the lamp of Israel!” ^21-17

**21:18** [^8]After this, fighting broke out again with the Philistines, at Gob; that was when Sibbecai the Hushathite killed Saph, a descendant of the Raphah.[^9] ^21-18

**21:19** Again there was fighting with the Philistines at Gob; and Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim[^10] the Bethlehemite killed Goliath the Gittite, whose spear had a shaft like a weaver’s bar. ^21-19

**21:20** Once again there was fighting, at Gath. There was a giant of a man,[^11] who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in all; he too was descended from the Raphah. ^21-20

**21:21** When he taunted Israel, Jonathan, the son of David’s brother Shimei, killed him. ^21-21

**21:22** Those four were descended from the Raphah in Gath, and they fell by the hands of David and his men. ^21-22

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^2]: Emendation yields “at Gibeon, on the mountain of the Lord” (cf. Septuagint and v. 9).
[^3]: So two Heb. mss., many Septuagint mss., and Peshitta; and cf. Targum, [[Sanhedrin 19#19b|Sanhedrin 19b]], and 1 Sam. 18.19. Most mss. and the printed editions read “Michal.”
[^4]: Heb. “them.”
[^5]: Septuagint adds “and the bones of those impaled.”
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^7]: Apparently a race of giants.
[^8]: This paragraph is found also in 1 Chron. 20.4–8; in part, also in 1QSama, with some variations.
[^9]: Apparently a race of giants.
[^10]: Perhaps a duplicate of ’oregim (“weavers”) at the end of the verse; meaning of Heb. uncertain. 1 Chron. 20.5 reads “And Elhanan son of Jair killed Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite.”
[^11]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
