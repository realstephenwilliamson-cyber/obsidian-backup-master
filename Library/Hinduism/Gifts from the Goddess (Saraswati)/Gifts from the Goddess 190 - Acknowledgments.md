## Acknowledgments

*Acknowledgments*

First and most of all, I would like to thank Guruji for allowing me to take this wonderful, terrible, difficult, engaging, fascinating, overwhelming, humbling and enlightening journey with him. To the very end of his physical stay among us on Earth, he supported and encouraged the development and execution of this project—both *The Goddess and the Guru* and *Gifts from the Goddess*, though neither published until after his passing (in keeping, I suspect, with his intentions). Sri Uma-Parvathinanda Natha Saraswati not only penned a stunning foreword but also shared powerful advice and encouragement at several key junctures during the book’s development.

Thanks also to Guruji’s wife Annapūrna (Guruji Amma), and their daughters Anantalakshmi Pisipati, Radha Marthi and Rama Kandarpa for their ongoing support of and engagement with this work. As she did for *The Goddess and the Guru*, Anantalakshmi once again organized the long-distance exchange of critical materials. Radha and her husband, Balkumar, shared a stash of newly recovered Guruji manuscripts dating back to 1992. These papers had gone missing while Guruji and Amma were visiting the couple’s home in the Netherlands, only to be discovered by a worker behind some built-in shelving 25 years later, when they were preparing to retire to India. Rama, her husband, Prabhakar, and son, Prajna, visited me at home when I was very ill and unable to attend the release party for *The Goddess and the Guru* in upstate New York. Haran Aiya of Sri Rajarajeswari Peetam once again offered essential advice and input at crucial moments throughout the process.

Existential thanks go out to my longtime editorial assistant Megha Gupta Chatterjee, who brought her formidable linguistic skill and astonishing stamina to this project, far beyond the call of duty. A devoted *śiṣya* of Haran Aiya, she painstakingly managed the insertion of IAST diacritical marks, while also checking Guruji’s (often obscure) Sanskrit usages and references against multiple sources for accuracy. Whenever she was stumped, Sankara Devarakonda of Sri Rajarajeswari Peetam would kindly and reliably step in to save the day.

Once again, this volume has been graced by the transcendent photography of Julianne Reynolds, Giri Ratna Singh and Oleg Zinkovetsky. My deepest thanks,

appreciation and respect to each of them (and to Oleg for his video work and friendship as well—our road trips are always a blast, *mоварищ*).

A very special round of thanks goes out to all the teachers who contributed essays on their experiences with Guruji—notably including Guruji’s sister-in-law Sundari Amma, whose legendary *satsangs* still draw crowds around the world; Balasingam Janahan, Uma Prabha Menon and Bill Thomas, all early disciples who have shared Guruji’s work with vast numbers of people over the years; Mani and Prasanna Avasarala, who are tireless in spreading Guruji’s ritual teachings in India and beyond (including my own chilly New England backyard—in December!); and Sergey Babkin, who has made extraordinary efforts in translating and propagating Guruji’s work in Russia and across the former Soviet states. Also to Max Stesenko in Kiev, who is preparing the Russian edition of *The Goddess and the Guru* for publication as I write this. I bow humbly as well before the illustrious authors, academics and other busy souls who were kind enough to generously give of their valuable time to pre-read and endorse this work.

Huge thanks to Devi Parvati, for so generously sharing her experience of helping Guruji write *USCP*, and for giving me a clearer sense of Guruji’s original vision for that publication; to Karthika Sugumaran for her patient ritual explanations, unflagging support and infectious good cheer; to Anastasia Nekrasova, for resurfacing after, oh, 25 years or so, to make several transformative contributions to this volume; and to N. Sankara Menon, my first Sri Vidya teacher, who put me on this roller-coaster by introducing me to Guruji in the first place—not to mention contributing some essential notes on this work that waited a decade or so before revealing themselves magnificently the moment my mind was ready to comprehend them. I would also single out Elethea “Ekta” Barraclough and Lalita Sarma for their important contributions and support. Most of all, thanks to everyone who supported the advance campaign for this book and for *The Goddess and the Guru*, and most especially to those of you who took the time to write and share your thoughts and experiences upon reading the first book. It is your interest, enthusiasm and support for Guruji and for this project that has most tangibly made these beautiful publications possible.

Warm appreciation to the entire team at Talk Science To Me, in particular Eve Rickert—who was instrumental to producing *The Goddess and the Guru*, but took a

During the preparation of *Gifts from the Goddess*, I lost my mother, Mary Teresa Howell Bowden, to a long and difficult battle with Alzheimer’s disease. An old-school Irish girl, she was sweetly open-minded toward and genuinely interested in this project (she even added an image of Guruji and a Lakshmi yantra to her precious collection of Roman Catholic devotional items). I sorely miss her warmth, conversation and laughter.

Last but certainly not least, love and gratitude go out to my wife, friend and longtime co-conspirator, Dr. Anna Pavlotsky, who once again gave me the time and space I needed to complete work on this very big, very complex book—despite a persistent barrage of often urgently competing demands and crises, from the mundane to the life-threatening (and all amid her own elemental exile’s journey of return). Love and thanks also to our sons, Alex and Dylan, who have—each in his own way—supported this work across many years. I am proud of your accomplishments, and even prouder of the fine men you have become and the worthwhile things you will accomplish in life. 

*И наконец для Софии, мой ангел дождя, еще раз спасибо за заботу о папе*.

Michael M. Bowden Boston October 2018
