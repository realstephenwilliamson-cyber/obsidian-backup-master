### 14.1–5: The 144,000

*[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-1|Text]], p. 561* · The unwholesome spectacle of the beast’s marked followers is balanced by the heavenly spectacle of the 144,000 with divine marks. (See “Names Inscribed on the Body,” p. 561.) ^14-1

> [!note] Names Inscribed on the Body *(p. 561)*
> In one of Revelation’s most mysterious and distinctive features, John imagines the righteous of Israel as “sealed” \[Gk *sphragizein*\] on their foreheads with the name of God and the Lamb (7.3–4; 14.1; 22.4; cf. *4 Ezra* 6.5), and the unrighteous, the dupes of the beast, as likewise receiving “marks” \[Gk *charagma*\] on the forehead or right hand with the encrypt-ed name of the Beast (13.16–18). The great whore of Babylon also has inscribed on her forehead a name—“a mystery”—that rather unmysterious-ly identifies her as “Babylon the great, mother of whores and of earth’s abominations” (17.5).
>
> Whether labeled seals, marks, or simply names, these insignia have functions beyond identifying their bearers. The sealing of the righteous follows explicitly from a scene in Ezekiel in which God calls an angel to put a *taw* (or X-mark) on the foreheads of everyone in Jerusalem who rejected the desecration of the Temple; only those inscribed will escape the executioner angels (Ezek 9). As with the doorpost marks that, in the Passover story of Exodus, safeguarded the Israelites from the slaying of the firstborn ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-21|Ex 12.21–27]]), these forehead marks are apotropaic, protecting their bearers from supernatural dangers.
>
> The magically protective and empowering force of seals with heavenly names appears in Greek ritual manuals from Roman Egypt (*Papyri Graecae Magicae* 3.226; 4.3039; 7.583; 36.39). In many Jewish and Christian apocalyptic texts the bearers of such seals are protected, during heavenly ascent, from the demons of the atmosphere and the lower ranks of angels (*Ascen. Isa.* 10.23–31; *Apoc. El*. 1.9–12; *Hekhalot Zutrati* 415–16; *Hekhalot Rabbati* 219–24). Some books, like the Gnostic *Books of Jeu*, even included diagrams of the seals to be drawn on the body or inscribed on metal or gemstone. Many museums hold large collections of such inscribed gems that may have served as protective “seals.” In Jewish tradition, the Tetragrammaton held special power to sanctify the bearer without diagrams, an idea that influenced later traditions that a golem could be activated through the inscription of a holy name.
>
> John carefully distinguishes the number 666 inscribed on the unrighteous as a *charagma* (“mark”) rather than a *sphragis* (“seal,” e.g., 13.16–17), and this choice of word might refer also to slave-branding in Roman antiquity, or simply to coinage. The number is, as John makes clear, a name, and while it does not protect its bearers from divine judgment, it does protect their ability to buy and sell goods—to mark them as consecrated insiders of their own sorts—and thus serves to parody the seals on the followers of the Lamb (14.1).

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-2|2]]:** *Many waters*, see 1.15n. ^14-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-3|3]]:** *Living creatures*, see 4.6n. ^14-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-4|4]]:** *Defiled themselves*, the purity of the 144,000 is indicated by their celibacy, which reflects Jewish traditions of priestly or holy war purity (1QM 7.3–7; on regulations as to sexual purity and abstinence in the “camp” and for the priests, see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-15|Ex 19.15]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-1|Lev 22.1–9]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-11|Deut 23.10–11]]; see [[Revelation 20#^20-9|Rev 20.9]]). John phrases celibacy not as the impure spilling of semen (as in Deut 23) but rather as *not defiled themselves with women*, which recalls the pollution the Watcher-angels incurred through sexual activity with human women (*1 En.* 7.1; 10.11; 15.3; 69.5). The implication is that the 144,000 are like angels, which early Jews considered to be male, and whose purity women would threaten. *First fruits*, harvest offering meant to sanctify the totality of the harvest (see, e.g., [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-19|Ex 23.19]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-22|34.22]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-9|Lev 23.9–14]]), signifying that the 144,000 are the first people to be redeemed and, as “fruits,” are dedicated for God (see also [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-23|1 Cor 15.23]]). ^14-4

### 14.6–11: Angelic announcements of judgment

*[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-6|Text]], p. 562* ^14-6

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-6|6]]:** *Eternal Gospels,* (Gk “euangelion,” as in [[Mark 01#^1-1|Mk 1.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-1|1 Cor 15.1]]), meant in the more general sense of “proclamation” of proper obeisance to God, lacking particular reference to Jesus. ^14-6-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-7|7]]:** *Fear God . . . springs of water*, the content of the “gospel” or proclamation. ^14-7

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-8|8]]:** Based on [[Isaiah 21#^21-9|Isa 21.9]], this announcement foreshadows the appearance of the whore *Babylon* (i.e., Rome) in chs 17–19 (see *4 Ezra* 3.1–2). *Wine of the wrath*, [[Jeremiah 25#^25-15|Jer 25.15–38]]. ^14-8

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-9|9–11]]:** The third angel’s declaration of imminent torment for the beast’s followers functions also as a curse, consigning them to eschatological torment. ^14-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-9|9]]:** *Mark*, see 13.15–17. ^14-9-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-10|10]]:** *Wine*, see v 8n. *Unmixed*, wine was typically diluted with water; unmixed wine was especially potent. *Fire and sulfur,* as in the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-24|Gen 19.24]]) and Gog and Magog ([[Ezekiel 38#^38-22|Ezek 38.22]]). The older name for *sulfur*, “brimstone,” survives in the phrase “fire and brimstone” (as in the KJV). ^14-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-12|14.12–13]]:** **Rewards of the saints.** ^14-12

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-12|12]]:** *Commandments*, Gk “entolas”—the translation of Heb “mitzvot.” John regards the redeemed saints as those who follow Jewish Torah in keeping the commandments of God. Those who die in holiness will achieve a blessed state (see *4 Ezra* 7.35; 8.33). ^14-12-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-13|13]]:** *Deeds follow them*, while in most early Jewish (and later rabbinic) images of final judgment the record of people’s behavior in life is preserved in great heavenly books (as in 20.12b), some texts also have people accompanied by their deeds, personified as witnesses to their earthly activities (see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 04#^4-20|Wis 4.20]]; *Apoc. Pet*. 6). ^14-13

### 14.14–20: Reaping of the earth

*[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-14|Text]], p. 563* ^14-14

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-14|14]]:** *White cloud*, [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]. The one *like the Son of Man* (see [[Daniel 07#^7-9|Dan 7.9–13]]) appears here as an eschatological destroyer, perhaps inspired by [[Psalms 110#^110-5|Ps 110.5–6]]. ^14-14-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-15|15–16]]:** The heavenly *Temple* serves as the source of eschatological instructions delivered by angels. *Sickle and reap*, horrific harvest imagery, inspired by Joel 4.13 ([[Hebrews 04#^4-13|Heb 4.13]]) and [[Isaiah 63#^63-1|Isa 63.1–6]] (see [[Isaiah 17#^17-4|Isa 17.4–6]]; [[Isaiah 24#^24-13|24.13]]; [[Jeremiah 51#^51-33|Jer 51.33]]), became a standard metaphor for eschatological judgment (*4 Ezra* 4.28–32; [[Matthew 13#^13-24|Mt 13.24–30]]; *2 Bar*. 70.2). ^14-15

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-18|18]]:** *Authority over fire*, see 8.3–5. ^14-18

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-19|19]]:** *Wine press*, [[Isaiah 63#^63-1|Isa 63.1–6]]. ^14-19

**[[NRSVue Revelation 14#^14-20|20]]:** *Outside the city*, where polluting activity takes place (22.15; cf. also [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-11|Deut 23.10–14]]; [[I Kings 21#^21-13|1 Kings 21.13]]; [[Hebrews 13#^13-11|Heb 13.11–12]]). Torrents of blood proceeding from massacred sinners ([[Joel 4#^4-13|Joel 4.13]]; [[Isaiah 63#^63-6|Isa 63.6]]) become a common motif in Roman Jewish (and Christian) texts: *6 Ezra* \[2 Esd 15–16\] 15.35–36; *1 En*. 100.3; see Josephus, *J.W.* 6.406 (innocents massacred by Romans). *Blood*, for wine as a metaphor for blood, see [[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^49-11|Gen 49.11]]; [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-14|Deut 32.14]]; [[Isaiah 63#^63-2|Isa 63.2–6]]; [[LXX Sirach 39#^39-26|Sir 39.26]]. *Horse’s bridle*, see 19.15. ^14-20
