CHAPTER NINE (vol. 6, notes 03-09): what is different from the brief (2 Nov 2026; adapted from the Chapter Two addendum)

1. The OCR lost the footnote numbers in this chapter, so a pre-pass made the definitions from the page images: it found each
   footnote's start on the image (the line in the footnote zone beginning with a superscript number), read the superscript
   numbers (template matching; good for nearly all), numbered the starts through the chapter, and made `[^n]: ` at the
   matching place in the note, mostly by splitting the long line into which the converter had run a page's footnotes.
   pages.txt shows, under each page, what the pre-pass did ("pre-pass:" lines) and its flags. "MANUAL:" = a start the lead
   placed by hand from the image.
2. CHECK EVERY DEFINITION ON YOUR PAGES against the image: its label must be the printed superscript number at the start of
   that footnote, and the definition must begin where that footnote begins. Fix a wrong start with def ops (brief, op 2).
   Where the pre-pass missed a footnote, or two footnotes still run together in one definition, make the missing one with a
   LETTER label (e.g. `57b` after 57): old = its first words (with any garble mark just before them), new = `[^57b]: ` + the
   same words, or "\n\n[^57b]: " + the words when it starts in the middle of a line; place its call `[^57b]`; give its
   printed number in per_note. Never use a free number; the lead relabels letter labels to the printed numbers.
   A definition that still begins with a mark standing for the superscript (`\^`, `%`, `+`, a stray digit, a Devanagari
   letter such as `र`): remove the mark with a def op. But keep:
   - `\[illegible\]` at the head of a definition: it stands for the superscript or the first word(s); it stays.
   - a `7`, `7?` or similar where the print has ĪP (Īśvarapratyabhijñā): that is the OCR's reading of the word, not of the
     number (e.g. `[^40]: 7 2/4/3-4.`, `[^157]: 7 3/2/10.`, `[^316]: 7? 1/5/16a.`, `[^67]: नौ? 2/4/2.`).
3. LABELS = PRINTED NUMBERS. The print numbers Chapter Nine's footnotes 1-576 through the chapter (notes 03 to 09); the
   labels the pre-pass gave are the printed numbers. Do not renumber anything. 561-565 and 568 (note 09) are already real:
   no op for them, unless a call stands in the wrong place.
   Known gaps: 76 (PDF 54) and 235 (PDF 118) have no text in the note (the converter dropped them): report them, add no
   definition and no call. 374: the images show 373 on PDF 181 and 375 on PDF 182; the print seems to skip 374: confirm.
4. CARRY-OVERS. A carried-over part of a footnote is plain text at the top of the next page's footnotes: after the pre-pass
   it stands as an unnumbered paragraph just before that page's first definition (or as the page's only footnote text).
   Join each such paragraph with a move op (brief, op 4): `after` = the last line of that footnote's text before it (its
   definition line, its last same-page continuation, or the line of the previous carried paragraph when it runs over
   several pages: chains are allowed; several paragraphs moved after the same line keep their order). Body text (the
   translation and commentary) that continues on the next page is NOT moved. The converter ran a footnote's further
   paragraphs into the same line, so there is usually nothing to indent; where a further paragraph of a footnote stands
   as a line of its own after the definition, indent it (op 3).
5. Body text the OCR put among the footnotes, or footnote text inside a body line: report, don't fix. An unnumbered
   asterisk note: follow the brief where it is printed inside or under a footnote; one printed on its own at the head
   of the footnotes (e.g. PDF 217, `* Instead of trividhena kañcukamalena, ...`): leave it plain and report it.
6. REPEATED PAGES. PDF 263-264 are a second scan of PDF 261-262 (printed pp. 244-245); the vault keeps both copies.
   Note 08 has 507 on PDF 261 and its copy on PDF 263: the copy is left plain by the pre-pass; make it a definition with
   the label `507b` (def op, dropping the garble before its first words) and place `[^507b]` at its call on PDF 263's
   body; report it (the lead decides its final label). Note 09's PDF 264 holds the copies of 508-511 (their originals are
   in note 08): in note 09 they keep the labels 508-511.
7. YOUR PAGES ONLY. Other readers have the other pages of the same note.txt. Write ops only for lines that lie on your
   pages (from your first page's marker line to the line before the marker of the page after your last). A move op's
   `after` may point to a line before your range (a footnote carried over from the page before yours). If the first
   footnote text on your first page carries over from the page before, join it (that is yours).
