# Sirach 38 (NRSVue)

## Concerning physicians and health

**38:1** Honor physicians for their services, for the Lord created them, ^38-1

**38:2** for their gift of healing comes from the Most High, and they are rewarded by the king. ^38-2

**38:3** The skill of physicians makes them distinguished, and in the presence of the great they are admired. ^38-3

**38:4** The Lord created medicines out of the earth, and the sensible will not despise them. ^38-4

**38:5** Was not water made sweet with a tree in order that its^[Or *his*] power might be known? ^38-5

**38:6** And he gave skill to human beings that he^[Or *they*] might be glorified in his marvelous works. ^38-6

**38:7** By them the physician^[Heb: Gk *he*] heals and takes away pain; ^38-7

**38:8** the pharmacist makes a mixture from them. God’s^[Gk *His*] works will never be finished, and from him health spreads over all the earth. ^38-8

**38:9** My child, when you are ill, do not delay, but pray to the Lord, and he will heal you. ^38-9

**38:10** Give up your faults and direct your hands rightly, and cleanse your heart from all sin. ^38-10

**38:11** Offer a sweet-smelling sacrifice and a memorial portion of choice flour, and pour oil on your offering, as much as you can afford.^[Heb: Lat lacks *as much as you can afford*; meaning of Gk uncertain] ^38-11

**38:12** Then give physicians their place, for the Lord created them; do not let them leave you, for you need them. ^38-12

**38:13** There may come a time when recovery lies in the hands of physicians,^[Gk *in their hands*] ^38-13

**38:14** for they, too, pray to the Lord that he grant them success in diagnosis^[Heb: Gk *rest*] and in healing, for the sake of preserving life. ^38-14

**38:15** Those who sin against their Maker will be defiant toward the physician.^[Heb: Gk *may they fall into the hands of the physician*] ^38-15

## On mourning for the dead

**38:16** My child, let your tears fall for the dead, and as one in great pain begin the lament. Lay out the body according to custom, and do not neglect the burial. ^38-16

**38:17** Let your weeping be bitter and your wailing fervent; make your mourning worthy of the departed, for one day or two, to avoid criticism; then be comforted for your grief. ^38-17

**38:18** For grief may result in death, and a sorrowful heart saps one’s strength. ^38-18

**38:19** In calamity, grief continues, but the life of the poor is a curse to the heart. ^38-19

**38:20** Do not give your heart to grief; drive it away and remember your own end. ^38-20

**38:21** Do not forget, there is no coming back; you do the dead^[Gk *him*] no good, and you injure yourself. ^38-21

**38:22** Remember his^[Heb: Gk *my*] fate, for yours is like it; yesterday it was his,^[Heb: Gk *mine*] and today it is yours. ^38-22

**38:23** When the dead are at rest, let their remembrance rest, too, and be comforted for them when their spirits have departed. ^38-23

## Trades and crafts

**38:24** The wisdom of the scribe depends on the opportunity of leisure; only the one who has little business can become wise. ^38-24

**38:25** How can one become wise who handles the plow and who glories in the shaft of a goad, who drives oxen and is occupied with their work, and whose talk is about the offspring of bulls? ^38-25

**38:26** He sets his heart on plowing furrows, and he loses sleep over fodder for the heifers. ^38-26

**38:27** So it is with every artisan and master artisan who labors by night as well as by day; those who carve the signets of seals—each is diligent in making a great variety; they set their heart on painting a lifelike image, and they lose sleep in order to finish their work. ^38-27

**38:28** So it is with the smith sitting by the anvil, intent on his ironwork; the breath of the fire melts his flesh, and he struggles with the heat of the furnace; the sound of the hammer deafens his ears,^[Cn: Gk *renews his ear*] and his eyes are on the pattern of the object. He sets his heart on finishing his handiwork, and he loses sleep to complete its decoration. ^38-28

**38:29** So it is with the potter sitting at his work and turning the wheel with his feet; he always lies down anxious about his work, and his every work is taken into account. ^38-29

**38:30** He molds the clay with his arm and makes it pliable with his feet; he sets his heart to finish the glazing, and he takes care in firing^[Cn: Gk *cleaning*] the kiln. ^38-30

**38:31** All these rely on their hands, and all are skillful in their own work. ^38-31

**38:32** Without them no city can be inhabited, and wherever they live they will not go hungry.^[Syr: Gk *and people can neither live nor walk there*] Yet they are not sought out for the council of the people,^[Most ancient authorities lack this line] ^38-32

**38:33** nor do they attain eminence in the public assembly. They do not sit in the judge’s seat, nor do they understand the decisions of the courts; they cannot expound discipline or judgment, and they are not found among the rulers.^[Cn: Gk *among parables*] ^38-33

**38:34** But they maintain the fabric of the world, and their concern is for^[Syr: Gk *prayer is in*] the exercise of their trade. How different the one who devotes himself to the study of the law of the Most High! ^38-34
