## Mūla

तदभावे तदर्थं तदाहृतं ज्ञानमादृतम् ।
एवं यो वेद तत्त्वेन तस्य निर्वाणगामिनी ॥ ^mula

tadabhāve tadarthaṃ tadāhṛtaṃ jñānamādṛtam |
evaṃ yo veda tattvena tasya nirvāṇagāminī || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VII
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 07 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v07 07 - Chapter Thirteen, part 3#^pg238|p. 216]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 07.pdf#page=238>))
>
> (Our) teachers who know the truth, led by the venerable Somānanda, Kalyāṇa, and Bhavabhūti, say that this is untrue. These sages have declared in (their) commentaries on the Trīśikāśāstra that that spontaneous (sāmsiddhika) insight (vijñāna) is said to be the (true) wish-granting gem. When that is absent, the (common) knowledge procured (in other ways) has that (higher intuitive knowledge) as its purpose, (and so) is respected (as is that knowledge). (with 13.149–151ab)
>
> “He who knows this in truth has (received) the (true) initiation, which, devoid of (outer offerings such as) sesame seeds, clarified butter (and the like), is sure, and leads to Nirvāṇa.” “He who knows this in reality, although he has not seen the Maṇḍala, always possesses (every) accomplishment (siddhi). He (is a true) yogi and (true) initiate, and, with respect to the sacrifice, (although) he does not know the procedure, he becomes one who (truly) does.” These and other things the Great Lord says in the Parātrīśikā clearly declare that insight (jñāna) is the true (and essential) nature of (all) the purifying rites, including initiation. (with 13.151cd–154)
