Another interpretation of the words:

“The world above must be filled”

If, then, Christ as man is the first fruits of our nature in relation to God the Father, and a kind of yeast that leavens the whole mass of humanity, so that in the idea of His humanity He is with God the Father, for He is the Word, who never at any time has ceased from or gone outside of His remaining in the Father, \[1280D\] let us not doubt that, consistent with His prayer to the Father, we shall one day be where He is now, the first fruits of our race. For inasmuch as He came to be below for our sakes and without change became man, exactly like us but without sin, loosing the laws of nature in a manner beyond nature, it follows that we too, thanks to Him, will come to be in the world above, and become gods according to Him through the mystery of grace, undergoing no change whatsoever in our nature. And thus, according to the wise teacher, the world above will again be filled, with the members of the body being gathered together with their bead, each according to its worth.
