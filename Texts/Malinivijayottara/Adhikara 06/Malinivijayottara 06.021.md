## Translation

>
> **The Deposition of the Parāparā Vidyā**
>
> (Now) the extension (*vyāpti*) of the Parts (of mantra) (*pada*) in their nineteen divisions will be explained.[^28] One should know that the 1-3) first three Parts there[^29] (extend) for two finger (spaces) each, 4-7) four for eight fingers[^30] (each), 8) (the eighth, which is) the next after that, ten fingers, 9-10) the (next) two parts for two fingers (one each), 11) the next for six fingers, 12) another for twelve then 13-14) two others for five each, 15-16) two Parts for four fingers (two each) and then (finally) 17-18) the last two (Parts) are two (fingers, one each) separately.[^31] (with 6.21–23)

[^28]: These are the Parts of Parāparā Vidyā, see above 4/19-23ab.
[^29]: TĀ 16/233d reads *tatra* – 'there' for MV 6/21b *tataḥ* – 'thus'.
[^30]: Read with TĀ 16/234a *aṣṭāṅgulāni* 'eight fingers' MV 6/22a reads saptāṅgulāni 'seven fingers'.
[^31]: TĀ 16/233cd-235 (234-236ab) = MV 6/21cd-23. TĀ 16/235d reads *tathānye dve dvi parvaṇī* – 'the other two, for two fingers (one each)' for MV 6/23d *dve 'pūrve dve pṛthak tataḥ* – 'the last two are then two (one each) separately'. In TĀ 16/227cd-229ab (228-229) Abhinavagupta paraphrases MV 6/21cd-23 as follows: 'There are three Parts (*pada*) on each of the first (three) pairs of fingers, and four Parts on each (of the four following sets of) eight (fingers). (Then) there are eleven Parts measuring, ten, one, one, six, twelve, five, five, two, two, one, and one finger, respectively. Counting them (all together,) there are eighteen (Parts). The other (remaining nineteenth) Part (is not counted separately,) because of its pervasive (presence). This is the inferior measure of the goddess (Parāparā),** when only the purifying mantra is deposited.' The nineteen Parts of the mantra are presented in TĀ 16/213ab-215 (213-216ab) as those of Parāparā. In this deposition, these Parts are related to the eighty-four-finger space which constitutes the lower measure of the body (see TĀ 16/99-100). In the reverse order, as before (see TĀ 16/212cd-213ab (213)), they are as follows: 1- 3), the first three Parts take up a space of six fingers, 4-7) the following four, thirty-two fingers, and the remaining eleven occupy a space of 8) ten, 9) one, 10) one, 11) six, 12) twelve, 13) five, 14) five, 15) two, 16) two, 17) one, and 18) one finger, respectively. So, we have 6 + 32 + 10 + 1 + 1 + 6 + 12 + 5 + 5 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 84 Parts. Taking care that the Vidyā is in the reverse order, these are as follows: 1) PHAṬ (2) 2) HAḤ (2) 3) HUṀ (2) = 6 fingers' space 4) PHAṬ (8) 5) RARA (8) 6) RURU (8) 7) HE (8) = 32 fingers' space 8) PIBA 10 fingers' space 9) VAMA 1 finger's space 10) BHĪṢAṆE BHĪMA 1 finger's space 11) GHORAMUKHI 6 fingers' space 12) HAḤ = 12 fingers' space 13) GHORARŪPE = 5 fingers' space 14) HUṀ = 5 fingers' space 15) PARAMAGHORE = 2 fingers' space 16) HRĪḤ = 2 fingers' space 17) AGHORE = 1 finger's space 18) OṀ = 1 finger's space 19) Pervasive
