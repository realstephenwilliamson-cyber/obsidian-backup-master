## Appendix: The Stanzas on Vibration (the Spandakārikā)

The Spandakārikaā 

. We praise that Śañkara who is the source of the power of the Wheel of Energies by whose expansion (unmesṣa) and contraction (nimesa) the universe is absorbed and comes into being. 

. That in which all this creation is established and from whence it arises is nowhere obstructed because it is unconditioned by (its very) nature. 

. Even when division prevails due to the waking and other states, it extends through that which is undivided (tadabhinna) because the perceiving subjectivity forever remains true to its own nature. 

. No notions such as “I am happy,” “I am miserable” or “Iam attached” (exist independently). They all clearly reside elsewhere, namely, (in that) which threads through (all) the states of pleasure and the rest. . That exists in the ultimate sense where there is neither pleasure nor pain, subject nor object, nor an absence of consciousness. 

. That principle should be examined with effort and reverence because this, its uncreated freedom, prevails everywhere. By virtue of it, the senses, along with the inner circle, (although) unconscious, behave as if conscious in themselves and move towards (their object), rest (there) and withdraw (from them). 

*[p. 182]* ^pg201

. Indeed the individual soul (puruṣa) does not activate the impulse of the will (which directs the body's activity) by himself alone, but through his contact with (his) own (inner) strength (bala), made in such a way that he identifies with it, (thus acquiring its power). . An individual who, (though) desirous of doing various things, (but) incapable of doing them due to his innate impurity (experiences) the supreme state (parampadam) when the disruption (kṣobha, of his false ego) ceases. 

Then (the soul realises) that his (true) uncreated quali- 10. 

ty (dharma) is (universal) agency and perceiving subjectivity, and so he knows and does whatever (he) desires. 

How can one who, as if astonished, beholds his own 11. 

nature as that which sustains (all things) be subject to this accursed round of transmigration? 

Nothingness can never be an object of contemplation 12. 

because consciousness is absent there. (It is a mistake to believe that one has perceived nothingness) because when reflection (subsequently) intervenes, one is certain that it was. 

Therefore consider that to be an artificial state similar 13. 

to deep sleep. That principle is forever perceived and not remembered in this way. 

Moreover two states called “the agent” and “product 14. 

of action” abide here. The product of action is subject to decay, whereas agency is imperishable. 

Only the effort directed toward the product of action 15. 

disappears here (in states of intense introverted contemplation). When that ceases, the unenlightened believes that his own existence ceases (with it). That inner being is the abode of omniscience and every other divine attribute. It can never cease to exist because nothing else can be perceived (outside it).

The fully awakened (yogi's) perception of that (reality) 

17\. 

is constant (and abides) unaltered in all three states, whereas others (perceive) that only at the end of the first (tadādyānte). 

18\. The omnipresent Lord appears in two states in union with (His) supreme power whose form is knowledge and its object. Elsewhere, apart from these (two states, He manifests) as pure consciousness. 

The streams of the pulsation (spanda) of the qualities, 19. 

along with the other (principles), are grounded in the universal vibration (of consciousness) and so attain to being. Therefore they can never obstruct the enlightened. 

20\. Yet for those whose intuition slumbers, (these vibrations of consciousness) are intent on disrupting their own abiding state of being (svasthiti), casting them down onto the terrible path of transmigration, which is so hard to cross. 

21\. Therefore he who strives constantly to discern the spanda principle rapidly attains his own (true) state of 

being even while in the waking state. 

Spanda is stable in the state one enters when extremely 22. 

angry, intensely excited, running or wondering what to do. 

23.—24. Once entered, that state which (the yogi) takes as his support and firmly resolves that “I will surely do whatever He says”; both the sun and moon set, following the ascending way, into the channel of suṣu-mna, once abandoned the sphere of the universe. 

25\. Then in that great sky, when the sun and moon dissolve away, the dull minded (yogi is cast down) into a state like that of deep sleep. The awakened, however, remains lucid. 

26\. Seizing that strength (bala), mantras, endowed with the power of omniscience, perform their functions, as do the senses of the embodied.

Everything arises (out of) the individual soul and so 28.—29. 

he is all things because he perceives his identity with the awareness (he has) of them. Therefore there is no state in the thoughts of words or (their) meanings that is not Ŝiva. It is the Enjoyer alone who always and everywhere abides as the object of enjoyment. 

30\. Or, constantly attentive and perceiving the entire universe as play, he who has this awareness (samvitti) is 

undoubtedly liberated in this very life. 

This indeed is the arising of that object of meditation 31. 

in the mind of the meditator, namely, the adept’s realisation of his identity with it by the force of (his) intent. 

This alone is the attainment of the nectar of immortali- 32. 

ty, this indeed is to catch hold of oneself, this is the initiation of nirvāna which bestows Ŝiva’s true nature (sadbhāva). 

Requested by the will, the benefactor makes the sun 33.—34. 

and moon rise and bestows on the embodied, while they wake, the objects that are in (their) heart—so also in the dream state (Siva), residing in the centre, manifests without exception, always and most vividly, the things (His devotee) desires because he never desists from (his) prayerful request.
