### 5.1–11: Ananias and Sapphira

*[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 230* · Joshua 7 offers a similar tale of Achan, who dies for misappropriat-ing things devoted to God. ^5-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-3|3–4]]:** Once having sold the property, Ananias was expected to donate the entire sum (4.34). ^5-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-3|3]]:** *Satan*, leader of demonic forces who tempted Jesus ([[Luke 04#^4-1|Lk 4.1–12]]) and led Judas to betray Jesus ([[Luke 22#^22-3|Lk 22.3]],[[Luke 22#^22-31|31]]). While Ananias may have been influenced by Satan, he remains responsible. *Holy Spirit*, because Ananias transgressed against the Holy Spirit, he merits immediate and severe punishment (v 5; see also [[Luke 12#^12-10|Lk 12.10]]). ^5-3-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-6|6]]:** *Buried him*, speedy burial, including the use of a shroud, is expected. See [[LXX Tobit 02#^2-8|Tob 2.8]]; Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.221; [[Sanhedrin 47#47a|*b. Sanh.* 47a]]. ^5-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-11|11]]:** *Fear*, in the sense of awe (2.43; 9.31; 10.2,35; 13.16,26; 19.17). *Church*, designating a specific community (8.1; 11.22,26; 13.1; 14.23,27; 15.3,4; 16.5; 18.22; 20.17) or more generally all who believe in Jesus (9.31; 20.28). ^5-11

### 5.12–16: Miraculous deeds

*[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-12|Text]], p. 231* · Miraculous healing attracts believers (9.35; 19.11–20). ^5-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-12|12]]:** *Solomon’s Portico*, see 3.11n. ^5-12-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-15|15–16]]:** The author ascribes similar miracles to Jesus ([[Luke 04#^4-40|Lk 4.40]]; [[Luke 05#^5-15|5.15]]; [[Luke 09#^9-11|9.11]]) and Paul (19.12). ^5-15

### 5.17–42: Apostles appear before the council

*[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-17|Text]], p. 231* · The miraculous deeds and the enthusiastic response prompt opposition by Jewish authorities (3.1–4.22). ^5-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-17|17]]:** *High priest . . . Sadducees*, see 4.1n. *Jealousy*, see 13.45; 17.5. ^5-17-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-18|18]]:** *Arrested,* see 4.3. *Public prison*, many cities in the Roman Empire had prisons, which were primarily used for confinement or coercion rather than punishment. ^5-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-19|19]]:** Miraculous escapes from prison suggest divine power or approbation (12.6–11; 16.25–26; Euripides, *Bacch.* ). ^5-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-21|21,25]]:** Jesus is frequently portrayed as teaching in the Temple ([[Mark 12#^12-35|Mk 12.35]]; [[Luke 20#^20-1|Lk 20.1]]; [[John 08#^8-20|Jn 8.20]]) and being accosted or rebuked for it ([[Luke 20#^20-1|Lk 20.1–20]]). Rabbinic traditions also speak of the Temple’s precincts as a site for teaching (*m. Orlah* 2.12; *t. Sanh.* 2.6). ^5-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-26|26]]:** *Stoned*, stoning is a form of capital punishment in the Tanakh ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-2|Lev 20.2]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-14|24.14–16]]; [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-35|Num 15.35–36]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-11|Deut 13.11]]; 17.5; 21.21) and rabbinic tradition ([[Shabbat 153#153b|*b. Shabb.* 153b]]–154a; *b. B. Kamma* 44b; *b. Sanh.* passim). It is unclear whether any Jewish authority did (*m. Sanh.* 6.4; *t. Sanh.* 6.6; [[Sanhedrin 52#52b|*b. Sanh.* 52b]]; Origen, *Africanus* 14a) or did not (*y. Sanh.* 1.1 \[18a\]; || *b. [[Ketubot 30#30a|Ketub. 30a]] b. [[Sanhedrin 37#37b|Sanh. 37b]]*; [[Sanhedrin 52#52a|*b. Sanh.* 52a]]; Origen, *Comm. Rom.* 6.7) perform executions during this period. Josephus reports that the high priest ordered the stoning of James, the brother of Jesus, for having transgressed the Law, but Jewish leaders and the Roman procurator disputed his actions (Josephus, *Ant.* 20.200–203). The rabbis both present themselves as authorities in the area of capital punishment (e.g., *m. Sanh.* 6–7) and express an aversion to imposing capital punishment; they established numerous requirements that made execution nearly impossible to carry out (cf. *m. Makk.* 1.10; [[Sanhedrin 17#17a|*b. Sanh.* 17a]]). The action here appears to be more an example of mob violence, as happens later to Stephen (7.58–59). The opening chapters portray popular support for the apostles (2.41,47; 4.4,21; 5.13–14). ^5-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-28|28–29]]:** See 4.17–20. *Bring this man’s blood on us*, the high priest criticizes the apostles for implicating Jewish authorities in Jesus’ death, like the notorious “blood cry” of [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]]. ^5-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-29|29]]:** *Obey God rather than any human authority,* a sentiment also voiced by Socrates (Plato, *Apol.* 29d) and Jewish messianic and nationalist groups, such as the Fourth Philosophy (Josephus, *Ant.* 18.23) and Eleazar ben Yair at Masada (Josephus, *J.W.* 7.323). ^5-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-30|30]]:** *God of our ancestors*, followers of Jesus fulfill and continue the promises God made to the patriarchs. *God . . . raised up Jesus, whom you had killed*, see 2.23–24; 3.15. *Hanging him on a tree*, corpses of persons punished by death were hung on a tree ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-22|Deut 21.22–23]]). Later this description becomes associated with crucifixion (see [[Galatians 03#^3-13|Gal 3.13]]; 1QpNahum frag. 3–4, 1, 7–8, a possible allusion to the 800 Jews crucified by the Hasmonean King Alexander Jannaeus \[Josephus, *J.W.* 1.97; *Ant.* 13.372–83\]). ^5-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-31|31–32]]:** Obedience to God now entails acknowledging Jesus’ authority beside God, through whom comes forgiveness of sins. ^5-31

### 5.33–39: Gamaliel’s speech

*[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-33|Text]], p. 232* · . ^5-33

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-34|34]]:** *Pharisee*, see “Pharisees,” p. 619. Acts depicts Pharisees as believers in divine providence (5.39) and resurrection (23.7–8), and as strict adherents to Torah (15.5); Josephus confirms these qualities (*J.W.* 1.110–14; 2.162–66; *Ant.* 13.171–73; 18.12–15). *Gamaliel*, according to rabbinic tradition, a highly esteemed first-century Jewish leader, scholar, and teacher (*m. Sot.* 9.15). ^5-34

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-36|36–37]]:** *Theudas*, eschatological prophet who in the mid-40s CE gathered his followers near the Jordan and who was executed under orders of the Roman procurator (Josephus, *Ant.* 20.97–98). *Judas the Galilean*, led a revolt against Rome’s transformation of Judea into a Roman province in 6 CE (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.117–19,433; *Ant.* 18.2–10,23–25). See “Messianic Movements,” p. 622. ^5-36

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-38|38–39]]:** Gamaliel’s teaching echoes Peter’s statement (v 29); Jews often understood that broad historical events were directed by God, and therefore opposing the human *strict orders not to teach in this name?* b Gk *than men* c Gk *Men, Israelites* authority responsible for those events was tantamount to fighting against God (Jeremiah and Babylonia, [[Jeremiah 27#^27-6|Jer 27.6–11]]; Josephus and Rome, Josephus, *J.W.* 5.378; Agrippa II and Rome, Josephus, *J.W.* 2.390). R. Yohanan the Sandal-maker (*m. Avot* 4.11) stated, “Every assembly which is for the Name of Heaven will in the end be established, but that which is not for the Name of Heaven will not in the end be established.” By having a respected Jewish leader issue the admonition, Acts portrays subsequent Jewish opposition to Christianity as both against God and contrary to Judaism. ^5-38

> [!note] Gamaliel *(p. 232)*
> Gamaliel (Heb *Gamliel*) was a well-respected first-century sage. According to one rabbinic tradition, “When Rabban Gamliel the Elder died, the glory of the Law ceased and purity and abstinence died” (*m. Sot.* 9.15). In Acts, Gamaliel is noted for two things: he prevents his Jewish colleagues from killing the apostles, and he is identified as Paul’s teacher (22.3, an assertion not confirmed in Paul’s own writings). Gamaliel’s agnostic position about the proclamation of Jesus (vv 38–39) contrasts sharply with the Jews later in the book who develop strong, even hostile, opposition to Jesus and those who preach in his name. The depiction of a highly tol-erant Gamaliel gave rise to later Christian traditions that he was baptized and converted to Christianity (*Clementine Recognitions* 1.65). The Eastern Orthodox Church recognizes Gamaliel as a saint.

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-41|41]]:** *Suffer dishonor*, suffering on behalf of Jesus is understood as honorable rather than shameful (9.16; [[Philippians 01#^1-29|Phil 1.29]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-8|2 Tim 1.8]]). ^5-41

**[[NRSVue Acts 05#^5-42|42]]:** Teaching in the Temple, see v 25. ^5-42
