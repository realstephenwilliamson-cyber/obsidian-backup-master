### 18.1–40: Arrest and trial

*[[NRSVue John 18#^18-1|Text]], p. 212* · *Kidron valley*, east of Jerusalem. A *garden*, called Gethsemane in [[Matthew 26#^26-36|Mt 26.36]]; [[Mark 14#^14-32|Mk 14.32]]. ^18-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-3|3]]:** *Soldiers . . . police*, Jesus’ arrest was carried out by Roman and Jewish functionaries. *Pharisees*, did not have their own police. ^18-3

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-5|5]]:** *I am he*, a double entendre: Jesus identifies himself with the “I am” formula ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]; see also Jn 4.26n.; 6.35; 8.58). ^18-5

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-6|6]]:** *Fell to the ground*, suggesting a theophany, as in [[Daniel 02#^2-46|Dan 2.46]]; [[Daniel 08#^8-18|8.18]]; [[Revelation 01#^1-17|Rev 1.17]]. ^18-6

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-9|9]]:** See 6.39; 10.28; 17.12. *To fulfill the word*, the Gospel presents Jesus’ words as prophecies, with the same authority as the scriptures. ^18-9

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-10|10]]:** See [[Matthew 26#^26-51|Mt 26.51–52]]; [[Mark 14#^14-47|Mk 14.47]]; [[Luke 22#^22-50|Lk 22.50]]. Only John names the perpetrator (Peter) and the victim (Malchus). ^18-10

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-11|11]]:** *Drink the cup*, see e.g., [[Isaiah 51#^51-17|Isa 51.17]]; [[Jeremiah 25#^25-15|Jer 25.15-17]]; [[Psalms 016#^16-5|Ps 16.5]]; [[Lamentations 4#^4-21|Lam 4.21]]. ^18-11

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-13|13]]:** *Annas*, high priest 6–15 CE. ^18-13

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-14|14]]:** *Caiaphas*, see 11.49–52. ^18-14

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-15|15–27]]:** Peter’s interrogation by the high priest’s slaves and police is interwoven with Jesus’ interrogation before Annas. ^18-15

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-15|15]]:** *Known to the high priest*, an unspecified connection, suggesting the disciple was from Jerusalem. Peter’s unwill-ingness to admit that he is Jesus’ follower fulfills Jesus’ prophecy in 13.38. ^18-15-2

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-17|17]]:** *I am not*, perhaps an ironic inversion of Jesus’ use of “I am” as divine self-designation ^18-17

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-20|20]]:** *Spoken openly*, see 7.14,37; 8.20n.; 8.59. ^18-20

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-24|24]]:** In contrast to Matthew, John depicts no trial before Caiaphas or the Sanhedrin; which, if any, of the Gospel trial accounts is historical is impossible to determine. See [[Matthew 26#^26-57|Mt 26.57–68]]. ^18-24

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-28|28]]:** *Avoid ritual defilement*, by keeping their distance from areas occupied by Gentiles prior to the festival. ^18-28

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-31|31]]:** *Are not permitted*, Jews were not allowed to impose the death penalty; thus Jesus is crucified, a Roman penalty. Whether this is historically accurate is a matter of scholarly debate. Acts 7.58–60; 23.20; 25.9–11,22,30 imply that the Jews, and/or the Sanhedrin, did have the power to carry out a capital sentence, though these verses can be explained in other ways. From a narrative point of view, this verse helps to account for why it was the reluctant Pilate who had jurisdiction in this situation while it was the Jews who retained moral culpability. ^18-31

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-32|32]]:** *To fulfill what Jesus had said*, likely a reference to 12.32–33. ^18-32

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-33|33]]:** *King of the Jews*, Romans regarded anyone claiming kingship without their permission as an insurrectionist. Rulers of the Jewish territories owed primary loyalty to Rome. There may be an ironic echo here of 6.15, in which Jesus withdrew from a crowd of Jews who, he perceived, wanted to make him king. ^18-33

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-35|35]]:** *I am not a Jew* . . . *own nation*, suggesting a broader participation than the Sanhedrin. ^18-35

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-36|36]]:** *My kingdom is not from this world*, in context, an argument against seeing Jesus as a political threat. *Handed over to the Jews*, Jesus is not in fact being handed over to the Jews, but this verse keeps readers/hearers focused on the Jews as the true culprits. ^18-36

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-38|38]]:** *What is truth*, more irony: Pilate cannot see the *truth* in front of him. ^18-38

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-39|39]]:** *You have a custom*, this custom has no external historical support (see [[Matthew 27#^27-15|Mt 27.15]]; [[Mark 15#^15-6|Mk 15.6]]; Lk 23.17). ^18-39

**[[NRSVue John 18#^18-40|40]]:** *Barrabas*, could be translated “son of a \[*or* the\] father.” *Bandit*, the Gk “lēstēs” can refer to a robber but Josephus uses this term to describe anti-Roman revolutionaries; see *J.W.* 2.253–54. ^18-40
