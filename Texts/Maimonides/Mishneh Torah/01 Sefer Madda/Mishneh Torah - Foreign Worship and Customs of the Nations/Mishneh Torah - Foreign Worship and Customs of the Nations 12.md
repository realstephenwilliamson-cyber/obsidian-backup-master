**1.** We may not shave[^1] the corners of our heads[^2] as the idolaters and their priests do,[^3] as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-27|Leviticus 19:27]]] states: "Do not cut off the corners of your heads."  
One[^4] is liable for each corner. Therefore, a person who shaves both his temples[^5] - even if he were to do so simultaneously and had received only a single warning – is [liable for] two measures of lashes.  
[This prohibition applies equally to] one who shaves off only the corners of his head and leaves the remainder of his hair, and to one who shaves his entire head at once.[^6] Since he has shaved the corners, he is [liable for] lashes.[^7]  
To whom does the above apply? To the person who shaves. The person [whose head] is shaven is not lashed[^8] unless he assists the one who is shaving him.[^9] One who shaves [the corners of] a child's[^10] [head] should be [liable for] lashes.[^11] ^12-1

**2.** A woman is exempt if she shaves the head of a man[^12] or[^13] has her own head shaven.[^14] [Since [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-27|Leviticus 19:27]]] states: "Do not cut off the corners of your heads and do not destroy the corners of your beards," [an association between the two prohibitions is established].[^15] Whoever is liable for shaving is liable for cutting off the corners. Therefore, because women are not liable for shaving[^16] - since they do not have beards[^17] - they are not liable for cutting off the corners [of their heads]. Accordingly, slaves[^18] are forbidden to cut off the corners of their heads, since they do possess beards.[^19] ^12-2

**3.** All the Torah's prohibitions apply equally to men and women,[^20] with the exception of the prohibition against shaving, cutting off the corners of one's head,[^21] and the prohibition against priests contracting impurity through contact with a dead body.[^22]  
Women are not obligated with regard to all positive commandments which apply from time to time and are not constant obligations,[^23] with the exception of the sanctification of [the Sabbath] day,[^24] eating *matzah* on Pesach night,[^25] eating and offering the Paschal sacrifice,[^26] *hakhel*,[^27] and the festive[^28] peace-offering for which they are obligated.[^29] ^12-3

**4.** The status of a *tumtum*[^30] and an *androgynous*[^31] is doubtful.[^32] Therefore, the stringencies applying to both a man[^33] and a woman[^34] are applied to them, and they are obligated by all [the mitzvot]. If, however, they transgress,[^35] they are not [liable for] lashes.[^36] ^12-4

**5.** Although a woman is permitted to shave the corners of her own head,[^37] she is forbidden to shave the corners of a man's head.[^38] She is even forbidden to shave the corners of a child's [head].[^39] ^12-5

**6.** The Sages did not determine the amount [of hair] which must be left in the corners of our temples.[^40] We have, however, heard from our elders that one must leave at least forty[^41] hairs.  
One may remove the [hairs[^42] from] the corners [of our heads] with scissors. The prohibition applies only to total removal with a razor.[^43] ^12-6

**7.** It is customary for pagan priests to remove their beards.[^44] Therefore, the Torah forbade the removal of one's beard.[^45]  
The beard has five "corners": the upper and lower cheek on both the right and left sides, and the hair on the chin.[^46] One is [liable for] lashes for the removal of each "corner."[^47] A person who removes them all at the same time is [liable for] five measures of lashes.[^48]  
One is liable only when one shaves with a razor, as [implied by [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-27|Leviticus 19:27]]]: "Do not destroy the corners of your beard." [We can infer that this applies only] to shaving which utterly destroys [one's facial hair]. Therefore, a person who removes his beard with scissors is exempt.  
A person who allows himself to be shaved is not [liable for] lashes unless he provides assistance. A woman who has facial hair is allowed to shave it.[^49] If she shaves a man's beard, she is exempt.[^50] ^12-7

**8.** It is permitted to shave one's mustache[^51] - i.e., the hair on the upper lip, and, similarly, the hair which hangs from the lower lip. Even though the removal [of this hair] is permitted, it is customary for the Jews not to destroy it entirely.[^52] Rather, its ends may be removed so that it will not interfere with eating or drinking.[^53] ^12-8

**9.** The Torah does not forbid the removal of hair from other portions of the body[^54] - e.g., the armpits or the genitalia. This is, however, prohibited by the Rabbis.[^55] A man who removes [such hair] is given stripes for rebelliousness.[^56]  
Where does the above apply? In places where it is customary only for women to remove such hair, so that one will not beautify himself as women do. In places where it is customary for both men[^57] and women to remove such hair, one is not given stripes. It is permitted[^58] to remove hair from our other limbs with scissors[^59] in all communities. ^12-9

**10.** A woman should not adorn herself as a man does[^60] - e.g., she may not place a turban or a hat on her head or wear armor[^61] or the like. She may not cut [the hair of] her head as men do.[^62]  
A man should not adorn himself as a woman does[^63] - e.g., he should not wear colored garments or golden bracelets in a place where such garments and such bracelets are worn only by women. Everything follows local custom.[^64]  
A man who adorns himself as a woman does, and a woman who adorns herself as a man does, are [liable for] lashes. When a man removes white hairs from among the dark hairs of his head or beard,[^65] he should be lashed[^66] as soon as he removes a single hair, because he has beautified himself as a woman does.[^67] Similarly, if he dyes his hair dark, he is given lashes after dyeing a single hair.[^68]  
A *tumtum* and an *androgynous*[^69] may not wrap their heads [in a veil] as women do, or cut [the hair of] their head as men do.[^70] If they do [either of the above], they are not [liable for] lashes.[^71] ^12-10

**11.** The tattooing which the Torah forbids[^72] involves making a cut in one's flesh and filling the slit with eye-color, ink, or with any other dye that leaves an imprint.[^73] This was the custom of the idolaters, who would make marks on their bodies for the sake of their idols,[^74] as if to say that they are like servants sold to the idol and designated for its service.[^75]  
When a person makes a mark with one of the substances that leave an imprint after making a slit in any place on his body, he is [liable for] lashes. [This prohibition is binding on] both men and women.[^76]  
If a person wrote[^77] and did not dye, or dyed without writing by cutting [into his flesh], he is not liable.[^78] [Punishment is administered] only when he writes and dyes, as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-28|Leviticus 19:28]]] states: "[Do not make] a dyed inscription [on yourselves]."  
To[^79] whom does this apply? To the person doing the tattooing. A person who is tattooed [by others], however, is not liable unless he assisted the tattooer to the extent that it is considered that he performed a deed. If he did not perform a deed, he is not lashed.[^80] ^12-11

**12.** A person who gouges himself for the dead[^81] is lashed, as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-28|Leviticus 19:28]]] states: "Do not gouge your flesh for the dead."[^82] This [prohibition] applies both to priests and to Israelites.[^83]  
A person who makes a single gouge for five dead people or five gouges for a single dead person is [liable for] five measures of lashes,[^84] provided he is given a warning for each individual matter.[^85] ^12-12

**13.** Gashing and gouging oneself[^86] are [governed by] a single [prohibition].[^87] Just as the pagans would gouge their flesh in grief over their dead, they would mutilate themselves for their idols,[^88] as \[[[I Kings 18#^18-28|I Kings 18:28]]] states:[^89] "And they mutilated themselves according to their custom."  
This[^90] is also forbidden by the Torah, as \[[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deuteronomy 14:1]]] states: "Do not mutilate yourselves." [The difference between the two is that if one gouges himself in grief over] the dead, whether he did so with his bare hands or with an instrument, he is [liable for] lashes; for the sake of idols,[^91] if one uses an instrument, one is liable for lashes.[^92] If one does so with one's bare hands, one is exempt.[^93] ^12-13

**14.** This commandment also includes [a prohibition][^94] against there being two courts which follow different customs in a single city, since this can cause great strife.[^95] [Because of the similarity in the Hebrew roots,][^96] the prohibition against gashing ourselves [can be interpreted] to mean: "Do not separate into various different groupings."[^97] ^12-14

**15.** A person who creates a bald spot [on his head] for a dead person[^98] is [liable for] lashes,[^99] as \[[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deuteronomy 14:1]]] states: "Do not make a bald spot between your eyes[^100] for a dead person." When either a priest or an Israelite makes a bald spot [on his head] for a dead person, he is [liable for] only a single measure of lashes.[^101]  
A person who makes four or five bald spots for a single dead person is [liable for] a measure of lashes equivalent to the number of bald spots he made, provided he received a separate warning for each bald spot.[^102] There is no difference whether one created the bald spot with his hands[^103] or with a potion.[^104] If a person dipped his fingers into a potion and positioned them in five places on his head at the same time, since he created five bald spots, he is [liable for] five measures of lashes[^105] even though only a single warning was given,[^106] for they were all created at the same time.[^107]  
One is liable [for creating a bald spot] on any part of the head, [not only] "between the eyes"[^108] [as is inferred from [[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-5|Leviticus 21:5]]]: "Do not make a bald spot on your heads."  
What[^109] is the measure of a bald spot? An area on one's head the size of a *gris*[^110] which is free of hair.[^111] ^12-15

**16.** A person who makes a bald spot on his head or gouges his flesh[^112] because his house falls or because his ship sinks at sea is exempt.[^113] One is lashed only [if he carries out these acts] for the sake of a deceased person[^114] or if he gashes his flesh[^115] for the sake of an idol.[^116]  
[The following laws apply] when a person creates a bald spot on a colleague's head, makes a gash on a colleague's flesh, or tattoos his colleague's flesh while his colleague assists him.[^117] If they both intended to violate the prohibition, both receive lashes.[^118] If one violated the prohibition inadvertently and the other did so intentionally, the one who performed the act intentionally is [liable for] lashes, and his colleague is exempt.[^119] ^12-16
