# John 6 (JANT notes)

Text: [[NRSVue John 06|NRSVue]] · [[John 06|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT John - Introduction]]

### 6.1–15: Fourth sign: feeding of the multitudes

*[[NRSVue John 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 188* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-13|Mt 14.13–21]]; Mk 16.32–44; [[Luke 09#^9-10|Lk 9.10–17]]). ^6-1

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Other side*, eastern shore of the Sea of Galilee. ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-3|3]]:** *Up the mountain*, see Mt 5–7. Mountains were places in which to seek God’s word (Moses in [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-3|Ex 19.3]]; Elijah in [[I Kings 19#^19-11|1 Kings 19.11]]). ^6-3

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-4|4]]:** *Passover*, in contrast to his usual practice, Jesus does not go on pilgrimage to Jerusalem but stays in Galilee, where people flock to him rather than the Temple. This scene fulfills his prophecy to the Samaritan woman in 4.21 and may reflect a post-70 perspective in which worship in the Temple is no longer possible, and from John’s viewpoint, no longer necessary. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-6|6]]:** *Test*, presumably their faith in Jesus’ ability. ^6-6

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-11|11]]:** *Given thanks*, following Jewish practice of blessing bread before eating a meal. Gk for “giving thanks,” “eucharistein,” may allude to the Eucharist ([[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22–25]]; [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|1 Cor 11.23–26]]; see “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674). ^6-11

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-12|12]]:** *Gather up the fragments*, see [[Mark 06#^6-43|Mk 6.43]]. The Exodus generation was fed by manna that they gathered, including gathering enough for two days to avoid work on the Sabbath ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-14|Ex 16.14–26]]). ^6-12

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Twelve baskets*, perhaps corresponding to the twelve tribes of Israel, or usual day’s wage for a laborer the number of Jesus’ apostles, signifying God’s people. ^6-13

> [!note] Rabbi (JN 1.38,49; 3.2,26; 4.31; 6.25; 9.2; 11.8) *(p. 188)*
> In Second Temple Judaism this title does not indicate a religious functionary but conveys respect toward a person recognized as having authority. *Teacher* captures the connotation. In Hebrew sources, “rabbi” does not appear before the *Mishnah*, though
>
> Matthew (e.g., 26.25), Mark (e.g., 9.5), and John use it to refer to Jesus. [[Matthew 03#^3-7|Mt 3.7–8]] refers more generically to “rabbis” and may be the earliest evidence of its usage to denote learned individuals. “Rabbi” later came to indicate a person qualified to pronounce on matters of Jewish law and practice.

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-14|14]]:** *Prophet*, this may be an allusion to the theme of Jesus as the prophet-like-Moses who will be known by his deeds (see [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-15|Deut 1.15–22]]). ^6-14

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Make him king*, anyone acclaimed king risked a Roman accusation of treason and therefore crucifixion. ^6-15

### 6.16–21: Walking on water

*[[NRSVue John 06#^6-16|Text]], p. 189* · Jesus demonstrates power over the natural world ([[Matthew 14#^14-22|Mt 14.22–27]]; [[Mark 06#^6-45|Mk 6.45–51]]). Similarly, Elisha makes an ax head float ([[II Kings 06#^6-4|2 Kings 6.4–7]]). ^6-16

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-20|20]]:** *It is I*, God calms waves ([[Psalms 089#^89-10|Ps 89.9]]). ^6-20

### 6.22–40: The bread of life discourse

*[[NRSVue John 06#^6-22|Text]], p. 189* · The imagery of the body and blood is extended, though connection to the Eucharist ritual remains uncertain. ^6-22

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-23|23]]:** *The Lord had given thanks*, this phrase may not be original (see translators’ note *d*). ^6-23

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-25|25]]:** *Rabbi*, see ”Rabbi,” p. 188. ^6-25

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-26|26]]:** *Very truly*, see 1.51n. ^6-26

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-27|27]]:** *Perishes . . . endures*, manna perished after one night; *eternal life* can only result from “the bread of life” (v 35), Jesus. *Seal*, symbol of authority ([[Revelation 05#^5-2|Rev 5.2]]). ^6-27

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-28|28–29]]:** *Works of God . . . believe in him whom \[God\] has sent*, the contrast is between “working” for food (v 27) and participating in God’s life (v 33). ^6-28

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-31|31]]:** *Manna*, see [[Psalms 078#^78-24|Ps 78.24]] (quoted); see also [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-4|Ex 16.4]],[[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-15|15]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-8|Num 11.8]]. Like Nicodemus and the Samaritan woman, the crowds misunderstand (see 3.5; 4.15). *Bread from Heaven*, see [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-4|Ex 16.4]], which refers to the manna as bread from heaven. ^6-31

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-33|33]]:** *Bread of God*, bread sent by God, parallel to the manna. ^6-33

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-35|35]]:** *I am*, expresses divinity and implies Jesus’ unity with God (1.1–3; 6.20; 8.58; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]). ^6-35

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-38|38]]:** *Not to do my own will*, Jesus is not identical with God, but is God’s agent. ^6-38

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-39|39–40]]:** *Raise it . . . them up . . . last day*, Gk “anasteso . . . te eschate hemera,” lit., “resurrect . . . the day at the end of the age.” Hebrew/Aramaic term, as in [[Daniel 12#^12-13|Dan 12.13]]. *given thanks* ^6-39

### 6.41–51: Implications of the discourse

*[[NRSVue John 06#^6-41|Text]], p. 190* ^6-41

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-41|41]]:** *Jews began to complain*, Jesus’ claim to be from heaven is offensive, given their knowledge of his parentage. ^6-41-2

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-42|42]]:** *Son of Joseph*, the Gospel describes Jesus primarily as God’s son, but does not include a narrative that explicitly denies Joseph’s paternity. ^6-42

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-44|44]]:** *Unless drawn by the Father,* see 3.27n. ^6-44

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-45|45]]:** *Prophets*, see [[Isaiah 54#^54-13|Isa 54.13]]. ^6-45

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-51|51]]:** *Flesh*, the language evokes cannibalism, which creates a “shock factor,” but the intention is metaphorical. Scholars debate the possible Eucharistic overtones of “drinking blood” and “eating flesh.” In contrast to the Synoptics ([[Matthew 26#^26-26|Mt 26.26–29]]; [[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22–24]], [[Luke 22#^22-19|Lk 22.19–20]]), John does not have the institution of the Eucharist as part of the Last Supper. ^6-51

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-52|6.52–71]]:** **Disputation over the discourse.** In contrast to the Synoptic Gospels, John’s Gospel lacks an account of a Last Supper memorial. Jesus alludes to his death and resurrection, in that he became flesh (1.14) and offered himself to God, thus sacrificing his life for the life of the world. ^6-52

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-53|53]]:** *Flesh . . . blood*, the literal meaning is offensive because Jews are not to ingest blood ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^9-4|Gen 9.4]]; [[Torah 25 - Tzav#^7-26|Lev 7.26–27]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^17-15|17.15]]). The passage suggests the practice of theophagy (lit., “eating the god”) associated with Greco-Roman mystery cults such as those of Demeter and Dionysus. The allusion implies the Gospel writer’s familiarity with such cults and supports the hypothesis that the audience included Gentiles. ^6-53

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-60|60]]:** Many followers were offended, perhaps because of the teaching’s cannibalistic overtones (see 6.53n.). ^6-60

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-63|63]]:** *Flesh . . . useless*, the meaning of terms in this Gospel is fluid (see also “world” at 3.16 and 16.33; 17.14–16). ^6-63

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-69|69]]:** *Holy One of God*, title not elsewhere present in the Fourth Gospel. See [[Psalms 106#^106-16|Ps 106.16]] (Aaron). ^6-69

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-70|70]]:** *The twelve*, that is, the group of disciples closest to Jesus. *Devil*, see 13.2. Judas is aligned with cosmic forces hostile to Jesus. ^6-70

**[[NRSVue John 06#^6-71|71]]:** *Iscariot*, that is, a man (Heb “ish”) from the (Judean) town of Kerioth. ^6-71
