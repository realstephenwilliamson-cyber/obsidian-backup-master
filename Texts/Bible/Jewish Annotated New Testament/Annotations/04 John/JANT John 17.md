### 17.1–26: Jesus’ prayer

*[[NRSVue John 17#^17-1|Text]], p. 211* · Jesus addresses God on behalf of his followers ([[Matthew 06#^6-9|Mt 6.9–13]]; [[Luke 11#^11-2|Lk 11.2–4]]). In Mt and Lk, Jesus teaches his followers how and what to pray; here he prays for himself and on their behalf. ^17-1

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-3|3]]:** *Eternal life*, defined as faith in Christ. *Jesus Christ*, otherwise found only in the Prologue (1.17); possibly a later insertion reflecting a confessional or liturgical formula of the later community; see [[1 John 04#^4-2|1 Jn 4.2]]. ^17-3

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-4|4]]:** *Glorified*, refers to the revelation of God’s power and expresses Jesus’ desire to return to the Father. ^17-4

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-5|5]]:** *Before the world existed*, see 1.1–3; 8.58. ^17-5

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-9|9]]:** *On behalf of the world*, although 3.16 refers to God’s love for “the world,” implying a universal idea, here the term “world” is negative, referring to those who rejected Jesus. ^17-9

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-12|12]]:** *Destined to be lost*, Judas Iscariot. *Scripture*, the precise reference is unclear; perhaps [[LXX Proverbs 24#^24-22|Prov 24.22]]\[LXX\], which refers to the “son of perdition.” ^17-12

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-15|15]]:** *Evil one*, Satan. ^17-15

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-17|17]]:** *Truth*, see 8.32; 14.6. ^17-17

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-19|19]]:** *Sanctify myself*, normally it is God who consecrates or sanctifies someone for a particular purpose, e.g., in [[Torah 23 - Pekudei#^40-13|Ex 40.13]] God consecrates Aaron as high priest. If Jesus consecrates himself, then it may express *name those whom* c Gk *except the son of destruction* d Or *among themselves* e Or *from evil* f Other ancient authorities read *be one in us* the idea that it is God the Father who has given him the power to do so, in the same way, e.g., that he gave him authority to judge in 5.27. ^17-19

**[[NRSVue John 17#^17-24|24]]:** *Foundation of the world*, Jesus’ preexistence (1.1–18). ^17-24
