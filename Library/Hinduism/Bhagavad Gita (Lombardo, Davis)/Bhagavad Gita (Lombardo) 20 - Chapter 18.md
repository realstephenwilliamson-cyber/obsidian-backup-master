## Chapter Eighteen

Arjuna then said:

\[1\] “I wish to know, Mighty Armed One,
exactly what renunciation is
and what is meant by abandonment,
each of them, Slayer of Keshin.”

The Blessed Lord spoke:

\[2\] “By renunciation the sages mean
forsaking desire-driven actions.
Relinquishing all fruits of action
is what they call abandonment.

\[3\] Some sages say that all action
is evil and should be renounced.
Sacrifice, giving, austerity
should not be renounced, others say.

\[4\] Now listen to what I conclude
concerning this, O Arjuna.
Renunciation is of three kinds,
O Best of the Bharatas.

\[5\] Sacrifice, giving, austerity
must be performed, not renounced.
Sacrifice, giving, austerity
purify the minds of the wise.

\[6\] But these actions must be performed
while renouncing all attachment
to their fruits. This, Son of Pritha,
is without doubt my deep belief.

[p. 96] \[7\] And renunciation is not proper
when actions are obligatory.
Such deluded renunciation
is declared to be *tamasic*.

\[8\] Not acting because it is hard
or out of fear of suffering
is *rajasic*. One does not obtain
fruit from such renunciation.

\[9\] Performing an ordained action
with discipline, O Arjuna,
and no attachment to its fruit,
that is *sattvic* renunciation.

\[10\] The wise man, his doubt severed,
the renunciant, filled with goodness,
does not hate displeasing action,
nor is attached to pleasant action.

\[11\] Embodied beings are not able
to forgo action entirely.
But one who forgoes action’s fruits
is said to be a renunciant.

\[12\] The fruits of action are threefold
for non-renunciants when they die:
unpleasant, pleasant, and a mixture.
But for renunciants none at all.

\[13\] And now, Mighty Armed Arjuna,
learn from Me of the five factors,
as declared in *Samkhya* doctrine,
for accomplishing all actions.

\[14\] First the basis, second the agent,
third, the various kinds of organs,
[p. 97] fourth, the different kinds of motions,
fifth, the relevant deities.

\[15\] Whatever action one performs
whether with body, speech, or mind,
and whether it is right or wrong,
these five are the causal factors.

\[16\] This being so, whoever sees
himself alone as the agent
has imperfect understanding
and does not really see, the fool.

\[17\] One who is not egocentric,
whose intellect is untainted,
even though he kills these people,
he does not kill, and is not bound.

\[18\] Knowledge, the known, and the knower
are the threefold drivers of action.
The means, the act, and the agent
are action’s three constituents.

\[19\] Knowledge, action, and the agent
are of three kinds, each distinguished
by the *gunas*. Learn these also,
as they are declared in *Samkhya*.

\[20\] The knowledge by which one sees
a single being in all creatures,
undivided and eternal,
know that knowledge to be *sattvic*.

\[21\] But the knowledge by which one sees
different beings of various kinds
in all the creatures of the world,
see that knowledge as *rajasic*.

[p. 98] \[22\] And the knowledge that is attached
to one paltry deed as being all,
without reason or real purpose,
that knowledge is called *tamasic*.

\[23\] Restrained action free from clinging,
done without desire or hatred
and with no yearning for its fruit,
such an action is called *sattvic*.

\[24\] Action done to attain desires,
or done out of selfishness,
or performed with too much effort
is declared to be *rajasic*.

\[25\] Deluded action that ignores
consequences, loss, hurting others,
and one’s own capability
is spoken of as *tamasic*.

\[26\] Free from clinging and self-interest,
steadfast, resolved, and unperturbed
either by success or failure,
such an agent is called *sattvic*.

\[27\] Passionate, craving action’s fruits,
greedy, violent, and impure,
beset both by joy and sorrow,
such an agent is *rajasic*.

\[28\] Undisciplined, vulgar, stubborn,
wicked, idle, and deceitful,
desperate and always dawdling,
such an agent is *tamasic*.

\[29\] Hear now the threefold distinction
of intellect and steadfastness
[p. 99] set forth in reference to the *gunas*,
one by one, leaving nothing out.

\[30\] Knowing action and inaction,
what to fear and what not to fear,
knowing bondage and liberation,
that is a *sattvic* intellect.

\[31\] An intellect incapable
of distinguishing right from wrong,
what is duty from what is not,
is *rajasic*, Son of Pritha.

\[32\] An intellect that, cloaked in darkness,
is convinced what is wrong is right
and that all things are perverted,
is *tamasic*, Son of Pritha.

\[33\] The steadfastness that through *Yoga*
holds the functions of the senses,
the mind and breath without swerving,
is called *sattvic*, Son of Pritha.

\[34\] The steadfastness with which one holds
on to duty, wealth, and pleasure
with attachment, desiring fruit,
is called *rajasic*, Arjuna.

\[35\] The steadfastness with which the dull
do not give up sleep, fear, sorrow,
lethargy, and self-importance,
is called *tamasic*, Arjuna.

\[36\] And now, Bull of the Bharatas,
learn from Me the threefold happiness
enjoyed through practice, and by which
one comes to the end of suffering.

\[37\] [p. 100] Happiness at first like poison
but then transformed into nectar,
born from one’s own luminous mind,
is considered to be *sattvic*.

\[38\] First like nectar through the contact
of the senses with their objects,
but then transformed into poison,
that happiness is called *rajasic*.

\[39\] That happiness, which both at first
and afterward deludes the self,
born of sleep, sloth, and confusion,
is declared to be *tamasic*.

\[40\] There is no being, either on earth
or even in heaven among the gods,
that can exist at all without
these three *gunas* born from matter.

\[41\] All the duties of the *brahmins*,
the *kshatriyas*, the *vaishyas*,
and the *shudras* are apportioned
according to their innate *gunas*.

\[42\] Calmness, restraint, austerity,
honesty, patience, purity,
knowledge, faith, and discernment
are the *brahmins*’ natural duties.

\[43\] Heroic courage, majesty,
expertise, staunchness in battle,
generosity, lordliness,
are the *kshatriyas*’ natural duties.

\[44\] Plowing, cowherding, and trade
are the *vaishyas*’ innate duties.
[p. 101] Everything to do with service
is the *shudras*’ natural duty.

\[45\] Contented with his own duties,
a man attains full perfection.
Listen now how one contented
with his duties finds perfection.

\[46\] Worshipping through his own duty
the origin of all beings
who pervades the entire universe
is how man attains perfection.

\[47\] Better one’s own duty poorly
than another’s well performed.
Performing one’s inborn duty,
a person never incurs guilt.

\[48\] Never abandon inborn duty
even when faulty, Arjuna.
All new actions are enveloped
with faultiness, as fire by smoke.

\[49\] With intellect always unattached,
with self conquered, desire dispelled,
one attains by renunciation
perfect, unexcelled nonaction.

\[50\] Learn from me briefly, Arjuna,
how having attained perfection
one also attains to *Brahman*,
the consummate state of wisdom.

\[51\] Conjoined with a pure intellect,
firmly in control of the self,
abandoning all the senses,
rejecting both desire and hatred,

[p. 102] \[52\] Dwelling alone, eating lightly,
controlling speech, body, and mind,
devoted to *Dhyana* *Yoga*,
taking refuge in dispassion,

\[53\] Relinquishing egoism,
force, pride, desire, anger,
and possessions; selfless, tranquil,
one is fit to unite with *Brahman*.

\[54\] Absorbed in *Brahman*, self serene,
without sorrow or desire,
impartial to all, one attains
devotion to Me that is supreme.

\[55\] Devoted to Me, one comes to know
how great and who I really am.
Knowing Me in reality,
immediately one enters Me.

\[56\] And, performing every action
always with reliance on Me,
from My grace one attains the eternal
and imperishable abode.

\[57\] Concentrating on renouncing
all action in Me as supreme,
relying on *Buddhi* *Yoga*,
constantly keep your thoughts on Me.

\[58\] Thinking of me you will transcend
every obstacle by My grace.
But if out of egoism
you do not listen, then you perish.

\[59\] If you retreat into your ego
and say to yourself, ‘I will not fight,’
[p. 103] your resolve will prove untrue.
Nature itself will force your hand.

\[60\] Bound by your *karma*, Arjuna,
that is born from your own nature,
you will do what in delusion
is contrary to your wishes.

\[61\] The Lord abides deep in the hearts
of all beings, O Arjuna,
making them move by illusion,
like puppets mounted on a machine.

\[62\] Go to Him alone, your refuge,
with all your being, Bharata!
By His grace you will attain
supreme peace in eternity.

\[63\] This knowledge that I’ve expounded
is the secret of all secrets.
Consider it in its entirety,
and then do just as you desire.

\[64\] Hear again My most secret word
and My ultimate utterance.
You are assuredly loved by Me,
and so I will speak for your good.

\[65\] Worship Me with devotion,
sacrifice to Me, bow to Me.
So shall you truly come to Me,
I avow, for you are dear to Me.

\[66\] Abandoning all your duties,
take refuge in Me alone.
I will free you from all evils;
you should never grieve or sorrow.

[p. 104] \[67\] Always keep this from those without
austere practice and devotion,
from those who do not wish to listen,
and from those who speak ill of Me.

\[68\] Whoever sets forth this highest,
secret teaching to My votaries,
and has worshipped Me devoutly,
will come to Me without a doubt.

\[69\] And no one among men will do
more pleasing work for Me than he;
and no other upon this earth
will be to Me more beloved.

\[70\] Whoever studies and recites
this sacred dialogue of ours,
will have cherished Me, I avow,
with the sacrifice of wisdom.

\[71\] Even one who only hears it,
full of faith and without scoffing,
will be freed in the happy worlds
of people who have acted purely.

\[72\] Have you now heard this with your mind
in one-pointed concentration?
Have your ignorance and delusion
been destroyed, O Son of Pritha?”

Arjuna answered:

\[73\] “My delusion is gone; I have gained
wisdom through your grace, O Krishna,
and am standing with doubt dispelled.
I will carry out your orders.”

[p. 105] Then Sanjaya said:

\[74\] “Thus I have heard from Lord Krishna
and the great-souled son of Pritha—
this miraculous dialogue
that caused my hair to stand on end.

\[75\] By Vyasa’s grace I have heard
this supreme and secret *Yoga*
that Krishna, the Lord of *Yoga*
Himself, divulged before my eyes.

\[76\] O King, each time I remember
this miraculous dialogue
between Arjuna and Krishna,
I rejoice again and again.

\[77\] And each time that I remember
Krishna’s most astonishing form,
I am overcome with wonder
and I rejoice anew, O King.

\[78\] Wherever there is the Lord of *Yoga*,
wherever Arjuna, the archer,
there will be splendor, victory,
wealth, and goodness, I am certain.”
