### 15.1–13: Living in view of God’s faithfulness

*[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-1|Text]], p. 317* · The argument from ch 14 continues, calling for gracious service toward these Jews in Rome, following the model of Christ’s suffering, and invoking a vision of these Gentiles worshipping within the midst of the congregation of Israel, as scripture foretold. ^15-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-1|1]]:** Lit., “but we, the able, are obligated to support the stumbling of the unable (ones).” *We*, the first reference to the audience, together with Paul, as the subject. There may be a bit of ironic insult here: how “able” are they if they are *un*able to understand that faithfulness involves caring for the neighbor’s interests? The interpreter must fill in a specific reference for the ability or inability. *The failings of the weak*, the NRSV reverses the phrase: Paul does not write of the inabilities of the weak; rather, he writes of the weaknesses of the unable ones. The NRSV reading supports the traditional view of Paul as Torah-free, able to move “beyond” Torah in Christ; thus, the “weak” are unable to give up Torah as Paul believes they should. He nonetheless calls to service those, who, like himself, are free to tolerate the immaturity of “the weak.” The alternative, which reflects the Greek, indicates that because these Jews were unable to understand that Christ was the Torah’s goal for righteousness for the nations as well as Israel, they stumbled over the proclamation of Christ to Gentiles, and the positive response of some Gentiles. Paul, the Torah-observant Jew, shares with his Gentile audience the “ability” to believe that God has granted righteousness through the faithfulness of Christ to all of the nations; at the same time, he maintains a responsibility to support or hold up those Israelites who are not similarly persuaded. *Put up with*, Gk “bastazō” has a more positive sense here: Paul has already taught against causing stumbling or dispute; now he exhorts them to *please* the neighbors (v 2). The Gentile believers are to “support,” that is, to help the stumbling to be able to stand (or the weak to be enabled). ^15-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-3|3]]:** [[Psalms 069#^69-10|Ps 69.9]], which Paul has cited in 11.9. Cf. [[Philippians 02#^2-1|Phil 2.1–18]]. ^15-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-4|4]]:** See [[LXX 1 Maccabees 12#^12-9|1 Macc 12.9]]. As Paul has just applied scripture to instruct them, so too they are to draw from scripture to muster the required endurance for taking the posture of serving the other, and they are to be encouraged (or exhorted) by those who have similarly suffered in the present age, but with hope in God’s promise of justice and restoration; cf. 14.18–15.4. ^15-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-6|6]]:** *Voice*, lit., “mouth.” ^15-6

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-8|8–12]]:** Paul concludes this section by reiterating (11.30–32) the interwoven destinies of Jews and Gentiles. ^15-8

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-8|8]]:** *Promises given to the patriarchs*, that all the nations would be blessed, or bless themselves, by the (circumcised) seed ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-16|Gen 22.16–17]]). ^15-8-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-9|9]]:** See [[Psalms 018#^18-50|Ps 18.49]] (17.50\[LXX\]; also [[LXX 2 Kingdoms 22#^22-50|2 Sam 22.50]]\[LXX\]). Vv 8–9 probably draw from the next verse of [[Psalms 018#^18-51|Ps 18.50]]\[LXX 17.51\], which declares that God “is showing mercy to his anointed one, to David and his seed forever.” The psalm describes David confessing God among the nations. That is how Paul understands himself, as a representative of Israel. *Sing praises*, lit., “sing psalms.” Paul envisages the nations among Israelites together blessing God (Heb “ ʿamim,” “peoples,” and “goyim,” “nations,” can both include Israel \[e.g., [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-6|Ex 19.6]]\]). ^15-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-10|10]]:** See [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-43|Deut 32.43]]. ^15-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-11|11]]:** See [[Psalms 117#^117-1|Ps 117.1]]; mercy is mentioned in the next verse of the psalm (v 2; 116.2\[LXX\]); again, the contrast retained between the other nations and the people Israel indicates unison. ^15-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-12|12]]:** See [[Isaiah 11#^11-10|Isa 11.10]] (cf. [[Romans 14#^14-17|Rom 14.17]]). *Root of Jesse*, the Davidic line (Jesse is David’s father), hence the Messiah. ^15-12

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-13|13]]:** *In believing*, or “by which to (sustain) faithfulness”; *by the power of the Holy Spirit*, or “by an enabling spirit of holiness.” ^15-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-16|16]]:** *Priestly service*, perhaps inspired by [[Isaiah 66#^66-19|Isa 66.19–23]], Paul sees himself like a priest preparing pure sacrifices ([[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^28-35|Ex 28.35]],[[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^28-43|43]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-17|Phil 2.17]]). Alternatively, *offering of the Gentiles* may refer to the collection (vv 25–32). ^15-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-19|19]]:** *Of the Spirit of God*, “of God” not in some mss. ^15-19

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-20|20]]:** *Someone else’s foundation*, work undertaken by other evangelists. ^15-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-21|21]]:** See [[LXX Isaiah 52#^52-15|Isa 52.15]]\[LXX\], concerning the (suffering) servant. *Of him,* not in Gk. ^15-21

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-25|25]]:** *Ministry to the saints*, to deliver the collection to the Jerusalem assembly. ^15-25

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-27|27]]:** *They ought*, lit., “they are obligated.” ^15-27

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-28|28]]:** *What has been collected*, lit., “this fruit.” ^15-28

**[[NRSVue Romans 15#^15-31|31]]:** *The unbelievers*, lit., “the ones not-being-persuaded.” ^15-31
