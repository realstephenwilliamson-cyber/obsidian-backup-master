### 15.1–35: Jerusalem council

*[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-1|Text]], p. 253* · Gentiles have been accepted among the people of God (10.1–11.18; 13.1–14.28); now the church addresses whether these believers should observe particular practices that the Torah commands for Jews. Luke presents this issue as having been resolved amicably (see 6.1–6). Paul provides a substantially different account of the meeting ([[Galatians 02#^2-1|Gal 2.1–10]]). He indicates that an uncircumcised Gentile, Titus, accompanied him to Jerusalem, the debate was contentious, he was designated as the apostle to the Gentiles, and the only requirement imposed on Gentiles was that they remember the poor; nor does Paul mention the writing of a letter to Gentiles. ^15-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-1|1–5]]:** The position that Gentile believers need to observe the law. ^15-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-1|1]]:** *Circumcised*, a marker of Jewish identity ([[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-15|1 Macc 1.15]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.192; Tacitus, *Hist.* 5.5.2) that Jews trace to Abraham ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-9|Gen 17.9–14]]; see also [[Torah 27 - Tazria#^12-3|Lev 12.3]]; “Circumcision,” p. 673). Advocates of this position argue that salvation requires both belief in Jesus *and* being a Jew, and therefore wish Gentile believers to convert and become Jews. The ensuing debate and resulting decision reinforce the position of Acts that salvation requires only belief in Jesus see v 11). *Saved*, see 2.40n. The teaching contrasts with that of apostles who preach that belief in Jesus saves (2.21; 4.12; 11.14; 16.30–31). ^15-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-2|2]]:** *Appointed to go up*, Paul describes the decision as coming from a revelation ([[Galatians 02#^2-2|Gal 2.2]]), rather than a human committee. ^15-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-4|4]]:** *Elders*, see 11.30. ^15-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-5|5]]:** *Pharisees*, dedicated observers of Jewish Law ([[Galatians 01#^1-14|Gal 1.14]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 1.110). Acts presents Pharisees as largely sympathetic with Christians (e.g., 5.34; 23.8), in part because both groups share a belief in resurrection. This portrayal contrasts sharply to the **Chs 15–18:** Second missionary journey of Paul. largely negative characterization of Pharisees in the Gospel ([[Luke 05#^5-30|Lk 5.30]]; [[Luke 06#^6-7|6.7]]; [[Luke 11#^11-42|11.42–43]]; [[Luke 12#^12-1|12.1]]; [[Luke 16#^16-14|16.14]]; [[Luke 18#^18-11|18.11–13]]), and elsewhere in the NT. These Pharisees accept Gentile believers in Jesus, but require that they undergo circumcision and thus become Jews. *Keep the law of Moses*, clarifies what is implied by v 1. ^15-5

### 15.6–21: The speeches

*[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-6|Text]], p. 254* · The major participants in the meeting, Peter, Paul, Barnabas, and James, also appear in Paul’s account ([[Galatians 02#^2-1|Gal 2.1]],[[Galatians 02#^2-9|9]]). ^15-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-7|7–11]]:** Peter’s speech: Peter’s main points — that he is the apostle to the Gentiles ([[Galatians 02#^2-9|Gal 2.9]]), Gentiles received the Holy Spirit (e.g., [[Romans 05#^5-5|Rom 5.5]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-2|Gal 3.2]]), God makes no distinction between Jew and Gentile ([[Romans 03#^3-23|Rom 3.23]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-28|Gal 3.28–39]]), the Law presents an unreasonable burden ([[2 Corinthians 03#^3-16|2 Cor 3.16]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-23|Gal 3.23–24]]), and salvation comes through grace ([[Romans 03#^3-20|Rom 3.20]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-11|Gal 3.11]]) — are all arguments made by Paul. ^15-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-7|7]]:** *Much debate*, the absence of defense by the Pharisees makes the proceedings seem harmonious. ^15-7-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-8|8–9]]:** Peter summarizes his actions with Cornelius, see 10.1–11.18. ^15-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-9|9]]:** *Cleansing their hearts*, see [[Psalms 051#^51-12|Ps 51.10]]\[HB v 12\]. *No distinction*, 10.34. ^15-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-10|10]]:** *Putting God to the test*, 5.9; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^17-2|Ex 17.2]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-16|Deut 6.16]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-41|Ps 78.41]]. *Yoke*, neckpiece used with draft animal. Many Jewish texts use the image metaphorically to express hardship, especially foreign oppression ([[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-48|Deut 28.48]]; [[Isaiah 14#^14-25|Isa 14.25]]; [[Jeremiah 27#^27-12|Jer 27.12]]; [[LXX Sirach 40#^40-1|Sir 40.1]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 13#^13-41|1 Macc 13.41]]). Rabbinic writings use it to represent proper ac- ceptance of God’s sovereignty (*m. Ber.* 2.2; *b. Seb.* 13a; *b. Ker.* 7a; *Ex. Rab.* 30.5), which extends blessings to those who wear it (*m. Avot* 3.5). ^15-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-11|11]]:** *Saved through the grace*, Paul speaks of justification through grace (e.g., [[Romans 03#^3-24|Rom 3.24]]). Most Jewish tradition recognizes the “yoke of the ancestors” (i.e., observance of the Law) and “salvation through grace” (i.e., divine mercy) as complementary, not oppositional (e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]]; [[Hosea 11#^11-3|Hos 11.3–4]],[[Hosea 11#^11-8|8–9]]; Ps 51; 103.7–13). ^15-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-12|12]]:** *Signs and wonders*, see 13.11; 14.8–10. ^15-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-13|15.13–21]]:** **James’s speech.** James concurs with Peter, adds scriptural support for Gentile inclusion, and proposes a set of rules for Gentile believers. ^15-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-13|13]]:** *James*, brother of Jesus; see 12.17; Josephus, *Ant.* 20.200. ^15-13-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-14|14]]:** *Simeon*, Semitic form of Simon, Peter’s given name. *People for his name*, a term normally used to describe Israel (3.12; 4.10; 5.34; [[Luke 07#^7-16|Lk 7.16]]; [[Luke 19#^19-47|19.47]]; [[Luke 21#^21-23|21.23]]). **16–18**: See Am 9.11–12. The Hebrew version speaks of Israel’s possessing other nations. The version here, taken from the LXX, refers to God’s restoration of all peoples, Jews and Gentiles. ^15-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-20|20]]:** *Things polluted by idols*, presumably foods sacrificed to idols (v 29; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-15|Ex 34.15]]; [[Daniel 01#^1-8|Dan 1.8]]; 1 Cor 8; 10.18–22). *Fornication*, improper sexual activity ([[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-6|Lev 18.6–30]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-1|1 Cor 5.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-18|6.18]]). *Strangled*, prohibition against consuming meat from improperly slaughtered animals ([[Torah 25 - Tzav#^7-24|Lev 7.24]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^17-15|17.15]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-21|Deut 14.21]]); although laws of proper slaughter are never explicit in the Tanakh, they may be assumed by several texts and are fully developed by the rabbinic period. *Blood*, probably a reference to murder (i.e., bloodshed, e.g., [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.13]]); it might also indicate a prohibition against the consumption of blood through proper ritual slaughtering, although this understanding would seem to duplicate the previous prohibition. The rules distance Gentile believers from worship of pagan deities and the immoral effects of such worship (e.g., Wis 13–15; *Sib. Or.* 3.762–64). Jewish texts understand Gentiles are to observe some of these same requirements ([[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^17-10|Lev 17.10–15]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-26|18.26]]; *Jub*. 7.20). The rabbis later develop the concept of the Noahide Laws (an interpretation of the covenant with Noah in [[Torah 02 - Noach#^9-1|Gen 9.1–17]]), which require Gentiles to refrain from idolatry, blasphemy, murder, incest, stealing, and consuming flesh from a living creature, and to establish justice in an orderly legal system (*t. Avod. Zar.* 8.4; *b. Sanh.* 52b; *Gen. Rab.* 16.6). The rules may also correspond to the rabbinic understanding that three acts — idolatry, shedding blood, and sexual misconduct, are so hei-nous that Jews may never engage in them (*b. Pesah.* 25a-b; *b. Sanh.* 74a). See “The Law,” p. 655. ^15-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-21|21]]:** James justifies his position by invoking Moses, possibly suggesting that these requirements stem from the Torah. ^15-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-22|22]]:** *Barsabbas*, see 1.23. Nothing else is reported of him. *Silas*, Paul’s frequent companion (16.16–18.17). *God from of old are all his works.’* c Other ancient authorities lack *and from whatever has been strangled* d Gk *from among them* ^15-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 15#^15-25|25]]:** *Unanimously*, the church is in complete agreement. ^15-25
