# Ecclesiastes 6 (NRSVue)

## The frustration of desires

**6:1** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it lies heavy upon humankind: ^6-1

**6:2** those to whom God gives wealth, possessions, and honor, so that they lack nothing of all that they desire, yet God does not enable them to enjoy these things, but a stranger enjoys them. This is vanity; it is a grievous ill. ^6-2

**6:3** A man may father a hundred children and live many years, but however many are the days of his years, if he does not enjoy life’s good things or has no burial, I say that a stillborn child is better off than he. ^6-3

**6:4** For it comes in vanity and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered; ^6-4

**6:5** moreover, it has not seen the sun or known anything, yet it finds rest rather than he. ^6-5

**6:6** Even though he should live a thousand years twice over yet enjoy no good—do not all go to one place? ^6-6

**6:7** All human toil is for the mouth, yet the appetite is not satisfied. ^6-7

**6:8** For what advantage have the wise over fools? And what do the poor have who know how to conduct themselves before the living? ^6-8

**6:9** Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of desire; this also is vanity and a chasing after wind. ^6-9

**6:10** Whatever has come to be has already been named, and it is known what humans are and that they are not able to dispute with those who are stronger. ^6-10

**6:11** The more words, the more vanity, so how is one the better? ^6-11

**6:12** For who knows what is good for mortals while they live the few days of their vain life, which they pass like a shadow? For who can tell them what will be after them under the sun? ^6-12
