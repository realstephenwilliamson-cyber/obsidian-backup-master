The Twelve Kālīs

The Four Kālis Who Consume the Sphere of Objectivity

1\) Srṣtṭikālī - The Kālī of Creation. The Emanation of Objectivity.

tataśca prāgiyaṁ śuddhā tathābhāsanasotsukā |

Srṣtikālī) in the scriptures. (148)

‘Arising from me, Her form Emptiness (vyoman) and, located in Emptiness, She is devoid of Emptiness. She is everything and, free of all things, She destroys emanation in all this universe. She is the energy (kalaa) Whose glorious power (vibhava) is all things and, capable of serving as the instrument of emanation, She inwardly attains tranquility and is known as S.r.s.tikaalii.’

‘Knowledge’ (jñāna) is abstention from forming thought constructs in relation to (this reality thus) divided. ‘Enumeration’ (samrkhyāna) is (on the contrary, the formation of) thought constructs, determined by the mutual exclusion (of the subject and the many objects from it and one another). ‘Movement’ (gati) is mounting (of all things) into one’s own nature in the manner of a reflection (in a mirror). ‘Sound’ (nāda) is the reflective awareness of one’s own nature which is all that remains by

destroying these (forms of perception). (173cd-175)

Thus the Goddess Parā, Who performs these five operations (kalanā), is called Kāli and Kālakarṣiṇī (She who Attracts time). Thus She is called Mātrsadbhāva (the Essence of (All Perceiving) Subjects) because the subjectivity within the (seven orders of) subjects is clearly evident (only) within this ultimate level of consciousness (antasarhvitti). In the venerable Niśsisaricara she is called

Vāmeśvarī. (176-178ab)
