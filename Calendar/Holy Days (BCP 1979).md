# Holy Days (BCP 1979)

The Holy Days of the Prayer Book with their readings and collects, and where each falls from October 2026 to the end of 2029. Home's date header, the Daily Office, the switch between the Sunday's readings and the feast's, and the homily templates read this note. It is made only from the BCP 1979 notes in this vault: the Calendar of the Church Year (titles and ranks, pp. 15–17 and 32–33), the Holy Days of the Lectionary (psalms and lessons, pp. 921–925), the Collects for Holy Days (contemporary and traditional, pp. 237–246 and the traditional equivalents), and the Daily Office Lectionary's Holy Days (pp. 996–1000). Readings that the lectionary note prints without a link are linked here to the verse notes (10 links added). The last column links the RCL note that carries the same collect, where there is one. Made 18 Oct 2026 by a script; see [[Vault Guide]] (the calendar section) for how it is used.

The Principal Feasts other than All Saints (Christmas Day, the Epiphany, Easter, Ascension, Pentecost, Trinity Sunday) and the Holy Name have their propers in the seasonal notes and are not in this table.

## The rules

> [!bcp] Principal Feasts (BCP p. 15)
> These feasts take precedence of any other day or observance. All Saints' Day may always be observed on the Sunday following November 1, in addition to its observance on the fixed date.

> [!bcp] Sundays (BCP p. 16)
> All Sundays of the year are feasts of our Lord Jesus Christ. In addition to the dated days listed above, only the following feasts, appointed on fixed days, take precedence of a Sunday:
>
> The Holy Name The Presentation The Transfiguration
>
> All other Feasts of our Lord, and all other Major Feasts appointed on fixed days in the Calendar, when they occur on a Sunday, are normally transferred to the first convenient open day within the week. When desired, however, the Collect, Preface, and one or more of the Lessons appointed for the Feast may be substituted for those of the Sunday, but not from the Last Sunday after Pentecost through the First Sunday after the Epiphany, or from the Last Sunday after the Epiphany through Trinity Sunday.

> [!bcp] Holy Days (BCP pp. 16–17)
> The following Holy Days are regularly observed throughout the year. Unless otherwise ordered in the preceding rules concerning Sundays, they have precedence over all other days of commemoration or of special observance:

> [!bcp] Holy Week, Easter Week, Ash Wednesday and a weekday (BCP p. 17)
> Feasts appointed of fixed days in the Calendar are not observed on the days of Holy Week or of Easter Week. Major Feasts falling in these weeks are transferred to the week following the Second Sunday of Easter, in the order of their occurrence.
>
> Feasts appointed on fixed days in the Calendar do not take precedence of Ash Wednesday.
>
> Feasts of our Lord and other Major Feasts appointed on fixed days, which fall upon or are transferred to a weekday, may be observed on any open day within the week. This provision does not apply to Christmas Day, the Epiphany, and All Saints' Day.

> [!note] Where the vault goes beyond the text
> - The Prayer Book gives no date for Thanksgiving Day; the Daily Office view uses the fourth Thursday of November (the U.S. federal date).
> - When two feasts compete for the same open day (St. Stephen, St. John and the Holy Innocents when 26 December is a Sunday), the Daily Office engine keeps them in the order of their occurrence, as the Prayer Book says for Holy Week (p. 17); for other weeks the text says only "the first convenient open day" (p. 16). Stephen chose this reading on 18 Oct 2026.
> - The Prayer Book names the Collect, Preface and "one or more of the Lessons" for the Sunday substitution (p. 16), not the psalm; Home keeps the Sunday's psalm unless you change it. For the Holy Name, the Presentation and the Transfiguration, which take precedence of a Sunday, the switch back to the Sunday's readings was added at Stephen's request; the Prayer Book gives no such choice.
