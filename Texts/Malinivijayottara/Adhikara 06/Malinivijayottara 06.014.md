## Mūla

ततोऽप्यर्धाङ्गुलव्याप्त्या पुरषट्कमनुक्रमात् ।
चतुष्कं तु द्वयेऽन्यस्मिन्नेकमेकत्र चिन्तयन् ॥ ^mula

tato 'py ardhāṅgulavyāptyā puraṣaṭkam anukramāt |
catuṣkaṁ tu dvaye 'nyasminn ekam ekatra cintayan || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, *Mālinī*, January 2024 – April 2024
> p. 26 ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Malini (journal), January-April 2024.pdf#page=28>))
>
> Then, having visualized (the Principles), 1) the sixteen worlds ranging from Kālāgni to Vīrabhadra should be projected up to the ankle (each one occupying a quarter finger's space). 2) Beyond that, one should project the (thirty-nine) worlds from Lakulin to Dviraṇḍa, each of which occupies one finger-space. 3) Chagalāṇḍaka occupies (a space of) three fingers. 4) (The 16 worlds) of the eight divine beings and the eight Yogas together occupy four fingers space. 5) *After that, one should think that there are another six worlds, each of which occupies a half finger space (3 fingers), 6) (there are) another four in two fingers (space) (2 fingers) and one in one (finger space).'*[^18] (with 6.11cd–14)

[^18]: MV 6/14 is quoted in TĀv ad 8/446-452abc (445cd-452a). Abhinava helps us by offering an extended explanation of MV 6/11cd-14 in TĀ 16/114-119 and 123cd-128 where he writes: 'Now (let us move on to) the deposition of the worlds (*puranyāsa*). 1) The (element) Earth (extends up to) the ankle. There are sixteen worlds here (within it, and it occupies four fingers). 2) After that, each of (the thirty-nine worlds) from Lakulin up to Dviraṇḍa occupies one finger (space), 3) whereas Chagalāṇḍa occupies three fingers. 4) Now the two groups consisting of the eight gods (deva) and the eight Yogas are (contained) within four (finger spaces), as each one occupies a quarter finger (space). In this way, there are forty-six fingers (from Water) up to Nature, and fifty-six worlds, and before (them) sixteen, within the Earth (principle).' (114-116) Jayaratha explains in his commentary to this passage: 'The fifty-six worlds from Water to Nature comprise the forty worlds from Lakulin to Chagalāṇḍa, and the sixteen worlds of the eight gods and eight Yogas. There is a space of forty-two fingers up to Lakulin and Chagalāṇḍa (39 + 3), and four more for the two groups of eight (in Nature), thus making forty-six fingers in all. Below that are the sixteen worlds of the Earth principle that extend for four finger-spaces. Taking those into account, up to Nature, there are seventy-two worlds contained in a space of fifty fingers.' Abhinava continues: 5) 'After this, there are six worlds, pervading half a finger (each), they are contained in three fingers (breadths). (These are the worlds of Puruṣa). 6) There are four (more worlds in the principle of Attachment contained) in two (fingers' space), and one world in one finger (space). In this (same) way, the eleven worlds of (the principle of) attachment and the individual soul are (contained in a space of) six fingers. 7) Then comes the world of Krodheśa, that takes up one (finger space), and that of Caṇḍa that takes up two. These are the two worlds of the (principle of impure) Knowledge. 8) In the same way, (there are two worlds,) Samvarta and Jyotiṣ, in the principle of the Force (of limited agency). 9) (The worlds) Śūra and Pañcāntaka occupy one and two fingers, respectively, in the principle of Necessity (niyati).' (117-119) '10) The three worlds in (the principle of) Time (kāla), namely, Ekavīra, Śikheśa and Śrīkaṇṭha, are (contained) in three (fingers' space).' (122ab) Here the order of the Kañcukas from Puruṣa to Māyā is 1) Rāga 2) Vidyā 3) Kalā 4) Niyati 5) Time. The sequential order of the tattvas is practically the same in all its parts in all the sources except for these five of which we find several sequences. Even so, in the great majority of cases the order, starting from Puruṣa in ascent is: 1) Time 2) Niyati 3) Rāga 4) Vidyā and 5) Kalā. The order we have here is virtually the reverse of this one. The difference is considerable. Thus, Abhinava feels its appropriate to dedicate a few lines to explain why that is so. After this brief digression Abhinava continues: 'In this way, (the worlds) contained in the six principles beginning with the individual soul are deposited into (a space of) eighteen fingers. Then the group of eight (worlds) ending with Aṅguṣ˜hamātra, within the principle of Māyā, occupy four fingers' (space altogether), as each one (extends for) half a finger(-space). In this way, the twenty-eight worlds, (contained in the principles ranging) from the individual soul up to Māyā, (occupy) twenty-two finger-breadths. (123cd-125ab) There are five worlds in the four fingers (space) of Pure Knowledge. Three worlds (each occupy) two-thirds of a finger(-space), in due order, and (the other) two (occupy) two. Then, in the (following) four fingers' (space), there are the eight worlds of the Īśvara principle, (one for every) half finger(-space), in due order. After them comes the group of five (worlds) of Sadāśiva. Three are in two (fingers' space) and two in (the other) two fingers, (and so are contained) in this way in four (fingers' space altogether). Thus, there are eighteen (worlds in the space of) twelve (fingers). (125cd-127) The worlds in this way are sixteen, fifty-six, twenty-eight, and eighteen. The finger (-spaces) are four, forty-six, twenty-two, and twelve, respectively.' (128) In brief: 16 worlds (4 fingers) + 56 worlds (46 fingers) + 28 worlds (22 fingers) + 18 worlds (12 fingers) = 118 worlds in 84 finger-breadths.
