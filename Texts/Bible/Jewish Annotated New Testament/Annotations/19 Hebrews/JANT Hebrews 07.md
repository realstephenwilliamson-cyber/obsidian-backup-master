### 7.1–17: Melchizedek’s superiority to the levitical priesthood

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-1|Text]], p. 473* ^7-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-1|1–2]]:** A paraphrase of the encounter between Abraham and the priest-king Melchizedek ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^14-17|Gen 14.17–20]]); see 5.6n. *Salem*, understood as from Heb “peace” (“shalom”), identified with Jerusalem; it may have originally been a name of a Canaanite god, Shalim. Philo also offers an etymology connected to peace (*Leg. all.* 3.15.79). *King of*, Heb “malki-,” from “melek,” “king.” *Righteousness*, from Heb “tzedek.” *Most High God*, often simply “Most High” (Gk “hypsistos,” Heb “el elyon”), an epithet for Israel’s God ([[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-8|Deut 32.8]]; Ps 52.7). ^7-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-3|3]]:** *Without father*, except for Gen 14 and [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]], Scripture says nothing of Melchizedek. In the apocryphal *2 En.* 23.15–41, Melchizedek is miraculously born from his dead mother, is a priest from birth, is Noah’s nephew, and is kept safe from the flood in order to be the priest to Noah’s descendants. *Son of God*, Jesus. *Priest forever*, [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]] (see [[Hebrews 05#^5-6|Heb 5.6]]). On Melchizedek’s making Abraham the “priest forever” see [[Nedarim 32#32b|*b. Ned*. 32b]]; *Lev. Rab*. 25.6. ^7-3

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-4|4]]:** *A tenth of the spoils*, [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^14-20|Gen 14.20]]; [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^28-22|28.22]]. ^7-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-5|5]]:** *Descendants of Levi* are entitled *to collect tithes*, see [[Torah 38 - Korach#^18-21|Num 18.21–32]]. According to [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^29-34|Gen 29.34]], Levi is one of Jacob’s sons; his children were the Temple functionaries, the Levites. ^7-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-6|6]]:** *Does not belong to their ancestry*, Melchizedek is not a descendant of Abraham and thus not a member of the priestly tribe of Levi. Israelite priests all derive from the tribe of Levi, but at some point a distinct priestly class emerges, so that “priests” and “Levites” are treated as separate groups; see [[Torah 34 - Bamidbar#^3-5|Num 3.5–10]]; [[Luke 10#^10-31|Lk 10.31–32]]. ^7-6

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-7|7]]:** *Inferior is blessed by the superior*, the reverse is frequent; Melchizedek himself blesses “God Most High” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^14-20|Gen 14.20]]). ^7-7

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-8|8]]:** *Those who are mortal*, ordinary priests, descended from Levi. *One . . . \[who\] lives*, Melchizedek, since he is said to be without genealogy and there is no notice of his death in Genesis (see [[Hebrews 07#^7-23|Heb 7.23–25]]). ^7-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-9|9–11]]:** As if Levi tithed to Melchizedek because Levi descended from Abraham. As Melchizedek is superior to Abraham and so to Levi, so Jesus, the priest in the *order of Melchizedek*, is superior to the Jewish priesthood. *Perfection*, see 2.10; 5.8; 7.28; 12.2. *Received the law under this priesthood*, . . . *order of Aaron*, Aaron is a descendent of Levi but holds a special status because God ordains him a high priest at Sinai and makes an eternal covenant with him; see Ex 29. ^7-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-12|12]]:** *Change in the law*, it is unclear why the author makes this argument; perhaps he builds on the idea that priests were repositories of law (see [[Jeremiah 18#^18-18|Jer 18.18]]; [[Ezekiel 07#^7-26|Ezek 7.26]]). Like Plato, the author sees permanence as perfection and change as undesirable. ^7-12

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-13|13]]:** *Another tribe*, not the Levites but Judah (v 14). *Ever served*, only Levi’s descendants could serve as priests; see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-25|Ex 32.25–29]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-8|Deut 10.8]]. ^7-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-14|14]]:** *Judah*, Jesus was seen as of Davidic descent, and thus of the tribe of Judah; see [[Matthew 01#^1-6|Mt 1.6]]; [[Luke 03#^3-31|Lk 3.31]]; [[Romans 01#^1-3|Rom 1.3]]. Thus, he is here connected typologically to Melchizedek rather than genealogically to Aaron; typology is a type of interpretation that connects individuals or events based on shared characteristics. ^7-14

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-16|16]]:** *Power of an indestructible life*, Melchizedek is immortal (see vv 8,17) and God proclaimed him a priest forever in [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]]. ^7-16

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-17|17]]:** *Forever*, here understood in reference to personal life, not to the dynasty. [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]], cited also in [[Hebrews 05#^5-6|Heb 5.6]]; [[Hebrews 06#^6-20|6.20]]; [[Hebrews 07#^7-21|7.21]]. ^7-17

### 7.18–28: Jesus the high priest

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-18|Text]], p. 474* ^7-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-18|18]]:** *Abrogation*, because Jesus is not descended from Aaron or Levi and not appointed according to [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^29-1|Ex 29.1–35]]; Lev 8–9. ^7-18-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-19|19]]:** *The Law made nothing perfect*, although Hebrews sees the Torah as given by God, it cannot be *perfect*, because it is a human institution that exists in the material realm. Although the Tanakh rarely uses language of perfection for Torah, Ps 119 idealizes Torah as God-like. Jewish mystical traditions describe the Torah as the preexistent blueprint for creation; *Gen. Rab.* 1.1; *Zohar “*Terumah” 61. ^7-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-20|20–21]]:** [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]]. For the divine promise as *oath* see [[Hebrews 06#^6-17|Heb 6.17–18]]. ^7-20

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-22|22]]:** *Guarantee*, promise. *Better covenant*, a theme developed in chs 8–10. ^7-22

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-25|25]]:** *To make intercession*; see [[Job 33#^33-23|Job 33.23]]; [[Romans 08#^8-27|Rom 8.27]],[[Romans 08#^8-34|34]]; when Jesus speaks to his divine father as intercessor for his followers. Common in early Jewish and Christian tradition, intercession means assistance by a person of higher station who acts as an advocate for one who is more vulnerable. Jesus’ prayer in Jn 17 is an example of this kind of intercession. In some early Christian texts, intercession serves as a plea for the forgiveness of sin (see [[1 John 02#^2-1|1 Jn 2.1–2]]; *1 Clem*. 56.6). Although a priest who offers sacrifice in Leviticus may do so on behalf of others, it is not intercession in this sense, but a performance of an essential duty by a designated agent. ^7-25

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-26|26]]:** *Exalted above the heavens*, see [[Psalms 113#^113-4|Ps 113.4]] ^7-26

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 07#^7-28|28]]:** *Word of the oath*, referring to God swearing the oath in [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]] ([[Hebrews 05#^5-6|Heb 5.6]]; [[Hebrews 07#^7-17|7.17]],[[Hebrews 07#^7-21|21]]). *A Son*, Jesus (see 1.2). Because God speaks the oath through David in the Psalm, it comes *later than the law*, the Sinaitic covenant. God’s oath appointing Melchizedek takes precedence over the eternal covenant God made with Aaron in Exodus, not because the author regards the authority of Psalms as greater than the Torah, but because the divine words preserved in the Psalm supersede the words spoken in Genesis and Exodus temporally. If God said or did something revising an earlier action, the latter act supersedes the former. The logic of Hebrews is as follows**:** David wrote Ps 95, and since David lives after Aaron, the eternal ordination of Melchizedek supersedes Aaron’s. The practice of biblical interpretation from the rabbis to the present ascribes the highest scriptural authority to Torah. Other parts of the Tanakh are used in the service of interpreting the Torah, not the other way around. The prioritizing of Torah is evident in Jewish liturgical practice, where the “Haftarah” readings serve to support or illumine the weekly Torah reading. See also “*Logos*,” p. 688. ^7-28
