# Proverbs 17 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Proverbs 16|← Proverbs 16]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Proverbs 18|Proverbs 18 →]]

**17:1** Better is a dry morsel with quiet than a house full of feasting with strife. ^17-1

**17:2** A slave who deals wisely will rule over a child who acts shamefully and will share the inheritance as one of the family. ^17-2

**17:3** The crucible is for silver and the furnace for gold, but the LORD tests the heart. ^17-3

**17:4** An evildoer listens to wicked lips, and a liar gives heed to a mischievous tongue. ^17-4

**17:5** Those who mock the poor insult their Maker; those who are glad at calamity will not go unpunished. ^17-5

**17:6** Grandchildren are the crown of the aged, and the glory of children is their parents. ^17-6

**17:7** Excess speech is not becoming to a fool; still less is false speech to a ruler.^[Or *a noble*] ^17-7

**17:8** A bribe is like a magic stone in the eyes of those who give it; wherever they turn they prosper. ^17-8

**17:9** One who forgives an affront fosters friendship, but one who dwells on disputes will alienate a friend. ^17-9

**17:10** A rebuke strikes deeper into a discerning person than a hundred blows into a fool. ^17-10

**17:11** Evil people seek only rebellion, but a cruel messenger will be sent against them. ^17-11

**17:12** Better to meet a she-bear robbed of its cubs than to confront a fool immersed in folly. ^17-12

**17:13** Evil will not depart from the house of one who returns evil for good. ^17-13

**17:14** The beginning of strife is like letting out water, so stop before the quarrel breaks out. ^17-14

**17:15** One who justifies the wicked and one who condemns the righteous are both alike an abomination to the LORD. ^17-15

**17:16** Why should fools have a price in hand to buy wisdom when they have no mind to learn? ^17-16

**17:17** A friend loves at all times, and kinsfolk are born to share adversity. ^17-17

**17:18** It is senseless to give a pledge, to become surety for a neighbor. ^17-18

**17:19** One who loves transgression loves strife; one who builds a high threshold invites broken bones. ^17-19

**17:20** The crooked of mind do not prosper, and the perverse of tongue fall into calamity. ^17-20

**17:21** The one who fathers a fool gets trouble; the parent of a fool has no joy. ^17-21

**17:22** A cheerful heart is a good medicine, but a downcast spirit dries up the bones. ^17-22

**17:23** The wicked accept a concealed bribe to pervert the ways of justice. ^17-23

**17:24** The discerning person looks to wisdom, but the eyes of a fool to the ends of the earth. ^17-24

**17:25** Foolish children are a grief to their father and bitterness to her who bore them. ^17-25

**17:26** To impose a fine on the innocent is not right or to flog the noble for their integrity. ^17-26

**17:27** One who spares words is knowledgeable; one who is cool in spirit has understanding. ^17-27

**17:28** Even fools who keep silent are considered wise; when they close their lips, they are deemed intelligent. ^17-28
