## The tradition of the elders

**7:1** Now when the Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around him, ^7-1

**7:2** they noticed that some of his disciples were eating with defiled hands, that is, without washing them. ^7-2

**7:3** (For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, do not eat unless they wash their hands,^[Meaning of Gk uncertain] thus observing the tradition of the elders, ^7-3

**7:4** and they do not eat anything from the market unless they wash,^[Other ancient authorities read *and when they come from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they purify themselves*] and there are also many other traditions that they observe: the washing of cups and pots and bronze kettles and beds.^[Other ancient authorities lack *and beds*]) ^7-4

**7:5** So the Pharisees and the scribes asked him, “Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders but eat with defiled hands?” ^7-5

**7:6** He said to them, “Isaiah prophesied rightly about you hypocrites, as it is written, ‘This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me; ^7-6

**7:7** in vain do they worship me, teaching human precepts as doctrines.’ ^7-7

**7:8** “You abandon the commandment of God and hold to human tradition.” ^7-8

**7:9** Then he said to them, “You have a fine way of rejecting the commandment of God in order to keep your tradition! ^7-9

**7:10** For Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother must surely die.’ ^7-10

**7:11** But you say that if anyone tells father or mother, ‘Whatever support you might have had from me is Corban’ (that is, an offering to God^[Gk lacks *to God*]), ^7-11

**7:12** then you no longer permit doing anything for a father or mother, ^7-12

**7:13** thus nullifying the word of God through your tradition that you have handed on. And you do many things like this.” ^7-13

**7:14** Then he called the crowd again and said to them, “Listen to me, all of you, and understand: ^7-14

**7:15** there is nothing outside a person that by going in can defile, but the things that come out are what defile.”^[Other ancient authorities add 7.16: *“If you have ears to hear, then hear”*] ^7-15

**7:17** When he had left the crowd and entered the house, his disciples asked him about the parable. ^7-17

**7:18** He said to them, “So, are you also without understanding? Do you not see that whatever goes into a person from outside cannot defile, ^7-18

**7:19** since it enters not the heart but the stomach and goes out into the sewer?” (Thus he declared all foods clean.) ^7-19

**7:20** And he said, “It is what comes out of a person that defiles. ^7-20

**7:21** For it is from within, from the human heart, that evil intentions come: sexual immorality, theft, murder, ^7-21

**7:22** adultery, avarice, wickedness, deceit, debauchery, envy, slander, pride, folly. ^7-22

**7:23** All these evil things come from within, and they defile a person.” ^7-23

## The Syrophoenician woman’s faith

**7:24** From there he set out and went away to the region of Tyre.^[Other ancient authorities add *and Sidon*] He entered a house and did not want anyone to know he was there. Yet he could not escape notice, ^7-24

**7:25** but a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit immediately heard about him, and she came and bowed down at his feet. ^7-25

**7:26** Now the woman was a gentile, of Syrophoenician origin. She begged him to cast the demon out of her daughter. ^7-26

**7:27** He said to her, “Let the children be fed first, for it is not fair to take the children’s food and throw it to the dogs.” ^7-27

**7:28** But she answered him, “Sir,^[Or *Lord*; other ancient authorities prefix *Yes*] even the dogs under the table eat the children’s crumbs.” ^7-28

**7:29** Then he said to her, “For saying that, you may go—the demon has left your daughter.” ^7-29

**7:30** And when she went home, she found the child lying on the bed and the demon gone. ^7-30

## Jesus cures a deaf man

**7:31** Then he returned from the region of Tyre and went by way of Sidon toward the Sea of Galilee, in the region of the Decapolis. ^7-31

**7:32** They brought to him a deaf man who had an impediment in his speech, and they begged him to lay his hand on him. ^7-32

**7:33** He took him aside in private, away from the crowd, and put his fingers into his ears, and he spat and touched his tongue. ^7-33

**7:34** Then looking up to heaven, he sighed and said to him, “Ephphatha,” that is, “Be opened.” ^7-34

**7:35** And his ears were opened, his tongue was released, and he spoke plainly. ^7-35

**7:36** Then Jesus^[Gk *he*] ordered them to tell no one, but the more he ordered them, the more zealously they proclaimed it. ^7-36

**7:37** They were astounded beyond measure, saying, “He has done everything well; he even makes the deaf to hear and the mute to speak.” ^7-37
