### 9.2–13: Transfiguration of Jesus and prophecies about Elijah

*[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-2|Text]], p. 88* · ([[Matthew 17#^17-1|Mt 17.1–13]]; [[Luke 09#^9-28|Lk 9.28–36]]). Mark’s Gospel lacks a depiction of the resurrected Christ (see 16.8n.), and some scholars argue that the Transfiguration was originally an account of a resurrection appearance (like those in Mt 28, Lk 24, and Jn 20) that was placed back into the narrative of the life of Jesus. It is more likely that it is a visitation by two important figures from the past, Moses and Elijah (compare the appearances of Jeremiah and Onias in [[LXX 2 Maccabees 15#^15-12|2 Macc 15.12–16]]). That *his clothes became dazzling white* (v 3) is similar to Moses’ transformation at [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-24|Ex 34.24]]. This scene is also similar to the Roman depictions of the ascension of the emperor to heaven (Livy, *Hist.* 1.16). ^9-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-2|2]]:** The *mountain*, likely Mount Hermon (see 8.27n.). ^9-2-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-4|4]]:** *Moses* . . . *Elijah*, Moses’ burial place is unknown ([[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-6|Deut 34.6]]); Elijah was taken up in the whirlwind to heaven by chariots and horses of fire ([[II Kings 02#^2-1|2 Kings 2.1–12]]). ^9-4

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-5|5]]:** The *dwellings* may suggest Sukkot, the Feast of Booths; some prophets suggested that God would intervene at one of the pilgrimage festivals, usually Passover or Sukkot (Zech 14). *Rabbi*, Heb “my great one” or teacher, was not yet a technical term for a religious leader, but cf. [[Matthew 23#^23-8|Mt 23.8]] where it is evidently becoming such a term. ^9-5

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-7|7]]:** The *cloud* evokes the cloud of God’s presence on Sinai ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-15|Ex 24.15–18]]) and the cloud of the tent of meeting ([[Torah 23 - Pekudei#^40-34|Ex 40.34–38]]). *My son, the Beloved*, see 1.9–11n*.* ^9-7

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-8|8]]:** Moses and Elijah are now gone, as Jesus descends and they proceed on their way. ^9-8

### 9.14–29: Healing of a possessed boy

*[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-14|Text]], p. 88* · ([[Matthew 17#^17-14|Mt 17.14–21]]; [[Luke 09#^9-37|Lk 9.37–43]]). ^9-14

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-14|14]]:** The argument with the *scribes* is not explained. ^9-14-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-17|17–19]]:** The failure of the *disciples* is attributed to lack of faith, as contrasted with the father and with Jesus’ promise (v 23). Second Kings 4.11–37 relates the failure of Elisha’s servant, Gehazi, to perform a healing that Elisha later accomplishes. ^9-17

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-18|18]]:** At 6.7,13, the disciples were given the power to heal, but 6.5–6 also suggests that Jesus’ powers faltered as a result of people’s lack of faith. Here it is the disciples who are unable to heal someone. ^9-18

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-19|19]]:** Despairing over this *generation* (see 8.38n.), Jesus lumps together both followers and others (cf. [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-34|Deut 1.34–40]]; [[Isaiah 44#^44-22|Isa 44.22]]; Ezek 10–11; 43–44; [[Zechariah 13#^13-1|Zech 13.1–2]]; [[Zechariah 14#^14-20|14.20–21]]; 1QS 9.4, *Gen. Rab.* 30.1). ^9-19

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-20|20]]:** *The spirit*, see notes at 1.21–45; 2.1–12; 5.1–20. ^9-20

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-22|22–24]]:** Central is the role of faith; see 2.1–12nn. ^9-22

### 9.30–32: Second Passion prediction

*[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-30|Text]], p. 89* · ([[Matthew 17#^17-22|Mt 17.22–23]]; [[Luke 09#^9-43|Lk 9.43–45]]). This shorter prediction contains the elements of the first (see 8.27–33n.). ^9-30

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-31|31]]:** *Betrayed*, [[Isaiah 53#^53-8|Isa 53.8]]. ^9-31

### 9.33–10.31: Social teachings

*[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-33|Text]], p. 89* · Jesus’ followers, like those in most movements of religious renewal, understood themselves as holding to a superior ethical standard. This unit begins (9.33–35) and ends (10.31) with notices that the first shall be last and the last first. See note on “inclusio,” 8.22–10.52. ^9-33

### 9.33–50: Receiving disciples

*[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-33|Text]], p. 89* · ([[Matthew 18#^18-1|Mt 18.1–9]]; [[Luke 09#^9-46|Lk 9.46–50]]). ^9-33-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-34|34–37]]:** *Servant*, an exhortation to prepare for a lower-status position, followed by the example. The *child* represented innocence but also a subservient position. Symbolically, accepting a child *in my name* is analogous to receiving Jesus as sent from God (10.13–16). ^9-34

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-38|38–41]]:** See 13.6n. ^9-38

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-41|41]]:** *Christ* or Messiah; see 1.1; 8.29; 14.61–62. ^9-41

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-42|42]]:** *Put a stumbling block*, here means to discourage people from staying within the movement. ^9-42

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-43|43]]:** *Hell*, or “Gehenna” (see translators’ note *b*), originally a valley south of Jerusalem. The site of rites condemned in [[Jeremiah 07#^7-31|Jer 7.31]], it became the term for hell ([[Matthew 05#^5-22|Mt 5.22]]; [[Luke 12#^12-5|Lk 12.5]]). The image, like that of being drowned (v 42), is one of destruction (drowning, burning, and being devoured by worms all destroy the body). ^9-43

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-43|43–47]]:** *Hand . . . foot . . . eye*, in general, the means of carrying out sinful deeds (stealing, coveting, etc.); if sexual sins are meant specifically, there may be allusions to Heb “foot” as a euphemism for genitals ([[Isaiah 07#^7-20|Isa 7.20]]) and of the “eye” as transgressing sexual boundaries ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-17|Lev 20.17–21]]). ^9-43-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-45|45]]:** *Hell*, Gk “Geenna” from Heb “Gehinnom,” Valley of the Sons of Hinnom, a ravine south of Jerusalem. It was thought that judgment would take place there; see [[Matthew 23#^23-33|Mt 23.33]]. ^9-45

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-48|48]]:** [[Isaiah 66#^66-24|Isa 66.24]]. ^9-48

**[[NRSVue Mark 09#^9-49|49]]:** *Salted with fire*, perhaps preserved (from worse fate) by punishment that is short of destruction. ^9-49
