## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IV
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 04 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v04 11 - Chapter Six, part 4#^pg380|p. 365]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 04.pdf#page=380>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 6.143cd–144ab.
>
> Once the ashes (of the universe consumed by the Fire of Time) have been swept away by Brahmā’s yawn, the universe is inundated by the water of his sweat. Such is (its) condition when the night (of universal destruction) comes. (with 6.142cd–143ab)
>
> Therein, up to the end of the Night, all the creatures, who (are then) in subtle bodies, impelled by the impetus of the fire, are in Janaloka (the World of Living Beings) as souls who are isolated (from the universe) in the state of cosmic dissolution (layakevala). Whereas, the Kūṣmāṇdas (Pumpkins), Hāṭakas (Violent Removers) and the rest play (instead) in (Mahāloka, the world) called Great (mahat). (with 6.143cd–144)
