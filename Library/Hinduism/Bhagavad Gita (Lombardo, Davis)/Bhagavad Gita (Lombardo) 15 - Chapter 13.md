## Chapter Thirteen

Then Arjuna said:

“Matter and spirit, O Krishna, \*
the field and the field’s knower,
knowledge and the act of knowing—
all this I wish to understand.”

And the Blessed Lord said:

\[1\] “This very body, Arjuna,
is declared to be the field.
Whoever knows the body is called
by the sages the field’s knower.

\[2\] Understand that I am the knower
of all fields, scion of Bharata.
Knowledge of the field and its knower
I consider to be true knowing.

\[3\] What this field is, and its nature,
its changes and how they arise,
and the powers of its knower—
listen now to my brief account.

\[4\] Sages have often sung of this
in many different Vedic hymns,
in *sutras* concerning *Brahman*,
well-reasoned, irrefutable.

\[5\] The great elements and selfhood,
intellect and the unmanifest,
all of the eleven senses,
the five fields of their perception,

[p. 76] \[6\] Desire, loathing, pleasure, pain,
organic consciousness, and courage—
briefly put, all this is the field
and its so-called transformations.

\[7\] Freedom from arrogance and deceit,
nonviolence, patience, rectitude,
respect for teachers, purity,
stability, and self-restraint,

\[8\] An aversion to sense-objects,
absence of all *ahamkara*,
keeping in mind the misery
of birth, disease, old age, and death;

\[9\] Nonattachment, never clinging
to one’s family, home, and so forth,
and constant equanimity
when outcomes are desired or not;

\[10\] Undeviating devotion
toward Me and to *Yoga* only,
often going on retreats alone
and disdaining social contact;

\[11\] Constant awareness of the Spirit,
focus on truth and wisdom’s goal—
all this is proclaimed as knowledge.
Ignorance is the opposite.

\[12\] I will now say what must be known
to attain immortality,
beginningless, supreme *Brahman*,
neither being nor nonbeing.

\[13\] Its hands and feet are everywhere,
everywhere eyes, heads, and faces,
[p. 77] ears that hear the entire world,
it stands pervading everything.

\[14\] Manifest to all the senses
and yet free from all the senses,
unattached but all maintaining,
free from but enjoying *gunas*;

\[15\] Outside and inside of beings,
those that move and those unmoving,
too subtle to be comprehended,
far away yet intimate.

\[16\] Undivided, yet remaining
as if divided in all beings,
to be known as sustaining them,
devouring and creating them.

\[17\] This is also the light of lights,
said to be beyond all darkness,
knowledge and the goal of knowledge,
seated in the hearts of all.

\[18\] This is the field, knowledge of it,
and what must be known, briefly put.
Understanding this, My votary
comes close to My state of being.

\[19\] Know too that matter and spirit
are also both beginningless,
and that the field in all its modes,
and the *gunas*, arise from matter.

\[20\] Matter is said to be the cause
and the instrument of action.
Spirit is said to be the cause
of experiencing pain and pleasure.

[p. 78] \[21\] Spirit, embedded in matter,
feels the *gunas* born of matter.
Attachment to the *gunas* causes
its birth in good and evil wombs.

\[22\] That which witnesses and consents,
supports and experiences,
is called great Lord and highest Self,
supreme Spirit in the body.

\[23\] One who knows spirit in this way,
and knows matter and the *gunas*,
in whatever state he might exist,
that one will not be born again.

\[24\] Through meditation on the self
some perceive the Self in the self;
others through practicing *Samkhya*;
and others through *Karma Yoga*.

\[25\] Some, however, not knowing this,
worship as they hear from others,
and they also can transcend death,
devoted to what they have heard.

\[26\] Every being that is born,
whether standing still or moving,
comes from the union of the field
and the field’s knower, Arjuna.

\[27\] The one who sees the highest Lord
existing alike in all beings,
not perishing when they perish,
that is the one who truly sees.

\[28\] Seeing the same Lord everywhere,
established everywhere he looks,
[p. 79] he does not injure Self with self
and goes on to the highest goal.

\[29\] The one who sees that all actions
are performed by matter alone,
and so the Self is not the doer,
that is the one who sees indeed.

\[30\] Perceiving Being’s various states
as resting steady in the One
and spreading out from That alone,
one will then attain to *Brahman*.

\[31\] Without beginning or qualities
the Supreme Self cannot perish.
Even though it is embodied,
it neither acts nor is befouled.

\[32\] As the all-pervading ether,
being subtle, is not tainted,
so the Self, even when embodied,
never can itself be tainted.

\[33\] As the sun alone illumines
this entire world, so also
the Lord of the field illumines
the entire field, O Arjuna.

\[34\] Those who understand the difference
between the field and its knower,
and know beings’ release from matter,
they go beyond, to the Supreme.”
