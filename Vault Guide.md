# Vault Guide

How this vault is laid out, how its moving parts work, and how to add things without breaking them. Start at [[Home]]. What each AI session changed is in the [[AI log]]. The layout below is the one of 12 October 2026, when the top-level folders were rearranged.

> [!warning] Five rules that keep the vault working
> 1. **Rename, move and delete only inside Obsidian** (file explorer, right-click, or drag). Never in Windows Explorer, OneDrive, or a script. Obsidian rewrites every link when it renames or moves; nothing else does.
> 2. **File names in plain English letters** (ASCII). Keep diacritics in the `title` property and in the text, not in names. No `[ ] # ^ | : * ? " < > \ /` in names, no double spaces, no ` (1)` or ` copy` endings.
> 3. **Every library file gets a catalog note** in `Catalog/<Subject>/`, and the catalog note's `files:` link is the only place the file is referenced from.
> 4. **Keep Templater, Dataview and Bases on.** Home, the daily and weekly notes, homilies, hubs, the catalog indexes and the Sanskrit card all run on them.
> 5. **Back up before any bulk change**, and never let two AI tools work on the vault at the same time.

## Daily study

- **The date.** Home's first line gives the date, the Church day, the Hebrew date, the coming Shabbat's parashah, the day's daf of the Daf Yomi cycle (since 21 Oct 2026; the table is [[Daf Yomi 2026-2029]], and the line links the daf's note in `Texts/Talmud`, since 22 Oct 2026, or Sefaria on the days that have none: Shekalim, Kinnim and Middot), the tithi and, when it is near, when Shabbat begins; it links to [[Calendar]], whose box shows today's date, the Hebrew date (it turns at sunset), the day's daf, the day's Sanskrit letter, the panchang for Hickory with today's festival or observance (Ekādaśī, Pūrṇimā, Amāvāsyā, Saṅkrānti, Navreh, Mahāśivarātri and the other major festivals) and the next three coming up within two weeks, and when Shabbat or Yom Tov next begins and ends in Hickory and in New York (your study partner). Both read precomputed tables in `Calendar/`, so they work offline; they run to the end of 2029. To extend them or change a place, they have to be recomputed (the method is described at the top of each table). **Holy Days (since 19 Oct 2026):** the Church day names a Holy Day by its full title and rank, above the lesser commemoration, and on the day it is kept says "transferred from Sun 18 Oct" when it fell on a Sunday; on the Sunday itself it says where the feast is kept and whether the Prayer Book lets you use its readings and collect that day; the Calendar note's box lists the next three Holy Days. [[Holy Days (BCP 1979)]] is the table (made from the BCP notes only: rank from the Calendar of the Church Year, psalm and lessons from the Lectionary's Holy Days, both versions of the collect, the Daily Office readings and the RCL note that carries the same collect) with the Prayer Book's rules quoted with page numbers and the dates for 2026–2029.
- **Devanagari search:** press Ctrl+P and run *Devanagari search* (or open [[Devanagari search]]). Type Harvard-Kyoto (`tantrAloka`) or paste IAST, press Enter, and the vault search runs on the Devanagari spelling and the Roman spelling together. The note has the typing table. You can give the command a hotkey in Settings → Hotkeys. It is set up when Home first renders, so it is there as long as Home has opened once since Obsidian started.
- **The plan:** [[Tantraloka study plan]] sets out the Tantrāloka in 1,099 daily portions, from Advent Sunday 2026 to Advent 2029, with a practice run (Āhnikas 31–37) from 3 October to 26 November 2026. Each row also gives the Dyczkowski pages, the coming Shabbat's parashah and the coming Sunday in the RCL. **Three ways of counting years** meet in the vault and are kept apart: the plan's *Year 1, 2, 3* are the course's own years (Advent 2026–27, 2027–28, 2028–29; Home links them with their dates); the lectionary and the homilies use the RCL's *Years A, B, C*; the Daily Office uses the Prayer Book's *Year One* and *Year Two*. They do not coincide: plan year 1 is RCL Year B and Office Year One, year 2 is C and Two, year 3 is A and One (the year before the plan, Advent 2025 to November 2026, is RCL Year A).
- **Home → Today** shows the day's portion (*Today's verses* opens them inline: Devanagari, IAST and Dyczkowski's English under each verse; a paragraph he gives for several verses is shown once, and the fold below opens the portion's first page in the Markdown copy of his volume, with the PDF page on the computer) and buttons that open or create the day's note, the week's note and the coming Sunday's homily, plus a homily button for any feast with its own readings that falls before that Sunday (Thanksgiving, Christmas Day). The **Daily Office** line gives today's psalms and lessons from the BCP 1979 Daily Office Lectionary for the right year (Year One from Advent 2026), week and Holy Day, with transfers and Eves; open it for the links. [[Daily Office Engine]] explains the rules, each with its BCP page. Clicking a day in the Calendar does the same as the day button. **The switch** (19 Oct 2026): on a Sunday on which a feast's collect and readings may be used in place of the Sunday's (BCP p. 16, "when desired"; All Saints on the Sunday after 1 November, p. 15), Today shows a button, for example *Use Saint Luke the Evangelist's readings and collect*, and *Use the Sunday's readings and collect instead* once it is pressed. The Holy Name, the Presentation and the Transfiguration take precedence of a Sunday, so they are the default there, and the button goes back to the Sunday's (the Prayer Book gives no such choice; added at your request). Where the Prayer Book bars the substitution (the Last Sunday after Pentecost through the First Sunday after the Epiphany, and the Last Sunday after the Epiphany through Trinity Sunday) there is no button and the header says why. The choice is one row for the date in [[Holy Days - my choices]] (you can edit or delete a row by hand); Home, the Daily Office and the homily template all read it. Without a row the Prayer Book's default stands. The homily button names the feast when its readings are in use (*Start a homily for Saint Luke the Evangelist*); a feast on a weekday that the study plan does not list now gets a homily button too.
- **The daily note** (since 19 Oct 2026) starts with the date line for its day and **Today**: the day's portion, then the CIS step and the day's letter card (`Templates/Views/today-items`). **Habits** are properties, present only on the days they apply (checkboxes, except `reading_min`, the minutes read, target 45): `office`, `letter` and `reading_min` every day, `tantraloka` on days with a portion, `cis` Monday to Friday, `exercise` Monday to Friday, `calls` Friday, `church` Sunday, and `puja` on Pūrṇimā and Amāvāsyā (from the panchang table). Tick them with the Meta Bind toggles in the note's **Habits** section (the File properties pane shows the same values); [[Habits.base|Habits]] shows the last 30 days and each new weekly note its week. **Chores** (since 21 Oct 2026) lists the weekday's chores from [[Chores]] (`Journal/Chores.md`: one line per chore, `- Name — days`, the days `daily`, `weekdays`, `weekend` or day names) as tasks dated that day, so Home's *Due today or overdue* shows them until they are ticked, and each weekly note lists its week's chores by day under *Chores this week*. **Day Planner** lists the day's time blocks in Day Planner OG's form (`- [ ] 09:00 Church`; a gap is `BREAK`, and the day ends with `24:00 BED`): the plugin, in its Daily mode, draws them as a timeline, shows the current block in the status bar and ticks past blocks; Home's list of undated tasks leaves this heading out. The blocks come from the daily template's *YOUR SETTINGS* (since 25 Oct 2026: AM Rituals, Lunch and Dinner every day, PMCS on Monday morning, Church on Sunday morning, Gym Monday to Friday, Study & Chores Monday to Thursday; the pūjā and CIS sessions have no block, and their toggles stay). Then come the day's verses (Devanāgarī embedded from the verse notes), the Phoneme of the day, **Letter practice** (an Ink writing section on a pen device, or the [[Sanskrit scratchpad|scratchpad]]), the Daily Office for that date, a Reflection section, a **Words** section, and **Earlier on these verses**. The links are stored as properties (`verses_read`, `parashah`, `sunday`).
- **Words:** write one `- word:: …` line per word (for example `- word:: prakāśa: light, manifestation`). [[Vocabulary (Tantraloka)]] gathers them all.
- **Sanskrit:** Home shows the **Phoneme of the day**: one of the Tantrāloka's 108 most frequent conjuncts, with its letters and words from the day's verses ([[Conjunct cycle (Tantraloka)]], [[Letters]]). Today's flashcard (from [[Sanskrit 108 Flashcard Register|the register]], with its place in the body from TĀ 15.117–120) is in [[Sanskrit Daily Letter Cycle]], one click from Home (*Today's flashcard*). Write it by hand with Ink — in the day's note (*Letter practice*), on the card note (*Practice*) or on the [[Sanskrit scratchpad]]. See [[Sanskrit Daily Letter Cycle|Sanskrit Daily Letter Cycle]].
- **CIS (Ruppel):** [[CIS study plan]] takes you once through the *Cambridge Introduction to Sanskrit*: each chapter is three or four steps with checkboxes, and the first one not ticked is the current step. Home (under the Phoneme of the day) and the daily note show it with the day's minutes (`cis_mwf`, `cis_tth` on the plan note), Monday to Friday. The 108 flashcards have a note each in `Study/Sanskrit/Flashcards` ([[Flashcards.base|the card notes]]), each with two Spaced Repetition cards (`::`); a chapter's cards are in `Study/Sanskrit/CIS cards` (Ch. 1, 2, 2a and 3 so far). Each card note and chapter-card note has two buttons (26 Oct 2026): *Review this note* and *Cram this note*, which goes through every card, due or not, without changing the schedule (Meta Bind button templates `sr-note` and `sr-cram`, set in Meta Bind's settings on each device).
- **Tasks** (Tasks plugin): Home lists tasks that are overdue or due today, those due in the next two weeks, and open tasks without a date from Journal, Notes, Projects and Study (not the steps of the [[CIS study plan]]). Give a task a date with `📅 2026-10-10` and make it repeat with `🔁 every month` (or `every week`, `every year`); when you tick a repeating task, the next one is created.
- **On the phone:** [[Home (mobile)]] has the date line, today's portion and verses, the Daily Office, the Phoneme of the day, tasks due this week, and at the bottom three capture buttons: *New note*, *New handwritten note* (a note in `Notes/` opened with an Ink writing section; since 21 Oct 2026) and *Photograph marked pages*, which files a photo under the date in that book's note in `Notes/Marked pages/`; below them, *Add a book you have on paper* catalogs a book that has no PDF (see Adding things). On the phone and tablet the Homepage plugin is off (it slowed start-up, 16 Oct 2026), so keep *Home (mobile)* open in a tab: Obsidian reopens the tabs it had when it closed. For the other settings a new phone or tablet needs, see [[#Setting up a new device]].
- **Workspaces** (the Workspaces core plugin, on since 7 Oct 2026) are saved layouts of open notes. *Homily*: [[Homilies]] in the main pane, with the [[RCL to Jewish and Patristic Concordance|concordance]] and the [[RCL Index]] on the right. *Sanskrit*: the [[Sanskrit Daily Letter Cycle|daily letter cycle]], with the Ruppel base and the [[Sanskrit 108 Flashcard Register|flashcard register]] as tabs, and the [[Monier-Williams Quick Lookup Guide|Monier-Williams lookup]] over [[Devanagari search]] on the right. *Tantraloka (daily)*: Home, with [[Hub - Tantraloka]] and the [[Tantraloka study plan|study plan]] as tabs, and [[Vocabulary (Tantraloka)]] over the Monier-Williams lookup on the right. Switch with the ribbon's *Manage workspace layouts* button or Ctrl+P → *Workspaces: Load workspace layout*. A layout reopens the notes that were open when it was saved, so open this week's homily or today's note into the main pane; to keep a change to a layout, use *Workspaces: Save layout* under the same name. ## Homilies

- **Start one** from Home (*Start a homily for …*). The note is named `<date> <occasion>`; the template reads that day's occasion from the study plan and fills in the occasion, RCL year and readings. A feast with its own readings is that day's occasion; on a Sunday it replaces the Sunday only where the Prayer Book gives it precedence (All Saints on Sunday 1 Nov 2026 does; a feast that falls in Holy Week moves to the week after the Second Sunday of Easter, and the plan marks it on that Monday, as the Annunciation on 5 Apr 2027 and 9 Apr 2029). Dated a weekday without a feast, it takes the coming Sunday; made any other way in `Projects/Homilies`, it asks which Sunday or feast.
- **A Holy Day (19 Oct 2026):** a homily dated on a Holy Day takes the feast's collect and lessons where the Prayer Book gives the feast the day (a weekday, or the Presentation and the Transfiguration on a Sunday). On a Sunday where the feast may be used "when desired", the template asks *the Sunday's, the feast's, or both* unless a choice is already in [[Holy Days - my choices]], and records the answer there. A feast with an RCL note uses it (the RCL note for All Saints, the Annunciation …); the others take the Prayer Book's lessons and both collects from [[Holy Days (BCP 1979)]], as embedded verses. When the feast replaces only the collect and lessons on a Sunday, the psalm stays the Sunday's (p. 16 names no psalm) and the feast's psalm is shown beside it. The note gets `feast` and `propers` (`sunday`, `feast` or `both`); the *Earlier homilies* list matches the same feast only. A homily already written is not changed.
- **Polyphonic layout:** run *Templater: Create new note from template* → *Homily (Polyphonic)*. It takes the occasion and the week's parashah from the study plan (or asks), files the note in `Projects/Homilies` as `<date> <occasion> (polyphonic)`, and shows that parashah's Torah, Rashi, Mei HaShiloach and Zohar notes beside the readings.
- **In the note:** the collect of the day (Contemporary, from the lectionary note's `collect`), the readings (embedded from the lectionary note), the psalm in the Prayer Book's own words and verse numbers (from `psalm_bcp`), earlier homilies on the same readings, then Notes and Draft. In the Season after Pentecost (Propers 4–29) the lectionary notes hold both of the RCL's Old Testament tracks: track 1 (semicontinuous) under *Readings* and track 2 (complementary) under *Complementary track*. The homily note takes track 2, which is what your parish reads (checked against three bulletins, 21 Oct 2026); until the lectionary notes carry a Prayer Book numbering for the track 2 psalms, a track 2 psalm that is part of a psalm shows as a link to the Prayer Book psalm. Set `stage` to `idea`, `drafting`, `finished` or `preached`, and `preached_at`. [[Homilies]] and the *Homily list* Base beside it list them.

## Hubs and concepts

- **Hubs** map a project: key texts, your writing, and live lists. **Concepts** are single ideas; put hubs in a concept's `up` property and it appears on those hubs. A concept under two hubs is where two projects meet. Hubs are in `Study/Hubs`, concepts in `Study/Concepts`; [[Study]] is the index.

## Fields

Five inline fields let a line in your own notes say what kind of line it is, so the hubs can collect it. Write each as a bullet of its own (`- question:: …`) in `Journal`, `Study`, `Projects` or `Notes`. `word::` (the daily note's *Words*) works as before.

| Field | What it marks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `question::` | An open question a passage raised; open until an `answer::` is indented under it | `- question:: What does [[Tantraloka 31.025\|TĀ 31.25]] assume that [[Tantraloka 03.067\|TĀ 3.67]] already said?` |
| `answer::` | Indented under a question, closes it; the pair stays in the note | `    - answer:: Treated in [[Oudeis as Ontological Negation]].` |
| `claim::` | One sentence you would defend, with the passage or concept it rests on | `- claim:: [[John 14#^14-6\|John 14:6]] excludes a path, not a people.` |
| `connects::` | Passages from two or more traditions that read each other | `- connects:: [[Tantraloka 03.067\|TĀ 3.67]] ↔ [[John 01#^1-1\|John 1:1]]: the first stirring as speech.` |
| `cites::` | A source you used: the catalog note, the page, the passage | `- cites:: [[Dyczkowski - Tantraloka (tr., vols I-XI)\|Dyczkowski]] vol. XI p. 76, on [[Tantraloka 31.025\|TĀ 31.25]].` |

- **Where they show.** Each hub's *Dynamic Feeds* list the lines that belong to it, of the kinds that hub shows: open questions, claims, connections (with the traditions each joins) or sources cited. As approved on 13 Oct 2026, *John* shows questions, claims and connections; *Tantrāloka* questions and connections; *Technics* questions, claims and sources cited; *The Second Domestication* questions, claims and connections. [[Study]] lists every open question. The script is `Templates/Views/hub-fields/view.js`.
- **How a line reaches a hub: through its links.** A line belongs to a hub when it links the hub, a concept whose `up:` names the hub, a note the hub itself links (so listing a book on a hub makes citations of it show there), or, for the Tantrāloka and John hubs, a note in their text folders (`Texts/Tantraloka`; John in the New Testament, the NRSVue, the JANT annotations and the Douay-Rheims, and Augustine, Chrysostom and Origen on John). A line with no link counts for the note it sits in, and in a daily note for that day's verses.
- **The templates** for daily, weekly, homily and concept notes carry empty prompt lines (`- question:: `); an empty line is skipped.
- **Not:** on a task (`- [ ] question::` is not read), in `Texts/`, `Library/` or `Catalog/` (the hubs don't look there), or in the daily note's *Words* section.

## Cross-references

Scripture, Zohar and Tantrāloka references written in the notes are links, so every verse or folio note's **Linked mentions** (backlinks) is a concordance: open `John 14` or `Isaiah 07` and the backlinks list every lectionary, homily, Zohar note, Hasidic text and essay that cites it.

- **Bible:** `John 3:16` links to the verse, `[[John 03#^3-16|John 3:16]]`. The Tanakh notes follow the JPS 1985 translation with Hebrew chapter and verse numbers, and the Torah verses live in the parashah notes, so `Genesis 1:1` links to `[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-1|Genesis 1:1]]`. Each Tanakh chapter note, and each chapter heading in the parashah notes, has a *Compare* line to the same chapter in the NRSVue; where the numbers differ it says how (`Heb 9:1–20 = NRSVue 9:2–21`), and [[NRSVue and JPS numbering]] lists every such chapter.
- **Two numberings.** Where Christian and Hebrew Bibles number a verse differently, the link goes to the Hebrew verse and the text stays as written. Lectionaries, the BCP, your writing and other Christian or general sources use the English numbering, so `Joel 2:28` links to Joel 3:1, `Malachi 4:5` to Malachi 3:23, `Psalm 51:10` to Psalm 51:12, `Exodus 20:17` to Exodus 20:14. The Zohar, Hasidic texts, Rashi and the Judaica already use the Hebrew numbering and are linked as written. The folder does not decide every case: a reference marked LXX, Septuagint or Vulgate links to its `Texts/Bible/LXX` note as written (`LXX Ps 81:6`); a psalm verse the Prayer Book cites links to its BCP Psalm note, because the BCP Psalter numbers verses its own way (BCP Psalm 74:15 is JPS 74:16); the parashah ranges in the crosswalk tables are in Hebrew numbering and are linked as written; and where an author cites the Hebrew numbers (Ehrman's `Ps 22:2`), the link follows the author. A linking pass lists every shifted link outside `Texts/RCL/` and `Texts/BCP 1979/` for a human look. In the Church Fathers only New Testament references are to be linked, because their Old Testament numbering follows the Greek and Latin Bibles; Each of Augustine's *Tractates on John* opens with a **John:** line linking the passage it expounds, and each John chapter links its tractates; the references inside the sermons are not linked.
- **Zohar:** `Zohar 2:121a` links to the Pritzker note that contains that folio (`#^z2-121a`); a folio that is not in the Pritzker volumes here links to the Sefaria folio note; `ZḤ 36b` (Zohar Ḥadash) links to volumes 10, 11 and 12 (`#^zh-36b`). Lists such as `Zohar 1:10b, 72a, 77a–b; 2:26b` are linked item by item. Where two notes carry a folio, the composition named after the reference decides (26 Oct 2026): `1:156a (*ST*)` opens Sitrei Torah (11.09), `(*Tos*)` the Tosefta (11.08, or 12.12 for the passages printed as Tosefta on 1:32a, 37a, 54b, 60a and 3:55b), `(*Heikh*)` the Heikhalot; a group mark such as `(both *ST*)` or `(all *Tos*)` covers the items it names; a folio with no composition named opens the main text (the Parashat note). References to works the vault does not translate (*RM*, *TZ*, *Tiq*, *Tosafot*) stay plain. Since 26 Oct 2026 the rule holds across the vault (1,536 more references linked, most in the Pritzker notes' own footnotes). Where the named composition's note has no marker for the folio, the link goes to that note (its top where the composition begins partway down a page, otherwise the nearest marked folio before the passage), or to the note that holds the folio when the passage cited is there; a reference that names two compositions stays plain.
- **Tantrāloka:** `TĀ 15.117`, `Tantrāloka 4.122` and `T.Ā. 3.67` link to the verse note.
- **The converted books** in `Library/` (13 Oct 2026): 21,149 scripture, Zohar, Tantrāloka and Avot references in 813 chapter notes are links, and more were linked the same evening: 400 by rules for particular books, then others whose own context settles the verse, so a verse's backlinks also list the books that discuss it. Each book's numbering was read from the book itself (a verse that exists in only one numbering, or a printed "[English 23:3]"), and a reference that would move to another verse number is linked only where the book shows its numbering. Left unlinked there: indexes, contents and front matter; Psalms and Samuel/Kings in the Church Father translations (Greek book names and numbers), except in the three Maximus translations that say they follow the Septuagint (Constas's *Responses to Thalassios*, *Questions and Doubts*, Sherwood), which link to the LXX notes as written; Psalm references beside Augustine, Jerome, the Vulgate or the Septuagint; Tantrāloka verses within three of a chapter's end, where the KSTS count can differ; lines in Greek, Hebrew or Devanagari. The [[B3 linking report (13 Oct 2026)|report]] lists what was left and why; what was left, with the decision on each, is in [[Bible references to decide later]].
- **The texts added on 12–13 Oct** (Hobbes's *Leviathan* of 1651, Aquinas's *Summa*, Chrysostom and Origen on John, the Jewish Annotated New Testament): 21,365 references linked on 13 Oct 2026. The *Summa*'s Old Testament references link to the Douay-Rheims as printed (Vulgate numbering); in Chrysostom and Origen only New Testament references are linked; the JANT uses both numberings, so a reference whose number differs between them is linked only where the JANT prints the Hebrew number beside it ("Ps 51.5 [HB v 7]") or its context settles the verse; the rest are listed, with the decision on each, in [[Bible references to decide later]].
- **Talmud and Maimonides (22 Oct 2026):** in the Talmud, Maimonides and Kitzur notes, verse references are links as printed (Hebrew numbering), and Talmud references (`Berakhot 3a`, *Shabbat* 31a) link to that side of the daf note (`[[Berakhot 3#3a|Berakhot 3a]]`); a line of the daf is a block (`#^3a-5`). Each daf's margins (Torah Or, Mesoret HaShas, Ein Mishpat, In this vault) come from Sefaria's links, and Ein Mishpat links to the *Mishneh Torah*'s halakhot (`#^1-9`). Talmud references in the older notes link to the daf notes too (22 Oct 2026: 13,024 in the Zohar, the Hasidic texts, the Jewish Annotated New Testament and the library, e.g. *Shabbat* 31a, b. Hag. 15a; the Jerusalem Talmud and the Tosefta are left unlinked). [[Talmud View]] shows one side of a daf laid out like the printed page, with these margins around the text (22 Oct 2026).
- **Concepts and hubs:** in your notes, concepts, hubs, writing and the crosswalks, the first mention of a concept or hub name (or one of its aliases) in a note links to it.
- **Chapters:** a chapter named with its book but no verse, such as `Psalm 145` or `John 8`, links to the chapter's first verse (`[[John 08#^8-1|John 8]]`).
- **Not linked:** references inside headings, code, existing links and the Bible text notes themselves; a verse range without a book name (a bare "80:1-7"); verses that do not exist (misprints in a source, such as "Psalms 1:20"); folios that no Pritzker note marks (listed in [[Zohar folio index]]).
- **New notes are not linked automatically.** When you write, use the same forms (`Isaiah 7:14`, `Zohar 1:15a`, `TĀ 3.1`) and type `[[` to link, or ask for the linking pass to be run again over new material. Inside a table, write the pipe as `\|`: `[[John 01#^1-14\|John 1:14]]`.

## The catalog

Every note in `Catalog/<Subject>/` has this block, and is named `Author - Short title` (an editor as `Name (ed.)`, a translator as `Name (tr.)`; on 12 Oct 2026, 302 notes named by title alone were renamed so, keeping the old name as an alias). The Bases show the name, so they have no title or author column (*By author* keeps its author column). Keep the values in these forms: the live tables sort and filter on them.

About 120 catalog notes of well-known works end with an **About** section on the work itself (who made it, its dates, why it matters), drafted by AI on 13 Oct 2026 from the sources it lists.

```yaml
title: "Full title with diacritics: and subtitle"
subtitle: ""
author: ["First Author", "Second Author"]   # plain names
editor: []
translator: []
year: 1990                 # a number, or leave empty
publisher: ""
series: ""
volume: ""
container: ""              # journal or book a chapter/article is in
subject: [Trika]           # Trika | Judaica | Christianity | Philosophy | Sanskrit-Study | Hinduism | Biblical-Studies | Other
type: book                 # book | article | chapter | thesis | collection | multi-volume-set | text | other
language: [English]
pages: 230                 # the page count (a number), or empty
text: searchable           # searchable | scan | print (every catalogued PDF was checked on 5 Oct 2026)
status:                    # empty, or reading | review | delete
print: false               # tick if you own it in print
files:
  - "[[Library/Trika/Author - Short title (1990).pdf]]"
  - "[[Library/Trika/Short title (Author)/Short title (Author)]]"   # the Markdown copy's index note, if converted
aliases: []
```

- **subject:** one or more of the eight subjects in the block above (a note with two shows in both indexes). A narrower term may follow them, for example `[Philosophy, Political-philosophy]`; no index or base filters on it.
- **type:** `collection` is an edited volume with several contributors; one author's essays are a `book`, and one author's numbered volumes a `multi-volume-set`. `text` is a primary text kept as notes in `Texts/` (the Bibles, the *Summa*, Plato, the DCS tantras): its `files:` links the text's index note instead of a library file.
- **pages:** the page count, a number. Notes that cover part of a book (converted chapters, the Zohar and Prayer Book notes) keep their printed page range as text in `page_range` instead, and converted notes their PDF pages in `pdf_pages` (since 15 Oct 2026).
- **status:** empty once the details are settled; `reading` puts it in *Currently reading*; `review` means the details still need checking against the file (*Needs review* lists them); `delete` means redundant (*To delete* lists them; the note says which copy stays).
- **text:** `searchable` means the file has a text layer you can search and copy from; `scan` means it doesn't yet (*Scans without text* lists those); `print` means there is no digital copy at all: a book you have only on paper, whose `files:` stays empty (*On paper only* in *Catalog.base* lists those).
- **print:** tick it for books you own in print, on your shelf; *Physical Library* lists those, *Shelf check* lists everything with the box so you can go along the shelf.
- **work** and **role** (the *Devī Māhātmya* only, since 13 Oct 2026): the work a catalog note belongs to (`work: "[[Devi Mahatmya]]"`) and how it relates to it (`role`: version, recitation, study or related); the *In the library* table on [[Devi Mahatmya]] reads both. The 16 recitation texts in `Texts/Devi Mahatmya` carry `collection: "Devī Māhātmya"` and their own `role` (their place in the recitation).
- **Below the properties:** a `# Title` line, then a sentence on where the details came from (title page, PDF metadata, looked up online with a link) and anything worth knowing. Many notes also carry a **Text layer** line from the October PDF audit; files that got OCR text on 5 Oct 2026 have a dated line saying how. Their original scans went with the trash when you emptied it on 6 Oct 2026, so a note that still says they are in `.trash` is out of date.
- **One work, several files** (volumes, or a scan plus a text copy): one catalog note listing every file, and say in the body which is which.
- **Converted books:** `files:` lists the PDF and the Markdown copy's index note. The box under the title (`Templates/Views/open-pdf`) shows *Read in the vault* first, one button per Markdown copy (Ctrl-click opens a new tab), with how many notes the copy has. Then comes *Open the PDF*, which opens the PDF in the computer's PDF reader. On a phone or tablet it says *The PDF is on the computer* instead, because PDFs do not sync. Every catalog note that lists a file has the box. `Catalog/Catalog.base` (view *All*) has a *Read* column that links the Markdown copy.

## Adding things, step by step

### A book, article or chapter (PDF, DjVu)

1. **Name the file first, on your computer, before it goes in:** `Author - Short title (year).pdf`, for example `Torzsok - Yogini and Goddess Possession (2013).pdf`. Plain letters only, under about 100 characters. If it is already in the vault, rename it inside Obsidian instead.
2. **Put it in its subject folder:** drag it from Windows onto `Library/<Subject>/` in Obsidian's file explorer, or copy it into that folder in Windows and wait a moment for Obsidian to see it. (Adding new files from Windows is fine; renaming or moving existing ones there is not.)
3. **Make the catalog note** in `Catalog/<Subject>/`, named the same way without the extension (`Torzsok - Yogini and Goddess Possession.md`). Either run *Templater: Insert template* → *Catalog Item*, or copy the block above.
4. **Fill in the properties.** Copy title, authors and year from the title page, not the file name. Link the file with its full path and extension in `files:`; typing `[[` and part of the name lets Obsidian complete it.
5. **Check:** the work appears in its subject's index (`Catalog/<Subject>/<Subject>.md`) and in *Catalog.base*; the `files:` link opens the PDF. If you are not sure of the details yet, set `status: review`.
6. **Scans:** if you cannot select text in the PDF, set `text: scan`; it then shows under *Scans without text* in *Catalog.base*. A text layer can be added later with OCR that keeps the page images (ocrmypdf with Tesseract's `Latin` and `san` models keeps IAST diacritics and Devanagari; the plain English model drops them).
7. **The Markdown copy** is made by an AI session with [[Add new files from Downloads|Add new files from Downloads]], which converts each new book as it files it (the converter is in `AI/Prompts/Scripts/Library conversion/`).

### A book you have only on paper

Open [[Home (mobile)]] (on the phone it is the start page; on the laptop it is linked from Home's *Go to* row), then *Add a book you have on paper (no PDF)*. Fill in the title, author and year, choose the subject, paste a link to where the details came from if you have one (Google Books, the publisher), and press *Add book*, or *Add book and photograph pages* to go straight to the camera. It makes the catalog note in `Catalog/<Subject>/` with `text: print` and an empty `files:`, marked *reading* and *I own it on paper* unless you untick them, so the book appears in *Photograph marked pages*, *Currently reading* and *Physical Library*. [[Ruiz - Structural Violence]] is one. Check the details against the title page when you can. If you later get a PDF of the book, add the file to that note's `files:` and change `text`; don't make a second note.

### An e-book converted to Markdown

Give it a folder `Library/<Subject>/<Short title (Author)>/` with an index note (`type: book`, the table of contents as links) and one note per chapter; images go in `_images/` inside that folder. The catalog note links the index note in `files:`. Keep notes as footnotes and page numbers as `[p. N]` markers, as in the Fink and Lombardo/Davis conversions.

### A primary text in `Texts/`

One folder per text, one note per natural unit (verse, chapter, parashah), names `Work NN.NNN` with zero-padded numbers so they sort, properties as numbers (`chapter: 3`, `verse: 12`), and *previous · chapter · next* links at the top like the Tantrāloka verse notes. Big imports are best done in one go with a script and a backup, then checked in Obsidian; never rename hundreds of text notes outside Obsidian.

### Daily, weekly and homily notes

Use the buttons on Home (or Calendar). Don't create them by hand in Windows: Templater fills a note only when Obsidian creates it in `Journal`, `Journal/Weeks`, `Projects/Homilies`, `Study/Concepts` or `Study/Hubs`. Templater fills them only on a device where Settings → Templater → *Trigger Templater on new file creation* is on; that switch is kept per device and does not sync, so turn it on once on each computer, phone and tablet. If a note ever comes out blank, open it and run *Templater: Open insert template modal*, then choose its folder's template (*Daily note*, *Weekly note*, *Homily*, *Concept* or *Hub*); *Replace templates in the active file* leaves a blank note blank.

### Concepts and hubs

Create the note in `Study/Concepts/` (or `Study/Hubs/`) from the file explorer; the template fills it. Give a concept an `up:` with one or more hubs. Check the name is not already used elsewhere (for example in `Projects/`), or links will be ambiguous.

### Your own writing

`Projects/<Project>/<Title>.md`. Old drafts stay in `Projects/_Originals/` (excluded from search and the graph); link them with the full path and extension, e.g. `[[Projects/_Originals/My Writing/Locke.docx]]`.

### Images and attachments

Pasting or dragging an image into a note puts it in an `Attachments` folder beside that note (Settings → Files and links). Name images meaningfully before pasting when you can.

### Renaming, moving, deleting

- Renaming and moving: in Obsidian only. *Automatically update internal links* is on (Settings → Files and links); leave it on.
- Deleting: right-click → Delete. Files go to the vault's own `.trash` folder (Settings → Files and links → *Deleted files*), so they can be restored until you empty it. To empty it, open the vault folder in Windows Explorer and delete what is inside `.trash`; that is the one deletion that is fine outside Obsidian. For a duplicate library file, remove it from its catalog note's `files:` first, then delete it.
- After a large change, check that nothing new is unresolved: [[Vault Health & Phantom Link Manager|Vault Health]] lists the unresolved links in `Study/Hubs` and `Study/Concepts`; for the whole vault, the monthly health report counts them without an AI (`health-report.js` in `AI/Prompts/Scripts/Health checks/`, run through the runner; see [[Monthly check-up]]).

### Properties and tags

- Use the property names already in use, all lower case: `title`, `author`, `year`, `subject`, `status`, `type`, `up`, `stage`, `date`. Don't make `Title` or `Author` variants.
- Numbers as numbers (`year: 1990`, not `"1990"`), dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`, lists as lists. Obsidian sets one type per property name for the whole vault, so a mismatched value shows a warning and drops out of Bases filters.
- `parasha` and `parashah` are different properties: `parasha` is the portion's name as text, in the Torah, Rashi and Mei HaShiloach notes; `parashah` is the week's portion as links, in the daily, weekly and polyphonic-homily notes.
- Tags are yours: tag your own notes as you like. Nothing an AI builds reads or writes tags, and since 19 Oct 2026 the machine-made notes (texts, library, catalog, calendar) carry none (house rule 12). Properties do the work in this vault.

### Links, names and scripts

- Links are `[[Note name]]`; add `|text` for display text. For a file whose name contains brackets, use a Markdown link with angle brackets: `[text](<path/with [brackets].pdf>)`.
- Two notes with the same name make `[[Name]]` ambiguous; give one a fuller name.
- The divine name is kept exactly as the source writes it in quoted texts; it is not added to file names, headings, aliases or link labels.
- Devanāgarī, Hebrew and Greek are fine in note text and in the Greek lexicon's names; everywhere else, names are plain letters.

### Callouts

Built-in callouts (`note`, `info`, `quote`, `warning`, `abstract`, `question` …) plus the vault's own. **Callout Studio** (Settings → Callout Studio) defines `source`, `lexicon`, `external`, `mula` (root text), `viveka` (commentary such as Jayaratha's), `reflection`, `hermeneutics`, `torah`, `philosophy`, `matrika`, `navigation`, `seealso` and `links`. The **callout-styles** snippet defines the rest: `xref`, `commentary`, `translation`, `question`, `tanakh`, `nt`, `bcp`, `fathers`, `tantraloka`, `trika`, `purana`, `zohar`, `chasidut`, `stiegler`, `secondary`, `scholar` and `today` (the Calendar note's date box). Each callout has one definition. Write `> [!reflection] Title`. A callout without a definition still shows, as a plain note callout, with its content and links.

### What not to change or do

Each item below breaks something that is not obvious. Nothing else about the plugins is kept in the vault (Stephen's decision of 20 October 2026, after setting every plugin up on every device himself): what a plugin does and how its settings work is in its own documentation, and the settings themselves are in Settings on each device. Each item below was read from the plugin's own code or seen in a test; the date is when. (This list was the separate note *Plugins* until 22 October 2026, when it was folded in here at Stephen's request; everything else about the plugins is his to experiment with.)

### Across devices

- **Set each community plugin up on each device it runs on, and check there.** A plugin's settings cannot be counted on to travel through Sync (Spaced Repetition arrived on the phone with the plugin's defaults, 20 Oct 2026), and neither can its files (Meta Bind had to be installed on the phone by hand the same day). The core plugins' settings do travel.
- **Keep *Active community plugin list* off in Sync's settings on the phone and tablet.** With it on, the first sync of a device hands it another device's on/off list (the tablet got the laptop's, 20 Oct 2026).
- **One device edits a note at a time.** Make the daily note once, on the laptop, and let it sync; review cards on one device per sitting; write in an Ink section on one device and let it sync before using it on another. Sync merges Markdown line by line; card notes carry the review schedule.
- **Give every device its own name in Settings → Sync.** Day Planner OG decides whether it may write by comparing the device's Sync name with the writer it has stored.
- **Restart a device after changing a plugin's settings there.** Plugins read their settings when they load.
- **The three saved workspace layouts are laptop layouts.** Loading one on a phone or tablet restores panes that are not there.

### Day Planner OG

- **Only the laptop is the writer** (*Set current device as writer*, run once on the laptop, 19 Oct 2026). Never run that command on the phone or tablet. A writer device rewrites every line of today's planner section every two seconds; two writers fight over the Journal, and a device with no writer stored at all writes too (code).
- **On the phone and tablet keep Mode *Daily*, *Complete past planner items* off and *Mark in progress planner item* off.** That is the read-only setup: the timeline and status bar work and nothing is written (tested from the laptop, 20 Oct 2026).
- **Never leave it in File mode**, which is the default on a fresh install: it makes `Day Planners/Day Planner-YYYYMMDD.md` every day (it did so from the phone on 20 Oct 2026 before the mode was set). Set the mode before anything else on a new device.
- **Make today's daily note first thing on the laptop.** From midnight until the note exists, the plugin saves its settings file every two seconds (seen 20 Oct 2026).
- Don't tick a future planner block by hand (unticked within two seconds); don't read a past tick as "done" (the plugin ticks everything that is past); don't title a block beginning *Break* or *Bed*, its break and end labels. A skipped block is written with a `-` status, which the plugin leaves alone.
- **The day ends with `24:00 BED`** (since 25 Oct 2026): the daily template puts a BREAK after the last block and BED at 24:00, and *End task label* is BED on the laptop. Set the same label on the phone and tablet (Settings → Day Planner OG → *End task label*); a device still on END shows BED as an ordinary block. Write midnight as 24:00: the plugin reads 00:00 as the start of the morning.
- The section heading must end in `# Day Planner` with that capital P; the plugin's search is case-sensitive (code).

## Setting up a new device

Obsidian Sync's own settings stay on the device where you set them, and so do Templater's trigger and, in practice, every community plugin's settings (20 Oct 2026). The notes, the scripts, the theme and snippets, the core plugins' settings and the plugin files come through Sync once the switches below are on. Sync was off from 4 to 15 Oct 2026, while the October plan ran. As decided on 14 Oct 2026, the old remote vault was deleted and a new one made from this computer (Settings → Sync → *Manage*) on 14–15 Oct 2026, before anything synced; Sync has been on since 15 Oct 2026. Go through this list on this computer first, then on the phone and on the tablet as each one first opens the vault; later, on any new device and after reinstalling Obsidian.

1. **Connect.** In Settings → Sync, log in and *Connect to remote vault* (the new one made from this computer); choose **Do not start syncing yet**, set step 2, then *Start syncing*. On this computer, where the remote vault was created, the same: settings first, then the first upload. What step 2 leaves out is then never uploaded.
2. **Sync settings on this device** (Settings → Sync):
    - *Sync all other types*: **on**. Home's scripts (`Templates/Views/*/view.js`) and the Monier-Williams dictionary (a `.tsv` file) are "other types"; without them Home's blocks show *custom view not found* and the lookup box cannot load the dictionary.
    - *Images*: on, for the Sanskrit card's pictures and the photos of marked pages.
    - *PDFs*: off on every device, this computer included. The PDFs stay in the vault on this computer and out of Sync, so on a phone or tablet the PDF page under *Today's verses* is not offered (the English and the Markdown page are), and a catalog note's box says *The PDF is on the computer* while its *Read in the vault* buttons open the Markdown copy as usual.
    - Under *Excluded folders*, on every device: `Texts/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-DEVANAGARI` and `Texts/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST_2026_02_20` (0.41 GB of plain `.txt` e-texts, which Obsidian does not open; they stay on this computer). On a phone or tablet also `Projects/_Originals` (29 MB of old drafts).

They may bring the plugins' files (Meta Bind had not reached the phone by 20 Oct 2026: install by hand whatever is missing; settings are set on each device), the core plugins' settings, the Minimal theme and the CSS snippets, the daily-note folder, and the main settings: links updated when a note is renamed, deleted files to `.trash`, pasted images and photos to `Attachments`, `Projects/_Originals`, `AI/Archive` and `Templates` kept out of search, and *Strict line breaks* (Settings → Editor; off), so a note looks the same in Reading view on every device.

3. **Let the first sync finish** (on a phone the first download is about 0.5 GB), then close and reopen Obsidian.
