## Mūla

प्रकाशनायां वै न स्यात्प्रकाशस्य प्रकाशता ।
संबोधनविभक्त्यैव विना कर्मादिशक्तिताम् ॥ ^mula

prakāśanāyāṃ vai na syātprakāśasya prakāśatā |
saṃbodhanavibhaktyaiva vinā karmādiśaktitām || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 08 - Chapter Seventeen#^pg171|p. 151]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=171>))
>
> Thus, how can here be an invocation within that which is invocation (itself)? (How can an awakening be brought about within that (reality) which is awakened consciousness by nature?) It is not the luminosity of the light that is present in the act of illumination (it is the object of illumination that is illumined, not the light). (with 17.21cd–22ab)
>
> In order to show that, by virtue of (its inherent) freedom, (Mantra) has the (semantic) power of the object (of a verb) and the rest (of the grammatical cases that function together to execute the action expressed by the verb), (the Mantra the teacher intones even) without (the name of the deity) in the vocative case (operates equally well by simply declaring) “I invoke the God”. Then, (after saying) ‘to the God’ (devāya) in the dative, (comes) the Energizer (dīpaka) which, in accord with the previous practice (yukti), 15 NAMAṆ, SVĀHĀ or the like, that bestows the plenitude (of consciousness and completes the Mantra). (with 17.22cd–24ab)
