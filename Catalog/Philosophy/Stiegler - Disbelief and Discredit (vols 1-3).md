## About

*Disbelief and Discredit* (*Mécréance et discrédit*) is a three-volume work by the French philosopher Bernard Stiegler (1952–2020) on how the industrial organization of production, and then of consumption, has damaged ways of living. The Wikipedia article this note links covers only the first volume, and gives no account of its argument.

- **Volume 1:** *La décadence des démocraties industrielles* (Paris: Galilée, 2004); in English *The Decadence of Industrial Democracies* (Cambridge: Polity, 2011). Wikipedia names the translators as Daniel Ross and Suzanne Arnold; Tom Bunyard's review in *Radical Philosophy* names Daniel Ross alone.
- **Volume 2:** *Les sociétés incontrôlables d'individus désaffectés* (2006); in English *Uncontrollable Societies of Disaffected Individuals* (Polity, 2013).
- **Volume 3:** *L'esprit perdu du capitalisme* (2006); in English *The Lost Spirit of Capitalism* (Polity, 2014).
- **Argument:** Wikipedia's article on Stiegler says the series is concerned with the loss of savoir-faire and savoir-vivre (knowing how to do and how to live), which has resulted in what Stiegler calls "generalised proletarianisation". The aim, it says, is not to overcome capitalism but to change its industrial base, so that the loss of spirit it suffers from can be prevented. Volume 2 introduces the "Antigone complex", the effect on politics and on the young of the destruction of authority.
- **Key terms:** Bunyard (*Radical Philosophy* 174, 2012), reviewing volume 1, singles out tertiary retention, psychic and collective individuation, grammatization, the pharmakon (both poison and cure), and the call to rebuild a libidinal economy.
- **Reception:** Bunyard reads volume 1 as arguing for a revolution of capitalism rather than against it, and faults its view of capitalism as effectively classless and its reformism. He adds that it assumes familiarity with Stiegler's earlier work.
