# Guide for the Perplexed 1.20

← [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.19]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed]] · [[Guide for the Perplexed 1.21]] →

*Part 1, Chapter 20* · [[Guide for the Perplexed]]

THE word ram (high) is a homonym, denoting elevation in space, and elevation in dignity, i.e., greatness, honour, and power. It has the first meaning in “And the ark was lifted up (va-tarom) above the earth” ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^7-17|Gen. 7:17]]); and the latter meaning in “I have exalted (harimoti) one chosen out of the people” ([[Psalms 089#^89-20|Ps. 89:20]]); “Forasmuch as I have exalted (harimoti) thee from amongst the dust” ([[I Kings 16#^16-2|1 Kings 16:2]]); “Forasmuch as I exalted (harimoti) thee from among the people” ([[I Kings 14#^14-7|1 Kings 14:7]]).

Whenever this term is employed in reference to God, it must be taken in the second sense: “Be thou exalted (rumah), O God, above the heavens” ([[Psalms 057#^57-12|Ps. 57:12]]).

In the same manner does the root nasa (to lift up) denote both elevation in space and elevation in rank and dignity. In the former sense it occurs in “And they lifted up (va-yisseü) their corn upon their asses” ([[Torah 10 - Miketz#^42-26|Gen. 42:26]]) and there are many instances like this in which this verb has the meaning “to carry,” “to move” from place to place: for this implies elevation in space. In the second sense we have “And his kingdom shall be exalted” (ve-tinnase) ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-7|Num. 24:7]]); “And he bare them, and carried them” (va-yenasseëm) ([[Isaiah 63#^63-9|Isa. 63:9]]); “Wherefore do ye exalt yourselves” (titnasseü) ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Num. 16:3]]).

Every form of this verb when applied to God has this latter sense—e.g., “Lift up thyself (hinnase), thou judge of the earth” ([[Psalms 094#^94-2|Ps. 94:2]]); “Thus saith the High (ram) and Exalted (nissa) One” ([[Isaiah 57#^57-15|Isa. 57:15]])—denoting elevation in rank, quality, and power, and not elevation in space.

You may be surprised that I employ the expression, “elevation in rank, quality, and power,” and you may say, “How can you assert that several distinct expressions denote the same thing?” It will be explained later on (chap. 1. seqq.) that those who possess a true knowledge of God do not consider that He possesses many attributes, but believe that these various attributes which describe His Might, Greatness, Power, Perfection, Goodness, etc., are identical, denoting His Essence, and not anything extraneous to His Essence.

I shall devote special chapters to the Names and Attributes of God; our intention here is solely to show that “high and exalted” in the passage quoted denote elevation in rank, not in space.
