# 2 John 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue 2 John 01|NRSVue]] · [[2 John 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 2 John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-1|1–3]]:** Opening.  ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Elect lady*, the personification of the community as a woman (Gk “kyria,” in LXX for “giberet,” “mistress,” is the feminine form of “kyrios,” “lord” or “master”), and its members as her children, parallels the practice of personifying Jerusalem as a woman (see, e.g., Isa 54). The Greek “eklektos” (*elect*) is frequently used in the Septuagint to translate “beḥyr,” “chosen,” in reference to Israel. The congregation is thus addressed as part of the covenant community (see also [[1 Peter 02#^2-9|1 Pet 2.9]]). ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-3|3]]:** *The Father’s Son*, the author stresses that Jesus is the Son of “the Father.” ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-4|4–6]]:** Mutual love.  ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-4|4]]:** The image of *walking* in good (or evil) paths echoes Prov (1.15; 2.20) and Ps (1.1,6), where one’s path represents moral orientation. Whereas in the Tanakh *walking in the truth* signifies obedience to God (see [[II Kings 20#^20-3|2 Kings 20.3]]; [[Psalms 026#^26-3|Ps 26.3–5]]), John defines the “true” path not as a moral choice but as correct belief. ^1-4-2

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-5|5]]:** The commandment to *love one another* reflects [[John 13#^13-34|Jn 13.34]]; [[1 John 02#^2-7|1 Jn 2.7–11]]; [[1 John 03#^3-11|3.11]], and encourages harmony within the community; the issue of care for outsiders is not addressed here. The stress on mutual love may derive from [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], “love your neighbor as yourself.” ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-6|6]]:** To *walk according to his commandments* probably refers not to “halakhah,” the way or path that one should follow with reference to the commands in the Hebrew Bible, but to Jesus’ commandment that his followers love one another ([[John 13#^13-34|Jn 13.34]]). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-7|7–11]]:** Warning against deceivers.  ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-7|7]]:** The term *antichrist* used to describe the deceivers is a neologism, used only here and in [[1 John 02#^2-18|1 Jn 2.18]]; [[1 John 04#^4-3|4.3]], to indicate that they are literally “against the Messiah.” The term does not suggest (as it does in later Christian literature \[see Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lectures* 15.11–15\]) that the opponents are satanic agents. ^1-7-2

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-8|8–9]]:** *Reward . . . has both the Father and the Son* implies that the goal of the community’s life is not an external reward but rather life in God’s love. This idea echoes [[1 John 02#^2-22|1 Jn 2.22–23]], which claims that the antichrist, who denies that Jesus is the Christ, denies both Father and Son. ^1-8

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-10|10–11]]:** The prohibition against giving hospitality to deceivers is supported on moral grounds: to share hospitality is to *participate in* their *evil deeds.* Both Greco-Roman and Jewish traditions considered hospitality a primary virtue. The NT word for “hospitality” (“philoxenia,” lit., “love of the stranger” in e.g., [[Romans 12#^12-13|Rom 12.13]]) is not used here; instead, the Elder warns congregants that they incur “guilt by association” if they *welcome* (Gk “chairein,” “greet with joy”) sinners (cf. *Avot de R. Natan* 30: “He who joins himself to those who commit transgressions, though he does not do what they do, will nevertheless receive punishment as one of them”). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-12|12–13]]:** Closing and greetings.  ^1-12

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Paper* (Gk “chartes”), a papyrus sheet. *Face to face* (see also 3 Jn 14), a concern for intimacy; the expression appears in the Tanakh in reference to the conversations between God and select figures, especially Moses ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-11|Ex 33.11]]; [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-1|Deut 34.1]]; see also [[1 Corinthians 13#^13-12|1 Cor 13.12]]; [[2 Corinthians 10#^10-1|2 Cor 10.1]]). In the Thessalonian correspondence, Paul speaks of his desire to see the congregation *face to face* ([[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-17|1 Thess 2.17]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-10|3.10]]). *Joy*, Gk “chara,” an echo of the opening “chairo,” grace, in v 3. ^1-12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 John 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Children* . . . *elect sister*, see v 1n. ^1-13
