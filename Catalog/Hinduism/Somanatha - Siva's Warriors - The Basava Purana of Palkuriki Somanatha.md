## About

A 13th-century Telugu poem by Pālkuriki Somanātha on the life of Basava (Basavaṇṇa), the 12th-century Vīraśaiva leader, joined to legends of other Vīraśaiva saints. It is a sacred text of the Vīraśaiva tradition.

- **Author:** Pālkuriki Somanātha. Princeton University Press says Vīraśaivas in Andhra and Karnataka revere him as a saint and count him a major poet in both Telugu and Kannada.
- **Form:** Somanātha chose the native (*deśi*) style over the *campū*, which mixes verse and prose, and wrote in *dvipada* couplets (Wikipedia).
- **Subject:** Basava was chief of the royal treasury under the Kalachuri king Bijjala I (reigned 1156–67), according to Britannica. Wikipedia gives his dates as 1134–1196; Britannica says only that he flourished in the 12th century, and adds that Kalachuri inscriptions suggest he revived an existing sect rather than founding a new one.
- **Kannada version:** Bhīma Kavi's Kannada *Basava Purāṇa* (Wikipedia: 1369; Britannica: 14th century) was based on Somanātha's poem. Wikipedia says it became the standard biography of Basava and inspired further Kannada and Sanskrit Liṅgāyat Purāṇas.
- **English translations:** Wikipedia says C. P. Brown made the first, in 1863. *Siva's Warriors* followed from Princeton in 1990, in the Princeton Library of Asian Translations, reissued in 2016 (340 pp.). Princeton names Velcheru Narayana Rao and Gene H. Roghair as translators and calls it the first English translation; Wikipedia credits Narayana Rao alone.
- **Why it matters:** it is both a hagiography of Basava and an anthology of saints' legends, and through Bhīma Kavi it shaped the Kannada tradition about him. Princeton adds that no Telugu Vīraśaiva work had earlier been translated or explained in English.
