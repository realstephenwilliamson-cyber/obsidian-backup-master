### 14.1–12: John the Baptist is beheaded

*[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-1|Text]], p. 37* · ([[Mark 06#^6-14|Mk 6.14–29]]; [[Luke 09#^9-7|Lk 9.7–11]]). Cf. Josephus, *Ant*. 18.109–19. ^14-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-1|1]]:** *Herod*, son of Herod the Great (see 2.1n.), ruled Galilee from 4 BCE–39 CE (see “Jewish History, 331 BCE–135 CE,” p. 583). ^14-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-2|2]]:** *John the Baptist*, see 3.1–12. *Raised from the dead*, ironic comment, given that Matthew depicts Jesus, not John, as eventually raised. ^14-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-3|3]]:** *Herodias*, niece of Herod the Great, sister of Agrippa I ([[Acts 12#^12-1|Acts 12.1]]), and wife not of Antipas’s brother Philip but of another half-brother, Herod Boethus. ^14-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-4|4]]:** *Not lawful*, marrying a brother’s wife constituted incest ([[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-16|Lev 18.16]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-21|20.21]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-5|Deut 25.5–10]]; Josephus, *Ant*. 18.136; cf. 5.31–32). ^14-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-5|5]]:** *Feared the crowd*, Josephus confirms John’s popularity. ^14-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-6|6]]:** *Daughter of Herodias*, Josephus names her Salome (*Ant*. 18.136–37). ^14-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-7|7]]:** *Whatever she might ask*, a rash promise; see [[Esther 05#^5-3|Esth 5.3]]. ^14-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-10|10]]:** *Had John beheaded*, see 17.12–13. ^14-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-12|12]]:** *His disciples came*, an ironic foreshadowing of Jesus’ death, where his disciples desert him (27.57). ^14-12

### 14.13–21: Feeding (more than) the five thousand

*[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-13|Text]], p. 38* · ([[Mark 06#^6-30|Mk 6.30–44]]; [[Luke 09#^9-12|Lk 9.12–17]]; [[John 06#^6-1|Jn 6.1–15]]), the only miracle reported in all four canonical Gospels; see 14.15–21. ^14-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-19|19]]:** *Blessed and broke the loaves*, see 26.20–29; the description recollects the feeding of Israel in the wilderness (Ex 16) and anticipates the Last Supper. *2 Bar.* 29.8 connects Ex 16 with the messianic age. ^14-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-20|20]]:** *All ate and were filled*, see [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-15|Ex 16.15–18]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-31|Num 11.31–32]]; cf. [[John 06#^6-31|Jn 6.31–33]]; [[Revelation 02#^2-17|Rev 2.17]]) and Elisha’s food miracle ([[II Kings 04#^4-42|2 Kings 4.42–44]]). ^14-20

### 14.22–36: Jesus walks on water

*[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-22|Text]], p. 38* · ([[Mark 06#^6-45|Mk 6.45–52]]; [[John 06#^6-16|Jn 6.16–21]]). ^14-22

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-25|25]]:** *Walking toward them on the sea*, like God, Jesus has power over the seas ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-9|Gen 1.9–10]],[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-21|21]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-21|Ex 14.21–22]]; [[Isaiah 43#^43-16|Isa 43.16]]; [[Isaiah 51#^51-9|51.9–10]]; [[Habakkuk 3#^3-15|Hab 3.15]]; [[Psalms 077#^77-20|Ps 77.19]]; [[Job 09#^9-8|Job 9.8]]; [[Job 26#^26-11|26.11–12]]). See 6.33n. ^14-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-26|26]]:** *Ghost*, Gk “phantasma,” “apparition.” ^14-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-27|27]]:** See [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]; [[Torah 52 - Vayeilech#^31-6|Deut 31.6]]. A rabbinic story depicts the recitation of Scripture during a storm (*b. Bat*. 73a). ^14-27

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-28|28–30]]:** Perhaps foreshadowing Peter’s lack of faith. ^14-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-31|31]]:** *Little faith*, frequent rebuke of the disciples, 6.30; 8.26; 14.31; 16.8; 17.20. *Doubt*, see 28.17. ^14-31

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-33|33]]:** *Son of God*, here indicating Jesus’ divine nature (see 2.15; 3.17; 4.3,6; 8.29); the phrase may have been a messianic reference (4Q246; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.28–29; 13.32); no Jewish texts identify the Messiah as the son of God. ^14-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-34|34]]:** See [[Mark 06#^6-53|Mk 6.53]]. *Gennesaret*, on the northwestern shore of the Sea of Galilee (Josephus, *J.W.* 3.516–21). ^14-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 14#^14-36|36]]:** *Fringe*, see 9.20n. ^14-36
