# The Three Duties — Lectio, Disputatio, Praedicatio

> [!abstract] Definition
> The pedagogical curriculum of the medieval university (crystallized by Peter the Chanter in the 12th century), asserting that scholarship consists of three interdependent operations: *lectio* (reading/glossing), *disputatio* (public adversarial debate), and *praedicatio* (ethical/homiletical proclamation).

## The Breakdown of Modern Academia
In [[Second Domestication outline 2026-07-07|The Second Domestication]]:
- The medieval university understood that reading (*lectio*) was inert without debate (*disputatio*) to test truth, and disputation was sterile without proclamation (*praedicatio*) to orient knowledge toward communal life.
- The modern secular research university systematically retained only the first third (*lectio* converted into private research output) while abandoning *disputatio* and *praedicatio*—the two stages that formed human character and moral judgment.
