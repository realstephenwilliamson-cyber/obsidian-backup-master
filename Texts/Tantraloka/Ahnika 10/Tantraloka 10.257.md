## Mūla

वेद्यच्छायोऽवभासो हि मेयेऽधिष्ठानमुच्यते ।
यत्त्वधिष्ठातृभूतादेः पूर्वोक्तस्य वपुर्ध्रुवम् ॥ ^mula

vedyacchāyo'vabhāso hi meye'dhiṣṭhānamucyate |
yattvadhiṣṭhātṛbhūtādeḥ pūrvoktasya vapurdhruvam || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 14 - Chapter Ten, part 5#^pg479|p. 460]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=479>))
>
> The manifestation of a trace (chāyā) of objectivity within the object of knowledge (perceived in a dream) is said to be the sustaining (objectivizing consciousness) (adhiṣthāna) (within it). (with 10.257ab)
>
> The state of deep sleep is profoundly silent. It is said to prevail when the body of the creatures and the rest mentioned above (i.e. the principles and denotation) is (one with) the sustaining consciousness (adhiṣtṭhātr) which is the fixed (eternal) (dhruva) seed of all things. The same cognizing subject who is present in the direct experience (proper to the waking state) and in the thought (proper to dreaming) is present (in deep sleep also). There is no perception of phenomenal existence (bhāva), and so (the state in which he reposes in himself alone) is called deep sleep. (with 10.257cd–259ab)

## Viveka

> वेद्यच्छायोऽवभासो हि मेयेऽधिष्ठानमुच्यते ।
> यत्त्वधिष्ठातृभूतादेः पूर्वोक्तस्य वपुर्धुर्वम् ॥
> बीजं विश्वस्य तत्तूष्णीभूतं सौषुप्तमुच्यते ।
