### 17.1–8: The Transfiguration

*[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-1|Text]], p. 42* · ([[Mark 09#^9-2|Mk 9.2–8]]; [[Luke 09#^9-28|Lk 9.28–34]]). See also [[2 Peter 01#^1-17|2 Pet 1.17–18]]. ^17-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-1|1]]:** *Peter . . . James . . . John*, like Moses, Jesus takes three named disciples for a revelatory experience (see [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-1|Ex 24.1]]). *High mountain*, associated with Moses and Elijah (see 5.1–2n.; [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-20|Ex 19.20]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-9|24.9–18]]; [[I Kings 19#^19-8|1 Kings 19.8–18]]). ^17-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-2|2]]:** *Transfigured*, Gk “metamorphoō,” “change in form or appearance” (see [[Romans 12#^12-2|Rom 12.2]]; [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-18|2 Cor 3.18]]). *His face shone*, allusion to Moses ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-35|Ex 34.35]]); *like the sun*, cf. [[Daniel 10#^10-6|Dan 10.6]], “. . . his face like lightning, his eyes like flaming torches. . .”; [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-18|2 Cor 3.18]]; [[Revelation 10#^10-1|Rev 10.1]]. ^17-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-3|3]]:** *Moses and Elijah*, perhaps suggesting that Jesus is the fulfillment of the Law and the Prophets (5.17; 7.12; 17.12; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15]],[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-18|18]]). Rabbinic texts also link Moses and Elijah (see *Deut. Rab*. 3.17, which notes that God will bring both Moses and Elijah back in the future). ^17-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-5|5]]:** See [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]. *Bright cloud*, indicating God’s presence ([[Torah 23 - Pekudei#^40-35|Ex 40.35–38]]; see also [[Revelation 14#^14-14|Rev 14.14]]; Josephus, *Ant*. 4.326). *Listen to him*, see 3.17; cf. [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15]] of the prophet like Moses. ^17-5

### 17.9–13: Elijah the prophet

*[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-9|Text]], p. 43* · ([[Mark 09#^9-9|Mk 9.9–13]]). ^17-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-9|9]]:** *Tell no one*, messianic secret; see 8.4n. Cf. 9.30; 16.20. ^17-9-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-10|10]]:** *Scribes*, see 7.28–29n.; 13.52n. *Elijah must come first*, as forerunner, here specifically of the Messiah rather than in the Tanakh as forerunner of the messianic age ([[Malachi 3#^3-22|Mal 4.4–6]] \[HB 3.22–24\]; see 11.4–5n.). ^17-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-12|12–13]]:** *Elijah has already come*, referencing John the Baptist (3.3; [[Luke 01#^1-17|Lk 1.17]]; see also [[John 01#^1-21|Jn 1.21]]). Rabbinic literature varies regarding Elijah’s precise messianic role and duties. See 11.14–15n. In *m. Ed.* 8.7, Elijah, comes to make peace in the world ([[Malachi 3#^3-23|Mal 4.5–6]] \[HB 3.23–24\]); in *b. Sanh.* 98a, Elijah refers to his earthly return; and *b. Eruv*. 43b states that Elijah will not return on certain festival eves (see *Ex. Rab*. 18.12). ^17-12

### 7.14–21: Jesus heals a “moonstruck” boy

*[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-14|Text]], p. 43* · ([[Mark 09#^9-14|Mk 9.14–29]]; [[Mark 11#^11-22|11.22–23]]; [[Luke 09#^9-37|Lk 9.37–43]]; [[Luke 17#^17-6|17.6]]). ^17-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-15|15]]:** *Epileptic*, Gk “seleneizetai,” “moonstruck”; the exorcism indicates demonic possession (8.16,28–34). ^17-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-16|16]]:** *Could not cure him*, the disciples’ faith was insufficient (17.20). ^17-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-17|17–19]]:** *Faithless and perverse*, see 12.39n.; 16.4. ^17-17

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-20|20–21]]:** *Mustard seed*, 13.31–32n.; 14.30–31n. ^17-20

### 17.22–23: Second Passion prediction

*[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-22|Text]], p. 43* · ([[Mark 09#^9-30|Mk 9.30–32]]; [[Luke 09#^9-43|Lk 9.43–45]]). See 16.21; 20.17–19; 26.2. ^17-22

### 17.24–27: Temple tax

*[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-24|Text]], p. 43* · See 22.15–22. ^17-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-24|24]]:** *Capernaum*, see 4.13n. *Temple tax*, Jewish males annually paid the half-shekel tax for the Jerusalem Temple ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^30-11|Ex 30.11–16]]; [[Nehemiah 10#^10-33|Neh 10.33]]). Some Jewish sources state that all males over age 20 (i.e., non-minors) paid the tax (*m. Seqal.* 1.1–3); others indicate it was not compulsory (*m. Seqal*. 1.4; *b. Men.* 65a; 4Q159, frag. 1, col. 2, lines 6–7, suggests that the tax only had to be paid once during a lifetime). *Does your teacher not pay*, Jesus’ teachings may have aroused *kind does not come out except by prayer and fasting* e Other ancient authorities read *living* f Gk *didrachma* g Gk *he* h Gk *stater* ; the stater was worth two didrachmas suspicion (12.6). The question would also be pertinent to Jews within Matthew’s community. ^17-24-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-26|26]]:** *Children are free*, Jesus and his followers are technically exempt. ^17-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 17#^17-27|27]]:** *We do not give offense*, a lesson for Matthew’s day when Rome replaced the Temple tax with the *Fiscus Iudaicus*, which required Jews to support the temple of Jupiter Capitolina in Rome (see “Jewish History, 331 BCE–135 CE,” p. 583). *Fish*, rabbinic sources speak of fish containing riches (*b. Sanh. 119a; Pesiq. Rab.* 23.6; *Eccl. Rab.* 11.1). *Coin*, a Tyrian “stater,” worth four drachmas, enough to pay for two people. ^17-27
