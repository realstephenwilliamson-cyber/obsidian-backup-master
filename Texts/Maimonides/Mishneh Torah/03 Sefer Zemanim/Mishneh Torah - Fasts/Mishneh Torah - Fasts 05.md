**1.** There are days when the entire Jewish people[^1] fast[^2] because of the calamities that occurred to them then,[^3] to arouse [their] hearts and initiate [them in] the paths of repentance.[^4] This will serve as a reminder of our wicked conduct and that of our ancestors, which resembles our present conduct and therefore brought these calamities upon them and upon us.[^5] By reminding ourselves of these matters, we will repent[^6] and improve [our conduct], as \[[[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-40|Leviticus 26:40]]] states: "And they will confess their sin and the sin of their ancestors."[^7] ^5-1

**2.** These days are the following:  
The[^8] Third of Tishrei. This is the day on which Gedaliah ben Achikam[^9] was slain[^10] and the ember of Israel that remained was extinguished, causing their exile to become complete.[^11]  
The Tenth of Tevet. This is the day Nebuchadnezzar, the wicked, the King of Babylon, camped[^12] against Jerusalem and placed the city under siege.[^13]  
The Seventeenth of Tammuz. Five tragedies took place on this day:[^14]  
a) The Tablets were broken;[^15]  
b) In the First Temple, the offering of the daily sacrifices[^16] was nullified;[^17]  
c) [The walls of] Jerusalem were breached in [the war leading to] the destruction of the Second Temple;  
d)[^18] Apostmos, the wicked,[^19] burned a Torah scroll;[^20] and  
e) He[^21] erected an idol in the Temple.[^22] ^5-2

**3.** On the Ninth of Av, five tragedies occurred:  
It[^23] was decreed that the Jews in the desert would not enter *Eretz Yisrael*;  
The[^24] First and the Second Temples were destroyed;  
A[^25] large city named Betar was captured. Thousands and myriads of Jews inhabited it.[^26] They were ruled by a great king whom the entire Jewish people and the leading Sages considered to be the Messianic king.[^27] The city fell to the Romans and they were all slain, causing a national catastrophe equivalent to that of the Temple's destruction.[^28]  
On that day designated for retribution, the wicked Tineius Rufus[^29] plowed the site of the Temple and its surroundings,[^30] thereby fulfilling the prophecy \[[[Micah 3#^3-12|Micah 3:12]]], "Zion will be plowed like a field."[^31] ^5-3

**4.** These four fasts are explicitly mentioned in the prophetic tradition \[[[Zechariah 08#^8-19|Zechariah 8:19]]]: "The fast of the fourth [month],[^32] the fast of the fifth [month]...." "The fast of the fourth [month]" refers to the Seventeenth of Tammuz,[^33] which is in the fourth month; "the fast of the fifth [month]," to Tish'ah B'Av, which is in the fifth month; "the fast of the seventh [month]," to the Third of Tishrei which is in the seventh month; "the fast of the tenth [month]," to the Tenth of Tevet, which is in the tenth month.[^34] ^5-4

**5.** And the entire Jewish people follow the custom of fasting at these times and on the Thirteenth of Adar,[^35] in commemoration of the fasts that [the people] took upon themselves in the time of Haman,[^36] as mentioned [in [[Esther 09#^9-31|Esther 9:31]]]: "the matter of the fasts and the outcries."  
If[^37] the Thirteenth of Adar falls on the Sabbath, the fast is pushed forward[^38] and held on Thursday, which is the eleventh of Adar.[^39] If, however, any of the [dates of] other fasts fall on the Sabbath, the fasts are postponed until after the Sabbath.[^40] If [the dates of] these fasts fall on Friday, we should fast on Friday.[^41]  
On all these fasts, the trumpets are not sounded, nor is the *Ne'ilah* service recited.[^42] The passage *Vay'chal*[^43] is read from the Torah, however, in both the morning and the afternoon services.[^44]  
On all these [fasts], with the exception of Tish'ah B'Av, we may eat and drink at night.[^45] ^5-5

**6.** When the month of Av enters,[^46] we reduce our joy.[^47] During the week of Tish'ah B'Av,[^48] it is forbidden to cut one's hair,[^49] to do laundry,[^50] or to wear a pressed garment[^51] - even one of linen[^52] - until after the fast.[^53]  
It has already been accepted as a Jewish custom not to eat meat[^54] or enter a bathhouse during this week until after the fast.[^55] There are places that follow the custom of refraining from slaughtering from Rosh Chodesh Av until after the fast.[^56] ^5-6

**7.** All [the restrictions of] Tish'ah B'Av[^57] apply at night as well as during the day. One may not eat after sunset [of the previous day];[^58] [it is forbidden to eat] between sunset and the appearance of the stars,[^59] as on Yom Kippur.[^60]  
One should not eat meat or drink wine at the meal before the fast.[^61] One may, however, drink grape juice that has not been left [to ferment] for three days.[^62] One may eat salted meat that was slaughtered more than three days previously.[^63] One should not eat two cooked dishes.[^64] ^5-7

**8.** When does the[^65] above apply? When one ate [this meal] in the afternoon on the day preceding Tish'ah B'Av.[^66] If, however, one eats a meal before noon, although this is the last meal one eats before the fast, one may eat all that one desires.[^67]  
When the day before Tish'ah B'Av falls on the Sabbath,[^68] one may eat and drink to the full extent of one's needs, and one may serve even a meal resembling Solomon's feasts at one's table.[^69]  
Similarly, when Tish'ah B'Av falls on the Sabbath,[^70] one need not withhold anything at all.[^71] ^5-8

**9.** This is the rite observed by the people as a whole who cannot endure more. In contrast, the rite observed by the pious of the earlier generations was as follows:[^72] A person would sit alone between the oven and the cooking range. Others would bring him dried bread and salt. He would dip it in water and drink a pitcher of water while worried, forlorn, and in tears, as one whose dead was lying before him.  
The scholars should act in this or a similar manner.[^73] We never ate cooked food, even lentils, on the day before Tish'ah B'Av, except on the Sabbath. ^5-9

**10.** Pregnant women and those who are nursing[^74] must complete their fasts on Tish'ah B'Av.[^75]  
[On this day,] it is forbidden to wash in either hot or cold water;[^76] it is even forbidden to place one's finger in water.[^77] Similarly, anointing oneself for the sake of pleasure,[^78] wearing[^79] shoes,[^80] and sexual relations are forbidden,[^81] as on Yom Kippur.[^82]  
In places where it is customary to do work,[^83] one may work.[^84] In places where it is not customary to work, one should not.[^85] Torah scholars everywhere should remain idle on this day.[^86] Our Sages said,[^87] "Whoever performs work on this day will never see a sign of blessing forever."[^88] ^5-10

**11.** Torah scholars[^89] should not exchange greetings on Tish'ah B'Av.[^90] Instead, they should sit in agony and frustration like mourners.[^91] If a common person greets them, they should reply to him[^92] weakly, in a somber tone.[^93]  
On Tish'ah B'Av, it is forbidden to read from the Torah, the Prophets, or the Sacred Writings [or to study] the Mishnah, Torah law, the Talmud, or the Aggadic works.[^94] One may study only[^95] Job,[^96] Eichah,[^97] and the prophecies of retribution in Jeremiah.[^98] Children should not study in school on this day.[^99]  
There are some sages who do not wear the head *tefillin*.[^100] ^5-11

**12.** After the Temple was destroyed, the Sages of that generation ordained[^101] that one should never build a building[^102] whose walls are decorated with ornate designs like the palaces of kings. Instead, one should cover the walls of one's home with mortar and paint over them with lime, leaving a space one cubit square opposite the doorway[^103] that is unpainted.[^104] If, however, a person buys a dwelling whose walls have been decorated, it may remain as is; he is not obligated to scrape [the designs] from the walls. ^5-12

**13.** Similarly, they ordained that a person who sets a table for guests should serve slightly less [than usual] and should leave a place empty, [so that it obviously] lacks one of the dishes that would ordinarily be placed there.[^105]  
When a woman has a set of jewelry made for her, she should refrain from having one of the pieces appropriate for the set made, so that her jewelry is not perfect.[^106]  
Similarly, when a groom marries, he should place ashes on his forehead[^107] on the place where one wears *tefillin*.[^108] All of these practices were instituted to recall Jerusalem, as \[[[Psalms 137#^137-5|Psalms 137:5-6]]] states: "If I forget you, O Jerusalem, may my right hand lose its dexterity. Let my tongue cleave to my palate if I do not remember you, if I do not recall Jerusalem during my greatest joy." ^5-13

**14.** Similarly, they ordained that one should not play melodies with any sort of musical instrument. It is forbidden to celebrate with such instruments or to listen to them being played [as an expression of mourning][^109] for the destruction.[^110]  
Even songs [without musical accompaniment] that are recited over wine are forbidden, as \[[[Isaiah 24#^24-9|Isaiah 24:9]]] states: "Do not drink wine with song." It has, however, become accepted custom among the entire Jewish people to recite words of praise, songs of thanksgiving, and the like to God over wine.[^111] ^5-14

**15.** Afterwards, they ordained that grooms[^112] should not wear crowns at all, nor should they wear any diadems at all,[^113] as [implied by [[Ezekiel 21#^21-31|Ezekiel 21:31]]]: "Remove the miter and lift up the crown." Similarly, they ordained that brides should not wear crowns of silver or gold; a garland made from twisted cords is, however, permitted for a bride.[^114] ^5-15

**16.** When a person sees the cities of Judah in a state of destruction,[^115] he should recite \[[[Isaiah 64#^64-9|Isaiah 64:9]]]: "Your holy cities have become like the desert," and rend his garments.[^116] When one sees Jerusalem in a state of destruction,[^117] one should recite [the continuation of the verse,] "Zion is a desert...." When one sees the Temple in a state of destruction, one should recite [*ibid.*:10]: "Our holy and beautiful House [...has been burned with fire]" and rend one's garments.[^118]  
From which point is one obligated to rend one's garments? From Tzofim.[^119] Afterwards, when one reaches the Temple, one should rend them a second time.[^120] If one encountered the Temple first, because one came from the desert, one should rend one's garments because of the Temple, and add to the tear because of Jerusalem.[^121] ^5-16

**17.** In all these situations, one must rend one's garments with one's hands and not with a utensil.[^122] While standing,[^123] the person should rend all the garments he is wearing until he reveals his heart.[^124] He should never mend these tears at all.[^125] He may, however, have them stitched, hemmed, gathered closed, or sewn with a ladder pattern. ^5-17

**18.** [The following rules apply when a person] comes to Jerusalem frequently in his travels: If he comes within thirty days of his last visit, he is not required to rend his garments. If he comes after thirty days, he is.[^126] ^5-18

**19.** All these [commemorative] fasts will be nullified in the Messianic era[^127] and, indeed ultimately, they will be transformed[^128] into holidays and days of rejoicing and celebration,[^129] as \[[[Zechariah 08#^8-19|Zechariah 8:19]]] states: "Thus declares the Lord of Hosts, 'The fast of the fourth [month],[^130] the fast of the fifth [month],[^131] the fast of the seventh [month],[^132] and the fast of the tenth [month][^133] will be [times of] happiness and celebration and festivals for the House of Judah. And they shall love truth and peace.'[^134] ^5-19
