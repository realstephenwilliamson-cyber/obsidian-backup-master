## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. X
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 10 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v10 12 - Chapter Twenty-Nine, part 4#^pg358|p. 341]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 10.pdf#page=358>))
>
> This (sacrifice) is called Ādiyāga (the Foremost Sacrifice), because the essence of the main (mukhya) (Wheel of consciousness) is extracted (ādīyate) (thereby), and so is the main sacrifice. The all-pervasive Lord has praised its nature here and there in scriptures such as the venerable Viravali,, the Hārdeśa, the Khemata, the Arnava, and in the venerable Siddhā, Utphulla, Maryādahīna and the Caryākula. (with 29.164–166ab)
>
> By the grace of this pair (of seats), (he is) free of yogic practice and (the observance) of vows. Having constantly recollected (the deity), (the teacher) is intent solely on the Foremost Sacrifice. He should deposit the Vidyā and the seed syllable (kita)) on the body of Śakti and on his own (respectively), in due order, having meditated on the lotus (of the Śakti) which is like the Moon and his own (that shines with) the radiant brilliance of the Sun. He should (thus) unite here the two seats, that is, Vidyā (which is the lotus of the Moon) and Mantra (which is the lotus of the Sun). (with 29.166cd–168)
