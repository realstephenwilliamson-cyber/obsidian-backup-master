### 8.1–7: The collection

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-1|Text]], p. 364* · , was meant to bring economic relief for the Jerusalem church and to show unity between it and Gentile diaspora congregations; see [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-1|1 Cor 16.1–4]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-10|Gal 2.10]]; [[Romans 15#^15-25|Rom 15.25–27]]. Acts, the second half of which details Paul’s activities, does not mention the collection, leaving unanswerable the question of what happened to these funds. Chs 8 and 9 are probably two separate letters to two different regions in Achaia; see 9.2n. ^8-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-1|1]]:** *Grace* (Gk “charis”), used ten times in chs 8–9 to describe the collection, translated in 8.4 as “privilege”; in 8.6,7,19 as “generous undertaking”; in 8.9 as “generous act”; in 8.16 as “thanks”; in 9.8 as “blessing”; in 9.15 as “thanks.” Related words include Gk “eulogia,” “bountiful gift,” and “voluntary gift” (9.5); “bountifully” (9.6 twice); “ministry” (Gk “leitourgia”), 9.12; “sharing” (Gk “koinonia”), 8.4; 9.13. Paul thus attaches rich theological significance to the collection. Early Judaism understands support for the needy to be both a generous act (Heb “tzedekah,” righteousness) and an obligation based on recognition of dependence upon God for sustenance (e.g., [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-13|Deut 11.13–15]]). *Macedonia*, see 1.16n. Principally the Philippian and Thessalonian churches, but see 9.2. ^8-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-2|2]]:** *Severe ordeal*, possibly the event mentioned in [[Philippians 01#^1-29|Phil 1.29–30]]. ^8-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-4|4]]:** *Sharing* (Gk “koinonia”), here describing the collection (9.13; [[Romans 15#^15-26|Rom 15.26]]); the term also refers to participation in the Eucharist ([[1 Corinthians 10#^10-16|1 Cor 10.16]]) and in Christ’s suffering ([[Philippians 03#^3-10|Phil 3.10]]); see 8.1n. *Saints*, here and in 9.1,12, the Jerusalem believers. See 13.12n. ^8-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-6|6]]:** *Titus*, see 2.13n.; 8.16–24. ^8-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-7|7]]:** *Now as you excel in everything . . .* , using a common Greco-Roman rhetorical technique (“proparaitesis”), Paul flatters his readers before making his request. This same technique was recommended by the rabbis for use in prayer ([[Berakhot 34#34a|*b. Ber.* 34a]]): it is best to petition God once one has completed the *Amidah’s* first three benedictions, during which the person approaches God as a servant who offers the master a praise-filled eulogy. *In speech, in knowledge*, Paul praises the Corinthians’ knowledge of and ability to declare the gospel. ^8-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-8|8.8–15]]:** **Generosity relating to the collection.** ^8-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-8|8]]:** *Testing*, see 2.9n. ^8-8-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-9|9]]:** Close parallel of *though he was rich . . . he became poor* is 5.21, “He made him to be sin who knew no sin”; see also [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6–8]]. *He became poor*, cf. [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6-11]]. Paul asks the Corinthians, by giving of their wealth, to emulate Jesus, who gave up the riches of his eternal glory for the earthly mission that brings his followers the riches of salvation. ^8-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-11|11]]:** *Now finish doing it*, contrast *m. Avot* 2.16: “It is not your job to finish the work, but neither are you free to walk away from it.” ^8-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-12|12]]:** *According to what one has*, see [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-17|Deut 16.17]]. 1 ^8-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-5|5]]:** *“The one . . . too little,”* [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-18|Ex 16.18]]. In the case of manna, God assured that individuals received as much as, but no more than, their actual need. Paul urges those in Christ to behave similarly with their material wealth. ^8-5

### 8.16–24: The sending

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 08#^8-16|Text]], p. 365* · The sending of Titus and two *brothers* (vv 18,22–23) might indicate that the collection is modeled after the Temple tax ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^30-13|Ex 30.13–15]]); Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.76–78 (referring to “first fruits” and “ransom”) states that its delivery was carried out by “sacred ambassadors selected on account of their virtue.” ^8-16
