# Sirach 20 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Sirach 19|← Sirach 19]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Sirach 21|Sirach 21 →]]

## Silence and speech

**20:1** There is a rebuke that is untimely, and there is the person who is wise enough to keep silent. ^20-1

**20:2** How much better it is to rebuke than to fume! ^20-2

**20:3** And the one who admits his fault will be kept from ridicule. ^20-3

**20:4** Like a eunuch lusting to violate a young woman is the person who makes decisions under compulsion. ^20-4

**20:5** Some people keep silent and are found^[Gk: Heb *thought*] to be wise, while others are detested for being talkative. ^20-5

**20:6** Some people keep silent because they have nothing to say, while others keep silent because they know when to speak. ^20-6

**20:7** The wise remain silent until the right moment, but the arrogant and the fool miss the right moment. ^20-7

**20:8** Whoever talks too much is detested, and whoever pretends to authority is hated.^[Other ancient authorities add *How good it is to show repentance when you are reproved, for so you will escape deliberate sin!*] ^20-8

## Paradoxes

**20:9** There may be good fortune for a person in adversity, and a windfall may result in a loss. ^20-9

**20:10** There is the gift that profits you nothing and the gift to be paid back double. ^20-10

**20:11** There are losses for the sake of glory, and there are some who have raised their heads from humble circumstances. ^20-11

**20:12** Some buy much for little but pay for it seven times over. ^20-12

**20:13** The wise make themselves beloved by only few words,^[Heb: Gk *by words*] but the courtesies of fools are wasted. ^20-13

**20:14** A fool’s gift will profit you nothing,^[Other ancient authorities add *so it is with the envious who give under compulsion*] for he looks for recompense sevenfold.^[Syr: Gk *he has many eyes instead of one*] ^20-14

**20:15** He gives little and insults much; he opens his mouth like a town crier. Today he lends, and tomorrow he asks it back; such a one is hateful.^[Other ancient authorities add *to God and humans*] ^20-15

**20:16** The fool says, “I have no friends, and I get no thanks for my good deeds.” Those who eat his bread are evil-tongued. ^20-16

**20:17** How many will ridicule him, and how often!^[Other ancient authorities add *for he has not honestly received what he has, and what he does not have is unimportant to him*] ^20-17

## Inappropriate speech

**20:18** A slip on the pavement is better than a slip of the tongue; the downfall of the wicked will occur just as speedily. ^20-18

**20:19** A coarse person is like an inappropriate story, continually on the lips of the ignorant. ^20-19

**20:20** A proverb from a fool’s lips will be rejected, for he does not tell it at the proper time. ^20-20

**20:21** One may be prevented from sinning by poverty, so when he rests he feels no remorse. ^20-21

**20:22** One may lose his life through shame or lose it because of a foolish person.^[Other ancient authorities read *human respect*] ^20-22

**20:23** One may make promises to a friend out of shame and so make an enemy for nothing. ^20-23

## Lying

**20:24** A lie is an ugly blot on a person; it is continually on the lips of the ignorant. ^20-24

**20:25** A thief is preferable to a habitual liar, but both will inherit ruin. ^20-25

**20:26** The character of liars leads to disgrace, and their shame is ever with them. ^20-26

## PROVERBIAL SAYINGS^[This heading is included in the Gk text.]

**20:27** Wise persons advance themselves by their words, and those who are sensible please the great. ^20-27

**20:28** Those who cultivate the soil heap up their harvest, and those who please the great atone for injustice. ^20-28

**20:29** Favors and gifts blind the eyes of the wise; like a muzzle on the mouth they stop reproofs. ^20-29

**20:30** Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure—of what value is either? ^20-30

**20:31** Better are those who hide their folly than those who hide their wisdom.^[Other ancient authorities add 20.32: *Unwearied endurance in seeking the Lord is better than a masterless charioteer of one’s own life.*] ^20-31
