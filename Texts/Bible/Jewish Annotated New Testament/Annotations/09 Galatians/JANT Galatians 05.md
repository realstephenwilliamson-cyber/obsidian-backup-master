# Galatians 5 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Galatians 04|← Galatians 4]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Galatians 06|Galatians 6 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Galatians 05|NRSVue]] · [[Galatians 05|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Galatians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-1|5.1]]:** *Yoke of slavery*, Torah observance. See [[Acts 15#^15-10|Acts 15.10]]. Rabbinic literature too occasionally refers to Torah piety as the “yoke of the commandments” (*m. Ber.* 2.2; *Sifra* on [[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-43|Lev 11.43]]) or “the yoke of the kingdom of heaven” (*m. Ber.* 2.2; *b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 14#14b|14b]]), but for the rabbis this rhetoric serves to justify not the rejection of the commandments, as in Paul, but their affirmation. By observing the commandments we find favor with God. “A person is truly free only when he busies himself with the study of Torah,” says *m. Avot* 6.2. See 2.4n. ^5-1

### 5.2–12: Resisting circumcision

*[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-2|Text]], p. 384* · Paul asserts, but does not explain, that seeking righteousness through the Law, by means of circumcision, takes away the benefit of salvation through Christ. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-2|2]]:** *I, Paul*, emphatic ([[2 Corinthians 10#^10-1|2 Cor 10.1]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-18|1 Thess 2.18]]). ^5-2-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-3|3]]:** *Obliged to obey the entire law*, see 6.13n*.* ^5-3

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-4|4]]:** *Have cut yourselves*, this bad pun is absent from the Greek; lit., “you have become estranged,” or “you have been released from an association.” ^5-4

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-6|6]]:** *Circumcision nor uncircumcision* (lit., “foreskinhood,” see 2.7n.), which does not contradict 5.2. To those who are not circumcised, Paul preaches that they should not accept circumcision; to those who are already circumcised, Paul preaches that they should not remove the marks of circumcision (see 6.15 and [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-18|1 Cor 7.18]]**–**19). Removing the marks of circumcision, a procedure known as epispasm, involves drawing down the remains of the foreskin in order to give the circumcised organ the appearance of never having been circumcised. *Faith working through love*, faith is a main topic of this epistle, but love is not, although it is significant in some of Paul’s other writings (most notably, 1 Cor 13). Love (Gk “agape”*)* here anticipates 5.13–14,22 and echoes 2.20. The phrase appears to mean “faith/trust/confidence in God/Christ, made effective by love of God/Christ.” ^5-6

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-7|7]]:** *Running*, see 2.2. ^5-7

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-8|8]]:** *One who calls*, God, see 1.6n. ^5-8

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-9|9]]:** [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-6|1 Cor 5.6]] records the same proverb. Agents of change can be effective even if small. Since leaven can be a symbol of corruption ([[Matthew 16#^16-5|Mt 16.5–12]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-7|1 Cor 5.7]]; *b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 17#17a|17a]]), the proverb might have a negative valence. However, by itself the proverb is neutral. Rabbinic sages also observed that a little bit of leaven goes a long way (*b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 34#34a|34a]]). ^5-9

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-11|11]]:** *Still being persecuted* by fellow Jews (see 6.12n.). *If I am still preaching circumcision?* Apparently Paul’s opponents alleged that he did not really oppose circumcision, and that therefore the Galatian believers in Christ should become circumcised if they wished to join God’s people. *Offense* (Gk “skandalon”), lit., “obstacle, stumbling block.” Jesus’ crucifixion is an offense to Torah observant Jews, whether or not they are believers in Christ, because the crucifixion of Jesus meant for Paul that Jews could associate freely with Gentiles; were the Galatians to submit to circumcision/the Law, then the offense to Jews would end. See [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-23|1 Cor 1.23]]. It is possible that the “stumbling block” is the belief that the Messiah died on a cross, but that explanation does not seem to fit the flow of the passage. ^5-11

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-12|12]]:** *Would castrate themselves*, lit., “cut off,” “mutilate.” See [[Philippians 03#^3-2|Phil 3.2]]. The Romans sometimes regarded circumcision as a kind of castration; one cause of the Bar Kochba rebellion (132–135 CE) was a Roman ban against Jewish circumcision, an extension of a ban against castration (*Historia Augusta, “*Vita Hadriani” 14.2). ^5-12

### 5.13–15: Summarizing Torah

*[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-13|Text]], p. 385* · Although not under the Law, the Galatians should not abuse that freedom by yielding to temptation or strife. ^5-13

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Summed up*, lit., “fulfilled,” see [[Romans 08#^8-4|Rom 8.4]]. *Love your neighbor as yourself*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], see also [[Romans 13#^13-8|Rom 13.8–10]]; Paul does not seem to know that Jesus is reputed to have said something very similar ([[Matthew 22#^22-34|Mt 22.34–40]]). Rabbinic literature attributes analogous sentiments to Hillel (*b. Shabb.* [[Shabbat 31#31a|31a]]) and to R. Akiva (*Sifra Qedoshim* 2, *Gen. Rab.* 24) (see “The Concept of Neighbor,” p. 645). ^5-14

### 5.16–26: Living by the Spirit

*[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-16|Text]], p. 385* · On *flesh* vs. *Spirit* see 3.3n. ^5-16

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-18|18]]:** *Subject to the law*, see 4.21n. ^5-18

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-19|19–21]]:** Vice lists appear in the Gospels ([[Matthew 15#^15-19|Mt 15.19]]; [[Mark 07#^7-21|Mk 7.21–22]]) and in Paul’s letters ([[Romans 01#^1-29|Rom 1.29]]**–**31; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|1 Cor 6.9]]**–**10; [[2 Corinthians 12#^12-20|2 Cor 12.20]]). See *m. Avot* 2.7 (virtues and vices). ^5-19

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-21|21]]:** *Kingdom of God*, [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-50|1 Cor 15.50]] suggests that this is an eschatological idea, see [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|1 Cor 6.9]]**–**10; [[Romans 14#^14-17|Rom 14.17]] suggests that the kingdom presently exists. In rabbinic usage “kingdom of heaven” (Heb “malkhut shamayim”) is not eschatological but refers to the acknowledgement of God’s suzerainty in this world; see 5.1n. ^5-21

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-22|22–23]]:** Catalogue of virtues (see 5.19n.), [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-6|2 Cor 6.6]]**–**7; Phil 4.8; 3.17; 6.6. ^5-22

**[[NRSVue Galatians 05#^5-24|24]]:** *Crucified the flesh*, by identifying with Christ believers participate in his crucifixion (see 2.19n.), which in turn represents the mortification of the body (see 6.14; [[Romans 08#^8-13|Rom 8.13]]). Philo interpreted circumcision as “the excision of excessive and superfluous pleasure” (*Spec. Laws* 1.1**–**11*);* For Paul the mortification of the flesh comes not from circumcision, which is no longer necessary, at least for Gentiles, but from identification with the crucified Christ. ^5-24
