### 1.1–2.13: Salutation and statement of Paul’s bond with the Corinthians

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 353* ^1-1

### 1.1–2: Salutation,

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 353* · a greeting formula ([[1 Corinthians 01#^1-1|1 Cor 1.1]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-1|Gal 1.1]]). ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Apostle*, “Messenger” or “ambassador,” equivalent in meaning to the rabbinic “Shaliaḥ,” an “agent.” According to *m. Ber.* 5.5, “a person’s agent is as himself.” Paul was appointed *by* (lit., “through”) *the will of God*. Paul’s status resembles that of Hebrew prophets, appointed directly by God. In contrast, in rabbinic Judaism, the status of rabbi is a product of an individual’s study and personal commitment, a process which anyone can (and is encouraged to) pursue. *Timothy*, with Silvanus, accompanied Paul in founding the Corinthian congregation (v 19; [[Acts 18#^18-5|Acts 18.5]]) and acts as Paul’s representative ([[1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|1 Cor 4.17]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-10|16.10–11]]; see [[Acts 19#^19-22|Acts 19.22]]). *Brother*, a title of respect used, though infrequently, by rabbis; e.g., *b. B. Metz*. 107a. *Saints*, lit., “holy ones,” i.e., followers of Christ (see 13.12n). *Achaia*, Roman senatorial province of which *Corinth* was the capital. ^1-1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Grace to you and peace . . .* , standard Pauline greeting (except [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]) and familiar from Jewish sources, in which greetings of peace are common (e.g., *b. Ber*. 3a, where Rabbi Yose is said to greet the prophet Elijah with tidings of peace, which Elijah returns). *Father*, as a term of reference for God is commonplace in Jewish sources (e.g., 1QH 15, 20–24; 4Q372; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 06#^6-2|3 Macc 6.2–3]]; Philo, *Leg. all*. 114–15, 292–93; *m. Yoma* 8.9; *b. Taʾan*. 25b), though much less frequent in the Hebrew Bible (e.g., [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-6|Deut 32.6]]). See 1.3–7n. on “Father of mercies.” ^1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-3|1.3–7]]:** **Opening blessings.** Paul stresses his relationship with the recipients (e.g., [[Philippians 01#^1-3|Phil 1.3–11]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-2|1 Thess 1.2–10]]) by a blessing emphasizing God’s mercy and compassion ([[Romans 12#^12-1|Rom 12.1]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-1|Phil 2.1]]). These divine traits appear especially in Psalms (e.g., 86.15) and prominently in Jewish liturgy, which refers to God as “Father of mercies” (Heb “av ha-raḥamim”) in the prayer for the rebuilding of Jerusalem based on [[Psalms 051#^51-20|Ps 51.18]], recited on Sabbath and festival mornings. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-3|3–4]]:** *God of all consolation*, the phrase does not appear in early Jewish sources, but God as a consoling figure does (e.g., *b. Ber*. 48b). ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Sufferings of Christ*, *b. Sukk*. 52a refers to a Messiah ben Joseph, who dies prior to the Davidic messiah’s arrival. R. Dosa (active in the period of the destruction of the Second Temple) says that this event is reflected in [[Zechariah 12#^12-10|Zech 12.10–12]]’s prophecy of Jerusalem grieving as for a firstborn son. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Afflicted*, see 12.10. The nature of this affliction is unclear. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-8|1.8–11]]:** **God delivers Paul.** ^1-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-8|8–9]]:** *Asia*, Roman senatorial province (modern western Turkey) that included Ephesus. *Unbearably crushed . . . sentence of death*, the specific ordeal is unclear. See [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-32|1 Cor 15.32]] and [[Philippians 01#^1-19|Phil 1.19–24]]. While Paul’s expectation for resurrection is shaped by his experience of Jesus’ resurrection, his language follows Jewish liturgical formulas. *We would rely not on ourselves* resembles the early Aram prayer preserved in *Zohar*, *VeYakhel* 206a, accompanying, in later Jewish practice, the removal of the Torah scroll from the ark on the Sabbath (“I am a servant of the Holy One . . . Not on mortals do I rely . . . but on the God of the universe”). *God who raises the dead*, a Jewish commonplace, e.g., the second benediction of the “Amidah,” which recognizes God’s power to resurrect the dead (*m. Sot*. 9.15; *m. Sanh*. 10.1; cf. [[Isaiah 26#^26-19|Isa 26.19]]). ^1-8-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Deadly peril*, see 1.8–9n. *On him we have set our hope*, the same wording appears in the Aram prayer cited in 1.8–9n. ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Your prayers*, praying for the welfare of others, especially those in peril, is well established in the Jewish liturgy (e.g., the daily Morning Blessings, which recognize God, among other things, for releasing captives and raising the downtrodden). ^1-11

### 1.12–14: Paul’s conduct is a basis of solidarity

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-12|Text]], p. 354* ^1-12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Boast*, or confidence, a Pauline theme (e.g., [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-19|1 Thess 2.19]]) especially in 1 and 2 Corinthians. *Conscience*, the term has no Hebrew equivalent but the biblical Hebrew expression “his heart smites him,” e.g., [[I Samuel 24#^24-6|1 Sam 24.5]], which appears in the Talmud as well, *b. Ber*. 7b and *b. Meg.* 6b, suggests the same idea. *Earthly wisdom*, considerations of personal gain, contrasted with the truth that emerges when one displays *frankness* and *sincerity*. Paul insists that such truth is gained through God’s *grace*, an idea roughly equivalent to the Hebrew concepts “ḥen” or “ḥesed” which, in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Psalms 031#^31-17|Ps 31.16]],[[Psalms 031#^31-22|21]] \[HB vv 17,22\]) and later Jewish texts (e.g., *Gen. Rab*. 33.3; 78.13; *Ex. Rab*. 12.2), refer to God’s compassionate kindness even toward those who do not merit it. ^1-12-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Until the end* can mean “completely,” in contrast to “in part” in 1.14. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Day of the Lord*, Jesus’ return ([[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|1 Cor 5.5]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-6|Phil 1.6]],[[Philippians 01#^1-10|10]]). In contrast, the phrase in Amos (e.g., 5.18–20) and in the rest of the Tanakh typically evokes fear of judgment, not the welcome of vindication. *Boast*, a common idea, especially in Psalms, e.g., 44.8: “In God we have boasted continually, and we will give thanks to your name forever”; more often the term is used negatively, e.g., 75.4: “I say to the boastful, ‘Do not boast,’ and to the wicked, ‘Do not lift up your horn.’” ^1-14

### 1.15–2.4: Paul responds to misunderstandings caused by his canceled visit

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-15|Text]], p. 354* ^1-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Macedonia*, Roman province in northern Greece that included Philippi and Thessalonica. *Send me on* (Gk “propempein”), a technical term for provisioning ([[Romans 15#^15-24|Rom 15.24]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-6|1 Cor 16.6]],[[1 Corinthians 16#^16-11|11]]; cf. [[Acts 15#^15-3|Acts 15.3]]; [[Acts 20#^20-38|20.38]]; [[Acts 21#^21-5|21.5]]; [[Titus 03#^3-13|Titus 3.13]]; 3 Jn 6), including finance, escorts, and letters of recommendation. *Judea*, especially Jerusalem. The visit relates to deliv-ering the collection for the poor ([[2 Corinthians 08#^8-1|2 Cor 8.1]]–9.15nn.; [[Romans 15#^15-25|Rom 15.25–26]]). The Talmud depicts rabbis who traveled from Israel to the Babylonian academies to present legal rulings (e.g., *b. Ber*. 22a). ^1-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Vacillating*, lit., “trifling.” Paul defends against charges that he and his co-workers changed their minds frivolously and thus according to *human standards* of expedience or equivocation. **18**: *God is faithful*, God’s faithfulness (Heb “ḥesed”), especially to covenantal promises, features prominently in Jewish sources. At [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]], e.g., God is “abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness.” For example, the second benediction of the “Ami- dah” asserts God’s faithfulness in sustaining all living creatures and resurrecting the dead. *Yes and No*, indicating equivocation; see [[Matthew 05#^5-37|Mt 5.37]]; [[James 05#^5-12|Jas 5.12]]. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Son of God*, denoting Jesus’ divine nature. In Jewish texts, the phrase refers to the people of Israel ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-22|Ex 4.22]]; [[Jeremiah 31#^31-20|Jer 31.20]]; [[Hosea 11#^11-1|Hos 11.1]]), David ([[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-27|89.26–27]]), and especially in the Dead Sea Scrolls ( e.g., 4Q246; *Aram. Apoc*. 50.9–51.1), the Davidic messiah. In postbiblical Judaism, the term commonly applies to the just ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 02#^2-17|Wis 2.17–18]]; [[LXX Sirach 04#^4-10|Sir 4.10]]; *Jub*. 1.24–25). *Silvanus*, or Silas, Paul’s companion ([[Acts 15#^15-40|Acts 15.40–18.5]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]; [[2 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|2 Thess 1.1]]); see 1.1n. ^1-19

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Amen*, Heb, “certainly” or “let it be so”; an affirmation or response to a prayer; see e.g., [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-14|Deut 27.14–26]]; [[Nehemiah 05#^5-13|Neh 5.13]]; [[Nehemiah 08#^8-6|8.6]]; [[I Chronicles 16#^16-36|1 Chr 16.36]]. The rabbis (*b. Shabb*. 119b; *b. Sanh*. 111a) understand the consonants in “amen” to be an acronym for the declaration, “God, faithful king,” a declaration individuals make before reciting the Shema when praying apart from a prayer quorum (“minyan”). *Glory of God*, common idiom referring to God’s earthly manifestation (e.g., [[Isaiah 06#^6-3|Isa 6.3]]). ^1-20

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Anointed*, in the Tanakh, a commission, especially kingship or priesthood (e.g., [[I Samuel 24#^24-7|1 Sam 24.6]]). In postbiblical Judaism, “anointing” develops eschatological significance (*4 Ezra* 12 \[2 Esd\]; *2 Bar*. 29; 30; 72). In the Tanakh (e.g., [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^28-41|Ex 28.41]]), followed by later Talmudic sources (e.g., *b. Hor*. 7a), the high priest is anointed; here the term (Gk “chrisas”) lacks messianic connotations. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Seal*, indicating God’s permanent ownership of us; see [[Jeremiah 32#^32-9|Jer 32.9–15]], [[Song of Songs 8#^8-6|Song 8.6]]. *Giving us his Spirit* . . . *first installment* (the Gk for “first installment,” “arrabona,” is translated “guarantee” in 5.5; it connotes “down payment”) proves that God’s promises will be fulfilled (v 20). ^1-22

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-23|23]]:** *Call on God as witness*, see [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^31-50|Gen 31.50]]; [[Micah 1#^1-2|Mic 1.2]]. *I did not come again,* the canceled double visit (1.15–16). ^1-23

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 01#^1-24|24]]:** *Workers*, Paul is a partner, not an overseer or manager. The metaphor of *workers* in faith does not appear in early Judaism. *Joy*, Psalms and Isaiah especially depict knowledge of and closeness to God as *joy* (e.g., [[Psalms 043#^43-3|Ps 43.3]]; [[Isaiah 35#^35-10|Isa 35.10]]; [[Isaiah 51#^51-11|51.11]]). ^1-24
