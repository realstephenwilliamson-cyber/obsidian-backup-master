## Mūla

तस्मिञ्जिगमिषोरस्य यत्सव्यं तत्तु दक्षिणम् ।
तत्रैष चण्डतेजोभिर्भाति जाज्वल्यमानवत् ॥ ^mula

tasmiñjigamiṣorasya yatsavyaṃ tattu dakṣiṇam |
tatraiṣa caṇḍatejobhirbhāti jājvalyamānavat || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VIII
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 08 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v08 05 - Chapter Fifteen, part 4#^pg149|p. 131]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 08.pdf#page=149>))
>
> In same way, during an equinox, (the direction) from which (the sun) is visible initially (pūrva) is the east (pūrva). Where its shade is (located) is said to be the west (behind). (The place to) the right of (the sun), that intends to go into that (western quarter), is the south. It shines there with (its most) fierce radiant energies (tejas), like an intensely burning (fire). The north is in front of that. (There) its light is in brilliant patches (khacita) and is mild. Thus, it is pleasing (somya) and not (entirely) not illuminating. The place where it (finally) sets is the west. It is there itself where those who are in the west see the light (of the sun emerge) first (prāk). This is for them the east (pūrva), as I have already explained in (the chapter dealing with the) Path (of the Worlds). (with 15.213–217ab)
