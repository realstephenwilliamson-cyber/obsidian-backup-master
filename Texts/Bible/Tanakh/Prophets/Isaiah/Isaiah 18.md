## Chapter 18

**18:1** Ah,  
land in the deep shadow of wings,[^1]  
Beyond the rivers of Nubia! ^18-1

**18:2** Go, swift messengers,  
To a nation far and remote,  
To a people thrust forth and away[^2]—  
A nation of gibber and chatter[^3]—  
Whose land is cut off by streams;  
Which sends out envoys by sea,  
In papyrus vessels upon the water![^4] ^18-2

**18:3** [Say this:]  
“All you who live in the world  
And inhabit the earth,  
When a flag is raised in the hills, take note!  
When a ram’s horn is blown, give heed!” ^18-3

**18:4** For thus the LORD said to me:  
“I rest calm and confident[^5] in My habitation—  
Like a scorching heat upon sprouts,  
Like a rain-cloud in the heat of reaping time.”[^6] ^18-4

**18:5** For before the harvest,[^7] yet after the budding,  
When the blossom has hardened into berries,  
He will trim away the twigs with pruning hooks,  
And lop off the trailing branches.[^8] ^18-5

**18:6** They shall all be left  
To the kites of the hills  
And to the beasts of the earth;  
The kites shall summer on them  
And all the beasts of the earth shall winter on them. ^18-6

**18:7** In that time,  
Tribute shall be brought to the LORD of Hosts  
[From] a people far and remote,  
From a people thrust forth and away—  
A nation of gibber and chatter,  
Whose land is cut off by streams—  
At the place where the name of the LORD of Hosts abides,  
At Mount Zion. ^18-7

[^1]: Or “Most sheltered land”; cf., e.g., 30.2, 3; Ps. 36.8; 57.2; 61.5.
[^2]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; cf. 28.10. Biblical writers often characterize distant nations by their unintelligible speech; cf. 33.19; Deut. 28.49; Jer. 5.15.
[^4]: Brought down from beginning of verse for clarity. The Hebrew verb for “sends” agrees in gender with “nation,” not with “land.”
[^5]: Cf. hibbit “to rely” (Job 6.19). The related noun mabbat· occurs with similar meaning in Isa. 20.5, 6.
[^6]: I.e., like a threat of disaster; cf. Eccl. 11.4.
[^7]: Emendation yields “vintage.”
[^8]: A figure of speech for the defeated enemy.
