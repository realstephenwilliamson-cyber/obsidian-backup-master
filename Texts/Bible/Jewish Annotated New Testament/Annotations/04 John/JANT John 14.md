**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-7|7–11]]:** To “see” Jesus is not a visual experience, but one of personal knowledge; therefore to know Jesus and his works is to understand and know God. ^14-7

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-12|12–14]]:** *Works*, not just “signs” but Jesus’ willingness to offer his life (15.13). *In my name*, in accordance with my true character. ^14-12

> [!note] “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (JN 14.6) *(p. 207)*
> The passage emphasizes a point made throughout the Gospel: the only way to be in covenantal relationship with God is through faith in Jesus as God’s son and Israel’s Messiah. The passage therefore functions in both an inclusive and an exclusionary way: it includes all Christ-confessors but also emphasizes that those who are not Christ-confessors are excluded from a relationship with God. Other passages that emphasize this point include the “love commandments” (13.34; 14.15,21; 15.12–17). Such statements strongly affirm the Gospel’s belief in Jesus’ central and essential role in God’s plan of salvation; no other way to salvation exists for this Gospel. These statements also emphasize the solidarity of the group united in this belief, over against all those who do not share this belief.

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-16|16–17]]:** *Another Advocate* (Gk “parakletos,” “one who stands beside,” a supporter or comforter), who is the *Spirit of truth*. Although Jesus is not called a paraclete or advocate in this Gospel, he serves this function in that he mediates the relationship between believers and God. This passage influenced Christian thought about the nature and role of the Holy Spirit. ^14-16

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-18|18]]:** *Orphaned*, a reference to bereave-ment in general, though it may allude to Jesus’ earlier reference to the disciples as “little children” (13.33). ^14-18

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-20|20]]:** *That day*, at the end of days. John’s Gospel suggests that Jesus is equivalent to God (10.30) and that Jesus is God’s son and agent (6.38). ^14-20

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-21|21]]:** *Be loved by my Father*, God’s love for Israel is expressed in numerous sources, e.g. [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-6|Deut 23.5]]; [[I Chronicles 17#^17-13|1 Chr 17.13]], and the entire Song of Solomon is read as a love song between God and Israel (e.g., *Song Rab.* ). ^14-21

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-22|22]]:** *Judas*, see [[Luke 06#^6-16|Lk 6.16]]; [[Acts 01#^1-13|Acts 1.13]]. ^14-22

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-27|27]]:** *Peace*, perhaps a standard greeting; see [[I Kings 01#^1-27|1 Kings 1.27]]; [[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]. ^14-27

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-30|30]]:** *Ruler of this world*, Satan (12.31; 16.11). For John’s Gospel, the Jews, who plotted Jesus’ death, are Satan’s agents (8.44). ^14-30

**[[NRSVue John 14#^14-31|31]]:** *Let us be on our way*, the discourse seems to be at an end, yet Jesus continues to speak for three more chapters. Jesus and his followers do not leave until 18.1, perhaps indicating that two or more sources have been combined in these Last Supper discourses. ^14-31
