# Ecclesiastes 08

## Chapter 8

**8:1** Who is like the wise man,[^1] and who knows the meaning of the adage:  
“A man’s wisdom lights up his face,  
So that his deep discontent[^2] is dissembled”? ^8-1

**8:2** I do! “Obey the king’s orders—and don’t rush[^3] into uttering an oath by God.”[^4] ^8-2

**8:3** Leave his presence; do not tarry[^5] in a dangerous situation, for he can do anything he pleases; ^8-3

**8:4** inasmuch as a king’s command is authoritative, and none can say to him, “What are you doing?” ^8-4

**8:5** One who obeys orders will not suffer from the dangerous situation.  
A wise man, however, will bear in mind[^6] that there is a time of doom.[^7] ^8-5

**8:6** For there is a time for every experience, including the doom; for a man’s calamity[^8] overwhelms him. ^8-6

**8:7** Indeed, he does not know what is to happen; even when it is on the point of happening, who can tell him? ^8-7

**8:8** No man has authority over the lifebreath—to hold back the lifebreath;[^9] there is no authority over the day of death. There is no mustering out from that war; wickedness[^10] is powerless to save its owner. ^8-8

**8:9** All these things I observed; I noted all that went on under the sun, while men still had authority over men to treat them unjustly. ^8-9

**8:10** And then I saw scoundrels coming from the Holy Site and being brought to burial,[^11] while such as had acted righteously were forgotten in the city.  
And here is another frustration: ^8-10

**8:11** the fact that the sentence imposed for evil deeds is not executed swiftly, which is why men are emboldened to do evil— ^8-11

**8:12** the fact that a sinner may do evil a hundred times and his [punishment] still be delayed. For although I am aware that “It will be well with those who revere God since they revere Him, ^8-12

**8:13** and it will not be well with the scoundrel, and he will not live long, because[^12] he does not revere God”— ^8-13

**8:14** here is a frustration that occurs in the world: sometimes an upright man is requited according to the conduct of the scoundrel; and sometimes the scoundrel is requited according to the conduct of the upright. I say all that is frustration. ^8-14

**8:15** I therefore praised enjoyment. For the only good a man can have under the sun is to eat and drink and enjoy himself. That much can accompany him, in exchange for his wealth, through the days of life that God has granted him under the sun. ^8-15

**8:16** For I have set my mind to learn wisdom and to observe the business that goes on in the world—even to the extent of going without sleep day and night— ^8-16

**8:17** and I have observed all that God brings to pass. Indeed, man cannot guess the events that occur under the sun. For man tries strenuously, but fails to guess them; and even if a sage should think to discover them he would not be able to guess them. ^8-17

[^1]: Some ancient versions read “Who here is wise.”
[^2]: Lit. “face”; cf. 1 Sam. 1.18; Job 9.27.
[^3]: Moved up from v. 3 for English word order.
[^4]: The answer to the inquiry about the implications of the proverb in v. 1 is given in the form of another proverb, of which only the first half is relevant and is enlarged upon.
[^5]: Or “Give ground before him; do not resist.”
[^6]: The same idiom as in 7.22.
[^7]: Lit. “time and doom”; cf. the synonymous “time of misfortune,” lit. “time and misfortune,” 9.11.
[^8]: Still another term for death; cf. “the time of calamity” for “the hour of death,” 9.12.
[^9]: From leaving the body when the time comes; see 12.7; cf. Ps. 104.29; 146.4.
[^10]: Emendation yields “riches.”
[^11]: Meaning uncertain; emendation yields “approaching [to minister]. They would come and profane the Holy Site.”
[^12]: See note on 6.12.
