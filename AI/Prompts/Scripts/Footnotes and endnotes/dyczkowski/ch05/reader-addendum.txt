5. CARRY-OVERS. Chapter Five's footnotes are often long: many run over one or more pages, and some pages hold nothing but
   footnote text (e.g. PDF 65-68, 72, 97, 185, 203). A carried-over part is plain text at the top of the next page's block
   (before that page's first definition, or the page's only text). Join each paragraph of it with a move op (brief, op 4):
   `after` = the last line of that footnote's text before it (its definition line, its last same-page continuation, or the
   line of the previous carried paragraph when it runs over several pages: chains are allowed; several paragraphs moved after
   the same line keep their order). Body text (the translation and commentary) that continues on the next page is NOT moved.
6. PARAGRAPHS OF A FOOTNOTE. Same-page further paragraphs of a footnote (verse lines, quotations, numbered lists such as
   "1) The Emptiness ..." or "1. Joy ...", new paragraphs) that are not indented: indent them (op 3). Unnumbered asterisk notes
   (*, **, ***, e.g. on PDF 74, 121, 122, 142, 166) are paragraphs of the footnote they stand in or under (brief). Body text
   the OCR put among the footnotes, or footnote text inside a body line: report, don't fix.
7. YOUR PAGES ONLY. Other readers have the other pages of the same note.txt. Write ops only for lines that lie on your pages
   (from your first page's marker line to the line before the marker of the page after your last). A move op's `after` may
   point to a line before your range (a footnote carried over from the page before yours). A call may stand on the page
   before its footnote: if that page is outside your range, still write the call op (it is the one op allowed outside your
   range) and say so in problems.
