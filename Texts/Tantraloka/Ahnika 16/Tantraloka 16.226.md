## Mūla

अन्यप्रकरणोक्तं यद्युक्तं प्रकरणान्तरे ।
ज्ञापकत्वेन साक्षाद्वा तत्किं नान्यत्र गृह्यते ॥ ^mula

anyaprakaraṇoktaṃ yadyuktaṃ prakaraṇāntare |
jñāpakatvena sākṣādvā tatkiṃ nānyatra gṛhyate || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 07 - Chapter Sixteen, part 3#^pg118|p. 98]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=118>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 16.225cd–226ab.
>
> Although that (is taught there) in order to purify the body, (even so, that deposition) is in actual fact (basically) the same as this (kind of deposition that we are concerned with here). A thing said in a (particular) context (may well be) sound in a different context as suggesting (something else,) or even directly (normative). So why should that not be accepted elsewhere (in a different context)? (with 16.225cd–226)
