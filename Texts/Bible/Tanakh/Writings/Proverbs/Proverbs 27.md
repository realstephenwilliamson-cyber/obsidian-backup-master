# Proverbs 27

← [[Proverbs 26]] · [[Tanakh]] · [[Proverbs 28]] →

Compare: [[NRSVue Proverbs 27|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 27

**27:1** Do not boast of tomorrow,  
For you do not know what the day will bring. ^27-1

**27:2** Let the mouth of another praise you, not yours,  
The lips of a stranger, not your own. ^27-2

**27:3** A stone has weight, sand is heavy,  
But a fool’s vexation outweighs them both. ^27-3

**27:4** There is the cruelty of fury, the overflowing of anger,  
But who can withstand jealousy? ^27-4

**27:5** Open reproof is better than concealed love. ^27-5

**27:6** Wounds by a loved one are long lasting;  
The kisses of an enemy are profuse. ^27-6

**27:7** A sated person disdains honey,  
But to a hungry man anything bitter seems sweet. ^27-7

**27:8** Like a sparrow wandering from its nest  
Is a man who wanders from his home. ^27-8

**27:9** Oil and incense gladden the heart,  
And the sweetness of a friend is better than one’s own counsel. ^27-9

**27:10** Do not desert your friend and your father’s friend;  
Do not enter your brother’s house in your time of misfortune;  
A close neighbor is better than a distant brother. ^27-10

**27:11** Get wisdom, my son, and gladden my heart,  
That I may have what to answer those who taunt me. ^27-11

**27:12** The shrewd man saw trouble and took cover;  
The simple kept going and paid the penalty. ^27-12

**27:13** Seize his garment, for he stood surety for another;[^1]  
Take it as a pledge, [for he stood surety] for an unfamiliar woman. ^27-13

**27:14** He who greets his fellow loudly early in the morning  
Shall have it reckoned to him as a curse. ^27-14

**27:15** An endless dripping on a rainy day  
And a contentious wife are alike; ^27-15

**27:16** As soon repress her as repress the wind,  
Or declare one’s right hand to be oil. ^27-16

**27:17** As iron sharpens iron  
So a man sharpens the wit[^2] of his friend. ^27-17

**27:18** He who tends a fig tree will enjoy its fruit,  
And he who cares for his master will be honored. ^27-18

**27:19** As face answers to face in water,  
So does one man’s heart to another. ^27-19

**27:20** Sheol and Abaddon cannot be satisfied,  
Nor can the eyes of man be satisfied. ^27-20

**27:21** For silver—the crucible, for gold—the furnace,  
And a man is tested by his praise. ^27-21

**27:22** Even if you pound the fool in a mortar  
With a pestle along with grain,  
His folly will not leave him. ^27-22

**27:23** Mind well the looks of your flock;  
Pay attention to your herds; ^27-23

**27:24** For property does not last forever,  
Or a crown for all generations. ^27-24

**27:25** Grass vanishes, new grass appears,  
And the herbage of the hills is gathered in. ^27-25

**27:26** The lambs will provide you with clothing,  
The he-goats, the price of a field. ^27-26

**27:27** The goats’ milk will suffice for your food,  
The food of your household,  
And the maintenance of your maids. ^27-27

[^1]: Or “a stranger.”
[^2]: Lit. “face.”
