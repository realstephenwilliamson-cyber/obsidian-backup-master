# Zohar 3.016a

← [[Zohar 3.015b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 3.016b]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 7.03 - Parashat Va-Yiqra#^z3-16a|Parashat Va-Yiqra, at 3:16a]]

*Volume 3, folio 16a* · Vayikra

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayikra 35:258–36:263.*

**Vayikra 37:264** [^1]R. Abba was once sitting before R. Simeon, when R. Eleazar entered. R. Simeon thereupon quoted the verse: “The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree, etc.” (Ps. 92, 12). ‘The palm’, he said, ‘is the slowest of all trees to mature, taking seventy years. The reason why the righteous is compared here to the palm tree is one which the Companions are reluctant to reveal, although it is indicated by the Scripture. ^vayikra-37-264

**Vayikra 37:265** It has to do with the exile f Babylon, when the Shekinah did not return to its home till seventy years had passed. The “righteous” here is the Holy One, blessed be He, as in the verse, “For the Lord is righteous, he loveth righteousness” (Ps. 11, 8). ^vayikra-37-265

**Vayikra 37:266** He is also compared to a cedar, as in the verse, “Excellent as the cedars” (S.S. 15). “He shall grow in Lebanon”; this is the supernal Eden, of which it is written, “No eye hath seen, O God, save thine” (Isa. 64, 4). This Cedar shall grow in that exalted place; ^vayikra-37-266

**Vayikra 37:267** and this shall be in the last exile, when God shall be like that cedar which is long in coming up, but once it comes up grows in a day, and at the beginning of the next day already provides a shade against the sun. ^vayikra-37-267

**Vayikra 37:268** “They shall be planted in the house of the Lord”, at the time of the Messiah; “and flourish in the courts of our God, at the resurrection of the dead; “They shall still bring forth fruit in old age”, on the day when the world will be waste, “and they shall be full of sap and green” afterwards;[^2] and why all this? “To show that the Lord is upright, he is my rock and there is no unrighteousness in him.” ^vayikra-37-268

[^1]: The first 34 lines of the Hebrew text are untranslated as explained in the Translator’s note on Page 15b.
[^2]: Only the first 13 lines of the Hebrew text appear in the translation.
