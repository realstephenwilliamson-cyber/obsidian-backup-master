### 6.1–17: The first six seals

*[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 549* · In the interconnected world of heavenly symbols, the seals holding the scroll closed each release an angel of vengeance or catastrophe (reminiscent of those in [[Ezekiel 09#^9-2|Ezek 9.2]]), each linked to horses in a way that indicates the inspiration of Zech 6. ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Lamb*, see 5.6–7n. ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-2|2]]:** *White horse*, inspired by Zech 6, John introduces horses first, and by color (vv 2,4,5b,8a). Horses, associated with Greek and Roman armies, signified raw military power (*1 En*. 86.4; 88.3). The four horsemen represent conquest (v 2), internecine violence (v 4), famine and inflation (v 6), and death (v 8). ^6-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-5|5–6]]:** *Scales* and inflated prices signify economic breakdown. The famine is short lived; however, it affects *wheat* and *barley*. *Olive oil and the wine*, do not indicate luxury items but the protection of commodities that might lessen the bite of the famine, perhaps to confuse people. ^6-5

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-8|8]]:** *Death* (Gk “thanatos”), recalling Heb “Mot,” a northwest Semitic deity of death ([[Habakkuk 2#^2-5|Hab 2.5]]), is accompanied by *Hades*, the personification (as well as the place) of the Greek underworld. Their coupling may reflect the parallelism of “Mot” and “Sheol” in ancient Hebrew poetry ([[Isaiah 28#^28-15|Isa 28.15]],[[Isaiah 28#^28-18|18]]; [[Psalms 018#^18-6|Ps 18.5]]; [[Psalms 049#^49-15|49.14]]; 116.3). *With sword, famine, and pestilence*, a traditional prophetic formula for the manifestations of divine vengeance (e.g., [[Jeremiah 14#^14-12|Jer 14.12]]; [[Ezekiel 05#^5-12|Ezek 5.12]],[[Ezekiel 05#^5-17|17]]; [[Ezekiel 14#^14-21|14.21]]). ^6-8

> [!note] The Numerology of Revelation *(p. 550)*
> In antiquity certain numbers implied perfection in the cosmos or experience, and different cultures entertained different assortments of such perfect numbers. The Torah offers different types of numerological perfection in seven ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-1|Gen 2.1–3]]; [[Torah 02 - Noach#^7-2|7.2–4]]) and twelve (Gen 35), while four signified (as in most cultures) the cardinal directions, and forty conveyed an enormous but limited period.
>
> Apocalyptic authors regarded these numbers as divine in nature, part of the eternal structure of the heavenly world and linked intrinsically to God’s perfection. Enoch’s tours of the cosmos reveal four very different regions of activity (*1 En*. 17–36), and Ezekiel beheld four “living creatures” surrounding God’s throne, while many texts depicted four principal archangels and seven or twelve heavens (see “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682). The periodizations of history that some apocalypses offered to explain the alternation of fortune and misfortune in Jewish experiences usually unrolled according to some perfect number: 490 (= 7 x 70, Dan 9) or fourteen (= 12 + 1 + 1, *2 Bar.* 53–74). By the second century, some Christians envisioned their four most popular Gospels as expressing the perfection of the divine number four, even connecting them to Ezekiel’s four living creatures (Irenaeus, *Adv. Haer.* 3.11.8).
>
> Revelation is especially interested in the number seven: the main seven-fold entities consist of Temple materials (1.12) and angelic servants of the heavenly Temple (chs 8–9; 16), although this heavenly number is extended to the congregations to which the heavenly Christ, holding seven stars (1.16), addresses his letters (2–3) and to the number of seals on the mysterious scroll of judg- ment (6). Through numerology, the nature of the congregations becomes an intrinsic extension of the perfect order of the heavenly world.
>
> John also develops the symbolism of the number twelve, which signifies the special perfection of Jewish heritage (Gen 35), but probably had also acquired astrological associations by the Roman period (see [[Revelation 12#^12-1|Rev 12.1]]). The blessed of Israel are organized in twelve groups of twelve thousand each (equals 144,000; the word “thousand” signifies a sufficient enormity, 7.4–8; 12.1), and the mysterious “elders” John finds in heaven are themselves numbered at twenty-four (4.4). Recalling the perfection of measurements in Ezekiel (40–48) and the Qumran Temple Scroll (11QSTemple), John’s eschatological Jerusalem reflects the twelve-fold perfection of Israel (21.12–14,16–17).
>
> Revelation also offers three forms of “negative” numerology. The period designated for the rampage of the nations and the activity of the two martyrs in ch 11 is limited to forty-two months, precisely half of seven years and so an indication of incompleteness. The association of the dragon and the polymorphic beast with seven and ten (12.3; 13.1; 17.7–12) may indicate these creatures’ pretenses to holiness. On the other hand, the “number of the beast,” 666 (13.18), is merely the calculation of the numerical equivalents of the Hebrew letters that would spell “Nero Caesar.” This calculation reflects an ancient Jewish practice called “gematria”: exploring the mysteries of words through their corresponding numbers (on the assumption that Hebrew letters themselves had a divine origin). By itself, 666 (or 616 in some manuscripts) had no special numerological significance (see 13.18n.).

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-9|9]]:** Heavenly *altar*, a privileged sight in apocalyptic literature, reminiscent of apocalyptic topographies of the afterlife found in *1 En.* 22 and *Apoc. Zeph*. The slaughtered righteous constitute heavenly figures; this is not necessarily a reference to actual “martyrs” in John’s own world (it is unclear what *testimony* they give). Like early Christian martyrology, early Jewish martyrology (2 Macc 7; 4 Macc; the amoraic legend of the ten martyred rabbis \[*b. Avod. Zar.* 17b,18a; *b. Ber.* 61b; *Lam. Rab.* 2.2; and the much later *Midrash ‘Asarah Haruge Malkut*\]) stressed the specifics of injustice and pain; these are simply *slaughtered*. It is their *souls* that are visible, signifying their separation from their bodies. ^6-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-10|10]]:** The slaughtered righteous cry to *avenge our blood* (see *1 En.* 47; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 08#^8-3|2 Macc 8.3]]; *4 Ezra* 4.35), reminiscent of the blood of Abel in [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-10|Gen 4.10]]. ^6-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-11|11]]:** *White robe*, see 1.14n. *Number*, speculation on such divine plans for the number of righteous dead was common in Jewish and Christian apocalyptic literature (e.g., *1 En.* 47.4; *4 Ezra* 4.35–37; *2 Bar.* 23.5; see [[Romans 11#^11-25|Rom 11.25]]; *5 Ezra* \[2 Esd 1–2\] 2.38–41). ^6-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-12|12–14]]:** John envisions cosmic collapse in the wake of divine wrath according to a traditional series of motifs, from darkness, to the disappearance of land-forms, to conflagration, earthquakes, and mass-annihilation (see, e.g., [[Joel 3#^3-4|Joel 2.30–32]] \[HB 3.4\]; Isa 24–26). ^6-12

**[[NRSVue Revelation 06#^6-17|17]]:** People appeal to the rocks and mountains to conceal (and even bury them) from the *day of their wrath* (see [[Zephaniah 1#^1-14|Zeph 1.14–16]]). ^6-17
