### 3.1–5: Faith vs. works

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 379* ^3-1

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-1|1]]:** **The crucified Christ was preached by me before your very eyes—how could you have forgotten?** ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Receive the Spirit*, possession by the Holy Spirit, especially in relation to baptism ([[Acts 02#^2-38|Acts 2.38]]) or the laying on of hands ([[Acts 08#^8-15|Acts 8.15–16]]; [[Acts 19#^19-5|19.5–6]]), evinces the efficacy of faith ([[Acts 02#^2-4|Acts 2.4]]; [[Acts 10#^10-44|10.44]]). *By doing the works of the law or by believing what you heard*, lit., “by works of law or by hearing of faith” (also 3.5). ^3-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-3|3]]:** *Spirit . . . flesh*, an important contrast for Paul (4.29; 5.16–25; 6.8; [[Romans 02#^2-28|Rom 2.28–29]]; [[Romans 08#^8-4|8.4–17]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|1 Cor 5.5]]), but not common in either the Tanakh or rabbinic literature. *Flesh,* may allude to circumcision ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-13|Gen 17.13]]; [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^15-2|Lev 15.2]]). ^3-3

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-5|5]]:** *Miracles*, activity attesting to the Spirit’s presence. ^3-5

### 3.6–9: Abraham, the model of faith

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-6|Text]], p. 379* · 3.6–4.31 centers on Abraham. Paul’s opponents likely adduced Abraham, because the Torah declares circumcision an everlasting covenant between God and Abraham’s descendants; the Israelite uncircumcised are “cut off” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-9|Gen 17.9–14]]). ^3-6

> [!note] Neither Jew nor Greek *(p. 379)*
> According to the second-century ce historian Diogenes Laertius, Socrates (or some other Greek sage) said he had three blessings: “that I was born a human being and not a beast, a man and not a woman, a Greek and not a barbarian.” According to *t. Ber.* 6.18, R. Judah says that a man is obligated daily to recite: “Blessed is God who has not made me a Gentile, who has not made me a boor, who has not made me a woman.” *B. Men.* 43b–44a substitutes “slave” for “boor,” and these three separate blessings—“Blessed is God who has not made me a Gentile . *. .*  a slave . *. .*  a woman”—still appear in the Orthodox prayer book. (In fourteenth-century Spain the custom arose for women to recite “who has made me according to his will” instead of simply skipping “who has not made me a woman,” and this custom is still followed in most Orthodox circles.) These three benedictions have raised much controversy in modern times. Some Jewish movements have done away with them altogether, or have converted the negative into a positive: “Blessed is God who has made me an Israelite (or: Jew) . . . a free-person . . . a human being (or, in some versions: in his image).” Some modern Christians have tried to read Paul as a liberation theologian, as if Paul, anticipating the struggles of our time, was opposed to the power structures of his time. But this reading of Paul is most unlikely—Paul and the Pauline tradition explicitly counsel women to remain subject to their husbands ([[1 Corinthians 11#^11-3|1 Cor 11.3]]; [[1 Corinthians 14#^14-34|14.34–35]]; see [[Ephesians 05#^5-22|Eph 5.22–24]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-18|Col 3.18]]; [[1 Timothy 02#^2-12|1 Tim 2.12]]; [[Titus 02#^2-4|Titus 2.4–5]]; see also [[1 Peter 03#^3-1|1 Pet 3.1–5]]). Paul and the Pauline tradition similarly do not protest against slavery ([[Ephesians 06#^6-5|Eph 6.5]]**–**9; [[Colossians 04#^4-1|Col 4.1]]; [[1 Timothy 06#^6-1|1 Tim 6.1]]**–**3, and Philemon). The phrase in [[Galatians 03#^3-28|Gal 3.28]] recurs in somewhat different form in [[Romans 10#^10-12|Rom 10.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-13|1 Cor 12.13]]; and [[Colossians 03#^3-11|Col 3.11]], but the only distinction that appears in all four is that between Jew and Greek (Gentile), because the effacement of that distinction is the one that matters most to Paul. His argument is that while these distinctions persist in human society, they no longer matter in the eyes of God.

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-6|6]]:** [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]. Paul states that Abraham, even before his circumcision in Gen 17, was declared by God to be righteous through faith (or trust; see 2.16n.). Many Jewish texts of the Second Temple (e.g., *Jub*., Josephus) and the rabbinic (e.g., *Gen. Rab.* ) periods regard Abraham as the founder of the belief in the one true God, but none of these texts uses Abraham’s faith (or trust) as evidence that circumcision is not required of the males of God’s people. ^3-6-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-7|7]]:** Hence those *who believe* (or have trust) become *descendants* \[lit., “sons,” the better translation given the allusion to circumcision\] of Abraham (3.7), blessed with Abraham (3.9, cf. 3.14), and *Abraham’s offspring* (3.29). See also Rom 2–4. ^3-7

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Scripture* (Gk “graphê”), usually referring to a specific verse, corresponding to the rabbinic Heb “hakatuv,” “what is written.” *All the Gentiles shall be blessed in you* ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Gen 12.3]], see 18.18). The Heb is ambiguous. Paul understands *in you* to mean “through you”: through Abraham the nations attain God’s blessing. Jewish exegetes understand *in you* as “by you.” Rashi on [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Gen 12.3]] explains that even Gentiles will say “may God bless you as he blessed Abraham” (by you = by invoking you). *Gen. Rab.* on [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Gen 12.3]] explains that God tells Abraham that the blessings of nature will reach all humanity “by you,” i.e., “on your account.” ^3-8

### 3.10–14: The curse of the Law

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-10|Text]], p. 380* ^3-10

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-10|10]]:** *Cursed is everyone*, [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-26|Deut 27.26]]. Paul’s citation adds *the book*, not found in our Heb or the LXX. ^3-10-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-11|11]]:** *The one who is righteous*, the ambiguous Heb of [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]] (see [[Romans 01#^1-17|Rom 1.17]]) probably originally meant, “The one who is righteous shall live by his faith,” but Paul’s argument implies, “The one who is righteous by faith shall live.” For Paul, [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]] and [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]] prove righteousness is attained through faith (confidence, trust, 2.16n.), not “works of the law.” For the Talmud, [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]] demonstrates the opposite: the Torah contains 613 commandments, but Habakkuk concentrated them in one, “the righteous live by their faith” (*b. Makk.* 24a). ^3-11

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-12|12]]:** [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-5|Lev 18.5]]. ^3-12

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-13|13]]:** [[LXX Deuteronomy 21#^21-23|Deut 21.23]] \[LXX\], which Paul cites to create a parallel to [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-26|Deut. 27.26]] quoted in 3.10. [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-23|Deut 21.23]] speaks of hanging (exposing) the corpse of an executed criminal; for Paul “tree” means “cross,” and the verse speaks of the execution itself, i.e., crucifixion (11QSTemple and 4QpNahum also understand [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-23|Deut 21.23]] as referring to crucifixion). According to Paul the curse is pronounced against everyone, since no one can observe the entire Torah. See 6.13n. ^3-13

### 3.15–18: Covenant and Law

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-15|Text]], p. 380* · God’s *covenant* (Heb “berit,” Gk “diathêkê”) with Abraham (Gen 15 and 17) included a *promise* that he would be the progenitor of a mighty nation. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Has been ratified*, Mosaic Torah does not nullify the earlier Abrahamic covenant, see 3.17. On this point Paul and the later rabbis agree. ^3-15-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-16|16]]:** *Offspring* (Gk “sperma,” lit., “seed”), [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-7|Gen 12.7]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|15.5]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-8|17.8]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-17|22.17]]. In the context in Genesis, “seed” is a collective noun (grammatically sing.) referring to the people of Israel. Paul, however, reads the noun as a singular, referring to Christ. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-17|17]]:** [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-40|Ex 12.40]] places the Israelites in Egypt for *four hun- dred thirty years*. Paul, following the Septuagint, has those four hundred and thirty years begin with Abraham’s sojourn in Canaan. According to rabbinic chronology (*Seder Olam Rab.* 3) Israel’s sojourn in Egypt was only 210 years. ^3-17

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-18|18]]:** Paul posits a mutually exclusive relationship between the covenant, *the promise*, and *the inheritance* on one side, and *law* on the other. ^3-18

### 3.19–22: The purpose of the Law

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-19|Text]], p. 381* · See Rom 7. ^3-19

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-19|19]]:** *Because of transgressions*, the Law either restrains sin (3.23–24), brings knowledge of sin ([[Romans 03#^3-20|Rom 3.20]]; [[Romans 07#^7-7|7.7]]), or provokes sin ([[Romans 05#^5-20|Rom 5.20]]). In rabbinic Judaism none of these possibilities is true. The Torah exists to provide opportunities for Jews to observe God’s will. *Offspring*, see 3.16n. For *angels* having a role in the revelation of Torah see [[LXX Deuteronomy 33#^33-2|Deut 33.2]] LXX; [[Acts 07#^7-38|Acts 7.38]],[[Acts 07#^7-53|53]]; [[Hebrews 02#^2-2|Heb 2.2]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 15.136. Paul’s point is that Torah, ordained by angels, is of lesser status than Abraham’s covenant, which came from God. *By*, lit., “by the hand of”; perhaps a Hebraism ([[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-46|Lev 26.46]]; [[Torah 43 - Masei#^36-13|Num 36.13]]), perhaps to be taken literally ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-19|Ex 32.19]]). *Mediator*, Moses ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-5|Deut 5.5]]). Philo (*Life of Moses* 2.166) calls Moses a mediator. In [[1 Timothy 02#^2-5|1 Tim 2.5]]; [[Hebrews 08#^8-6|Heb 8.6]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-15|9.15]]; [[Hebrews 12#^12-24|12.24]], Jesus is the mediator. ^3-19-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-20|20]]:** *More than one party*, thus the Law did not come directly from God. ^3-20

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-21|21]]:** The logic of the argument implies that the Law is opposed to divine promises, but Paul is unwilling to draw this conclusion (see [[Romans 11#^11-1|Rom 11.1]]). ^3-21

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-21|21–22]]:** The Law was to imprison or confine *all things under the power of sin*, not to *make alive. Faith in Jesus Christ*, see 2.16n. ^3-21-2

### 3.23–29: Children of God through faith

*[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-23|Text]], p. 381* ^3-23

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-23|23]]:** *We*, humans, or perhaps, we Jews, since the Jews received the Law. ^3-23-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-24|24–25]]:** *Disciplinarian* (Gk “pedagogue”), a household slave charged with keeping the master’s son out of trouble and escorting him outside the house. Calling the Law a pedagogue indicates its temporary role and the immaturity of its wards. ^3-24

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-26|26]]:** *You,* Gentiles who believe in Christ*.* ^3-26

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-27|27]]:** *Were baptized into Christ*, immersion of converts to Judaism is not securely attested in pre-rabbinic texts, so there is much debate over whether Christian baptism of converts derives from Jewish practice. In Christian baptism, the convert is baptized “in” or “in the name of” Christ ([[Matthew 28#^28-19|Mt 28.19]]; [[Acts 08#^8-16|Acts 8.16]]; [[Acts 19#^19-3|19.3]],[[Acts 19#^19-5|5]]; [[Romans 06#^6-3|Rom 6.3]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-13|1 Cor 1.13]],[[1 Corinthians 01#^1-15|15]]); the Jewish conversion ritual has no baptizer and no “in the name of” language (*b. Yebam.* 47a-b). Verse 27 may indicate that v 28 is a pre-Pauline baptismal formula. *Clothed yourselves*, [[Romans 13#^13-14|Rom 13.14]]. ^3-27

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-28|28]]:** The law in general, and circumcision in particular, maintain social distinctions. See “Neither Jew nor Greek,” p. 379. *Male and female,* an allusion to [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-27|Gen 1.27]], hence not “male or female,” but *male and female.* ^3-28

**[[NRSVue Galatians 03#^3-29|29]]:** *Abraham’s offspring* (or “seed”*)*, united with Christ, believers receive Christ’s status as Abraham’s offspring (3.16n.). The Talmud says that converts, by accepting the Torah, are deemed to be the offspring of Abraham, and consequently they are permitted to join native Jews in praying to “Our God and God of our fathers” (*y. Bik.* 1.4.64a). ^3-29
