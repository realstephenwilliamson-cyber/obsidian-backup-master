## God’s election of Israel

**9:1** I am speaking the truth in Christ—I am not lying; my conscience confirms it by the Holy Spirit— ^9-1

**9:2** I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart. ^9-2

**9:3** For I could wish that I myself were accursed and cut off from Christ for the sake of my own brothers and sisters, my own flesh and blood. ^9-3

**9:4** They are Israelites, and to them belong the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the worship, and the promises; ^9-4

**9:5** to them belong the patriarchs, and from them, according to the flesh, comes the Christ, who is over all, God blessed forever.^[Or *Messiah, who is God over all, blessed forever*; or *Messiah. May he who is God over all be blessed forever*] Amen. ^9-5

**9:6** It is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all those descended from Israel are Israelites, ^9-6

**9:7** and not all of Abraham’s children are his descendants, but “it is through Isaac that descendants shall be named for you.” ^9-7

**9:8** This means that it is not the children of the flesh who are the children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as descendants. ^9-8

**9:9** For the word of the promise is this: “About this time I will return, and Sarah shall have a son.” ^9-9

**9:10** Nor is that all; something similar happened to Rebecca when she had conceived children by one husband, our ancestor Isaac: ^9-10

**9:11** even before they had been born or had done anything good or bad (so that God’s purpose of election might continue, ^9-11

**9:12** not by works but by his call) she was told, “The elder shall serve the younger.” ^9-12

**9:13** As it is written, “I have loved Jacob, but I have hated Esau.” ^9-13

**9:14** What then are we to say? Is there injustice on God’s part? By no means! ^9-14

**9:15** For he says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion.” ^9-15

**9:16** So it depends not on human will or exertion but on God who shows mercy. ^9-16

**9:17** For the scripture says to Pharaoh, “I have raised you up for this very purpose, that I may show my power in you and that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth.” ^9-17

**9:18** So then he has mercy on whomever he chooses, and he hardens the heart of whomever he chooses. ^9-18

## God’s wrath and mercy

**9:19** You will say to me then, “Why then does he still find fault? For who can resist his will?” ^9-19

**9:20** But who indeed are you, a human, to argue with God? Will what is molded say to the one who molds it, “Why have you made me like this?” ^9-20

**9:21** Has the potter no right over the clay, to make out of the same lump one object for special use and another for ordinary use? ^9-21

**9:22** What if God, desiring to show his wrath and to make known his power, has endured with much patience the objects of wrath that are made for destruction, ^9-22

**9:23** and what if he has done so in order to make known the riches of his glory for the objects of mercy, which he has prepared beforehand for glory— ^9-23

**9:24** including us whom he has called, not from the Jews only but also from the gentiles? ^9-24

**9:25** As he also says in Hosea, “Those who were not my people I will call ‘my people,’ and her who was not beloved I will call ‘beloved.’ ” ^9-25

**9:26** “And in the place where it was said to them, ‘You are not my people,’ there they shall be called children of the living God.” ^9-26

**9:27** And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel, “Though the number of the children of Israel were like the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will be saved, ^9-27

**9:28** for the Lord will execute his sentence on the earth quickly and decisively.”^[Other ancient authorities read *for he will finish his work and cut it short in righteousness, because the Lord will make the sentence shortened on the earth*] ^9-28

**9:29** And as Isaiah predicted, “If the Lord of hosts had not left descendants to us, we would have fared like Sodom and been made like Gomorrah.” ^9-29

## Israel’s unbelief

**9:30** What then are we to say? Gentiles, who did not strive for righteousness, have attained it, that is, righteousness through faith, ^9-30

**9:31** but Israel, who did strive for the law of righteousness, did not attain that law. ^9-31

**9:32** Why not? Because they did not strive for it on the basis of faith but as if it were based on works. They have stumbled over the stumbling stone, ^9-32

**9:33** as it is written, “See, I am laying in Zion a stone that will make people stumble, a rock that will make them fall, and whoever trusts in him^[Or *it*] will not be put to shame.” ^9-33
