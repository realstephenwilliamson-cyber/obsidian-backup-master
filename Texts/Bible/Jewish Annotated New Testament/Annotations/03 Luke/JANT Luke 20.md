### 20.1–8: Questioning Jesus’ authority

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-1|Text]], p. 156* · ([[Matthew 21#^21-23|Mt 21.23–27]]; [[Mark 11#^11-27|Mk 11.27–33]]; [[John 02#^2-18|Jn 2.18–22]]). ^20-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-1|1]]:** *Good news*, see 1.19n. ^20-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-3|3]]:** *I will also ask you a question*, soon to be the target of rhetorical challenges to which simple “yes” or “no” answers cannot be given (20.21–26 on taxes; 20.27–28 on the logic of resurrection), Jesus begins the rhetorical challenge. ^20-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-4|4]]:** *Baptism of John*, see 3.3–21. ^20-4

### 20.9–19: Parable of the wicked tenants

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-9|Text]], p. 156* · ([[Matthew 21#^21-33|Mt 21.33–46]]; [[Mark 12#^12-1|Mk 12.1–12]]). ^20-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-9|9]]:** *Vineyard*, symbol for Israel ([[Isaiah 05#^5-1|Isa 5.1–7]]). ^20-9-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-13|13]]:** *Beloved* (3.22), identifies the son with Jesus. ^20-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-16|16]]:** *Destroy those tenants*, see 19.27n. *Others*, here, Jesus’ followers. ^20-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-17|17]]:** *The stone* . . . , the verse from [[Psalms 118#^118-22|Ps 118.22]]; served Jesus’ followers as an early proof text; see [[Acts 04#^4-11|Acts 4.11]]; [[Ephesians 02#^2-20|Eph 2.20]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-6|1 Pet 2.6]]. Ps. 118 is part of the Hallel (Heb for “praise”) Psalms (113–118), associated with festivals in rabbinic literature (e.g., *b. Pesah*. [[Pesachim 117#117a|117a]]). ^20-17

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-18|18]]:** See [[Isaiah 08#^8-14|Isa 8.14–15]]. ^20-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-19|19]]:** See 19.47. ^20-19

### 20.20–26: Taxes to the emperor

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-20|Text]], p. 157* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-15|Mt 22.15–22]]; [[Mark 12#^12-13|Mk 12.13–17]]). ^20-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-22|22]]:** *Lawful*, according to Jewish law. ^20-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-24|24]]:** *Denarius*, coin depicting a portrait of the emperor (see 12.59n.). Judas the Galilean (see 2.1n.) saw such taxation as “no better than an introduction to slavery, and exhorted the nation to assert their liberty” (Josephus, *Ant*. 18.4; see also 20.102; *J.W.* 2.117–118; 7. 253–258). *Head*, Gk “eikon” (whence “icon”), lit., “image,” cf. [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-16|Deut 4.16]] (prohibiting idols in human form). ^20-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-25|25]]:** *Things that are God’s*, Jesus’ interlocutors would need to determine whether anything belongs to the emperor, or whether everything belongs to God. Christian tradition reads the saying as advocating payment ([[Romans 13#^13-6|Rom 13.6–7]]). Jesus’ opponents charge him with forbidding paying taxes, a plausible interpretation of 23.2. ^20-25

### 20.27–40: Question concerning resurrection

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-27|Text]], p. 157* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-23|Mt 22.23–33]]; [[Mark 12#^12-18|Mk 12.18–27]]). ^20-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-27|27]]:** *Sadducees*, the elite, Jerusalem-based party; see “Jewish Movements of the NT Period,” p. 614. *There is no resurrection*, either they rejected the idea of physical resurrection ([[Acts 04#^4-1|Acts 4.1–2]]; [[Acts 23#^23-6|23.6–10]]; Josephus, *Ant*. 18.1.4) or perhaps denied that it could be found in Torah (*m. Sanh*. 10.1). ^20-27-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-28|28]]:** *Raise up children*, levirate marriage, designed to protect widows and preserve the dead husband’s name and estate ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-5|Deut 25.5–10]]; see also [[Torah 09 - Vayeshev#^38-8|Gen 38.8]]). ^20-28

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-34|34–35]]:** *Marry and are given in marriage*, see 17.27. ^20-34

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-36|36]]:** *Like angels*, having no need to procreate. *B. Ber*. 17a states, “In the world to come there is not eating and drinking or marrying or envy or hate, but the pious rest with crowns upon their heads, and are satisfied with the glory of God.” ^20-36

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-37|37]]:** *Story about the bush*, see [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-6|Ex 3.6]]. ^20-37

### 20.41–44: Question about David’s son

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-41|Text]], p. 158* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-41|Mt 22.41–46]]; [[Mark 12#^12-35|Mk 12.35–37a]]). ^20-41

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-42|42–43]]:** Citation of [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]] (early Christian writings, but not Jewish ones, frequently cite this text in reference to the Messiah; see [[Acts 02#^2-34|Acts 2.34–35]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-25|1 Cor 15.25]]; [[Hebrews 01#^1-4|Heb 1.4]]). ^20-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-44|44]]:** *How can he be his son*, the psalm depicts God (“the Lord”) speaking to a Davidic king (“my Lord”); the superscription ascribes the Psalm to David. ^20-44

### 20.45–47: Warnings concerning the scribes

*[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-45|Text]], p. 158* · (see [[Matthew 23#^23-6|Mt 23.6–7]]; [[Mark 12#^12-37|Mk 12.37b–40]]). ^20-45

**[[NRSVue Luke 20#^20-46|46]]:** In 11.43, Jesus places the same charge against Pharisees; neither group is associated with the Temple. ^20-46
