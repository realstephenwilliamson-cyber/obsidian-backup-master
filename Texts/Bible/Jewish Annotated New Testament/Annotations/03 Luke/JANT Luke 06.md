### 6.1–11: Sabbath practices

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 124* · ([[Matthew 12#^12-1|Mt 12.1–14]]; [[Mark 02#^2-23|Mk 2.23–3.6]]). ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-1|1]]:** The Tanakh enjoins “work” on the Sabbath (e.g., [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-10|Ex 20.10]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-14|31.14–15]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-3|Lev 23.3]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-14|Deut 5.14]]) but does not define what constitutes work, and different Jewish communities offered different answers. The Mishnah (*Shabb*. 7.2) lists thirty-nine categories of work, all related to the activity of creating. The extent to which Jesus’ (Gentile) followers observed the Sabbath is unclear (see, e.g., [[Colossians 02#^2-16|Col 2.16]]; the term does not appear in Paul’s undisputed letters). ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-2|2]]:** *Pharisees*, their presence in the fields on the Sabbath is odd. *Not lawful*, see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-21|Ex 34.21]]; for stricter rabbinic rules, see also *m. Shabb*. 7.2, which prohibits reaping on the Sabbath. Plucking grain in another’s field is legal ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-25|Deut 23.24–25]]). ^6-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-3|3]]:** *What David did*, see [[I Samuel 21#^21-2|1 Sam 21.1–6]]. ^6-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-4|4]]:** *Bread of the Presence*, see [[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-5|Lev 24.5–9]]. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-5|5]]:** *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. If this expression is verse 39 c Other ancient authorities read *On the second first sabbath* d Gk *he* e Other ancient authorities add *to do* taken in a non-messianic way, v 5 is comparable to Rabbi Simeon B. Menasya’s comment on [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-14|Ex 31.14]] (“You shall keep the Sabbath, because it is holy to you”): “The words ‘to you’ imply that the Sabbath is given to you, and that you are not given to the Sabbath” (*Mek. , Ki Tissa* \[to Ex 31\]). ^6-5

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-6|6]]:** *Synagogue*, see 4.15n. ^6-6

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-7|7]]:** *Cure on the sabbath*, see 4.31–37n. ^6-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-9|9]]:** *Save life*, “Pikuach nefesh” (“saving a life”) always overrides Sabbath law, see 6.5n.; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-40|1 Macc 2.40–41]]; *b. Yoma* [[Yoma 84#84b|84b]]. ^6-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-10|10]]:** *Hand was restored*, Jesus does not touch the man, and therefore prevents suspicion of “working.” ^6-10

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-11|11]]:** *Fury*, Gk “anoia” connotes “madness” or “lack of understanding.” *What they might do*, Pharisees have no political or juridical authority. Luke omits Mark’s point (3.6) that the Pharisees wanted to kill Jesus; Pharisees are absent from Luke’s Passion narrative. ^6-11

### 6.12–16: Twelve apostles

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-12|Text]], p. 125* · ([[Matthew 10#^10-1|Mt 10.1–4]]; [[Mark 03#^3-13|Mk 3.13–19a]]). See also [[Acts 01#^1-13|Acts 1.13]]. ^6-12

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-12|12]]:** *Pray*, see 3.21n. ^6-12-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Twelve* (8.1; [[Acts 06#^6-2|Acts 6.2]]; etc.), symbolizes reconstitution of the twelve tribes of Israel. *Apostles* (Gk “apostolos,” “those sent”), an agent or emissary; Heb “shaliaḥ shel ʾadam kemoto” (“a person’s agent is as the person himself”), one who fully represents the sender (*m. Ber*. 5.5; *b. Qidd*. [[Kiddushin 42#42b|42b]]–43a). ^6-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-14|14]]:** *Peter*, nickname meaning “rock.” The names of the Twelve vary among Gospels, but Peter is always named first and Judas Iscariot last. *James*, Gk “Iacobos,” Heb “Yaʿaqov” (Jacob). *John*, Gk “Ioannēs,” Heb “Yoḥanan.” *Bartholomew*, Heb/Aram “Bar Tolmai,” “Son of Tolmai.” ^6-14

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Zealot*, a political revolutionary in the revolt against Rome (66–70 CE); here suggesting Phineas ([[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^25-11|Num 25.11]]) and Elijah ([[I Kings 18#^18-40|1 Kings 18.40]]; [[I Kings 19#^19-10|19.10]]); see also [[2 Corinthians 07#^7-7|2 Cor 7.7]],[[2 Corinthians 07#^7-11|11]],[[2 Corinthians 07#^7-12|12]]. See “Jewish Movements of the NT Period,” p. 614. ^6-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-16|16]]:** *Iscariot*, perhaps “a man \[from the Judean village\] of Kerioth” ([[Joshua 15#^15-25|Josh 15.25]]); if so, Judas was the only Judean among the Twelve; alternately his name may derive from Aram “sheqarya,” “false one, liar.” ^6-16

### 6.17–49: Sermon on the plain

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-17|Text]], p. 125* · (Mt 5–7). ^6-17

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-17|17]]:** *Level place*, contrasts Matthew’s “Sermon on the Mount.” ^6-17-2

### 6.20–23: Beatitudes

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-20|Text]], p. 125* · ([[Matthew 05#^5-3|Mt 5.3–12]]). For the form, see [[Psalms 001#^1-1|Ps 1.1]]; [[Psalms 034#^34-9|34.8]]; etc.; *blessed* (Gk “makarioi”), corresponds to Heb “ ʾashrei,” “fortunate.” ^6-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-20|20]]:** *You who are poor*, compare Matthew’s “poor in spirit.” Jewish tradition regards the poor, the hungry, etc. not as cursed or impure but as deserving recipients of divine and earthly care (e.g., [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-11|Deut 15.11]]; [[Isaiah 49#^49-10|Isa 49.10]]; [[Jeremiah 31#^31-25|Jer 31.25]]; [[Ezekiel 34#^34-29|Ezek 34.29]]). ^6-20-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-22|22]]:** *Exclude you*, see [[Isaiah 66#^66-5|Isa 66.5]]. *Son of Man*, see 5.24n.; here, Jesus. ^6-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-23|23]]:** *Their ancestors*, e.g., [[Nehemiah 09#^9-26|Neh 9.26]]; [[II Chronicles 13#^13-15|2 Chr 13.15–16]]. ^6-23

### 6.24–26: Woes

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-24|Text]], p. 125* ^6-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-24|24]]:** Luke descries the *rich* who refuse to give alms (6.30n.) and encourages extreme generosity (11.41; 12.13–21,33–34; 16.13,19–31; 18.18–30; see also *1 En*. 94.8 \[*“*Woe to you rich, for you have trusted in your riches, and from your riches you shall depart, because you have not remembered the Most High in the days of your riches”\]); the condemnation of wealth is stronger in this Gospel than in, e.g., [[1 Timothy 06#^6-10|1 Tim 6.10]] and [[Hebrews 13#^13-5|Heb 13.5]] (where the issue is “love of money”; see also, e.g., [[Habakkuk 2#^2-5|Hab 2.5]]). See 6.30n. ^6-24-2

### 6.27–36: Avoiding violence

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-27|Text]], p. 126* · ([[Matthew 05#^5-38|Mt 5.38–48]]; [[Matthew 07#^7-12|7.12]]). ^6-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-27|27]]:** *Love your enemies*, Jewish teaching commands helping enemies (see e.g., [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-4|Ex 23.4–5]]; [[Proverbs 24#^24-17|Prov 24.17]]; [[Proverbs 25#^25-21|25.21]]); rabbinic commentary speaks of aiding (not “loving”) enemies in order to “subdue the evil inclination” (*b. B. Metz*. [[Bava Metzia 32#32b|32b]]). Jesus’ command may be hyperbole, similar to the use of “hate” in 14.26, or, more likely, an extension of the concern to treat enemies with fairness. ^6-27-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-28|28]]:** *Pray for those who abuse*, see 23.34. ^6-28

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-29|29]]:** *Takes away your coat*, [[Matthew 05#^5-40|Mt 5.40]] suggest a lawsuit; Luke suggests robbery. ^6-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-30|30]]:** *Give to everyone*, Luke sanctions voluntary poverty (see 6.24n.; 12.33; 18.22); rabbinic sources do not, as personal impoverishment would create greater hardships for the family and community. Jewish sources mandate almsgiving (“tzedakah”); see e.g., [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-11|Deut 15.11]]. Rav Assi (third century) called almsgiving “equal in value to all other commandments” (*b. B. Bat*. [[Bava Batra 9#9a|9a]]). ^6-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-31|31]]:** *Do to others*, compare Hillel’s “What is hateful to you, do not do to anyone else” (Tob 4.15; *b. Shabb*. [[Shabbat 31#31a|31a]]). ^6-31

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-32|32–34]]:** *What credit*, Luke reflects the reciprocal system of benefaction. ^6-32

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-35|35]]:** *Expecting nothing in return*, more generous than the Tanakh’s forbidding of interest on a loan ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-24|Ex 22.25]]; [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-36|Lev 25.36–37]]). *Children of the Most High*, see 1.32,35; 8.28. ^6-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-36|36]]:** *Be merciful*, compare [[Matthew 05#^5-48|Mt 5.48]], where the injunction is perfection; for imitating divine compassion ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]]), see *b. Shabb*. [[Shabbat 133#133b|133b]]; *Mek*. 15.2; *Sifre Deut*. 11.22; 49 (“As God is called compassionate and gracious, so you too must be compassionate and gracious, giving gifts freely”). ^6-36

### 6.37–42: Judging

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-37|Text]], p. 126* · ([[Matthew 07#^7-1|Mt 7.1–5]]; [[Matthew 12#^12-36|12.36–37]]; [[Matthew 15#^15-14|15.14]]; [[Mark 04#^4-24|Mk 4.24–25]]). ^6-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-37|37]]:** *Do not judge*, see *b. Rosh Ha-Shanah* [[Rosh Hashanah 16#16b|16b]]; *b. Meg*. [[Megillah 28#28a|28a]]. ^6-37-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-38|38]]:** *Measure you give*, a biblical (e.g., [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-21|Ex 22.22–24]]; Ob 1.15) and rabbinic (Heb “midah ke-neged midah” \[“measure corresponding to measure”\]) idea; see e.g., *m. Sot.* 1.7 (“By that same measure by which a man metes out \[to others\], they mete out to him”); *m. Avot* 2.6, attributed to Hillel (“He saw a skull floating on the water and said to it, ‘because you drowned others, they drowned you, and in the end those who drowned you will be drowned’ ”). ^6-38

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-41|41–42]]:** *Log in your own eye*, *b. B. Bat.* [[Bava Batra 15#15b|15b]] expresses the same idea: “If the judge said to a man, ‘Take the splinter from between your teeth,’ he would respond, ‘Take the beam from between your eyes.’” See also *b. Arak*. [[Arakhin 16#16b|16b]]. 3b. *Hypocrite*, see [[Psalms 026#^26-4|Ps 26.4]]; [[LXX Sirach 01#^1-29|Sir 1.29]]; [[LXX Sirach 32#^32-15|32.15]]. ^6-41

### 6.43–45: Bearing good fruit

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-43|Text]], p. 126* · ([[Matthew 07#^7-15|Mt 7.15–20]]; [[Matthew 12#^12-33|12.33–35]]). See [[James 03#^3-11|Jas 3.11–12]]. ^6-43

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-45|45]]:** *Heart*, the moral center ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^8-21|Gen 8.21]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5–6]]; etc.). On the relation of heart (Heb “lev”) to lips (Heb “peh”), see [[Psalms 019#^19-15|Ps 19.15]]. ^6-45

### 6.46–49: Wise and foolish builders

*[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-46|Text]], p. 127* · ([[Matthew 07#^7-21|Mt 7.21–27]]). See [[James 01#^1-22|Jas 1.22–25]]. ^6-46

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-46|46]]:** *Lord, Lord,* confession without attendant works is meaningless. For the invocation, see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]] and compare the recitation of the thirteen “midot ha-ra ḥamim” (“attributes of \[divine\] mercy”) in the Seliḥot (penitential prayers). ^6-46-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 06#^6-47|47–49]]:** See R. Eleazar b. Azariah’s similar aphorism concerning trees with weak vs. strong roots (*m. Avot* 3.22, “He would say, ‘Anyone whose wisdom is greater than his deeds—to what is he to be likened? To a tree with abundant foliage, but few roots . . .’ ”). ^6-47
