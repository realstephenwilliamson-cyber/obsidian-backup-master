# 4 Maccabees 15 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 14|← 4 Maccabees 14]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 16|4 Maccabees 16 →]]

**15:1** O reason of the children, tyrant over the passions! O piety, more desirable to the mother than her children! ^15-1

**15:2** Two courses were open to this mother, that of piety and that of preserving her seven sons for a time, as the tyrant had promised. ^15-2

**15:3** She loved piety more, the piety that preserves them for eternal life according to God’s promise.^[Gk *according to God*] ^15-3

**15:4** In what manner might I express the passions of parents who love their children? We impress upon the character of a small child a wondrous likeness both of mind and of form. Especially is this true of mothers, who because of their birth pangs have a deeper sympathy toward their offspring than do the fathers. ^15-4

**15:5** For to the extent that mothers are of tender spirit and bear more children, so much the more attached are they to their children. ^15-5

**15:6** The mother of the seven boys, more than any other mother, loved her children. In seven pregnancies she had implanted in herself tender love toward them, ^15-6

**15:7** and because of the many pains she suffered with each of them she had sympathy for them, ^15-7

**15:8** yet because of the fear of God she disdained the temporary safety of her children. ^15-8

**15:9** Not only so, but also because of the nobility of her sons and their ready obedience to the law, she felt a greater tenderness toward them. ^15-9

**15:10** For they were just and self-controlled and courageous and magnanimous and loved their brothers and their mother so that they obeyed her even to death in keeping the ordinances. ^15-10

**15:11** Nevertheless, though so many factors influenced the mother to suffer with them out of love for her children, in the case of none of them were the various tortures strong enough to pervert her reason. ^15-11

**15:12** But each child separately and all of them together the mother urged on to death for piety’s sake. ^15-12

**15:13** O sacred nature, parental affection, tender love toward offspring, nursing, and indomitable maternal passions! ^15-13

**15:14** This mother, who saw them tortured and burned one by one, for piety’s sake did not change her attitude. ^15-14

**15:15** She watched the flesh of her children being consumed by fire, their toes and fingers scattered^[Or *quivering*] on the ground, and the flesh of the head to the chin exposed like masks. ^15-15

**15:16** O mother, tried now by more bitter pains than even the birth pangs you suffered for them! ^15-16

**15:17** O woman, who alone gave birth to such perfect piety! ^15-17

**15:18** Neither when the firstborn breathed his last, it did not turn you aside, nor when the second in torments looked at you piteously nor when the third expired, ^15-18

**15:19** nor did you weep when you looked at the eyes of each one in his tortures gazing boldly at the same agonies and saw in their nostrils the signs of the approach of death. ^15-19

**15:20** When you saw the flesh of children burned^[Other ancient authorities read *the amputated flesh of children*] upon the flesh of other children, severed hands upon hands, scalped heads upon heads, and corpses fallen on other corpses, and when you saw the place filled with many spectators because of the children’s torments, you did not shed tears. ^15-20

**15:21** Neither the melodies of sirens nor the songs of swans attract the attention of their hearers as did the voices of the children in torture calling to their mother. ^15-21

**15:22** How great and how many torments the mother then suffered as her sons were tortured on the wheel and with the hot irons! ^15-22

**15:23** But pious reason, giving her heart a man’s courage in the very midst of her passions, strengthened her to disregard, for the time, her parental love. ^15-23

**15:24** Although she witnessed the destruction of seven children and the ingenious and various rackings, this noble mother disregarded all these^[Other ancient authorities read *having bidden them farewell, surrendered them*] because of faith in God. ^15-24

**15:25** For as in the council chamber of her own soul she saw mighty advocates—nature, family, parental love, and the instruments of torture awaiting her children— ^15-25

**15:26** this mother held two ballots, one bearing death and the other deliverance for her children. ^15-26

**15:27** She did not approve the deliverance that would preserve the seven sons for a short time, ^15-27

**15:28** but as the daughter of God-fearing Abraham she remembered his fortitude. ^15-28

**15:29** O mother of the nation, vindicator of the law, and defender of piety who carried away the prize of the contest in your heart! ^15-29

**15:30** O more noble than males in steadfastness and more courageous than men in endurance! ^15-30

**15:31** Just as Noah’s ark, carrying the world in the universal flood, stoutly endured the waves, ^15-31

**15:32** so you, O guardian of the law, overwhelmed from every side by the flood of your passions and the violent winds—the torture of your sons—endured nobly and withstood the wintry storms raging on piety’s account. ^15-32
