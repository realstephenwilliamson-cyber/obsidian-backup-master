## Chapter 7

**7:1** [^1]A good name is better than fragrant oil, and the day of death than the day of birth.[^2] ^7-1

**7:2** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to a house of feasting; for that is the end of every man, and a living one should take it to heart. ^7-2

**7:3** Vexation is better than revelry;[^3] for though the face be sad, the heart may be glad. ^7-3

**7:4** Wise men are drawn to a house of mourning, and fools to a house of merrymaking. ^7-4

**7:5** It is better to listen to a wise man’s reproof than to listen to the praise of fools. ^7-5

**7:6** For the levity[^4] of the fool is like the crackling of nettles under a kettle. [^5]But that too is illusory; ^7-6

**7:7** for cheating[^6] may rob the wise man of reason and destroy the prudence of the cautious.[^7] ^7-7

**7:8** The end of a matter is better than the beginning of it.  
Better a patient spirit than a haughty spirit. ^7-8

**7:9** Don’t let your spirit be quickly vexed, for vexation abides in the breasts of fools. ^7-9

**7:10** Don’t say, “How has it happened that former times were better than these?” For it is not wise of you to ask that question. ^7-10

**7:11** Wisdom is as good as a patrimony, and even better, for those who behold the sun. ^7-11

**7:12** For to be in the shelter of wisdom is to be also in the shelter of money,[^8] and the advantage of intelligence is that wisdom preserves the life of him who possesses it. ^7-12

**7:13** [^9]Consider God’s doing! Who can straighten what He has twisted? ^7-13

**7:14** So in a time of good fortune enjoy the good fortune; and in a time of misfortune, reflect: The one no less than the other was God’s doing; consequently, man may find no fault with Him.[^10] ^7-14

**7:15** In my own brief span of life, I have seen both these things: sometimes a good man perishes in spite of his goodness, and sometimes a wicked one endures in spite of his wickedness. ^7-15

**7:16** So don’t overdo goodness and don’t act the wise man to excess, or you may be dumfounded. ^7-16

**7:17** Don’t overdo wickedness and don’t be a fool, or you may die before your time. ^7-17

**7:18** It is best that you grasp the one without letting go of the other, for one who fears God will do his duty[^11] by both. ^7-18

**7:19** Wisdom is more of a stronghold to a wise man than ten magnates[^12] that a city may contain. ^7-19

**7:20** [^13]For there is not one good man on earth who does what is best[^14] and doesn’t err. ^7-20

**7:21** Finally, don’t pay attention to everything that is said, so that you may not hear your slave reviling you; ^7-21

**7:22** for well you remember[^15] the many times that you yourself have reviled others. ^7-22

**7:23** All this I tested with wisdom. I thought I could fathom it,[^16] but it eludes me. ^7-23

**7:24** [The secret of] what happens is elusive and deep, deep down; who can discover it? ^7-24

**7:25** I put my mind to studying, exploring, and seeking wisdom and the reason of things, and to studying wickedness, stupidity, madness, and folly. ^7-25

**7:26** Now, I find woman more bitter than death; she is all traps, her hands are fetters and her heart is snares. He who is pleasing to God escapes her, and he who is displeasing is caught by her. ^7-26

**7:27** See, this is what I found, said Koheleth, item by item in my search for the reason of things. ^7-27

**7:28** As for what I sought further but did not find, I found only one human being in a thousand, and the one I found among so many was never a woman. ^7-28

**7:29** But, see, this I did find: God made men plain, but they have engaged in too much reasoning. ^7-29

[^1]: The author now offers a number of practical maxims, which, however, he concludes (vv. 23–24) are of limited value.
[^2]: Until a man dies, there is always danger that he may forfeit his good name.
[^3]: For empty revelry precludes real happiness; cf. 2.2.
[^4]: Emendation yields “praise” (shbḥ.)
[^5]: This section, to end of verse 7, is apparently a continuation of the thought in vv. 11–12 and 19.
[^6]: Emendation yields “riches.”
[^7]: Lit. “caution”; cf. postbiblical mathun, “cautious.”
[^8]: Emendation yields “For the possessor of wisdom becomes a possessor of money.”
[^9]: Vv. 13–14 continue the thought of v. 10.
[^10]: So Rashi; cf. the same thought in Job 1.22; 2.10.
[^11]: Cf. postbiblical yaṣa yede.
[^12]: Emendation yields “the riches of the magnates”; cf. Prov. 18.11.
[^13]: Apparently continuing the thought of v. 16.
[^14]: Refers back to 6.12.
[^15]: Lit. “your heart knows”; the same idiom occurs again in 8.5.
[^16]: Refers back to 6.12.
