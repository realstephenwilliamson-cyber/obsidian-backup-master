# 1 John 2 (JANT notes)

[[JANT 1 John 01|← 1 John 1]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 1 John 03|1 John 3 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 1 John 02|NRSVue]] · [[1 John 02|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-1|2.1]]:** *Little children*, the author positions himself as the wise parental figure, perhaps as a strategy to strengthen his powers of persuasion over his readers. *Advocate* (Gk “paraklētos,” lit., “one who stands alongside”), a term found in the NT only here and in [[John 14#^14-16|Jn 14.16]],[[John 14#^14-26|26]]; [[John 15#^15-26|15.26]]; [[John 16#^16-7|16.7]] (where it refers to the Holy Spirit whose presence replaces that of Jesus within the community). It can mean “one who speaks on behalf of another” or “one who offers aid to another.” It can also be translated “comforter” or “counselor.” *Righteous*, or “one who is just.” ^2-1

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Atoning sacrifice* (Gk “hilasmos”), see 4.10; these are the only places in the NT where this word is used; in LXX it appears, e.g., in [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-26|Lev 23.26]] (Day of Atonement); [[Torah 35 - Nasso#^5-8|Num 5.8]] (ram of atonement); in the Torah’s priestly system, disobedience renders a section of the Tabernacle or Temple impure, requiring a purification sacrifice, translated in NRSV as a “sin offering”; see “Atonement,” p. 524. ^2-2

> [!note] Antichrist *(p. 520)*
> The term “Antichrist” or “antichrists” appears in the New Testament only in John’s two epistles ([[1 John 02#^2-18|1 Jn 2.18]],[[1 John 02#^2-22|22]]; [[1 John 04#^4-3|4.3]]; 2 Jn 7). First [[John 02#^2-18|John 2.18]] refers to *the* “antichrist” that “is coming” and as well as to “many antichrists” that are already in the world. While few details are provided about the former, the latter are identified as the writer’s contemporaneous opponents: they were actively “against the Messiah,” which conveys the literal meaning of the term. These opponents are described as having separated from the author’s community, as rejecting Jesus as the Messiah ([[1 John 02#^2-22|1 Jn 2.22]]), and as denying that the Messiah had come in the flesh ([[1 John 04#^4-3|1 Jn 4.3]]).
>
> The concept of “Antichrist” changed and developed considerably in later periods; the term as used in 1 John does not reflect the more technical usage manifest in later Christian writings. Christian writers continued to identify rivals they deemed heretical as antichrists (e.g., Tertullian uses the term to describe Marcion and his disciples in *Adv. Marc.* 5.16.4). Third-century Christian apocalyptic literature speaks of a singular Antichrist expected to appear immediately prior to the *eschaton*, who would deceive followers by performing miracles in imitation of Jesus but using demonic powers (see Hippolytus’s treatise “On the Christ and the Antichrist”). Cyril of Jerusalem suggested that the Antichrist will be a Jewish pseudo-Messiah who will rebuild the Temple in order to attract Jewish followers (*Cat-echeses* 15.15; see [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-3|2 Thess 2.3–12]]).

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-3|3]]:** *Know*, a favorite Johannine term, here alluding to the personal knowledge of God that leads one to love community members ([[John 13#^13-34|Jn 13.34]]). ^2-3

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-4|4–6]]:** *Obey*, in conforming one’s behavior to God’s expectations one exemplifies divine love. *Abide in*, participate in the life of. *Walk*, live one’s life; see e.g., [[Psalms 001#^1-1|Ps 1.1]]; [[Psalms 015#^15-2|15.2]]; the rabbis encouraged the imitation of the attributes of God, e.g., “Just as He is merciful and compassionate, be you too merciful and compassionate” (*Mekh. , be-Shallah* 14.2; *Sifra* 19.1) ^2-4

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-7|7]]:** *New commandment*, see [[John 13#^13-34|Jn 13.34]]; 2 Jn 5; and see Introduction. ^2-7

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-9|9]]:** Dualistic formulations and demonization of opponents reflective of inter-communal strife are also found in the Dead Sea Scrolls, e.g., walking in “light” versus walking in “darkness” (1QS 3.20–21) and the “sons of righteousness” versus the “sons of deceit” (1QS 3.20–21). ^2-9

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Believer*, lit., “brother” (see translators’ note *e*). ^2-11

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-12|12–14]]:** The threefold *children . . . fathers . . . young people* characterize various groups within the community; the rhythmic construction and repetition make the exhortation memorable. *Sins . . . name*, forgiveness is from God; the *name* is not an arbitrary label but the expression of the person, including innate authority. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-13|13–14]]:** *Evil one* (Gk “poneros”), see also [[Matthew 06#^6-13|Mt 6.13]], “rescue us from evil” or “the evil one”; [[John 17#^17-15|Jn 17.15]]. *You have conquered*, through the word of God. The author praises community members for overcoming the devil’s temptations. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-15|15]]:** Johannine literature often uses *world* (Gk “kosmos”) to represent the sphere of opposition to God (see, e.g., [[John 15#^15-18|Jn 15.18–19]], but note [[John 03#^3-16|Jn 3.16]]: “God so loved the world”). ^2-15

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-18|18]]:** *Antichrist*, this term is found in the NT only in John’s two letters (1 Jn 1.18,22; 4.3 and 2 Jn 7). The Gk noun “christos” means “anointed one” or “messiah”; the prefix “anti-” means “against” or “in place of.” The singular form of the term in 1 John refers to the end-time adversary of God and Christ. Antecedent ideas of an end-time person, or personification, of evil appear in, e.g., [[Daniel 07#^7-19|Dan 7.19–27]]; [[Daniel 08#^8-9|8.9–11]],[[Daniel 08#^8-23|23–25]]; [[Daniel 09#^9-27|9.27]]; CD 8.2; 1QM 17.5–8; 11QMelch 2.12–13; and *Sib. Or*. 3.75–92,611–15. See “Antichrist,” p. 520. *Last hour*, God’s intervention into human history and defeat of evil are expected at any time. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-19|19]]:** *They went out from us*, the recent schism. These former members are now adversaries identified as antichrists (2.18–19). ^2-19

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-20|20]]:** *Anointed by the Holy One*, anointing (marking with oil) indicates starting a new role; the one who has marked the members of the community is the Spirit. ^2-20

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-23|23]]:** *Denies the Son*, possibly a reference to those who deny Jesus’ corporeality or humanity. ^2-23

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-25|25]]:** *Eternal life*, life after death. ^2-25

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-27|27]]:** *Abide in him*, or “abide in it,” referring to the anointing. ^2-27

**[[NRSVue 1 John 02#^2-28|28]]:** *Coming* (Gk “parousia”), the judgment at the Messiah’s return. “Parousia” could simply refer to someone’s arrival (Titus in [[2 Corinthians 07#^7-6|2 Cor 7.6]]; Paul in [[Philippians 01#^1-26|Phil 1.26]]) or presence (of Paul in [[2 Corinthians 10#^10-10|2 Cor 10.10]]), but as a theological term it refers to the eschatological arrival of Christ, what is known as the “second coming” (e.g., [[Matthew 24#^24-3|Mt 24.3]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-23|1 Cor 15.23]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-19|1 Thess 2.19]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-13|3.13]]; [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-15|4.15]]; [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-1|2 Thess 2.1]]). ^2-28
