## Mūla

यथाप्सु शान्तये मन्त्रास्तद्वदस्त्रादिषु ध्रुवम् ।
शत्रुच्छेदादिकर्तारः काम्योऽतः सङ्करोज्झितः ॥ ^mula

yathāpsu śāntaye mantrāstadvadastrādiṣu dhruvam |
śatrucchedādikartāraḥ kāmyo'taḥ saṅkarojjhitaḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 18 - Chapter Twenty-Seven#^pg440|p. 420]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=440>))
>
> Just as Mantras (projected) into water serve to bring peace, in the same way (conversely, those projected) onto weapons and the like certainly destroy (one’s) enemies etc. Thus (it is for this reason) that a magical rite (performed with a desire for a specific goal) (kāmya) is free of admixture.
