# LXX Proverbs 26

← [[LXX Proverbs 25]] · [[LXX]] · [[LXX Proverbs 27]] →

*Proverbs · [[LXX]]*

**26:1** As dew in harvest, and as rain in summer, so honour is not *seemly* for a fool.  ^26-1

**26:2** As birds and sparrows fly, so a curse shall not come upon any one without a cause.  ^26-2

**26:3** As a whip for a horse, and a goad for an ass, so *is* a rod for a simple nation.  ^26-3

**26:4** Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou become like him.  ^26-4

**26:5** Yet answer a fool according to his folly, lest he seem wise in his own conceit.  ^26-5

**26:6** He that sends a message by a foolish messenger procures for himself a reproach from his own ways.  ^26-6

**26:7** *As well* take away the motion of the legs, as transgression from the mouth of fools.  ^26-7

**26:8** He that binds up a stone in a sling, is like one that gives glory to a fool.  ^26-8

**26:9** Thorns grow in the hand of a drunkard, and servitude in the hand of fools.[^1]  ^26-9

**26:10** All the flesh of fools endures much hardship; for their fury is brought to nought.[^2]  ^26-10

**26:11** As when a dog goes to his own vomit, and becomes abominable, so is a fool who returns in his wickedness to his own sin. 11a [There is a shame that brings sin: and there is a shame *that is* glory and grace.]  ^26-11

**26:12** I have seen a man who seemed to himself to be wise; but a fool had more hope than he.[^3]  ^26-12

**26:13** A sluggard when sent on a journey says, *There is* a lion in the ways, and *there are* murderers in the streets.  ^26-13

**26:14** As a door turns on the hinge, so does a sluggard on his bed.  ^26-14

**26:15** A sluggard having hid his hand in his bosom, will not be able to bring it up to his mouth.  ^26-15

**26:16** A sluggard seems to himself wiser than one who most satisfactorily brings back a message.[^4]  ^26-16

**26:17** As he that lays hold of a dog's tail, so is he that makes himself the champion of another's cause.  ^26-17

**26:18** As those who need correction put forth *fair* words to men, and he that first falls in with the proposal will be overthrown;  ^26-18

**26:19** so are all that lay wait for their own friends, and when they are discovered, say, I did it in jest.  ^26-19

**26:20** With much wood fire increases; but where there is not a double-minded man, strife ceases.  ^26-20

**26:21** A hearth for coals, and wood for fire; and a railing man for the tumult of strife.  ^26-21

**26:22** The words of cunning knaves are soft; but they smite *even* to the inmost parts of the bowels.  ^26-22

**26:23** Silver dishonestly given is to be considered as a potsherd: smooth lips cover a grievous heart.  ^26-23

**26:24** A weeping enemy promises all things with his lips, but in his heart he contrives deceit.  ^26-24

**26:25** Though *thine* enemy intreat thee with a loud voice, consent not: for there are seven abominations in his heart.  ^26-25

**26:26** He that hides enmity frames deceit: but being easily discerned, exposes his own sins in the public assemblies.  ^26-26

**26:27** He that digs a pit for his neighbour shall fall into it: and he that rolls a stone, rolls it upon himself.  ^26-27

**26:28** A lying tongue hates the truth; and an unguarded mouth causes tumults.  ^26-28

[^1]: 26:9 Compare *Heb.*
[^2]: 26:10 Great variation from Hebrew here.
[^3]: 26:12 *Gr.* by.
[^4]: 26:16 Compare *Heb.*
