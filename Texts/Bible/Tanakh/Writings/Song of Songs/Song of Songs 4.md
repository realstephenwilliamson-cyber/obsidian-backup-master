## Chapter 4

**4:1** Ah, you are fair, my darling,  
Ah, you are fair.  
Your eyes are like doves  
Behind your veil.  
Your hair is like a flock of goats  
Streaming down Mount Gilead. ^4-1

**4:2** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes[^1]  
Climbing up from the washing pool;  
All of them bear twins,  
And not one loses her young. ^4-2

**4:3** Your lips are like a crimson thread,  
Your mouth is lovely.  
Your brow behind your veil  
[Gleams] like a pomegranate split open. ^4-3

**4:4** Your neck is like the Tower of David,  
Built to hold weapons,[^2]  
Hung with a thousand shields—  
All the quivers of warriors. ^4-4

**4:5** Your breasts are like two fawns,  
Twins of a gazelle,  
Browsing among the lilies. ^4-5

**4:6** [^3]When the day blows gently  
And the shadows flee,  
I will betake me to the mount of myrrh,  
To the hill of frankincense. ^4-6

**4:7** Every part of you is fair, my darling,  
There is no blemish in you. ^4-7

**4:8** From Lebanon come with me;  
From Lebanon, my bride, with me!  
Trip down from Amana’s peak,  
From the peak of Senir[^4] and Hermon,  
From the dens of lions,  
From the hills[^5] of leopards. ^4-8

**4:9** You have captured my heart,  
My own,[^6] my bride,  
You have captured my heart  
With one [glance] of your eyes,  
With one coil of your necklace. ^4-9

**4:10** How sweet is your love,  
My own, my bride!  
How much more delightful your love than wine,  
Your ointments more fragrant  
Than any spice! ^4-10

**4:11** Sweetness drops  
From your lips, O bride;  
Honey and milk  
Are under your tongue;  
And the scent of your robes  
Is like the scent of Lebanon. ^4-11

**4:12** A garden locked  
Is my own, my bride,  
A fountain locked,  
A sealed-up spring. ^4-12

**4:13** Your limbs are an orchard of pomegranates  
And of all luscious fruits,  
Of henna and of nard— ^4-13

**4:14** Nard and saffron,  
Fragrant reed and cinnamon,  
With all aromatic woods,  
Myrrh and aloes—  
All the choice perfumes. ^4-14

**4:15** [You are] a garden spring,  
A well of fresh water,[^7]  
A rill of Lebanon. ^4-15

**4:16** Awake, O north wind,  
Come, O south wind!  
Blow upon my garden,  
That its perfume may spread.  
Let my beloved come to his garden  
And enjoy its luscious fruits! ^4-16

[^1]: Cf. 6.6; exact nuance of qeṣuboth uncertain, perhaps “shorn ones.”
[^2]: Apparently a poetic figure for jewelry; meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: See notes at 2.17.
[^4]: Cf. Deut. 3.9.
[^5]: Emendation yields “lairs”; cf. Nah. 2.13.
[^6]: Lit. “sister”; and so frequently below.
[^7]: Emendation yields “The spring in my garden / Is a well of fresh water.”
