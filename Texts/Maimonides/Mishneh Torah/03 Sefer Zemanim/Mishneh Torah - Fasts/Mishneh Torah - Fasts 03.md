**1.** [We should adhere to the following procedure when] no rain at all has descended from the beginning of the rainy season onward:[^1] If the seventeenth of Marcheshvan arrives and no rains have descended,[^2] the Torah scholars should begin to fast,[^3] [starting on] a Monday[^4] [and continuing on] the [following] Thursday, and the [following] Monday.[^5] All students [of the Torah] are fit to accept these [fasts] upon themselves.[^6] ^3-1

**2.** If Rosh Chodesh Kislev arrives without the rains having descended,[^7] the court should decree three communal fasts, [starting on] a Monday,[^8] [and continuing on] the [following] Thursday, and the [following] Monday.[^9] It is permitted to eat and drink at night.[^10] The men serving in the [weekly] priestly watch[^11] should not fast with them because they are involved in the Temple service.[^12]  
On these days, the entire people should enter the synagogue, pray, cry out [to God],[^13] and make supplications as is customary on all fasts.[^14] ^3-2

**3.** If these [fasts] pass without [their prayers] being answered,[^15] the court should decree an additional three communal fasts.[^16] On these fasts, we eat and drink while it is still day [on the day before the fast], as on the fast of Yom Kippur.[^17]  
The men serving in the [weekly] priestly watch should fast for part of the day,[^18] but should not complete the fast.[^19] The men of the *beit av*[^20] - those individuals who are involved in the Temple service that day - should not fast at all.  
On a fast for which we are required to cease eating while it is still day, once a person has ceased eating[^21] and decided not to eat any more,[^22] he may not change his mind and eat, even though there is still time during the day.[^23] ^3-3

**4.** On these three fasts, all people are forbidden[^24] to perform work during the day,[^25] but they are permitted during the [previous] night.[^26] It is forbidden for a person to wash his entire body in hot water,[^27] but one may wash one's face, hands, and feet.[^28] For this reason, the bathhouses are closed.[^29]  
It is forbidden to anoint oneself.[^30] One may, however, do so to remove filth.[^31] Sexual relations are forbidden,[^32] as is wearing shoes in a city. One may, however, wear shoes on a journey.[^33] We pray in the synagogues,[^34] cry out [to God], and make supplications as is customary on all fasts.[^35] ^3-4

**5.** If these [fasts] pass without [their prayers] being answered, the court decrees another seven communal fasts, [beginning on the next] Monday, [and continuing as follows]: Thursday, Monday, Thursday, Monday, Thursday, and Monday.  
It is only on these seven fasts[^36] that pregnant and nursing women[^37] are required to fast. [On the other fast days,] although they are not obligated to fast,[^38] they should not indulge in delicacies. Instead, they should eat only what is necessary to maintain their babies. ^3-5

**6.** On these seven fasts the men serving in the [weekly] priestly watch should fast for the entire day. The men of the *beit av* should fast for a portion of the day, but should not complete their fast.[^39] All the prohibitions in force during the second set of fasts are also in force during these last seven fasts.[^40] ^3-6

**7.** There are additional dimensions [of severity] to these [fasts]:[^41] it is on these alone that we sound the trumpets,[^42] pray in the street of the city,[^43] call on an elder to admonish the people [and motivate] them to repent from their [evil] ways,[^44] add six blessings in the morning[^45] and afternoon prayers[^46] - thus, we recite twenty-four blessings,[^47] and close the stores.[^48]  
On Mondays, the doors of the stores[^49] are left slightly ajar towards evening[^50] and they may be opened [for business].[^51] On Thursdays, [the stores] may be opened the entire day [to allow people to purchase food] in honor of the Sabbath.[^52] If a store has two entrances, one entrance should be opened and the other closed.[^53] If the store has a display area[^54] in front of it, it may be opened in the normal manner on a Thursday without concern [for the above restriction].[^55] ^3-7

**8.** If these [fasts] pass without [their prayers] being answered,[^56] we should minimize our commercial activity,[^57] construction projects associated with joy[^58] - e.g., those involving decorative patterns on the ceilings and walls, plantings[^59] associated with joy[^60] - e.g., that of myrtle trees, and the erection of tents.[^61]  
We also minimize betrothals and marriages,[^62] unless one has not fulfilled the mitzvah of being fruitful and multiplying.[^63] Whoever has fulfilled this mitzvah is forbidden to engage in sexual relations in a year of famine.[^64]  
We also reduce the exchange of greetings, and the Torah sages should not exchange greetings at all.[^65] Rather, [they should conduct themselves as people] who have been rebuffed and ostracized by God.[^66] When a common person greets them, they may return the greeting[^67] in a weak and concerned tone.[^68] ^3-8

**9.** The Torah scholars alone continue to fast,[^69] [beginning on the next] Monday, [and continuing on the following] Thursday and Monday[^70] [in this manner] until the month of Nisan[^71] - as determined in relation to the spring season[^72] - passes. This is not, however, required of the community. No more than these thirteen communal fasts are decreed because of a lack of rainfall.[^73]  
When these individuals fast until Nisan passes, they are allowed to eat at night[^74] and are allowed to perform work, to wash, to anoint themselves, to engage in sexual relations, and to wear shoes as on other fast days.[^75] They do not fast on Rashei Chodashim, nor on Purim.[^76]  
After the month of Nisan, as determined in relation to the spring season, passes[^77] - i.e., when the sun enters the sign[^78] of the bull[^79] - they should cease fasting. Since no rain has descended from the beginning of the year, rain in this season would be a sign of a curse.[^80] ^3-9

**10.** Where does the above apply? In *Eretz Yisrael* and in similar lands.[^81] In contrast, in places where the rainy season begins before or after the seventeenth of Marcheshvan,[^82] when the time [for the rains] arrives and no rain descends, individuals should [begin a series of three] fasts, [starting on] Monday [and continuing on the following] Thursday and Monday. They should not fast on Rashei Chodashim, Chanukah, or Purim.[^83]  
Afterwards, they should wait approximately seven days.[^84] If rain does not descend, the court should decree thirteen communal fasts, according to the order described above.[^85] ^3-10

**11.** [The following laws apply to] all the communal fasts decreed in the diaspora: It is permitted to eat during the night,[^86] and they are governed by the same laws as other fasts.[^87] A communal fast resembling Yom Kippur is decreed only in *Eretz Yisrael,*[^88] and only because of [a lack of] rain.[^89] This refers to the latter ten fasts - i.e., the middle series of three fasts and the final series of seven fasts. ^3-11

    The Rambam - and other commentaries and authorities, including the *Shulchan Aruch* (*Orach Chayim* 575:10) - explain that the law which applies to Babylon, applies to the entire diaspora. It is not that there can be no need for a communal fast of this nature, but rather that, outside of *Eretz Yisrael*, the Jewish court lacks the authority to declare such a fast. (See the Jerusalem Talmud, *Ta'anit* 2:1.)  
    The Ramban explains that the reason for the lack of authority is that there is no *Nasi* in the diaspora, and the declaration of a communal fast would require his approval. The Rashba states that the lack of authority stems from the lack of judges who have received the *semichah* (ordination) extending back in a chain to Moses. (See *Hilchot Sanhedrin*, Chapter 4.) According to these opinions, even in *Eretz Yisrael* today it is impossible to ordain communal fasts of this nature. Note the gloss of the *Birkei Yosef* on the *Shulchan Aruch* (*Orach Chayim* 575), where this concept is discussed and a rationale is given for fasting in *Eretz Yisrael* today.  
    (Homiletically, it can be explained that the unity necessary to establish a community is possible only in *Eretz Yisrael*. In contrast, in the diaspora our greater involvement with material affairs prevents the establishment of true oneness among our people.)
[^89]: Thus, such severe fasts are not ordained for any of the other difficulties mentioned in the second chapter. Although the Ramban differs with the Rambam on this point, the Rambam's view is accepted as halachah.
