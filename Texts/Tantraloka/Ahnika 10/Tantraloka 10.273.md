## Mūla

अपरं परापरं च द्विधा तत्सा परा त्वियम् ।
रूपकत्वादुदासीनाच्च्युतेयं पूर्णतोन्मुखी ॥ ^mula

aparaṃ parāparaṃ ca dvidhā tatsā parā tviyam |
rūpakatvādudāsīnāccyuteyaṃ pūrṇatonmukhī || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 14 - Chapter Ten, part 5#^pg489|p. 470]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=489>))
>
> The states of waking and rest are also the Lord’s power, which is of two kinds, namely inferior (apara) (as waking and dreaming) and middling (parāpara) (as deep sleep). This, (the Fourth State), is that supreme (para) (power). (with 10.272cd–273ab)
>
> Free of the (transcendental) detachment of the subject in deep sleep (rūpaka), this, (the Fourth state), tends towards fullness (pārnatonmukhī). Yogis (call) contemplative union (samāpatti) within it ‘Beyond Form’ (Rūpātīta) (which is the conscious subject). Tending towards fullness as it is, the man who meditates with intuitive insight (prasaṁikhyātr), at one with it, sees the universe gathered (within it into a single whole) (pracayatah). Thus this, (the Fourth State) is called ‘Accumulation’ (pracaya). (with 10.273cd–275ab)
