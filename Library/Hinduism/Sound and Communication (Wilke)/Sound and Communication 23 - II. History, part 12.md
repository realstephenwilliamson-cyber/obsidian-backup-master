The consonants are individuations (vyakti) of the vowels. According to Abhinavagupta they are a parable for the objects of the external world, which could not exist without the consciousness. ##### 5.8.5 The Audible Peacock's Tail

The example of the peacock’s tail was a clever choice by the music-lover Abhinavagupta. It makes it clear that at the level of discursive language there is always a loss — ultimately it is never possible to describe the overall color impression, no matter how painstaking the analysis.

##### 5.9.3 The Disappearance of Language in Music

For the great bhakti movements, which became very strong in India from about the eighth century AD, musical presentation is actually the norm. Innovative religious literatures arose over the last thousand years, above all in the bhakti, and the normal form of a bhakti text is a song. The movement seems to have started in Tamil Nadu in Southern India, with the itinerant singers of the Viṣṇuitic Ālvārs and the Śivaitic Nāyanmārs, who toured from temple to temple to offer their devotional songs to the deities. In addition to the unusual vernacular of these songs it was principally the very personal link between these singers and their deity which was new and went on to become so popular across India that we find bhakti poetry and songs in the most varied regional languages. Although they developed out of one indigenous impulse, it was probably not least the political situation in the following centuries that contributed to the total success of this vernacular literature. Most of all in Northern India under Muslim rule a ceremonial temple religion did not have such an easy time of it. The iconoclastic raging of characters

Instead of an elaborate ambience consisting of sacred architecture and religious statues, group singing and the praying of names took on major significance as means of creating sacred atmospheres. Furthermore, Bhajan rites are extremely multi-functional. They are praise to god, contemplation, entertainment and sometimes even “asceticism” — namely when the participants promise to praise the god by singing the whole night through. 

Apart from the preference for the medium of music, there is another new development in the bhakti literature. An individualization and personalization of the literature becomes tangible. The poets generally give their names in the last verse of their songs. This convention amongst the song-writers (including non-religious ones) certainly also had the function of a copyright. This is worthy of comment because of the contrast with the great religious Sanskrit works of literature such as the Vedas, epics and Purāṇas, which are all anonymous. An author in antiquity, e.g. a Purāṇic poet, would have considered his earthly self to be much too insignificant to be mentioned in something like a hymn to Śiva. In his next life he will be a completely different being, but Śiva is greater even than millennia of world cycles. Here we find that the bhakti saints make a radical break. They never speak about their worldly ego, but their own lives are definitely important when the point is their relationship to the deity and the role that it plays in their personal lives. Their close relationship to the deity allows them to participate in it. One can see the influence of the growing importance of Gurus. Even if Sanskrit Hinduism has lost much of its significance today, the history of religion continues, as we can see from modern gurus. Guru practice is an institution that cannot really be traced back to the language elite of the Veda-trained Brahmins and Brahmanized Hinduism. A guru does not necessarily have to have good knowledge of Sanskrit. He does not even have to be especially eloquent in the vernacular. Language competence is not the primary qualification by any means. What counts is charisma and the way that listeners feel emotionally affected by the personality of the guru. Contact with the guru is highly participatory. The guru’s followers seek in him a tangible god, so to speak. Possibly Nāda-Brahman only infiltrated Indian devotional literature and the popular Tantra as late as this time.
