### 2.1–5: Paul’s gospel

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 325* · Paul tailored his actions to illustrate the inverted roles of foolishness and divine wisdom by speaking with *weakness . . .  fear and . . .  trembling* rather than *lofty words* (1.18–31). ^2-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Mystery* (Heb “raz” or “sod”; some manuscripts have *testimony* \[Gk “martyrion”\]), the gospel (LXX [[LXX Daniel (Greek) 02#^2-30|Dan 2.30]]); Jewish traditions posit undisclosed divine secrets about the messiah’s identity (*b. Sanh.* 94a, which rereads [[Isaiah 24#^24-16|Isa 24.16]] “razi,” “my leanness,” as “my secret”). *Lofty words or wisdom,* characteristic of philosophers and orators. ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-3|3]]:** *Fear and . . .  trembling*, see \[LXX\][[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-16|Ex 15.16]]; [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^2-25|Deut 2.25]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-25|11.25]]; [[LXX Judith 02#^2-28|Jdt 2.28]], and elsewhere, possibly in response to hostile reception of the gospel. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Words of wisdom*, philosophy, mere sophistry (see 1.17). *Spirit and . . .  power*, i.e., of God, demonstrated through both Jesus’ resurrection and the congregation’s charismatic gifts. ^2-4

### 2.6–16: Divine wisdom

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-6|Text]], p. 325* ^2-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-6|6]]:** *Mature*, compare 3.1 *infants*; human development was a common metaphor for intellectual and spiritual progress in Jewish and other ancient literature (Philo, *Dreams* 2.234; Xenophon, *Cyropaedia* 1.2.3). *Rulers*, political leaders, angelic beings, or both (see Bar 3; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-4|2 Cor 4.4]]). For human vs. divine wisdom, see also Dan 2. *Of this age*, the pre-eschatological era. ^2-6-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Hidden*, primordially (see [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^29-28|Deut 29.29]]). The idea that God’s eschatological plans were *decreed before the ages* appears in apocalyptic and rabbinic literature ([[Daniel 12#^12-9|Dan 12.9]]; *1 En.* 68.1; *Tanh. Naso* 11; *b. Pesah.* 54a). On the primordial creation of Wisdom see, e.g., [[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22]]; [[LXX Sirach 01#^1-4|Sir 1.4]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-9|Wis 9.9]]. ^2-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-8|8]]:** Those who *crucified the Lord of glory* were ignorant of the divine plan (see [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|1 Thess 2.14–15]]). While the “glory of the Lord” is a common expression, *Lord of glory*, perhaps reflecting Heb “ʾel ha-kavod” in [[Psalms 029#^29-3|Ps 29.3]], is unique to this letter (cf. [[Acts 07#^7-2|Acts 7.2]]). ^2-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-9|9]]:** Similar to [[Isaiah 52#^52-15|Isa 52.15]]; [[Isaiah 64#^64-3|64.3]] (see also *b. Sanh.* 99a; *L. A. B.* 26.13), this saying is attributed to Jesus in the *Gos. Thom.* (17) and appears in Muslim tradition (*Sahih Bukhari* 9.93.589; *Sahih Muslim* 4.6783). Elsewhere, Paul only uses *as it is written* to introduce Scriptural passages ([[Romans 01#^1-17|Rom 1.17]]; [[2 Corinthians 08#^8-15|2 Cor 8.15]]). On the Heb version of “as it was written,” “kakatuv,” see 1.19n. *Love,* (Gk “agape”) unites the community (see 13.1-13). ^2-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Spirit*, the im-manent presence of God (Heb “ruaḥ”; LXX [[LXX Psalms 50#^50-11|Ps 50.11]], Gk “pneuma”) endows believers with gifts through their union with Christ (for a list of gifts, see [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-8|1 Cor 12.8–10]]); Spirit is identified with Wisdom (e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^31-3|Ex 31.3]]), which encompasses *the depths of God* ([[Job 11#^11-7|Job 11.7–8]]). The *Spirit* therefore relates to God as (ideally) one’s spiritual life relates to one’s self-knowledge. ^2-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Spirit of the world*, knowledge of this-worldly affairs; the expression does not appear elsewhere in the LXX or NT. *Gifts*, lit., “things graciously given,” include spirit-filled activities (12.1–31), forgiveness of sins (15.3), promise of resurrection (6.14), *of wisdom* d Or *interpreting spiritual things in spiritual language*, or *comparing spiritual things with spiritual* and salvation (15.2). ^2-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Not taught by human wisdom*, on human vs. divine speech, see Philo, *Worse* 39,44,133. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Unspiritual,* (Gk “psychikos,” breath-formed, or merely a living being) ranked below *spiritual* (2.15) (Gk “pneumatikos,” or spirit-formed; see 15.45 on the “first man, Adam . . . the last Adam”). The hierarchical contrast evokes both the process of creation and philosophical distinctions between air/breath/soul and heaven/spirit/mind ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]]; Philo, *Dreams* 1.16,20,30). ^2-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Discern,* (Gk “anakrinō,” judge)*,* a pun also meaning “interrogate” or “examine.” Holy ones, or saints, act as judges in the context of *interpreting spiritual things,* such as spiritual gifts and end-time revelation (2.13). ^2-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 02#^2-16|16]]:** *Mind*, see LXX [[LXX Isaiah 40#^40-13|Isa 40.13]], the sole instance of Heb “ruaḥ” rendered in the LXX as “nous” rather than “pneuma”; see also [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-13|Wis 9.13]]; [[Romans 11#^11-34|Rom 11.34]]. Paul equates knowing the *mind of the Lord* with having the *mind of Christ*. ^2-16
