*Volume 3, folio 180a* · Chukat

*Passage Chukat 2:4 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 3.179b#^chukat-2-4|Chukat 2:4]].*

**Chukat 2:5** The latter thereupon discoursed on the text: “Now this was the custom in former time in Israel concerning redeeming and concerning exchanging, to confirm all things”, etc. (Ruth 4, 7). ‘This verse’, he said, ‘raises a problem. If the ancients adopted this custom on the basis of the Torah, and later generations abolished it, how could they do so, seeing that to abolish a thing laid down in the Torah is like laying waste the whole world? If, again, it was not an injunction of the Torah, but a mere custom, why was a shoe chosen for the purpose? ^chukat-2-5

**Chukat 2:6** The truth is that this was enjoined originally by the Torah, and because the ancients were pious and virtuous this thing was revealed to them, but when sinners multiplied the thing was done in a different way, in order to conceal matters which have a high mystic significance. ^chukat-2-6

**Chukat 2:7** Now when God said to Moses, “Draw not nigh hither” (Ex. 3, 5), He also said, “Put off thy shoes from off thy feet”; and it has been explained that by these words He enjoined him to part from his wife and attach himself to another wife of holy supernal radiance, to wit the Shekinah. ^chukat-2-7

**Chukat 2:8** and the drawing off of the shoe removed him from this world and placed him in another world. ^chukat-2-8

**Chukat 2:9** Similarly with a dead man who has departed from the world without children. ^chukat-2-9

**Chukat 2:10** The Shekinah does not gather him in, and he is driven to and fro about the world, but God has pity on him and bids his brother redeem him so that he may be set right by means of other dust. ^chukat-2-10

**Chukat 2:11** Now if that redeemer is not willing to establish seed for his brother in this world, he must tie a shoe on his foot and the wife must loosen it and take it to herself. Why was a shoe chosen for this purpose? Because the shoe was the support of the dead man in this world, and the woman, by taking it, signifies that the dead man who was wandering about among the living ^chukat-2-11

**Chukat 2:12** will now through that shoe no longer wander about among them. ^chukat-2-12

**Chukat 2:13** She must dash the shoe on the ground to show that she has laid to rest the body of the dead, and God then, or after a time, has pity on him and receives him into the future world. ^chukat-2-13

**Chukat 2:14** Therefore it was that whoever desired to confirm an undertaking took off his shoe and gave it to his neighbour. This was beforetime in Israel when they were pious and holy, ^chukat-2-14

**Chukat 2:15** but when sinners multiplied they concealed the matter under another form, using the corner of a garment. ^chukat-2-15
