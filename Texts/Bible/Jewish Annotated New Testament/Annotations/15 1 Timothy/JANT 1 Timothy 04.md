# 1 Timothy 4 (JANT notes)

[[JANT 1 Timothy 03|← 1 Timothy 3]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 1 Timothy 05|1 Timothy 5 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04|NRSVue]] · [[1 Timothy 04|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 Timothy - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-1|4.1–16]]:** **Practice.** ^4-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-1|1]]:** *The Spirit expressly says*, perhaps a reference to words of prophecy. *Later times*, or “the last times” (see translators’ note *f* ): the period leading up to the end of the present age. ^4-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-3|3]]:** *Forbid marriage*, an extension of Paul’s preference for celibacy (see 2.13–15n.). *Abstinence from foods*, perhaps a vestige of Pauline debates over Jewish dietary regulations ([[Romans 14#^14-2|Rom 14.2]]); perhaps a concern for fasting ([[Mark 02#^2-18|Mk 2.18]]). ^4-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-4|4–5]]:** *Everything . . . sanctified*, all foods are licit (see [[Mark 07#^7-19|Mk 7.19]]; [[Acts 10#^10-15|Acts 10.15]]). Gen 1 proclaims that *Which* d Or *justified* e Or *by the Spirit* f Or *the last* everything created is good. ^4-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-4|4]]:** *Created by God*, the author returns to Genesis to counter asceticism. ^4-4-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-5|5]]:** *Sanctified*, lit., “made holy”; things holy are set apart for God. *God’s word*, i.e., command (Gk “logos”), is the means by which all that God created was good ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-31|Gen 1.31]]). ^4-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-7|7]]:** *Myths* (lit., “profane” \[things\]), see 1.4–5nn. *Old wives’ tales* (lit., “oldwomanish myths”), or worse, demonic (v 1). Labeling one’s opponents’ teachings “demonic” or “false” was a known first-century practice (e.g., [[John 08#^8-44|Jn 8.44–47]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-22|Wis 14.22–28]]; 1QpHab 14.22). *Godliness*, lit., “good (or proper) reverence” (Gk “eusebeia”; the same word is translated “religion” in 3.16). ^4-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-8|8]]:** *Physical training*, a Greco-Roman cultural idea, part of the good life; the point is that spiritual training (reverence) is valuable not only in the present but also in the *life to come*. ^4-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-9|9]]:** *Saying is sure*, see Intro. ^4-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-10|10]]:** *Living God* (Heb “Elohim ḥayyim”), [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-23|Deut 5.26]]; [[Joshua 03#^3-10|Josh 3.10]]; [[I Samuel 17#^17-26|1 Sam 17.26]],[[I Samuel 17#^17-36|36]]; [[Psalms 042#^42-3|Ps 42.2]]; [[Psalms 084#^84-3|84.2]], etc. *Savior of all people*, that the God of Israel is the Savior of all people follows from Gen (see 1.31) that all God’s creations are good. See also [[Isaiah 45#^45-22|Isa 45.22–23]]. ^4-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-13|13]]:** *Public reading of scripture*, see [[Nehemiah 07#^7-72|Neh 7.73b–8.8]]; [[Luke 04#^4-16|Lk 4.16–20]]. “Qeriʾat HATORAH,” “reading of the Torah” was Jewish practice and was taken over by Jesus’ followers; it had limited biblical precedence ([[Torah 52 - Vayeilech#^31-10|Deut 31.10–13]]; [[Joshua 08#^8-34|Josh 8.34–35]]; [[II Kings 23#^23-1|2 Kings 23.1–3]]; Neh 8), but by the first century ce, was the core of the synagogue service (see Josephus, *Ag. Ap*. 2.175 and “The Synagogue,” p. 662). See also *m. Seqal.* 1.1, “On the fifteenth day of that month they read the Megillah \[Scroll of Esther\] in walled cities” (and *m. Meg*. passim); *m. Yoma* 7.1, “The high priest came to read \[in the Women’s court\]; *m. Taʾan*. 4.3; *m. Git.* 5.8, etc. ^4-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-14|14]]:** *Prophecy* . . . *elders* how a ministry was authorized and initiated (see [[Acts 06#^6-6|Acts 6.6]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-6|2 Tim 1.6]]). See also 3.1n. *Laying on of hands*, for consecration (see [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^29-15|Ex 29.15]] for sacrifice), commission ([[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-9|Deut 34.9]] regarding Joshua); for healing ([[Mark 09#^9-18|Mk 9.18]]). *Elders*, (Gk “presbyteroi”) were, along with the bishop (see 3.1n.), leaders of local congregations. Jewish Diaspora communities had “archisynagōgoi” or “rulers of the synagogue” (see [[Mark 05#^5-22|Mk 5.22]]; see “The Synagogue,” p. 662), but their roles are more likely to be of patrons rather than of sanctioned officials. ^4-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 04#^4-16|16]]:** *Will save*, from sin and to eternal life. ^4-16
