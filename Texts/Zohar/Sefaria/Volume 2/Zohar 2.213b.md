# Zohar 2.213b

← [[Zohar 2.213a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 2.214a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 6.05 - Parashat Va-Yaqhel#^z2-213b|Parashat Va-Yaqhel, at 2:213b]]

*Volume 2, folio 213b* · Vayakhel

*Passage Vayakhel 25:363 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 2.213a#^vayakhel-25-363|Vayakhel 25:363]].*

**Vayakhel 25:364** Happy is the portion of whoever can penetrate into the mysteries of his Master and become absorbed into Him, as it were. ^vayakhel-25-364

**Vayakhel 25:365** Especially does a man achieve this when he offers up his prayer to his Master in intense devotion, his will then becoming as the flame inseparable from the coal, and his mind concentrated on the unity of the lower firmaments, to unify them by means of a lower name, then on the unity of the higher firmaments, and finally on the absorption of them all into that most high firmament. ^vayakhel-25-365

**Vayakhel 25:366** Whilst a man’s mouth and lips are moving, his heart and will must soar to the height of heights, so as to acknowledge the unity of the whole in virtue of the mystery of mysteries in which all ideas, all wills and all thoughts find their goal, to wit, the mystery of the *En-sof* (Infinite, Illimitable). We should repeat the same endeavour at each prayer, so as to adorn each day with the crown of the mystery of its corresponding supernal day, by means of our prayer. ^vayakhel-25-366

**Vayakhel 25:367** At night a man should represent to himself that he is about to quit this world, and that his soul will leave him and return to the Master of all. For every night the Supreme Point absorbs in itself the souls of the righteous. ^vayakhel-25-367

**Vayakhel 25:368** Here is a recondite truth for the initiate. The lower firmament, as mentioned above, is sustained by that Point. That firmament, moreover, is a fusion of the upper and the lower worlds, having its basis in the lower world, much as the dark flame of a lamp merges into the white flame above while having its basis below in a wick sunk in oil. So in the daytime that Point is essentially above, but in the night it is essentially below, becoming absorbed in the souls of the righteous, since at night all things return to their original root and source. ^vayakhel-25-368

**Vayakhel 25:369** So the soul mounts up, returning to its source, whilst the body lies still as a stone, thus reverting to its own source of origin. ^vayakhel-25-369

**Vayakhel 25:370** Whilst in that state the body is beset by the influences of the “other side”, with the result that its hands become defiled and remain so until they are washed in the morning, as explained elsewhere. The souls of the righteous, in ascending in the night into their own celestial spheres, are woven into a crown, as it were, with which the Holy One, blessed be He, adorns Himself. ^vayakhel-25-370

**Vayakhel 25:371** There are night attendants who have charge of those souls, take them up on high and offer them up as an acceptable sacrifice to their Master. The supreme chieftain of those legions bears the name of Suriya, and each soul, as it passes through all the firmaments, is first brought before him, and he inhales its scent, as it says: “And he will inhale the scent of the fear of the Lord” (Isa. 11, 3). He takes them under his charge, and passes them on higher, until they arrive at the place of sacrifice. ^vayakhel-25-371

**Vayakhel 25:372** There all the souls are absorbed in the Supreme Point; as a woman conceives a child, so does the Supreme Point conceive them, experiencing a rapturous pleasure in absorbing in itself the souls with all their good deeds and Torah studies performed during the past day. ^vayakhel-25-372

**Vayakhel 25:373** The souls then re-emerge, that is to say, they are born anew, each soul being fresh and new as at its former birth. This is the inner meaning of the words, “They are new every morning; great is thy faithfulness” (Lam. 3, 23). That is to say, “they (the souls) are new every morning”, because “great is thy faithfulness” to absorb them and then let them out as newly-born. Happy are the righteous in this world and in the world to come.’ ^vayakhel-25-373
