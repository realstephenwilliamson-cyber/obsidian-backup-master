## About

A medieval Sanskrit text of the tantric Shakta tradition, framed as a dialogue in which Dattatreya teaches Parashurama. *Rahasya* means secret or mystery; *Tripura* refers to the goddess Tripura Sundari and to the three states of waking, dream and deep sleep, hence renderings such as "the mystery beyond the three cities".

- **Attribution:** also called the Haritāyana Saṃhitā, after Haritāyana, son of Hārita. In the traditional chain, Shiva taught Vishnu, who as Dattatreya taught Parashurama, who taught Haritāyana.
- **Date:** between the 11th and 17th centuries CE, according to Rigopoulos (1998), as cited by Wikipedia. The World Wisdom publisher's page simply calls it ancient.
- **Structure:** said to have 12,000 ślokas in three parts: the Jñāna Khaṇḍa, on wisdom (6,687), the Māhātmya Khaṇḍa, on the greatness of the Goddess (2,163), and a third part, on conduct, that cannot be traced.
- **Teaching:** it mixes stories with argument (World Wisdom). Wikipedia compares it with Advaita Vedanta and Kashmir Shaivism's Pratyabhijñā, but notes that, unlike post-Shankara Advaita, it does not treat the world as illusion (māyā). Ananda Coomaraswamy, quoted by the publisher, likened it to Plato's *Republic* for its ideal City of Resplendent Wisdom.
- **English translation:** Ramana Maharshi often quoted it and regretted that it was not in English, so his disciple Munagala S. Venkataramaiah (later Swami Ramanananda Saraswathi) began a translation in 1936. Wikipedia says it ran in the Mythic Society's journal from January 1938 to April 1940 and lists the book as 1960; the Penn catalogue has Sri Ramanasramam, Tiruvannamalai, 1960. World Wisdom, whose edition is titled *The Secret of the Supreme Goddess*, says it was first published in 1938 and republished in 1959.
- **Other versions:** Wikipedia also lists Gopinath Kaviraj (1925), Rajmani Tigunait (1993) and T. B. Lakshmana Rao's Māhātmya Khaṇḍa with English (2011).
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia presents it as a Shakta teaching on liberation, naming the Absolute Shiva and its manifestation Shakti; World Wisdom notes it was a favourite of Ramana Maharshi.
