B

For *upāsanā* with mantras, we have already listed the forms of the mantras. The mantra *ōm* is to be recited alone—this leads to yoga. Other mantras—*ōm hrīm strīm hum phaṭ*, *hrīm strīm hum phaṭ*, *hrīm strīm hum*—can be practiced by a *parāśakti* and *parama-puruṣa*, in an attitude of eliminating the illusions of others.[^473]

In Tārā *upāsanā*, unlike Kali *upāsanā*, if physical union is to be used, it must be with a *parāśakti* not a *sva-śakti*,[^474] because there is to be no ejaculation—in Buddhist union, seed emission is forbidden. In Hindu union, seed emission is allowed only after repetition of the required number of mantras is complete. In Tārā, the two religions meet. Both insist on a *parāśakti*, and both forbid ejaculation by the male, for the very reason that it *is* a *parāśakti*. Kali is Kundalini, so a *sva-shakti* is okay; but for Tārā, only a *parāśakti*.
