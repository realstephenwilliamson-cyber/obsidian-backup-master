Thus being born of Cukhulaka, resident of Kashmir, I, a black bee at the lotus-feet of Maheśvara, intent on lifting up mankind looking upto me (for spiritual succour), have written this commentary pregnant with the deliberation ofthe mystery of Trika.( 1 ) 

Who can estimate emphatically that so much only is the doctrine pertaining to Śiva? There is no bar to words in this matter. All that which is within my comprehension appears here regarding the Universal Spirit (akhilātmani). Therefore, the wise should not be averse to it. (2) 

This is a work of such nature that it makes firm the knowledge of the ignorant, of one who is full of doubts or of one who has contrary views. In the case of those in whom conviction has already started, in whom it is fully grown (rādhasya), it makes the settled conviction of their heart harmonize with its teaching. (3) 

In Kashmir, there was the chief minister of the king, Yaśsaskara. He was named Vallabhācārya. He was a Brahmin of the most excellent lincage. His son, Sauri is worthy of renown on account of all his good qualities and is like the ocean in dedication to the feet of the crescent-crested Siva, is the abode of virtue, one who fully deserves the great fame that has spread about him, is a pleasant object of affection and who has an inborn tendency towards compassion on all people.
