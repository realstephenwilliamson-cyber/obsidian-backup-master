### 10.1–11.18: Conversion of Cornelius

*[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 243* · The conversion of the Roman centurion marks another narrative turning point: now the church accepts Gentiles. ^10-1

### 10.1–33: Peter and Cornelius

*[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 243* · Visions, angels, and heavenly voices underscore God’s direction of the conversion of Gentiles. ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Caesarea*, see 8.40n. *Centurion of the Italian Cohort*, an officer in the Roman army who led several hundred soldiers. The Gospel also includes a story of a Roman centurion who is friendly toward Jews and interested in Jewish practices ([[Luke 07#^7-1|Lk 7.1–10]]). ^10-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Devout*, adheres to Jewish ideas and practices. Other Gentiles show similar interest in Jewish practices (see [[Luke 07#^7-1|Lk 7.1–10]]; [[Acts 13#^13-16|Acts 13.16]],[[Acts 13#^13-26|26]]; [[Acts 16#^16-14|16.14]]; [[Acts 17#^17-4|17.4]],[[Acts 17#^17-12|12]]; [[Acts 18#^18-7|18.7]]). ^10-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-3|3]]:** *Three o’clock*, see 3.1. *Vision*, see 9.10. *Angel*, see 5.19; 8.26; 12.7; 27.23. ^10-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-4|4]]:** *Lord*, see 9.5n. *Ascended as a memorial*, language frequently used of sacrifice ([[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^2-2|Lev 2.2]]; 5.12; [[Torah 25 - Tzav#^6-8|6.15]]; [[LXX Sirach 38#^38-11|Sir 38.11]]). ^10-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-9|9]]:** *Noon*, expected time to serve meals (*b. Shabb.* 10a). ^10-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-13|13–15]]:** *Voice*, the divine presence is heard, not seen; see 9.4,11. ^10-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-14|14]]:** *Profane or unclean*, Peter affirms his observance of Jewish dietary practices (Lev 11). ^10-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-19|19]]:** *Spirit*, on communication by Spirit, see 1.16; 2.4; 6.10; 13.2; 16.6; 21.11; 28.5. ^10-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-22|22]]:** *Well spoken of*, similarly describes Gamaliel (5.34). ^10-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-28|28]]:** *Unlawful for a Jew to associate with or to visit a Gentile*, while some Jews may have concurred with Peter’s statement (e.g., *Jub.* 22.16), most would have had regular contacts with Gentiles, while probably taking care to avoid idolatry and prohibited foods (e.g., *Ep. Arist.* 172–86; *m. Avod. Zar.* 3.4). Nonetheless, the idea that Jews did not associate with Gentiles is common (e.g., Philo, *Spec*. *Laws* 2.167; Tacitus, *Hist.* 5.1–13). *Not call anyone profane*, Peter interprets his vision as applying to people, not food. ^10-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-30|30]]:** *Dazzling clothes*, attribute of angelic being ([[Luke 24#^24-4|Lk 24.4]]; [[Revelation 19#^19-8|Rev 19.8]]). ^10-30

### 10.34–43: Peter’s speech

*[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-34|Text]], p. 244* · The speech summarizes major themes in Acts: Jesus is the prophesied anointed one and Lord (2.36; 4.33; 8.16; 15.11; 16.31; 19.5; 28.31); his death has been vindicated by God (2.24,32; 3.15; 4.10; 5.30; 13.30); the apostles are chosen witnesses to proclaim Jesus (1.8; 2.32; 3.15; 13.31; 23.11; 26.16); all experience forgiveness of sins through believing in Jesus (2.38; 5.31; 13.38; 26.18). ^10-34

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-34|34]]:** *No partiality*, God’s attribute as impartial judge ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-17|Deut 10.17–18]]; [[LXX Sirach 35#^35-12|Sir 35.12–13]]) now applies to God’s dealings with Jews and Gentiles ([[Romans 02#^2-11|Rom 2.11]]). ^10-34-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-35|35]]:** *Fears him*, see [[Psalms 015#^15-1|Ps 15.1–2]]. Cornelius models this description, vv 2,22. Fearing God and doing right echo the Jewish teaching that the most important commandments are loving God (Deut 6) and one’s neighbor (Lev 19); see Philo, *Spec*. *Laws* 2.63; *T. Dan* 5.3. God’s people are no longer constituted by the ethnic division between Jew and Gentile but by a religious distinction, those who fear God, as manifested by accepting Jesus as Lord, and do what is right, and those who neither believe nor act appropriately. ^10-35

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-36|36]]:** *Preaching peace*, the message of reconciliation ([[2 Corinthians 05#^5-18|2 Cor 5.18–19]]). *Lord of all*, title of pagan gods (Plutarch, *Is. Os.* 355E), Roman emperors (Epictetus, *Diatr.* 4.1.12), and God ([[Joshua 03#^3-11|Josh 3.11]]; [[Psalms 097#^97-5|Ps 97.5]]; [[Zechariah 06#^6-5|Zech 6.5]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-7|Wis 6.7]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 08#^8-3|8.3]]). ^10-36

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-39|39]]:** *They*, most likely meant as Jews/Jewish word b Gk *he* c Gk *brothers* leaders held responsible for the crucifixion (2.23; 3.15; 5.30). *Tree*, the cross (see 5.30; 13.29). ^10-39

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-41|41]]:** *Ate and drank with him*, see [[Luke 24#^24-30|Lk 24.30]],[[Luke 24#^24-42|42–43]],[[Luke 24#^24-48|48]]. ^10-41

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-43|43]]:** *All the prophets*, unclear which ones (see [[Isaiah 33#^33-24|Isa 33.24]]; [[Isaiah 55#^55-7|55.7]]; [[Jeremiah 31#^31-34|Jer 31.34]]). *His name*, that of Jesus (2.38; [[Luke 24#^24-47|Lk 24.47]]). ^10-43

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-44|44–48]]:** The scene repeats the events of Pentecost: Spirit poured out (2.17–18), speaking in tongues (2.4–11), astonished reaction (2.12); but now offered to Gentiles (v 45). ^10-44

**[[NRSVue Acts 10#^10-44|44]]:** *Holy Spirit*, confirming sign of divine favor and transformation (see 1.8; 2.4; 4.31). ^10-44-2
