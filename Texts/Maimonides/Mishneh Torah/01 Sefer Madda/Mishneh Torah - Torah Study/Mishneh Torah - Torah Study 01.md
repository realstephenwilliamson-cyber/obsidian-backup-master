**1.** Women,[^1] slaves,[^2] and minors[^3] are free from the obligation of Torah study.[^4] Nevertheless,[^5] a father is obligated[^6] to teach his son Torah[^7] while he is a minor, as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-19|Deuteronomy 11:19]]] states: "And you shall teach them to your sons to speak about them."  
A[^8] woman is not obligated to teach her son,[^9] for only those who are obligated to learn are obligated to teach.[^10] ^1-1

**2.** Just as a person is obligated to teach his son, so, too, is he obligated to teach his grandson,[^11] as \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-9|Deuteronomy 4:9]]] commands: "And you shall teach them to your sons and your grandsons."  
[Furthermore,[^12] this charge is not confined] to one's children and grandchildren alone. Rather, it is a mitzvah for each and every wise man[^13] to teach all students, even though they are not his children, as \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-7|Deuteronomy 6:7]]] states: "And you shall teach them to your sons..." The oral tradition[^14] explains: "Your sons," these are your students, for students are also called sons,[^15] as \[[[II Kings 02#^2-3|II Kings 2:3]]] states: "And the sons of the prophets went forth."  
If[^16] so, why do the commandments[^17] [explicitly mention] one's son and grandson? To grant precedence[^18] to one's son over one's grandson, and one's grandson over the son of a colleague.[^19] ^1-2

**3.** Also,[^20] one is obligated to hire a teacher for one's son,[^21] while one is not required to undertake any expense to teach a colleague's son.[^22]  
A person who was not instructed by his father is obligated to arrange for his own instruction[^23] when he can understand,[^24] as \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-1|Deuteronomy 5:1]]] states: "And you shall study them and take heed to perform them."  
Similarly, in every place, one finds that study takes precedence over deed,[^25] for study brings about deed.[^26] However, deed does not bring about study.[^27] ^1-3

**4.** If a person wants to study Torah and he has a son whom he should teach Torah,[^28] his [study] takes priority over [that of] his son.[^29] If his son is wiser and a more creative thinker and thus capable of understanding what he studies more than he [himself] is, his son is given priority.[^30]  
Even though his son is granted priority, he should not neglect [his own studies].[^31] For just as he is commanded to teach his son, he is commanded to teach himself.[^32] ^1-4

**5.** A person should always[^33] study Torah and, afterwards, marry.[^34] If he marries first, his mind will not be free for study.[^35] However, if his natural inclination overcomes him to the extent that his mind is not free,[^36] he should marry, and then study Torah.[^37] ^1-5

**6.** At what age is a father obligated to teach [his son][^38] Torah?  
When he[^39] begins to speak, he[^40] should teach him[^41] *Torah tzivah lanu Moshe...*[^42] ([[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^33-4|Deuteronomy 33:4]]) and *Shema Yisrael[^43]...* (*ibid.* 6:4).  
Afterwards, he should teach him [selected verses],[^44] little by little, verse by verse, until he is six or seven[^45] - depending on his health[^46] - [at which time] he should take him[^47] to a teacher of young children.[^48] ^1-6

**7.** If it is local custom for a teacher of young children to take payment, he should be paid.[^49] [The father] is obligated to pay for his instruction until he can read the entire[^50] written Torah.[^51]  
In a place where it is customary to receive a wage for teaching the written Torah, one is permitted to do so.[^52] However, it is forbidden to take a wage for teaching the Oral Law,[^53] as [implied by [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-5|Deuteronomy 4:5]]]: "Behold, I have taught you laws and statutes, as God commanded me."[^54] [Our Sages teach that Moses was implying:] Just as I learned at no cost,[^55] so, too, have you been taught from me at no cost.[^56] Teach the coming generations in a like manner. Teach them at no cost as you have learned from[^57] me."  
[Nevertheless,] if a person cannot find someone to teach him at no cost, he must pay for his studies, as [implied by [[Proverbs 23#^23-23|Proverbs 23:23]]]: "Buy truth."[^58] May he charge to teach others? We learn [*ibid.*]: "but do not sell." Thus, it can be derived that it is forbidden to charge to teach Torah even though one's teacher charged to instruct him.[^59] ^1-7

**8.** Every Jewish man is obligated to study Torah,[^60] whether he is poor or rich,[^61] whether his body is healthy and whole or afflicted by difficulties,[^62] whether he is young[^63] or an old man whose strength has diminished.[^64]  
Even if he is a poor man who derives his livelihood from charity and begs from door to door, even if he is a husband and [a father of] children,[^65] he must establish a fixed time for Torah study during the day and at night, as \[[[Joshua 01#^1-8|Joshua 1:8]]] commands: "You shall think about it day and night."[^66] ^1-8

**9.** The greater Sages of Israel included wood choppers,[^67] water drawers,[^68] and blind men.[^69] Despite these [difficulties],[^70] they were occupied with Torah study day and night and were included among those who transmitted the Torah's teachings from [master] to [student in the chain stretching back to] Moses, our teacher.[^71] ^1-9

**10.** Until when is a person obligated to study Torah? Until the day he dies,[^72] as \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-9|Deuteronomy 4:9]]] states: "Lest you remove it from your heart, *all* the days of your life."[^73] Whenever a person is not involved with study, he forgets.[^74] ^1-10

**11.** A person is obligated to divide his study time in three: one third should be devoted to the Written Law; one third to the Oral Law;[^75] and one third to understanding and conceptualizing the ultimate derivation of a concept from its roots, inferring one concept from another and comparing concepts,[^76] understanding [the Torah] based on the principles of Biblical exegesis,[^77] until one appreciates the essence of those principles and how the prohibitions and the other decisions which one received according to the oral tradition[^78] can be derived using them. The latter topic is called *Gemara*.[^79] ^1-11

**12.** How is the above expressed?[^80] A person who is a craftsman may spend three hours each day involved in his work, and [devote] nine hours to Torah study:[^81] In those nine hours, he should spend three reading the Written Law; three, the Oral Law; and three, meditating with his intellect to derive one concept from another.[^82]  
The "words of the prophetic tradition"[^83] are considered part of the Written Law;[^84] and their explanation,[^85] part of the Oral Law. The matters referred to as *Pardes*[^86] are considered part of the *Gemara*.[^87]  
The above applies in the early stages of a person's study.[^88] However, when a person increases his knowledge and does not have the need to read the Written Law, or occupy himself with the Oral Law constantly,[^89] he should study the Written Law and the oral tradition at designated times. Thus, he will not forget any aspect of the laws of the Torah.[^90] [However,] he should focus his attention[^91] on the *Gemara* alone for his entire life,[^92] according to his ambition and his ability to concentrate. ^1-12

**13.** A woman who studies Torah will receive reward.[^93] However, that reward will not be [as great] as a man's, since she was not commanded [in this mitzvah]. Whoever performs a deed which he is not commanded to do, does not receive as great a reward as one who performs a mitzvah that he is commanded to do.[^94][^95]  
Even though she will receive a reward, the Sages commanded that a person should not teach his daughter Torah,[^96] because most women[^97] cannot concentrate their attention on study, and thus transform the words of Torah into idle matters because of their lack of understanding.[^98]  
[Thus,] our Sages declared:[^99] "Whoever teaches his daughter Torah is like one who teaches her tales and parables."[^100] This applies to the Oral Law. [With regard to] the Written Law: at the outset, one should not teach one's daughter. However, if one teaches her, it is not considered as if she was taught idle things.[^101] ^1-13
