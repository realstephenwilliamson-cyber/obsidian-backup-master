# Zohar 1.229b

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 3.07 - Parashat Va-Yhi#^z1-229b|Parashat Va-Yḥi, at 1:229b]]

*Volume 1, folio 229b* · Vayechi

*Passage Vayechi 38:409 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.229a#^vayechi-38-409|Vayechi 38:409]].*

**Vayechi 39:410** As R. Hizkiah and R. Jose and R. Judah were once journeying together, R. Jose said: ‘Let each one of us give some exposition of the Torah.’ R. Judah thereupon began with the verse, “Remember not against us the iniquities of our forefathers, let thy tender mercies speedily prevent us” (Ps. 79, 8). He said: ‘God in His great love for Israel allows no one to sit in judgement on them save Himself, and when He tries them, He is filled with compassion for them like a father for his children, and when He finds they have done wrong, He removes their offences one by one until there are none left to place them in the power of the other side. ^vayechi-39-410

**Vayechi 39:411** Hence it says, “let thy mercies prevent us”, because otherwise Israel would not be able to exist, in face of all the accusers and all the adversaries who are lying in wait for them above. Hence it continues “for we are very poor”, that is, poor in good deeds in the sight of God. ^vayechi-39-411

**Vayechi 39:412** For were Israel rich in good deeds before God, idolatrous nations would not be able to exist in the world. It is Israel who enable other nations to hold their head high, because but for their sins the nations would be subdued before them. ^vayechi-39-412

**Vayechi 39:413** And, as we have already said, had not Israel by their sins brought the other side into the Holy Land, the idolatrous nations would not have gained possession of it, and Israel would not have been exiled from their land. Hence, because “we are brought very low”, therefore “let thy tender mercies speedily prevent us”.’ ^vayechi-39-413

**Vayechi 40:414** R. Jose discoursed on the verse: “Serve the Lord with gladness, come before his presence with singing” (Ps. 100, 2). He said: ‘The service of prayer offered by man to the Holy One, blessed be He, should be carried out with gladness and with singing, ^vayechi-40-414

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayechi 40:415.*

**Vayechi 40:416** so that he may associate with him the Community of Israel; and then he should proclaim the unity in the fitting manner, as it says: “Know ye that the Lord he is God” (*Ibid.* 3). ^vayechi-40-416

**Vayechi 40:417** These two activities of gladness and song correspond to the two prayers of morning and afternoon, and to the two daily sacrifices-gladness in the morning and singing in the afternoon. ^vayechi-40-417

**Vayechi 40:418** The evening prayer, on the other hand, is optional, because at that time she (the Shekinah) is distributing sustenance to all her hosts, and it is not the time for blessing. In the daytime she is to be blessed from these two sides, morning and afternoon, out of gladness and singing, and at night time she divides the blessings among all in the fitting manner.’ ^vayechi-40-418

**Vayechi 41:419** R. Hizkiah took for his text the verse: “Let my prayer be established like incense before thee, the lifting of my hands like the evening oblation” (Ps. 141, 2). He said: ‘It may be asked, why did David mention here the oblation of the evening rather than of the morning? The answer may be given as follows. The offering of incense betokens joy, as it is written, “oil and incense rejoice the heart” (Prov. 27, 9). Hence the high priest, when he lit the candlestick, used to offer incense morning and evening (Ex. 30, 7, 8); in the morning, because that is the natural season of joy, and in the evening to rejoice the left side, as befits. We see, then, that incense always betokened joy. ^vayechi-41-419
