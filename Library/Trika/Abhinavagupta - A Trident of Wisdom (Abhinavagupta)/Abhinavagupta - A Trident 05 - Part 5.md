EXPOSITION

In the previous description, Abhinavagupta has shown how the various phases of nādātmaka I-consciousness represented by the first sixteen letters of Mālini are represented in the Mātrkā order with reference to para or supreme state of Siva tattva.

In this, he has shown how the various phases of aham-vimarsa of Ŝiva tattva represented by the first sixteen letters of Mālini are represented in the Mātrkā order with reference to parāpara-samvitti or supreme-cum-non supreme state of Śiva tattva.

Of the sixteen letters, seven viz. y, f, l, l, i, u and ā are bija letters or vowels. As such, they are aspects of Śiva both in Mālini and Maātrkā and therefore undergo no change. They have the same function in parāpara as in para.

The phonemes, according to Trika philosophy, have three aspects : sarvāgrarūpatā, sarvamadhyarūpatā and sarvāntagāmitā. Sarvā-grarūpatā refers to a phoneme as it is in itself. Sarvamadhyată refers to phonemes undergoing transformation, and sarvāntagāmitā refers to phonemes reaching finally the state of Suddhavidyā, Iśvara, Sadāśiva and Śakti.

Six consonants of Mālini, viz. na, tha, ca, dha, na and ba are reflected in Mātrkā in different phonemes in parāpara state under the law of sarvamadhya-rūpatā as shown in the chart.

If one were to object, “‘in that undifferentiated mass of consciousness, how can there be a division like: this is the organ of utterance, this the articulation, this the letter etc.?” my reply would be: “that unbounded, absolute freedom brings about in the supreme Self of every one different apprehensions like ‘this is a jar’ (external object); ‘this is happiness’ (internal object); ‘this is knowledge’ (pramāṇa); ‘I am the knower’ (pramātā or subject).” In this diversity of experiences what and how much is the exertion?”
