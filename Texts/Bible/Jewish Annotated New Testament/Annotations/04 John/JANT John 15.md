# John 15 (JANT notes)

[[JANT John 14|← John 14]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT John 16|John 16 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue John 15|NRSVue]] · [[John 15|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-1|15.1–6]]:** Often seen as a parable, similar to a Jewish “mashal,” though John, unlike the Synoptics, does not explicitly use the term “parable.” ^15-1

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-1|1]]:** *Vine*, common image for God’s people; see [[Isaiah 05#^5-1|Isa 5.1–10]]. This is an example of replacement theology, where John has Jesus take onto himself central Jewish institutions such as the Temple (see 2.13–22) and symbols (as here). ^15-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-3|3]]:** *Cleansed*, or “pruned” (see translators’ note *b*), perhaps referring to the foot washing in ch 13. ^15-3

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-5|5]]:** *Vine . . . branches*, the branches are part of the vine. cleansing ^15-5

> [!note] Advocate (Paraclete) (JN 14.26) *(p. 208)*
> The term “paraclete” can be both a legal term (advocate) and a relational term (comforter). In the Fourth Gospel, Jesus promises the paraclete to the disciples as the one who will continue to teach and guide them after Jesus returns to the Father. The Farewell Discourses associate the paraclete with the divine spirit (14.17,26; 15.26; 16.13). In the Tanakh, the term “spirit” (Heb *ruaḥ*) can refer to divine power as well as the breath of life, which God breathed into a lump of clay to animate the first human being ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]]), and which Jesus breathed on his disciples in [[John 20#^20-22|Jn 20.22]]. Although the Fourth Gospel does not present a well-developed Trinitarian view, according to which God, the Son, and the Spirit together constitute the Divine, the paraclete passages imply that a process of differentiation between God and Spirit was already taking place in theological reflection.

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-6|6]]:** See [[Matthew 03#^3-10|Mt 3.10]]. ^15-6

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-7|7]]:** *Ask for whatever you wish*, this is not carte blanche but likely refers forward to v 8, which mentions the expectation that they will bear much fruit and become disciples. Insofar as they are part of the “vine” that is Jesus, their requests pertinent to bearing fruit will be granted. ^15-7

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-13|13]]:** *Lay down one’s life*, dying for one’s friends appears in Aristotle’s extensive comments on friendship in the *Nicomachean Ethics* 9.1169a, which may underlie the discussion on friendship and love in the Farewell Discourses. ^15-13

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-16|16]]:** *I chose you*, probably not an expression of predestination, but of Jesus’ calling his followers (e.g., 1.43). ^15-16

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-18|18]]:** *The world*, is both the opposition to Jesus and his followers, and the area where their work takes place (17.15–18). ^15-18

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-22|22–24]]:** *Not have sin*, as elsewhere, sin is not a moral category but rather denotes lack of faith in Jesus. See especially 1.29; 8.21; 9.41; 15.22; 16.8,22; 19.11. *They have no excuse*, those who knew Jesus and saw his works cannot claim ignorance as an excuse for their opposition to him. ^15-22

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-23|23]]:** *Hates my Father*, Jews who do not believe in Jesus are said to “hate” God. ^15-23

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-25|25]]:** *Law*, used by extension of the entire Bible, as in later rabbinic literature; the quotation is not from the Torah (Pentateuch) but from [[Psalms 035#^35-19|Ps 35.19]]; [[Psalms 069#^69-5|69.4]]. The opponents do not understand *their* own *law*. ^15-25

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-26|26]]:** *Advocate*, see 14.16–17. ^15-26

**[[NRSVue John 15#^15-27|27]]:** *Testify*, presumably in public. ^15-27
