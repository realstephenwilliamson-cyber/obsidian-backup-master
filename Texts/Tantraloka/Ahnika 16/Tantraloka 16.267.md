## Mūla

तत्तस्यैव कुतोऽन्यस्य तत्कस्मादन्यकल्पना ।
एतदर्थं गुरोर्यत्नाल्लक्षणे तत्र तत्र तत् ॥ ^mula

tattasyaiva kuto'nyasya tatkasmādanyakalpanā |
etadarthaṃ guroryatnāllakṣaṇe tatra tatra tat || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 07 - Chapter Sixteen, part 3#^pg137|p. 117]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=137>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 16.266cd–267ab.
>
> Thus, (the Lord) has adopted Mantra (as a means to realization), observing that the notion (vikalpana) that arises from the special words (sounds) (śabda) and meanings of mantras is different from the one that arises from other (common) words (śabda). Moreover, the form of (noetic) consciousness (bodha) mentioned earlier of seed-syllables (bīja) and syllabic groups (piṇḍa) etc. (of Mantra) pertains to that alone, so how could it be that of (any) other (common word)? How could that be a notion (that is derived) from something else? (with 16.265cd–267ab)
>
> It is for this reason that when (scriptures define) the characteristics of a (true) teacher, (this one) characteristic is stated with (insistent) effort (yatnāt) in various places, namely, that he is an expert in Mantras and their application (mantratantraviśārada). (with 16.267cd–268ab)
