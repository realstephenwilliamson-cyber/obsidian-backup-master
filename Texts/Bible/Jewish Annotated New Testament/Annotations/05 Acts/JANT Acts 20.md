### 20.1–6: Paul’s return to Macedonia and Greece

*[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-1|Text]], p. 264* ^20-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-2|2]]:** *Encouragement*, see 14.22. ^20-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-3|3]]:** *Plot . . . by the Jews*, Acts often portrays Jews as Paul’s leading opponents (see 18.12). ^20-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-4|4]]:** *Sopater*, possibly Sosipater, described by Paul as his “relative” ([[Romans 16#^16-21|Rom 16.21]]). *Beroea*, see 17.10–15. *Aristarchus*, see 19.29. *Secundus from Thessalonica*, otherwise unknown. On Thessalonica, see 17.1–9. *Gaius*, another companion by that name came from Macedonia (19.29). *Derbe*, see 14.6,20–21;16.1. *Timothy*, see 16.1,3; 17.14–15; 18.5; 19.22. *Tychicus*, see [[Ephesians 06#^6-21|Eph 6.21]]; [[Colossians 04#^4-7|Col. 4.7]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-12|2 Tim 4.12]]; [[Titus 03#^3-12|Titus 3.12]]. *Trophimus*, accompanies Paul to Jerusalem (21.29). ^20-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-5|5]]:** *Us*, the first-person plural resumes (16.10–17). *Troas*, see 16.8–10. ^20-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-6|6]]:** *Philippi*, see 16.12. *Unleavened Bread*, the reference to the festival of Passover connects Paul with Jesus (see [[Luke 22#^22-1|Lk 22.1]]), both of whom begin a journey at this time which culminates in arrest (21.33) and travails (chs 21–28). ^20-6

### 20.7–12: Paul revives Eutychus

*[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-7|Text]], p. 265* · On Paul’s miraculous powers, see 13.11; 14.10; 19.11; 28.3–6. Luke attributes similar resuscitations to Peter (9.36–43) and Jesus ([[Luke 07#^7-11|Lk 7.11–17]]; [[Luke 08#^8-41|8.41–42]],[[Luke 08#^8-49|49–56]]). ^20-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-7|7]]:** *First day of the week*, after the Sabbath; Sunday or possibly Saturday evening. ^20-7-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-9|9]]:** *Eutychus*, meaning “fortunate,” might be a slave-name (e.g., “Lucky”). ^20-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-12|12]]:** The response is uncharacteristically underwhelming (3.10; 5.16; 9.35; 14.11). ^20-12

### 20.13–16: Journey to Miletus

*[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-13|Text]], p. 265* · From Assos Paul travels south by boat to Miletus. ^20-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-13|13]]:** *Assos*, northwest coast of Asia Minor. ^20-13-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-14|14]]:** *Mitylene*, major city on the island of Lesbos (in the Aegean Sea), south of Assos. ^20-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-15|15]]:** *Chios*, island south of Lesbos. *Samos*, island southeast of Chios. *Miletus*, prominent seaport city on the west coast of Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). ^20-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-16|16]]:** *Eager to be in Jerusalem*, see 19.21. *Pentecost*, Jewish festival of Shavuot (2.1), one of the three pilgrimage festivals. Paul’s desire to be in Jerusalem reflects his observance of Jewish Law and custom. **20**. ^20-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-17|17–38]]:** Paul’s speech to the Ephesian elders. Paul’s speech provides a suitable conclusion to his missionary journeys. It reflects on his previous work and anticipates the dangers awaiting him in Jerusalem, exhorts listeners to remain faithful, and defends his actions. ^20-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-17|17]]:** *Ephesus*, about 30 mi north of *Trogyllium* Miletus. *Elders*, see 11.30; 14.23; 15.2; 16.4. ^20-17-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-19|19]]:** *Plots of the Jews*, Jews are again singled out as Paul’s opponents (13.45; 17.5,13; 18.12; 20.3). ^20-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-23|23]]:** *Imprisonment and persecutions*, see 9.16; 16.23; 21.11,33. His sufferings were foreshadowed (9.16). ^20-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-25|25]]:** *Kingdom*, see 8.12. ^20-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-26|26]]:** *Not responsible for the blood*, Paul has done what is required; the believers are now responsible for maintaining the community and their hope for salvation (3.17; 17.30; [[Luke 23#^23-34|Lk 23.34]]). ^20-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-27|27]]:** *Purpose of God*, see 2.23; 4.28; 5.38–39; 13.36. ^20-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-28|28]]:** *Overseers*, Gk “episkopoi,” from which “bishop” is derived; identified previously as “elders” (v 17), Gk “presbyteroi,” showing that the author does not indicate a developed division of church offices (e.g., bishop, priest, deacon). *Flock . . . shepherd*, pastoral images often applied to Israel and its leaders; see [[Jeremiah 03#^3-15|Jer 3.15]]; [[Jeremiah 23#^23-1|23.1–4]]; [[Ezekiel 34#^34-1|Ezek 34.1–24]]; [[Psalms 074#^74-2|Ps 74.2]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-52|78.52]]; [[Psalms 095#^95-7|95.7]]. *Blood*, see [[Romans 03#^3-25|Rom 3.25]]; [[Ephesians 01#^1-7|Eph 1.7]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-12|Heb 9.12]]; [[1 John 01#^1-7|1 Jn 1.7]]; [[Revelation 01#^1-5|Rev 1.5]]. ^20-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-29|29–30]]:** *Savage wolves*, admonitions against false teachings commonly appear in early Christian writing; see [[Matthew 07#^7-15|Mt 7.15]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-3|1 Tim 1.3–7]]; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-1|4.1–3]]; [[2 Timothy 02#^2-14|2 Tim 2.14–18]]; Ignatius, *Phil.* 2.1–2. ^20-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-33|33]]:** See 16.19n. (8.18; 19.25). ^20-33

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-34|34]]:** *Worked with my own hands*, see 18.3; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|1 Cor 4.12]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-9|1 Thess 2.9]]. ^20-34

**[[NRSVue Acts 20#^20-35|35]]:** The saying does not appear in the Gospels, but it resonates with Luke’s interest in the poor ([[Luke 04#^4-18|Lk 4.18]]; [[Luke 06#^6-20|6.20]]; [[Luke 18#^18-22|18.22]]) and ethical statements attributed to Jesus in the Gospel of Luke ([[Luke 06#^6-30|Lk 6.30]]). Similar sentiments are found in Jewish (Wis 4.31) and Greek literature (Thucydides, *Hist.* 2.97.4; Seneca, *Ep.* 81.17; Plutarch, *Mor.* 173d). ^20-35
