# Philemon 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue Philemon 01|NRSVue]] · [[Philemon 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Philemon - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-1|1–3]]:** Introduction.  ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Timothy*, a co-sender also of 2 Corinthians, Philippians, and 1 Thessalonians. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Apphia* is mentioned only here; she may have been Philemon’s wife or perhaps a prominent community member, since she is singled out for notice. *Archippus*, see [[Colossians 04#^4-17|Col 4.17]]; fellow soldier, *co-worker* (see also [[Philippians 02#^2-25|Phil 2.25]]). *In your house*, the community likely met in Philemon’s house; this was common in the early years of the movement. The membership would likely have been at most 30–40 people, given the size of a “large” but not patrician home in Roman cities. ^1-2

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Grace* . *. . and peace . . . Lord Jesus Christ*, standard Pauline salutation (e.g., [[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]); Jesus and the Father are not equated. *Our Father*, following Jesus’ example (e.g., [[Matthew 06#^6-9|Mt 6.9]]). The Tanakh does not usually refer to God this way, but see [[Isaiah 63#^63-16|Isa 63.16]]; [[Jeremiah 03#^3-19|Jer 3.19]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-27|Ps 89.26]] \[HB v 27\]; the appellation became more common in the Second Temple period (e.g., [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|Sir 23.1]]; *m. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 3.8). ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-4|4–7]]:** Thanksgiving. Paul uses the singular for “you” in vv 4–22a; most commentators suggest it refers to Philemon although a few argue for Archippus. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Remember*, remembrance of people appears in ancient Jewish prayers and letters (e.g., [[LXX 1 Maccabees 12#^12-11|1 Macc 12.11]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 01#^1-2|2 Macc 1.2–6]]), frequently in the form of a pe-titioner beseeching God to “remember” and to deliver him or his people (e.g., [[Jeremiah 14#^14-21|Jer 14.21]]; [[Nehemiah 01#^1-8|Neh 1.8–9]]). Remembrance is an important theme in later rabbinic liturgies, including the “Yizkor” (lit., “remembrance”) prayer in memory of the dead. ^1-4-2

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Saints*, Gk “hagioi,” lit., “holy ones,” Paul’s usual characterization of believers (e.g., [[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-2|1 Cor 1.2]]). It suggests persons set apart for service to God (e.g., [[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-44|Lev 11.44–45]]). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Sharing*, Gk “koinonia,” fellowship; see v 17. *Good that we* (or “you”; see translators’ note *d*) *may do*, by bringing others into the community of believers. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Hearts* (Gk “splanchna”) lit., “viscera,” or “guts,” the site of the emotions; “compassion” derives from the same Greek root; see also vv 12,20. *Brother*, one of Paul’s usual names for his colleagues (e.g., [[Romans 16#^16-23|Rom 16.23]]). Such kinship terminology appears in Jewish letters from Arad (e.g., Arad 16) that antedate the Babylonian invasion of 597 BCE as well as the letters associated with the Second (Bar Kochba) Revolt against Rome in 132–135 CE (e.g., Murabbaʾat 43). *Brother(s)* appears frequently in the Tanakh and later Jewish writings (e.g., [[LXX Judith 07#^7-30|Jdt 7.30]]; [[LXX Judith 08#^8-14|8.14]]) to refer to the “family” of Israel (e.g., [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-46|Lev 25.46]]; [[Psalms 022#^22-23|Ps 22.22]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-8|8–22]]:** Paul’s appeal to Philemon.  ^1-8

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Bold* (Gk “parresia”), a term related to the interactions between friends. **9**: *Old man* (Gk “presbytes”), Paul is characterized as “young” in [[Acts 07#^7-58|Acts 7.58]], which describes events perhaps two or three decades before the date of this letter. His expectation that he would be treated with respect recalls the use of “elder” as a title in communities of Jesus’ followers (e.g., [[Acts 20#^20-17|Acts 20.17]]). Ancient Judaism had a deeply rooted tradition of respect for elders (e.g., [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-32|Lev 19.32]]; [[LXX Sirach 08#^8-9|Sir 8.9]] as well as [[1 Timothy 05#^5-1|1 Tim 5.1–2]]). ^1-8-2

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Onesimus*, see [[Colossians 04#^4-9|Col 4.9]]; Ignatius, *Eph*. 1; see Introduction. *Whose father I have his prisoner* f The name Onesimus means *useful* or (compare verse 20) *beneficial become*, see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-15|1 Cor 4.15]]. On familial language in early Jewish texts to describe the relationship of teachers to disciples, see, for example, *b. Sanh*. 19b, “Samuel b. Nahmani said in R. Jonathan’s name, ‘He who teaches the son of his neighbor the Torah, Scripture ascribes it to him as if he had begotten him.’” ^1-10

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Useful*, a pun on “Onesimus,” which means “useful”; it is not clear if this is a slave designation (as if nicknaming him “Handy”). However, to this point Paul has said nothing about slavery; the only explicit reference is in v 16. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Be of service*, the verb “diakoneō,” related to “diakonia,” “ministry” and to “diakonos,” “servant, deacon,” almost always means household service (e.g., [[Luke 22#^22-27|Lk 22.27]]). *Gospel*, here the preaching of the message of salvation through Jesus, which is the cause of his imprisonment. Paul does not explain why evangelizing would have prompted imprisonment; there appear to be no threats of imprisonment faced by the letter’s recipients. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Was separated*, referring to Onesimus’s absence; the use of the passive may be a way of softening the fact of Onesimus’s flight. ^1-15

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-16|16]]:** *No longer as a slave . . . a beloved brother*, although this verse describes a transformed relationship among Philemon and Paul and the converted Onesimus—they are now beloved brothers—it is not clear what this portends for Onesimus’s slave status or even whether Paul is using the term metaphorically. Paul may be asking Philemon to free Onesimus, or he may be referring solely to Onesimus’s changed status in the context of the church. Here the term “as” (Gk “hos”) may indicate a metaphorical rather than literal usage. Significantly, Paul does not call Onesimus a slave or Philemon a slave-owner. To complicate matters, “brother” in [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-12|Deut 15.12]] may refer to a slave who is also a community member, although this law mandates that a Hebrew slave, after six years of servitude, must be freed in the seventh year. ^1-16

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Partner*, the Greek “koi-nonos” suggests an intimate fellowship, as with the cognate term “koinonia”; see [[2 Corinthians 08#^8-23|2 Cor 8.23]]. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-18|18]]:** *If he has wronged you . . . or owes you anything*, Onesimus may have caused Philemon financial loss, either by stealing or by the loss of service, which Paul offers to repay. Alternatively, Paul may be using a rhetorical strategy to put himself in the position of benefactor rather than supplicant. ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Writing this with my own hand*, see [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-21|1 Cor 16.21]]; [[Galatians 06#^6-11|Gal 6.11]]; Paul usually dictated his letters to a scribe. His notice suggests a personal touch. *Owing me even your own self*, probable reference to Paul’s conversion of Philemon. ^1-19

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-21|21]]:** *You will do even more than I say*, perhaps Paul’s plea that Philemon manumit Onesimus. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Guest room*, Gk “xenia,” lodging place for strangers (i.e., those not of the house). *Restored to you*, lit., “be bestowed on you.” ^1-22

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-23|23–25]]:** Conclusion.  ^1-23

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-23|23–24]]:** *Epaphras . . . Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke*, greetings from co-workers, all of whom are named in [[Colossians 04#^4-10|Col 4.10–14]]; for Epaphras, see also [[Colossians 01#^1-7|Col 1.7]]. ^1-23-2

**[[NRSVue Philemon 01#^1-25|25]]:** *Your spirit*, a blessing on the recipient’s inmost being. ^1-25
