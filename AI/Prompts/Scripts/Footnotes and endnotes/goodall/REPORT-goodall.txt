## 1. What was wrong

Sampling pages with PyMuPDF (`get_text('rawdict')`, `get_texttrace()`, `get_drawings()`) and the embedded fonts (fontTools) showed four separate causes.

1. **Body references are not superscripts in the text layer.** Brill sets note numbers in the body with the font's own *superior* digit glyphs: nominal size 11 pt, same baseline, no superscript flag, same line bbox as the other characters. PyMuPDF therefore gives `Guhyasūtra 8:2` for *Guhyasūtra* 8:² (the colon is in the print). The only differences are the glyph id (Brill-Roman uses cids 4669–4678 for superior digits, 4594–4603 for ordinary digits, 4700–4703 for inferior digits as in V₁/V₂) and the advance width (≈0.75 of the normal digit). The converter's superscript rule (flag bit 0, or smaller size and raised bbox) cannot see this, and its fallback (digits straight after a word) refused `8:2` as a verse number — correctly, since it cannot tell.
2. **The margin number is a separate line.** In Brill's layout the note number stands at x = 56.7 and the note text at x = 68 (one digit) or 79.4 (two or three digits). `merge_same_line` joins a number to its text only when the gap is under one em (9 pt); the two-digit gap is ≈14 pt, so lines like `10` / `See Bisschop 2007 and 2013.` stayed apart. The footnote parser then saw no numbered line, took every line as a *continuation*, and appended the whole page's notes to the previous page's last note (e.g. current note 03, `[^9]: See Sanderson 2006 for references. 10 See Bisschop 2007 and 2013. 11 The manuscript …`). On pages whose previous page had no notes they became plain paragraphs.
3. **A margin number on the last line was taken for a page number.** A bare `26` at the foot of a page is a "pure number in the bottom zone" and became a header/footer; 107 notes lost their number this way (the page numbers of this book are all at the top).
4. **Pages that are mostly notes.** The per-page body-size estimate (`pbody`) takes the commonest size in the upper 60 % of the page; on pages where a long note fills most of the page, that is 9 pt, so nothing counted as "small" and no notes zone was found (e.g. PDF 142). Notes may also begin as high as 0.19 of the page height (PDF 302), above the converter's 0.45 limit, and the 10 pt bibliographies were "small" too.

Net result on 7 Oct: 191 notes recognised out of ≈1,130, 157 linked.

## 2. What I changed

All changes are in `/home/claude/d31c/conv.py`, each behind a job option (default off, so the original behaviour is unchanged for other books):

| option | change |
|---|---|
| `glyph_sups` | `raised_glyphs(doc)`: reads each embedded CFF font with fontTools and marks glyphs whose outline sits above the baseline (y0 > 0.2 em, y1 < 0.78 em, height < 0.55 em) as raised. `raised_positions(page)` collects the origins of digits drawn with such glyphs (`get_texttrace`); `split_raised()` splits the rawdict spans so a run of raised digits becomes a span flagged `sup`. Digits only — the font's asterisk and daggers are raised by design, and a zero-width glyph can share the origin (PDF 122). The regex-based `bodyrefs` is switched off under this option (it was linking `1.99–101` and the like). |
| `fn_num_merge` | In the notes zone a number-only line is joined to the text line beside it (either order; the text line sits slightly higher when it holds a raised character). A note *starts* only where the number is the margin span (`spans[0]`), which keeps apparatus entries such as `6a aśastān]` as continuation text. A lone margin number also counts as "numbered" for the zone test. |
| `fn_rule_cut` | Brill prints a short rule (45 pt wide, at the left margin, alone on its y) above the notes on every page that has them, including continuations. The notes zone is everything small below the *lowest* such rule, taken when the first line below it is a margin-number start or the previous page's zone continues (notes or continuation lines). A verse apparatus in the body has its own indented rule, and table rules come in rows, so they do not qualify. The block-quotation indent test is skipped for a zone found this way, and a note running over three pages is chained to its page. |
| `page_body: false` | the book-level body size (11 pt) holds on every page. |
| `fn_top: 0.15`, `fn_max_size: 9.2` | notes may begin at 15 % of the page height; the 10 pt bibliographies are not notes. |
| `pnum_bottom: false` | a bare number in the bottom zone is not a page number; a note line beside a margin number is not a footer; a line that contains the page number (`See below, note 115.` on p. 115) is not a footer; a line holding a raised number is not a header (`[…].112`). |
| `fn_carry_ref` | a raised number with no note on its page is linked to the note on the next page when it is the next number in sequence (2 cases: notes 89 and 109 in Ratié's chapter, where the note begins on the following page; both checked). |
| `hf_top: 50` | existing option: everything above 50 pt is header. The running heads are 10 pt lower-case lines at y ≈ 35, and those occurring fewer than 9 times in the book (Dezső, Törzsök, Hidas, Hanneder, Mallinson …) were left in the text on 7 Oct; 87 such lines are gone now (listed in `merge_log.json` → `running_head_dropped`). If they are wanted back, drop `hf_top` from the job. |
| (unconditional, small) | `line_markdown` keeps the space after a raised reference (the text layer has no space character there: `others5 have` → `others[^5] have`, previously `others[^5]have`). The `_report.json` now lists the notes with no reference (`noref`). |

Also: `text_refs: false` (no guessing from plain digits). The job's `toc` reproduces the current 40-page split; the TOC headings it generates are discarded and the current headings put back.
