### 12.1–10: Paul relates a mystical experience

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 370* · , since his opponents claim such experiences validate their spiritual prowess; see 5.13n. ^12-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-1|1]]:** *Visions and revelations*, extrasensory events that endow the seer with power and status; see v 7; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-1|1 Cor 9.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-5|15.5–8]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-12|Gal 1.12]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-1|2.1–2]]. ^12-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-2|2–3]]:** *I know a person* (also v 5), an oblique self-reference, following the apocalyptic convention of anonymous authorship; see vv 7–9. In rabbinic anecdotes, narrators may refer to themselves in the third person, generally as “that man” (e.g., *b. Moʾed Qat*. 17a). *Third heaven*, i.e., *Paradise* (v 4). Paul may be dismissing triumphant mystical enthusiasm and presenting himself as a failed or humble mystic (see v 7). Again, Paul ironically boasts that he is as great in spiritual matters as his adversaries even as he maintains that, for one in Christ, power is represented in weakness. Cf. vv 9–11. ^12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-4|4]]:** That the content of mystical revelations is to be kept secret is commonplace. See especially *m. Hag*. 2.1, “the works of creation and divine chariot may be expounded only before sages who already have comprehended these topics of their own accord.” ^12-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-7|7]]:** The nature of the *thorn* is unknown. *Messenger of Satan*, see 2.11n. In a statement assigned to early authorities, *b. B. Bat.* 16a states, “\[Satan\] comes down to earth and seduces, then ascends to heaven and awakens wrath”; the continuation of the passage equates Satan with what the rabbis call the “yetser ha-raʿ” (inclination to do evil). Paul’s own experience of Satan’s temptation prevented his becoming too self-satisfied. ^12-7

> [!note] Seven Heavens *(p. 370)*
> By the first centuries, Jews imagined the existence of multiple, hierarchically arranged heavens through which the soul after death or a person in a state of ecstasy in life could ascend to God’s throne (e.g., *Ex. Rab*. 25.7–8; 45.6, and, esp., *b. Hag*. 14b). Some texts, including the early *1 Enoch* 14 (probably second century bce), depict a three-tiered cosmology, while *Sepher Razim* (*The Book of Mysteries*, third-fourth centuries ce) describes the soul as passing through seven heavens, including a fourth one in which it observes the sun (which the soul addresses by the Greek “Kyrie” \[“Lord”\]) crossing from west to east under the world). It appears that by Paul’s day a full complement of seven heavens was commonly conceived. Thus, *4 Ezra* (2 Esd, late first century ce) depicts in parallel seven levels of degradation experienced after death by the souls of those who have scorned God and, at 7.90–98, seven heavens traversed by the souls of those who have “kept the ways of the Most High.” In the lower heavens, righteous souls witness the confusion in which the souls of the ungodly wander; later, upon reaching the highest, seventh heaven, the souls of the righteous behold the face of God, “the one whom they served in their lifetimes.”
>
> Rabbinic authorities debate the number of “fir-maments” and what they contain. *Ex. Rab.* 25.7–8 refers to the third heaven as paradise; in *b. Hag*. 12b, R. Simeon b. Laqish lists seven heavens, referred to as: curtain, firmament, clouds, lofty abode, dwelling, residence, heavy cloud. *B. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 24b portrays the diverse attendants that serve in the different levels of heaven. Although such passages discuss varied heavens, they do not, with one exception, portray either the soul’s or a mystic’s passage through them. Still, as proof that a journey such as Paul describes was common currency, one of the Talmud’s most famous passages (*b. Hag*. 14b) relates the story of four rabbis who entered “paradise,” an experience that only one of them survived intact so as to attain a vision of God.
>
> While mystical ideas appear to have been well known in the rabbis’ day, Talmudic sources infrequently refer to them. The rabbis’ depiction of mystical activity as dangerous, and their insistence that these topics not be generally studied (*m. Hag.* 2.1) point to the rabbis’ overall perspective, that mystical activity was to be shunned.

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-9|9–11]]:** Participation in angelic worship is acceptable only when it is characterized by Christ-like humility and reliance on God. Paul rejects mystical experiences that detract from the central message of faith. Note the similar rabbinic treatment of Honi the Circle Drawer (*m. Taʾan*. 3.8); the rabbis praise his spiritual power and closeness to God even as they assert that anyone else who makes demands of God as Honi does would be subject to excommunication (see “Jewish Miracle Workers,” p. 680). ^12-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-9|9]]:** *Sufficient*, unlike the Hellenistic ideal of sufficiency that transcends hardships (see 9.8), Paul accepts these hardships as real, because in their weakness they manifest God’s power; see 10.1n. ^12-9-2

### 12.11–13: Conclusion of the “fool’s speech.”

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-11|Text]], p. 371* ^12-11

### 12.14–21: Paul prepares for a third visit

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-14|Text]], p. 371* ^12-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-14|14–18]]:** Paul answers charges that he has been enriching himself through the collection (chs 8–9). ^12-14-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-14|14]]:** Third visit (also v 21; 13.1), see 2.1n. for the second “painful visit.” *Not be a burden*, see 11.9n. *Parents*, for Paul as father to his converts, see 11.2. ^12-14-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-16|16]]:** *I took you in by deceit*, some Corinthians evidently accused Paul of skimming the collection funds. ^12-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-18|18]]:** Paul sent *Titus* to oversee the collection (8.6,16,23); see also 2.13n. *The brother*, sent to accompany Titus (8.22–23); not the “brother” elected by the congregations in 8.18. ^12-18

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-19|19]]:** *Beloved*, see 2.4n. *Building you up*, see 10.8; 13.10. ^12-19

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-20|20–21]]:** Catalogue of vices, see [[Romans 01#^1-29|Rom 1.29–31]]; [[Romans 13#^13-13|13.13]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-10|1 Cor 5.10–11]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|6.9–10]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-19|Gal 5.19–21]]. ^12-20

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 12#^12-21|21]]:** Ironic use of *humble*, here meaning “humiliate”; see 11.7n. *being too elated* b Other ancient authorities read *my power* ^12-21
