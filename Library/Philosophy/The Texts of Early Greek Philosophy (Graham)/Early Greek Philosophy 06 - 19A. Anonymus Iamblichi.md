Introduction

lamblichus’ Protrepticus is a patchwork, or cento, of passages from different sources, including some we know, such as Plato’s Phaedo, some we can name and reconstruct, such as Aristotle's Protrepticus, and some we can reconstruct but not name, such as the present work. It was evidently a sophistic treatise. Scholars have tried to identify the author with a number of well-known figures, but as there js no consensus, we are forced to leave the work anonymous.’ Traditionally the author is known by the Latin appellation Anonymus lamblichi. What he writes is an apparently conventional sophistic account of education urging a virtuous application of the skills learned. The author is in no way an ‘enemy to virtue, but an advocate of a life of social responsibility. The basis for his argument is largely practical rather than theoretical; the lack of a deeper justification for virtue separates this and perhaps most sophistic accounts of virtue from the Socratic-Platonic one. Still, we see in Anonymus the commitment some ‘sophists had to a life of virtue.
