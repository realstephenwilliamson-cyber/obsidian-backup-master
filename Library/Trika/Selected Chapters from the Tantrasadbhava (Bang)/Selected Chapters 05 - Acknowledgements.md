## Acknowledgements

My first encounter with the *Tantrasadbhāva* took place while I was visting the University of Hamburg (Germany) as an exchange student from Dongguk University (South Korea). This fortunate opportunity allowed me to enter the world of Sanskrit scholarship grounded in manuscript studies. After that, thanks to the support of the Graduate School Scholarship Program and the Centre For the Study of Manuscript Cultures (CSMC) at the University of Hamburg, I was able to launch my PhD project in 2012.

First and foremost I would like to thank Prof. Harunaga Isaacson for supervising this thesis; his stunning knowledge made me aware of my ignorance, and his inexhaustible generosity and patience have enlightened me at every stage. The present work would have been impossible without help from many people and institutions. In the first semester of my project, Prof. Alexis Sanderson who was visiting the University of Hamburg from Oxford University, kindly offered to read the first draft of my edition of chapter one of the *Tantrasadbhāva*, even though it was a much poorer draft than I would have liked, and he guided me on how to deal with this text from the very beginning. And all of his groundbreaking works, needless to say, always have lightened my way in this present work.

While I was staying in Kyoto, Japan as a visiting researcher at Kyoto University, Prof. Diwakar Acharya (now in Oxford University), Prof. Somadeva Vasudeva, and Prof. Yuko Yokochi kindly met with me in regular reading sessions and saved me from various errors. And Prof. Judit Törzsök, Prof. Shaman Hatley, and Dr. Csaba Kiss, and their priceless publications guided me to a better understanding in different stages of my dissertation.

And also while I was staying in India, financed by a field scholarship at the École française d’Extrême-Orient, Pondicherry, Prof. Dominic Goodall helped me by not only removing my misunderstandings and mistakes but also by generously formatting my edition and translation; Dr. R. Sathyanarayanan gave me substantial advice on my editing work; and Dr. S. A. S. Sarma helped me better understand various Śaiva ritual

contexts. As a neophyte of manuscript studies, I am also indebted to Dr. Mark Dyczkowski, who has made manifold transcriptions of unpublished tantric texts available through the Digital Library of the Muktabodha Indological Research Institute, in particular supplying me with a diplomatic transcription of the *Tantrasadbhāva* which was of significant help in the early stage of my project, although I have not yet met him in person. If this present study makes any contribution to the understanding of the development of Śaiva literature, it is thanks to to all my direct and indirect teachers; and of course, any errors are my own.

During several visits to Tokyo, Japan for conferences, workshops, etc., Prof. Toru Tomabechi, Prof. Ryugen Tanemura, and Prof. Kano Kazuo gave me the chance to read a variety of Sanskrit texts that extended my knowledge of Tantras. And I am grateful for the classes and presentations of Dr. Kengo Harimoto, Prof. Dorji Wangchuk, and Dr. Orna Almogi throughout my stay at the University of Hamburg. From the beginning of my journey studying Indology, all my Korean teachers and senior colleagues, particularly, Prof. Seung-suk Jung, Prof. Sungdu Ahn, Dr. Ven. Wonkwa, Dr. Youngjin Lee, always have encouraged me with their knowledge, as has Tae-heung Jun who has provided limitless support.

Finally, I thank my family, my mother and sister, who have lifted me up whenever I had difficulty, and, in particular, Dr. Kenichi Kuranishi whose unconditional support and encouragement made everything possible. This work is dedicated to them.
