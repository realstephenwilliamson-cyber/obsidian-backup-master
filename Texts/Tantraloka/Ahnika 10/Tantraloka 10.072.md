## Mūla

धर्मी वेद्यत्वमभ्येति स सत्तासमवायवत् ।
ब्रूषे यथा हि कुरुते सत्ता सत्यसतः सतः ॥ ^mula

dharmī vedyatvamabhyeti sa sattāsamavāyavat |
brūṣe yathā hi kurute sattā satyasataḥ sataḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 11 - Chapter Ten, part 2#^pg359|p. 340]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=359>))
>
> Perceptibility is not like the colour blue or other (qualities of an object). Rather, it is (a fundamental) attribute (dharma) (of an object), like Being (sattā) and inherence (samavāya). Conjoined with that (yad yogāt), the substratum (dharmin), that possesses all the qualities, becomes perceptible. (with 10.71bcd–72ab)
>
> (An opponent may object at this point that,) what you are saying amounts to this, namely, (that perceptibility) acts as does Being (sattā) that, when it is present, makes what does not exist existent. So too, inherence conjoined (to something) conjoins things that are not conjoined. The last (particular) quality (viśeṣa) (of an entity) is separation, (that makes things) that are not separate, separate. Brilliant white (makes) what is not white, white. In the same way, movement (makes something move). Similarly, the bearer of attributes (dharmin) endowed with all its qualities, such as blue and the like, (although) in itself imperceptible (avedya), becomes, by virtue of the quality of perceptibility, perceptible. (with 10.72cd–75ab)
