# I Samuel 24

Compare: [[NRSVue 1 Samuel 24|NRSVue]] (Heb 24:1 = NRSVue 23:29; Heb 24:2–23 = NRSVue 24:1–22) · [[NRSVue 1 Samuel 23#^23-29|NRSVue 23:29]]

## Chapter 24

**24:1** David went from there and stayed in the wildernesses of En-gedi. ^24-1

**24:2** When Saul returned from pursuing the Philistines, he was told that David was in the wilderness of En-gedi. ^24-2

**24:3** So Saul took three thousand picked men from all Israel and went in search of David and his men in the direction of the rocks of the wild goats;[^1] ^24-3

**24:4** and he came to the sheepfolds along the way. There was a cave there, and Saul went in to relieve himself.[^2] Now David and his men were sitting in the back of the cave. ^24-4

**24:5** David’s men said to him, “This is the day of which the LORD said to you, ‘I will deliver your enemy into your hands; you can do with him as you please.’” [^3]David went and stealthily cut off the corner of Saul’s cloak. ^24-5

**24:6** But afterward David reproached himself[^4] for cutting off the corner of Saul’s cloak.[^5] ^24-6

**24:7** He said to his men, “The LORD forbid that I should do such a thing to my lord—the LORD’s anointed—that I should raise my hand against him; for he is the LORD’s anointed.” ^24-7

**24:8** David rebuked[^6] his men and did not permit them to attack Saul.  
Saul left the cave and started on his way. ^24-8

**24:9** Then David also went out of the cave and called after Saul, “My lord king!” Saul looked around and David bowed low in homage, with his face to the ground. ^24-9

**24:10** And David said to Saul, “Why do you listen to the people who say, ‘David is out to do you harm?’ ^24-10

**24:11** You can see for yourself now that the LORD delivered you into my hands in the cave today. And though I was urged[^7] to kill you, I showed you pity;[^8] for I said, ‘I will not raise a hand against my lord, since he is the LORD’s anointed.’ ^24-11

**24:12** Please, sir,[^9] take a close look at the corner of your cloak in my hand; for when I cut off the corner of your cloak, I did not kill you. You must see plainly that I have done nothing evil or rebellious, and I have never wronged you. Yet you are bent on taking my life. ^24-12

**24:13** May the LORD judge between you and me! And may He take vengeance upon you for me, but my hand will never touch you. ^24-13

**24:14** As the ancient proverb has it: ‘Wicked deeds come from wicked men!’ My hand will never touch you. ^24-14

**24:15** Against whom has the king of Israel come out? Whom are you pursuing? A dead dog? A single flea? ^24-15

**24:16** May the LORD be arbiter and may He judge between you and me! May He take note and uphold my cause, and vindicate me against you.” ^24-16

**24:17** When David finished saying these things to Saul, Saul said, “Is that your voice, my son David?” And Saul broke down and wept. ^24-17

**24:18** He said to David, “You are right, not I; for you have treated me generously, but I have treated you badly. ^24-18

**24:19** Yes, you have just revealed how generously you treated me, for the LORD delivered me into your hands and you did not kill me. ^24-19

**24:20** If a man meets his enemy, does he let him go his way unharmed? Surely, the LORD will reward you generously for what you have done for me this day.[^10] ^24-20

**24:21** I know now that you will become king, and that the kingship over Israel will remain in your hands. ^24-21

**24:22** So swear to me by the LORD that you will not destroy my descendants or wipe out my name from my father’s house.” ^24-22

**24:23** David swore to Saul, Saul went home, and David and his men went up to the strongholds. ^24-23

[^1]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^2]: Lit. “to cover his feet.”
[^3]: Vv. 5b–6 read well after 8a.
[^4]: Lit. “David’s heart struck him.”
[^5]: So several mss. and ancient versions; cf. v. 5. Most mss. and editions read “Saul’s corner.”
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^7]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^8]: Understanding the Heb. as an ellipsis of wattaḥos ‘eni (cf., e.g., Deut. 7.16).
[^9]: Lit. “[my] father,” cf. 2 Kings 5.13.
[^10]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain. Emendation yields “the generosity you have shown me.”
