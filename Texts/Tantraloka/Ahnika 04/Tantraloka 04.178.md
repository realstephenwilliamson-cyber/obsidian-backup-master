## Mūla

वामेश्वरीतिशब्देन प्रोक्तं श्रीनिशिसञ्चरे ।
इत्थं द्वादशधा संवित्तिष्ठन्ती विश्वमातृषु ॥ ^mula

vāmeśvarītiśabdena proktaṃ śrīniśisañcare |
itthaṃ dvādaśadhā saṃvittiṣṭhantī viśvamātṛṣu || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 09 - Chapter Four, part 8#^pg287|p. 274]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=287>))
>
> Thus, She is called Mātrsadbhāva (the Essence of (All Perceiving) Subjects) because the subjectivity within the (seven orders of) subjects is clearly evident (only) within this ultimate level of consciousness (antasaṁvitti). In the venerable Niśisaṁcara she is called Vāmeśvarī. (with 4.177–178ab)
>
> In this way, consciousness, that abides twelve-fold within all (perceiving) subjects (mātr), is one only, and so is not subject in any way to succession (krama). Again, because there is no succession (of differing elements), there is also no simultaneity (between the appearance of differing elements), and as there is no (simultaneity), there is also no succession. The principle of consciousness, extremely pure, transcends all talk of succession and its absence (kramākramakathātīta). (with 4.178cd–180ab)
