# 4 Maccabees 3 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 02|← 4 Maccabees 2]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 04|4 Maccabees 4 →]]

**3:1** But this argument is entirely ridiculous, for it is evident that reason rules not over its own passions but over those of the body. ^3-1

**3:2** No one of us can eradicate that kind of desire, but reason can provide a way for us not to be enslaved by desire. ^3-2

**3:3** No one of you can eradicate anger from the mind, but reason can help to deal with anger. ^3-3

**3:4** No one of us can eradicate malice, but reason can fight at our side so that we are not overcome by malice. ^3-4

**3:5** For reason does not uproot the passions but is their antagonist. ^3-5

## King David’s thirst

**3:6** Now this can be explained more clearly by the story of King David’s thirst. ^3-6

**3:7** David had been attacking the Philistines all day long and together with the soldiers of his nation had killed many of them. ^3-7

**3:8** Then when evening fell, he^[Other ancient authorities read *he hurried and*] came, sweating and quite exhausted, to the royal tent, around which the whole army of our ancestors had encamped. ^3-8

**3:9** Now all the rest were at dinner, ^3-9

**3:10** but the king was extremely thirsty, and though springs were plentiful there, he could not satisfy his thirst from them. ^3-10

**3:11** But a certain irrational desire for the water in the enemy’s territory tormented and inflamed him, undid and consumed him. ^3-11

**3:12** When his guards complained bitterly because of the king’s craving, two staunch young soldiers, respecting^[Or *embarrassed because of*] the king’s desire, armed themselves fully and taking a pitcher climbed over the enemy’s ramparts. ^3-12

**3:13** Eluding the sentinels at the gates, they went searching throughout the enemy camp ^3-13

**3:14** and found the spring and from it boldly brought the king a drink. ^3-14

**3:15** But David,^[Gk *he*] though he was burning with thirst, considered it an altogether fearful danger to his soul to drink what was regarded as equivalent to blood. ^3-15

**3:16** Therefore, opposing reason to desire, he poured out the drink as an offering to God. ^3-16

**3:17** For the temperate mind can conquer the drives of the passions and quench the flames of frenzied desires; ^3-17

**3:18** it can overthrow bodily agonies even when they are extreme and by nobility of reason spurn all domination by the passions. ^3-18

## An attempt on the temple treasury

**3:19** The present occasion now invites us to a narrative demonstration of temperate reason. ^3-19

**3:20** At a time when our ancestors were enjoying profound peace because of their observance of the law and were prospering, so that even Seleucus Nicanor, king of Asia, had both appropriated money to them for the temple service and recognized their way of life— ^3-20

**3:21** just at that time certain persons attempted a revolution against the public harmony and caused many and various disasters. ^3-21
