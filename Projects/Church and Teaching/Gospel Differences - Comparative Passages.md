### 1. The Ministry of John the Baptist

The expanded context reveals how each author frames John's authority. Matthew and Mark detail his ascetic lifestyle, Luke extends the Isaiah prophecy to emphasize universal salvation, and John focuses heavily on the Baptist's interrogation by the religious authorities.

### 2. The Baptism of Jesus

Matthew uniquely includes John's hesitation to baptize Jesus. Luke mentions Jesus was praying when the heavens opened. John's Gospel frames the entire event as a past revelation given specifically to the Baptist to identify the "Lamb of God."

### 3. The Cleansing of the Temple

The Synoptics (placing this in Holy Week) emphasize the immediate plot to kill Jesus by the religious leaders. John (placing this at the beginning of Jesus's ministry) uniquely mentions a whip of cords and introduces a theological discourse about replacing the temple with his body.

Matthew uniquely features the dramatic episode of Peter attempting to walk on the water. Mark highlights the disciples' lack of understanding ("their hearts were hardened"), while John notes the miraculous and sudden arrival of the boat at its destination.

### 7. Peter's Confession

The Synoptics establish the context of Jesus asking about public opinion ("Who do people say that I am?") before asking the disciples directly. John places this confession at a moment of crisis, immediately after a large group of followers abandons Jesus due to his difficult teachings.
