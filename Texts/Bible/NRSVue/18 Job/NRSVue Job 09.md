# Job 9 (NRSVue)

## Job replies: There is no mediator

**9:1** Then Job answered: ^9-1

**9:2** “Indeed, I know that this is so, but how can a mortal be just before God? ^9-2

**9:3** If one wished to contend with him, one could not answer him once in a thousand. ^9-3

**9:4** He is wise in heart and mighty in strength; who has resisted him and succeeded? ^9-4

**9:5** He removes mountains, and they do not know it when he overturns them in his anger; ^9-5

**9:6** he shakes the earth out of its place, and its pillars tremble; ^9-6

**9:7** he commands the sun, and it does not rise; he seals up the stars; ^9-7

**9:8** he alone stretched out the heavens and trampled the waves of the Sea;^[Or *trampled the back of the sea dragon*] ^9-8

**9:9** he made the Bear and Orion, the Pleiades and the chambers of the south; ^9-9

**9:10** he does great things beyond understanding and marvelous things without number. ^9-10

**9:11** Look, he passes by me, and I do not see him; he moves on, but I do not perceive him. ^9-11

**9:12** He snatches away; who can stop him? Who will say to him, ‘What are you doing?’ ^9-12

**9:13** “God will not turn back his anger; the helpers of Rahab bowed beneath him. ^9-13

**9:14** How then can I answer him, choosing my words with him? ^9-14

**9:15** Though I am innocent, I cannot answer him; I must appeal to my accuser for my right. ^9-15

**9:16** If I summoned him and he answered me, I do not believe that he would listen to my voice. ^9-16

**9:17** For he crushes me with a tempest and multiplies my wounds without cause; ^9-17

**9:18** he will not let me get my breath but fills me with bitterness. ^9-18

**9:19** If it is a contest of strength, he is the strong one! If it is a matter of justice, who can summon him?^[Compare Gk: Heb *me*] ^9-19

**9:20** Though I am innocent, my own mouth would condemn me; though I am blameless, he would prove me perverse. ^9-20

**9:21** I am blameless; I do not know myself; I loathe my life. ^9-21

**9:22** It is all one; therefore I say, ‘He destroys both the blameless and the wicked.’ ^9-22

**9:23** When disaster brings sudden death, he mocks at the calamity^[Meaning of Heb uncertain] of the innocent. ^9-23

**9:24** The earth is given into the hand of the wicked; he covers the eyes of its judges—if it is not he, who then is it? ^9-24

**9:25** “My days are swifter than a runner; they flee away; they see no good. ^9-25

**9:26** They go by like skiffs of reed, like an eagle swooping on the prey. ^9-26

**9:27** If I say, ‘I will forget my complaint; I will put off my sad countenance and be of good cheer,’ ^9-27

**9:28** I become afraid of all my suffering, for I know you will not hold me innocent. ^9-28

**9:29** I shall be condemned; why then do I labor in vain? ^9-29

**9:30** If I wash myself with soap and cleanse my hands with lye, ^9-30

**9:31** yet you will plunge me into filth, and my own clothes will abhor me. ^9-31

**9:32** For he is not a mortal, as I am, that I might answer him, that we should come to trial together. ^9-32

**9:33** There is no mediator^[Another reading is *Would that there were a mediator*] between us, who might lay his hand on us both. ^9-33

**9:34** If he would take his rod away from me and not let dread of him terrify me, ^9-34

**9:35** then I would speak without fear of him, for I know I am not what I am thought to be.^[Cn: Heb *for I am not so in myself*] ^9-35
