# Acts 16 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Acts 15|← Acts 15]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Acts 17|Acts 17 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Acts 16|NRSVue]] · [[Acts 16|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Acts - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-1|16.1–5]]:** Paul returns to Derbe and Lystra. ^16-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-1|1]]:** *Timothy*, Paul’s valued companion ([[Romans 16#^16-21|Rom 16.21]]; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|1 Cor 4.17]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-10|16.10]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|2 Cor 1.1]],[[2 Corinthians 01#^1-19|19]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-19|2.19]],[[Philippians 02#^2-22|22]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-2|3.2]],[[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-6|6]]). His family is mentioned in [[2 Timothy 01#^1-5|2 Tim 1.5]]. ^16-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-3|3]]:** *Had him circumcised*, if Timothy is considered a Gentile by virtue of his Greek father, then Paul’s act seems contrary to the Jerusalem council (15.19) and Paul’s own practice ([[Galatians 02#^2-3|Gal 2.3–4]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-2|5.2–11]]). If Timothy is a Jew by virtue of his Jewish mother, the passage might reflect the first indication of the Jewish principle of matrilineal descent, which became the norm by the third century CE (*m. Qidd.* 3.12); the Tanakh suggests that identity was determined by the father. Regardless of how Acts understands Jewish practice, it uses this episode as a way to demonstrate that Paul wishes to avoid any objection from Jews, an image that fits Acts’ portrayal of Paul as scrupulously following Jewish Law and thus beyond reproach (cf. 21.24). ^16-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-5|5]]:** *Churches were strengthened*, see 14.22. A summary depicting the expansion of believers (2.42–47; 4.32–35; 5.12–16; 6.7; 9.31; 16.5; 19.20). ^16-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-6|6–7]]:** The Spirit directs Paul’s movements to Greece (see 8.26; 10.19–20; 13.4). ^16-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-8|8]]:** *Troas*, Alexandria Troas in northwest Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). ^16-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-9|9–10]]:** *Vision*, divine guidance often comes through visions; see 9.10. **10**: *Macedonia*, Roman province in northern Greece. *We*, the first use of the first-person plural, which continues intermittently until the end of the book (16.10–17; 20.5–15; 21.1–18; 27.1–28.16). It may reflect either Luke’s use of an eyewitness account or his desire to create that impression. *circumcised and keep the law,’* c Gk *men* d Other ancient authorities lack *and from what is strangled* e Gk *When they* f Other ancient authorities add verse 34, *But it seemed good to Silas to remain there* ^16-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-11|16.11–40]]:** **Events in Philippi.** Paul was instrumental in establishing a Christian community in Philippi and wrote at least one letter to the church there. *Samothrace* . . . *Neapolis*, Paul travels westward along the coast of Thrace and Macedonia (present-day Greece). ^16-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-12|12]]:** *Philippi*, Greek city and later Roman colony in Macedonia. ^16-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-13|13]]:** Paul finds the local Jewish community (13.5,14; 14.1; 17.1,10,17; 18.4,19; 19.8), gathered on the Sabbath (13.14; 17.2; 18.4). *Place of prayer*, Gk “proseuche,” a term regularly used to designate synagogue buildings (cf. [[LXX 3 Maccabees 07#^7-20|3 Macc 7.20]]; Josephus, *Life* 277; 280; *JIGRE* 22,24,25,117). ^16-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-14|14]]:** *Lydia, a worshiper of God*, the phrase indicates that she is a Gentile involved in a Jewish community or expressing an interest in Judaism but who has not converted (10.2; 13.16,26; 18.7). *Thyatira*, city in the region of Lydia in western Asia Minor (present-day Turkey), known for producing and trading dyed cloth. ^16-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-15|15]]:** See 11.14. *My home*, indicating that she is established and prosperous. ^16-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-17|17]]:** *Most High God*, see [[Psalms 057#^57-3|Ps 57.2]]; 78.56; [[Daniel 03#^3-26|Dan 3.26]]; [[LXX Judith 13#^13-18|Jdt 13.18]]; [[LXX Sirach 07#^7-9|Sir 7.9]]; [[LXX Sirach 24#^24-23|24.23]]; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 07#^7-9|3 Macc 7.9]]; also applied to pagan deities, thus familiar to non-Jews. *Way of salvation*, see 2.40n.; 2.47; 4.12; 11.14; 13.26; 15.11; 16.30,31; 28.28. ^16-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-18|18]]:** *In the name of Jesus Christ*, healings are performed using this language (3.6; 4.10). ^16-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-19|19]]:** Acts disparages religious activities that yield financial gain (8.18; 19.19,25; 20.33). ^16-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-20|20–21]]:** *Customs*, while Roman magistrates might condemn anyone for offending public order, there was no general policy against Jews teaching non-Roman customs. No specific customs are mentioned; rather, Acts uses the general charge to impugn Jewish customs, and perhaps Jews themselves, as being incompatible with Roman society. ^16-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-26|26]]:** *Earthquake*, understood as a divine act; on miraculous prison escapes see 5.17–21; 12.6–11. ^16-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-27|27]]:** Following Peter’s escape, Herod executed the guards (12.19). ^16-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-30|30–31]]:** *What must I do to be saved,* salvation requires acknowledging Jesus as Lord (2.21,38; 4.12). See 2.40n. ^16-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-33|33]]:** See v 15. ^16-33

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-37|37]]:** *Roman citizens*, Paul’s letters do not mention this biographical detail. ^16-37

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-38|38–39]]:** Cities could be punished for mistreating Roman citizens (Tacitus, *Ann.* 12.58; Suetonius, *Aug.* 47; Cassius Dio 54.7.6). ^16-38

**[[NRSVue Acts 16#^16-40|40]]:** The episode ends as it began with Paul in the company of Lydia. ^16-40
