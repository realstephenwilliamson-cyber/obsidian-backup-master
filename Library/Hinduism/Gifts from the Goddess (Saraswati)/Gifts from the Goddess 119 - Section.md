*[p. 349]* ^pg378

This is what the *Kalpasūtra*<sup>386</sup> says—and just this much. Sometimes I add that I 386  The *Paraśurāma Kalpasūtra.* would like to invite the entire cosmos to come in as well. Somehow when I think of that transcendent being, the cosmos doesn’t even enter into my head. So to appreciate the fullness and grandeur of this being that we are invoking, consider adding the following statement: *śrī cakra gata sarva āvaraṇa dēvatā svarūpiṇīm sarvatōbhadra-maṇḍala gata*. This invokes all the deities in the universe into the Sri Chakra.<sup>387</sup> 387  Guruji notes that this invocation involves “a single mandala, Now we invoke all the deities:

composed of eight by eight squares, for a total of 64. Into it, all the gods and goddesses in the universe are invoked. It is called 

sarva āyatana dēvataḥ svarūpiṇīm chaturāyatana dēvataḥ svarūpiṇīm

the *sarvatōbhadra*. It is drawn during Śarada Navarātri. We place 

divya siddha manava augha gurumaṇḍala svarūpiṇīm

the coconut and *kalaśa* on top of 

samasta dēśa kāla vastugatta jīva caitanya svarūpiṇīm

it, where we invoke the Devi.”

samasta dēva gandharva yakṣa kinnara apasarā

sadya siddha mānuṣa strīpuruṣa svarūpiṇīm

śrī paradēvatām ānanda bhairavīm ānanda

bhairavēna parama śivēna saḥ ravantīm

ramayantīm svātma abhinām parachittim dhyāyami

trikhaṇḍa mudrā garbhita kusumāñjalau

This includes all those deities (*chaturāyatana dēvataḥ svarūpiṇīm*) residing around the Devi—Ganesh, Sūrya, Vishnu and Shiva; all these gods and their attendants. All the flows of the gurus are to be invoked as well (*divya siddha manava augha gurumaṇḍala svarūpiṇīm*). Now, saying *trikhaṇḍa mudrā garbhita kusumāñjalau*, we make the *trikhaṇḍa mudrā* with our hands (which is the *yōni mudrā* with the fingers opened out).<sup>388</sup> 388  The mudras are introduced later in this selection.

Then we invoke all of the light beings: Saraswati, who is symbolized by holding a white flower in your two fingers; Lakshmi, who is symbolized by holding a yellow flower; and Shakti (Lalitā), who is symbolized by holding a red flower. Then you place all the different-colored flowers in the middle.
