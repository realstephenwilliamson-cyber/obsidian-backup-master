## About

A one-paragraph story by the Argentine writer Jorge Luis Borges, in Spanish "Del rigor en la ciencia" (also translated "On Rigor in Science"). An empire's mapmakers produce a map the size of the empire; later generations neglect it, and it decays.

- **Fictional source:** presented as a passage from one Suárez Miranda, *Viajes de varones prudentes*, book 4, chapter 45, Lérida, 1658. Lauro Zavala calls Suárez Miranda the apocryphal author and internal editor of the text.
- **First publication:** *Los Anales de Buenos Aires* 1.3, March 1946, p. 53, in "Museo", a series of selections Borges compiled with Adolfo Bioy Casares under the joint pseudonym B. Lynch Davis (Wikipedia; Borges Center).
- **Collections:** the second edition of *Historia universal de la infamia*. Wikipedia dates that edition 1946; the Borges Center bibliography lists the story in the Emecé edition of 1954, which has a new prologue. It then moved to the "Museo" section of *El hacedor*: the Borges Center gives the Emecé edition of 1960 and Zavala gives 1960; Wikipedia says it has appeared there since 1961. Current Spanish editions of the *Historia* omit it.
- **English:** Wikipedia cites the Penguin translation in *A Universal History of Infamy* (London, 1975). *El hacedor* appeared in English as *Dreamtigers* and later, translated by Andrew Hurley, as *The Maker*.
- **Antecedent:** Lewis Carroll's *Sylvie and Bruno Concluded* (1893), with its map on the scale of a mile to the mile.
- **Why it matters:** Umberto Eco used it as the epigraph to an essay on the impossibility of a 1:1 map of the empire; Jean Baudrillard cited it as a forerunner of his idea of hyperreality in *Simulacra and Simulation* (1981); James C. Scott quoted it in *Seeing Like a State*, crediting Suárez Miranda. Zavala reads it as a parable: rigor that copies its object exactly becomes useless.
