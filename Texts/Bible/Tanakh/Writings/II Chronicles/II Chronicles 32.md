## Chapter 32

**32:1** [^1]After these faithful deeds, King Sennacherib of Assyria invaded Judah and encamped against its fortified towns with the aim of taking them over. ^32-1

**32:2** When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come, intent on making war against Jerusalem, ^32-2

**32:3** he consulted with his officers and warriors about stopping the flow of the springs outside the city, and they supported him. ^32-3

**32:4** A large force was assembled to stop up all the springs and the wadi that flowed through the land, for otherwise, they thought, the king of Assyria would come and find water in abundance. ^32-4

**32:5** He acted with vigor, rebuilding the whole breached wall, raising towers on it, and building another wall outside it. He fortified the Millo of the City of David, and made a great quantity of arms and shields. ^32-5

**32:6** He appointed battle officers over the people; then, gathering them to him in the square of the city gate, he rallied them, saying, ^32-6

**32:7** “Be strong and of good courage; do not be frightened or dismayed by the king of Assyria or by the horde that is with him, for we have more with us than he has with him. ^32-7

**32:8** With him is an arm of flesh, but with us is the LORD our God, to help us and to fight our battles.” The people were encouraged by the speech of King Hezekiah of Judah. ^32-8

**32:9** Afterward, King Sennacherib of Assyria sent his officers to Jerusalem—he and all his staff being at Lachish—with this message to King Hezekiah of Judah and to all the people of Judah who were in Jerusalem: ^32-9

**32:10** “Thus said King Sennacherib of Assyria: On what do you trust to enable you to endure a siege in Jerusalem? ^32-10

**32:11** Hezekiah is seducing you to a death of hunger and thirst, saying, ‘The LORD our God will save us from the king of Assyria.’ ^32-11

**32:12** But is not Hezekiah the one who removed His shrines and His altars and commanded the people of Judah and Jerusalem saying, ‘Before this one altar you shall prostrate yourselves, and upon it make your burnt offerings’? ^32-12

**32:13** Surely you know what I and my fathers have done to the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of the lands able to save their lands from me? ^32-13

**32:14** Which of all the gods of any of those nations whom my fathers destroyed was able to save his people from me, that your God should be able to save you from me? ^32-14

**32:15** Now then, do not let Hezekiah delude you; do not let him seduce you in this way; do not believe him. For no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to save his people from me or from my fathers—much less your God, to save you from me!” ^32-15

**32:16** His officers said still more things against the LORD God and against His servant Hezekiah. ^32-16

**32:17** He also wrote letters reviling the LORD God of Israel, saying of Him, “Just as the gods of the other nations of the earth did not save their people from me, so the God of Hezekiah will not save his people from me.” ^32-17

**32:18** They called loudly in the language of Judah to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to frighten them into panic, so as to capture the city. ^32-18

**32:19** They spoke of the God of Jerusalem as though He were like the gods of the other peoples of the earth, made by human hands. ^32-19

**32:20** Then King Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz prayed about this, and cried out to heaven. ^32-20

**32:21** The LORD sent an angel who annihilated every mighty warrior, commander, and officer in the army of the king of Assyria, and he returned in disgrace to his land. He entered the house of his god, and there some of his own offspring struck him down by the sword. ^32-21

**32:22** Thus the LORD delivered Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from King Sennacherib of Assyria, and from everyone; He provided for them on all sides. ^32-22

**32:23** Many brought tribute to the LORD to Jerusalem, and gifts to King Hezekiah of Judah; thereafter he was exalted in the eyes of all the nations. ^32-23

**32:24** At that time, Hezekiah fell deathly sick. He prayed to the LORD, who responded to him and gave him a sign. ^32-24

**32:25** Hezekiah made no return for what had been bestowed upon him, for he grew arrogant; so wrath was decreed for him and for Judah and Jerusalem. ^32-25

**32:26** Then Hezekiah humbled himself where he had been arrogant, he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and no wrath of the LORD came on them during the reign of Hezekiah. ^32-26

**32:27** Hezekiah enjoyed riches and glory in abundance; he filled treasuries with silver and gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and all lovely objects; ^32-27

**32:28** and store-cities with the produce of grain, wine, and oil, and stalls for all kinds of beasts, and flocks for sheepfolds. ^32-28

**32:29** And he acquired towns, and flocks of small and large cattle in great number, for God endowed him with very many possessions. ^32-29

**32:30** It was Hezekiah who stopped up the spring of water of Upper Gihon, leading it downward west of the City of David; Hezekiah prospered in all that he did. ^32-30

**32:31** So too in the matter of the ambassadors of the princes of Babylon, who were sent to him to inquire about the sign that was in the land, when God forsook him in order to test him, to learn all that was in his mind. ^32-31

**32:32** The other events of Hezekiah’s reign, and his faithful acts, are recorded in the visions of the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz and in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel. ^32-32

**32:33** Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and was buried on the upper part of the tombs of the sons of David. When he died, all the people of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem accorded him much honor. Manasseh, his son, succeeded him. ^32-33

[^1]: Cf. 2 Kings 18–20; Isa. 36–39.
