ACCENTUATION. 

In the contemporary pronunciation the rule of accentuation valid for Latin is extended to the last four syllables of a word. A certain stress thus rests on the penultimate syllable if this is long by nature or by position (two consonants following the vowel), on the antepenultimate syllable if the penultimate is short and it itself is long, otherwise—thus if the penultimate and antepenultimate syllables are short=—on the fourth-to-last syllable. Examples: utksipya, vănara, mūrkhéna, 1600 (bhis a. single cons.), udvéjayati, dbhihitah. In compounds each component usually retains its own accentuation.
