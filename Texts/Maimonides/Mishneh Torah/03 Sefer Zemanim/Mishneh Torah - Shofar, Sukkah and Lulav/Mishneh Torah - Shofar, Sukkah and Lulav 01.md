**1.** It is a positive commandment from the Torah[^1] to hear the sounding of the shofar[^2] on Rosh HaShanah, as \[[[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^29-1|Numbers 29:1]]] states: "It shall be a day of sounding [the ram's horn] for you."  
The[^3] shofar, which is sounded both on Rosh HaShanah and for the *yovel*,[^4] is a bent[^5] ram's horn.[^6] All shofarot other than that of a ram are unacceptable.[^7]  
Even though the sounding of the shofar on Rosh HaShanah[^8] is not explicitly mentioned in the Torah [it was derived by our Sages in the following manner]. Concerning the *yovel*, \[[[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-9|Leviticus 25:9]]] states: "You shall make a proclamation, sounding the shofar... you shall proclaim with the shofar." The oral tradition[^9] explains that just as the "sounding" required by the Torah in the *yovel* requires a shofar, so, too, the "sounding" on Rosh HaShanah requires a shofar.[^10] ^1-1

**2.** In the Temple,[^11] on Rosh HaShanah, they would blow [the shofar in the following manner]: There was one shofar and two trumpets, [one on either] side.[^12] The sounding of the shofar was extended,[^13] while that of the trumpets was shortened, because the mitzvah of the day is performed with the shofar.[^14]  
Why were the trumpets sounded together with it? Because \[[[Psalms 098#^98-6|Psalms 98:6]]] states: "You shall sound trumpets and the voice of the shofar before God, the King."[^15] However, in other places[^16] on Rosh Hashanah, only the shofar is blown. ^1-2

**3.** At the outset, we should not blow a shofar of idol worship.[^17] However, if one sounded it, one has fulfilled his obligation.[^18] [In contrast,] should one sound a shofar belonging to an apostate city,[^19] one has not fulfilled one's obligation.[^20]  
Concerning a stolen shofar:[^21] one who blows it fulfills his obligation, because the mitzvah is only to listen to the sound,[^22] even though the listener does not touch [the shofar][^23] or lift it up.[^24] The laws of theft do not apply to sound alone.[^25]  
Similarly, a shofar from an[^26] *olah* offering should not be sounded,[^27] but[^28] if one sounds it, he fulfills his obligation, because the laws of[^29] מעילה do not apply with regard to sound alone. If you ask: "Behold, he has derived benefit from hearing [the shofar's] sound?"[^30] - mitzvot were not given for our benefit.[^31]  
Based on this concept, a person who vows not to derive benefit from a shofar may use it to blow the *teki'ot* required to fulfill the mitzvah. ^1-3

**4.** Regarding a shofar to be used on Rosh HaShanah: It is forbidden to violate the festival laws to obtain it. This applies[^32] even when the forbidden practice is in the category of *sh'vut*.[^33]  
How is the above exemplified? If there is a shofar in a treetop or across a river[^34] - and that is the only shofar available - one may not climb the tree or swim across the water to bring it.[^35] Needless to say, we may not cut the shofar [from the animal's head][^36] or perform a forbidden labor[^37] [to prepare a shofar so that we may blow it].[^38]  
[The rationale for the above is:] Blowing the shofar fulfills a positive commandment,[^39] while [the observance of] the festivals fulfills both a positive and a negative commandment.[^40] The observance of a positive commandment does not negate the observance of both a positive and negative commandment.[^41]  
It is permitted to rinse a shofar with water, wine, or vinegar in order to improve its tone.[^42] However, as an expression of deference, one should never[^43] use urine [for that purpose], lest one view the mitzvot in a deprecating manner.[^44] ^1-4

**5.** The minimum size of a shofar is [a measure] sufficient that one may hold the shofar in one's hands [with the ends] visibly [protruding][^45] on either side.[^46]  
Should a shofar be cracked lengthwise,[^47] it is unacceptable.[^48] Should it be cracked along its width[^49] - if a measure equivalent to the minimum size of a shofar remains,[^50] it is kosher.[^51] It is considered as if it were cut off at the place of the crack.[^52]  
[Regarding a shofar with] a hole: If it was plugged[^53] with another substance,[^54] it is unacceptable.[^55] If it was plugged with its own kind, it is kosher [under the following conditions]:  
the majority of the shofar remained whole;  
the plugging of the holes did not alter its sound.[^56]  
If one [merely] perforated the insides of the horn,[^57] [but did not remove them,] it is kosher, because a substance of the same kind is not considered an intervening entity.[^58] Should one stick together fragments of *shofarot* until one has constructed a shofar, it is unacceptable.[^59] ^1-5

**6.** If one made any addition to a shofar[^60] - whether of its kind[^61] or from another substance[^62] - it is unacceptable.  
Should one coat it with gold[^63] from the inside or at the mouthpiece, it is unacceptable.[^64] Should one coat it[^65] on the outside: If its sound is changed from what it was originally, it is not acceptable.[^66] If its sound did not change, it is kosher.[^67]  
Should one place one shofar within another: If one hears the sound of the inner shofar,[^68] one has fulfilled one's obligation.[^69] If one hears the outer shofar,[^70] one has not fulfilled one's obligation.[^71]  
Should one widen the narrow portion of the shofar and narrow its wider end, the shofar is unacceptable.[^72] ^1-6

**7.** If a shofar was long and one shortened it,[^73] it is kosher.[^74] If one scraped away the horn[^75] - either from the inside or from the outside[^76] - even if one did so to the extent that all that remained was the thin external shell,[^77] it is kosher.[^78]  
Regardless of whether [the shofar's] sound is heavy, thin, or raspy,[^79] it is kosher, because all the sounds produced by the shofar are kosher.[^80] ^1-7

**8.** When a person sounds a shofar within a pit or within a cave,[^81] those standing within the pit or cave fulfill their obligation.[^82] Concerning those standing outside: If[^83] they hear[^84] the sound of the shofar, they fulfill their obligation.[^85] If they hear the sound of an echo,[^86] they do not fulfill their obligation.[^87]  
Similar principles apply regarding one who blows into a giant barrel. If he hears[^88] the sound of a shofar,[^89] he fulfills his obligation. If he hears an echo,[^90] he does not fulfill his obligation.[^91] ^1-8

[^91]: The *Taz* 587:1 explains that this concept is also relevant for synagogues with poor acoustics. If the people hear echoes together with the shofar's sound, they do not fulfill their obligation.  
    At present, there is a more common application of this principle. A person who hears the shofar through a microphone does not fulfill the mitzvah. In addition to the difficulties involved with the use of the microphone on a festival, there is a more essential problem. The listeners are not hearing the sound of the shofar, but rather a second sound, produced by a different mechanism. The microphone converts the sound waves of the shofar to electronic signals; these are then amplified and converted to a different set of sound waves. Hence, by hearing such a sound, we cannot fulfill the mitzvah obligating us to hear a shofar's call.
