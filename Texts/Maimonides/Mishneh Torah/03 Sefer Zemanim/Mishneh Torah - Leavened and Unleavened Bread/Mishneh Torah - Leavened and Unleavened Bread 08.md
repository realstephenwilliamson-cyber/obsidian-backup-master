**1.** The order of the fulfillment of these mitzvot[^1] on the night of the fifteenth [of Nisan][^2] is as follows: In the beginning,[^3] a cup [of wine] is mixed[^4] for each individual.[^5] They recite the blessing,  
בורא פרי הגפן and the *kiddush* of the day on it, and the blessing, *shehecheyanu*.[^6] Then, they drink [it].[^7]  
Afterwards, one recites the blessing, על נטילת ידים, and washes one's hands.[^8] A set table is brought,[^9] on which are[^10] maror, another vegetable,[^11] matzah, *charoset*, the body of the Paschal lamb, and the meat of the festive offering of the fourteenth of Pesach.[^12] At present,[^13] we bring two types of meat on the table: one in commemoration of the Paschal sacrifice[^14] and one in commemoration of the festive offering. ^8-1

**2.** He[^15] begins and recites the blessing, בורא פרי האדמה,[^16] takes the vegetable,[^17] dips it in *charoset*,[^18] and eats a כזית. He and all those eating together with him, each and every one, do not eat less than a כזית.[^19]  
[^20] Afterwards, the table is taken away[^21] from the person reciting the Haggadah alone.[^22] The second cup [of wine] is mixed.[^23] Here is where the son asks, and the one reciting [the Haggadah][^24] says:  
Why is this night different from all other nights?  
On all other nights, we are not required to dip even once. On this night, we dip twice?[^25]  
On all other nights, we eat chametz (leaven) or matzah. On this night, only matzah?  
On all other nights, we eat roasted, boiled, or cooked meat. On this night we eat only roasted?[^26]  
On all other nights, we eat any type of vegetables. On this night, we eat maror (bitter herbs)?  
On[^27] all other nights, we eat either sitting upright or reclining. On this night, we all recline?[^28] ^8-2

**3.** At present,[^29] one does not recite [the question], "on this night, only roasted," for we do not have a[^30] sacrifice.[^31]  
One begins [describing our people's] base [origins][^32] and recites until one concludes expounding on[^33] the entire passage that begins "An Aramean sought to destroy my father."[^34] ^8-3

**4.** The table is returned before him and he says: This Paschal sacrifice which we eat [is] because the Omnipresent passed over the houses of our ancestors in Egypt, as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-27|Exodus 12:27]]] states: "And you shall say: 'It is a Paschal sacrifice unto God.'"  
He lifts up the maror in his hands and says: This maror that we eat [is] because the Egyptians made the lives of our forefathers bitter in Egypt, as \[[[Torah 13 - Shemot#^1-14|Exodus 1:14]]] states: "and they embittered their lives."  
And he lifts up the matzah in his hand and says:  
This matzah which we eat [is] because the dough of our ancestors was not able to leaven before the Holy One, blessed be He, was revealed to them and redeemed them immediately, as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-39|Exodus 12:39]]] states: "And they baked the dough which they took out of Egypt [as cakes of matzah]."  
At present, he says: "This Paschal sacrifice, which our ancestors would eat when the Temple was standing, [is] because the Holy One, blessed be He, passed over the houses of our ancestors..." ^8-4

**5.** And he[^35] says:  
Therefore, we are obliged to thank, praise, laud, glorify, adore, exalt, magnify, and give eternal honor to the One who did all these miracles for us and took us out from slavery to freedom, from sorrow to joy, from deep darkness to great light.[^36] [Therefore,] let us recite before Him: Halleluyah!  
[He continues, beginning the Hallel,[^37] reciting from] "Halleluyah! Servants of God[^38] - offer praise;" until "the flintstone into a stream of water." He concludes:[^39]  
Blessed are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who redeemed us and redeemed our ancestors from Egypt and has enabled us to reach this night so that we may eat matzah and bitter herbs upon[^40] it."  
At present, he adds:[^41]  
So too, God, our Lord and Lord of our fathers, enable us to reach other festivals and holidays that will come to us in peace, celebrating in the rebuilding of Your city[^42] and rejoicing in Your service.[^43] Then, we shall eat of the sacrifices[^44] and of the Paschal offerings whose blood shall be sprinkled on the wall of Your altar to be graciously accepted. Then, we shall offer thanks to You [with] a new song for our redemption and for the deliverance of our souls. Blessed are You, God, who redeemed Israel.  
He recites the blessing, בורא פרי הגפן,[^45] and drinks the second cup. ^8-5

**6.** Afterwards, he recites the blessing, *al netilat yadayim*,[^46] and washes his hands a second time,[^47] for he diverted his attention [from his hands] during the time he was reciting the Haggadah.[^48]  
He takes two cakes [of matzah],[^49] divides one of them,[^50] places the broken half inside the whole [cake][^51] and recites the blessing, *hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz*.[^52]  
Why does he not recite a blessing on two[^53] loaves, as on other festivals?[^54] because \[[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-3|Deuteronomy 16:3]]] states "the bread of poverty." Just as a poor man is accustomed to eating a broken [loaf], so, too, a broken loaf should be used.[^55]  
Afterwards,[^56] he wraps matzah and maror together as one,[^57] dips it in *charoset*[^58] and recites the blessing:  
Blessed are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us regarding the eating of matzah and bitter herbs  
and eats them. If he eats matzah separately and maror separately, he[^59] recites a blessing for the former[^60] in its own right and the latter[^61] in its own right. ^8-6

**7.** Afterwards, he recites the blessing:  
Blessed[^62] are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us regarding the eating of the sacrifice[^63]  
and first,[^64] partakes of the meat of the *Chaggigah* offering of the fourteenth [of Nisan].[^65]  
[Then,] he recites the blessing:  
Blessed are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us regarding the eating of the Paschal sacrifice[^66]  
and eats[^67] from the body of the Paschal sacrifice.[^68] The blessing for the Paschal sacrifice does not free one of [the obligation of the blessing[^69] for] the [*Chaggigah*] offering. [Conversely, the blessing for the *Chaggigah*] offering[^70] does not free one of [the obligation of the blessing for] the Paschal sacrifice.[^71] ^8-7

**8.** At present, when there is no [Paschal] sacrifice,[^72] after one recites the blessing, *hamotzi lechem*,[^73] one then[^74] recites the blessing, *al achilat matzah*, dips the matzah in *charoset*,[^75] and eats it.[^76]  
Afterwards,[^77] one recites the blessing, *al achilat maror,* dips the maror in *charoset* and eats it.[^78] One should not leave [the bitter herbs] in the *charoset* for a prolonged period, lest their[^79] taste be negated, since [eating the maror] is a mitzvah ordained by the Sages.[^80]  
Afterwards, one should wrap matzah and maror together,[^81] dip them in the *charoset*, and eat them without reciting a blessing, to recall the Temple.[^82] ^8-8

**9.** Afterwards, one continues the meal, eating whatever one desires to eat and drinking whatever one desires to drink.[^83] At its conclusion, one eats from the Paschal sacrifice,[^84] even [as small a portion as] a כזית,[^85] and does not taste anything afterwards.[^86]  
At present, one eats a כזית of matzah[^87] and does not taste anything[^88] afterwards,[^89] so that, after the completion of the meal, the taste of the meat of the Paschal sacrifice or the matzah will [remain] in one's mouth, for eating them is the mitzvah. ^8-9

**10.** Afterwards, he washes his hands[^90] and recites the grace after meals over a third cup [of wine][^91] and drinks it.[^92]  
Afterwards, he pours out a fourth cup and completes the Hallel[^93] over it,[^94] reciting upon it[^95] the blessing of song—i.e.,[^96] "May all Your works praise You, God..."[^97] - recites the blessing, *borey pri hagefen*, [and drinks the wine].[^98] Afterwards, he does not taste anything,[^99] with the exception of water,[^100] throughout the entire night.[^101]  
It is permissible to mix a fifth cup[^102] and recite upon it "the great Hallel"[^103] - i.e., from "Give thanks to God, for He is good" until "By the rivers of Babylon." This cup is not an obligation like the other cups.[^104]  
One may complete the Hallel wherever one desires, even though it is not the place where one ate.[^105] ^8-10

**11.** In a place where it is customary to eat roasted meat on Pesach night, one may eat. [However,] in a place where it is customary not to eat [roasted meat],[^106] one should not eat it,[^107] lest it be said: "this is the meat of the Paschal sacrifice."  
In[^108] all places,[^109] it is forbidden to eat a whole sheep[^110] that has been roasted in its entirety[^111] on this night, for it would appear as though one were eating sacrificial animals outside [the area prescribed for them].[^112] If it has been cut in pieces, is lacking a limb,[^113] or one of the limbs attached to it has been boiled,[^114] it is permitted in a place where [roasted meat] is customarily [eaten]. ^8-11

**12.** A person who does not have any wine[^115] on the nights[^116] of Pesach recites the *kiddush* on bread,[^117] as he would do on the Sabbath. [Afterwards,] he carries out all the [above] matters according to this order.[^118]  
A person who has no other vegetable besides bitter herbs: At the outset,[^119] he recites two blessings over the bitter herbs: *borey pri ha'adamah* and *al achilat maror*,[^120] and partakes of them.[^121] When he concludes the Haggadah,[^122] he recites the blessing over the matzah and eats it. Afterwards, he eats from the bitter herbs[^123] without reciting a[^124] blessing.[^125] ^8-12

**13.** A person who has only a single[^126] כזית of *shemurah*[^127] matzah: When he concludes [eating] his meal from matzah[^128] which was not watched,[^129] he recites the blessing, *al achilat matzah*, eats that כזית[^130] and does not taste anything afterward.[^131] ^8-13

**14.** A person[^132] who slept in the midst of the meal and then woke up, does not begin to eat again.[^133] [However,] if some members of a company slept in the middle of a meal,[^134] they may eat again.[^135] If they all fell into a sound slumber[^136] and then awoke, they should not eat.[^137] If they all [merely] dozed,[^138] they may eat. ^8-14
