### 10.1–12: Mission of the seventy

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 135* · ([[Matthew 09#^9-37|Mt 9.37–38]]; [[Matthew 10#^10-7|10.7–16]]). ^10-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Seventy*, see [[Torah 02 - Noach#^10-2|Gen 10.2–31]] (LXX lists seventy-two names; see translators’ note *b*); *Jub*. 44.34 (seventy nations of the world); in the wilderness, Moses was aided by *seventy* elders ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-1|Ex 24.1]],[[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-9|9]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-16|Num 11.16]],[[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-24|24–25]]). *Sent them on ahead*, perhaps echoing [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^1-22|Deut 1.22–25]]. ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Harvest is plentiful*, see 3.17n. ^10-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-4|4]]:** *Carry no purse*, see 9.3n. *Greet no one*, see [[II Kings 04#^4-29|2 Kings 4.29]]. ^10-4

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-5|5]]:** *Peace*, standard greeting; see 24.36; [[I Samuel 25#^25-6|1 Sam 25.6]]; [[LXX Tobit 12#^12-17|Tob 12.17]]. ^10-5

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-7|7]]:** *Laborer deserves to be paid*, see [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-15|Deut 24.15]]; classical rabbis were not paid for teaching. ^10-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-8|8]]:** *Eat what is set before you*, anticipating Acts 10. Since no Gentile mission is yet enjoined, the food would be according to Jewish dietary practices although not necessarily prepared or tithed according to Pharisaic norms. ^10-8

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-9|9]]:** *Cure the sick*, in imitation of Jesus and as a sign of the kingdom; Jesus’ followers display this ability in the Book of Acts (e.g., [[Acts 03#^3-6|Acts 3.6]]**–**10). ^10-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-11|11]]:** *Dust*, see 9.5n. ^10-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-12|12]]:** *Sodom*, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-24|Gen 19.24–28]]; for the comparative fate, see [[Ezekiel 16#^16-45|Ezek 16.45–58]]; [[Lamentations 4#^4-6|Lam 4.6]]. ^10-12

### 10.13–16: Woes to unrepentant cities

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-13|Text]], p. 135* · ([[Matthew 11#^11-20|Mt 11.20–24]]). ^10-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-13|13]]:** *Chorazin*, 2 miles (3 km) north of Capernaum. *Bethsaida*, see 9.10n. *Tyre and Sidon*, predominantly Gentile cities north of Galilee known for wealth and pride (Isa 23; Ezek 26–28). In contrast to Mk 7 and Mt 15, Luke depicts no mission in this area. *Sackcloth and ashes*, see [[Isaiah 58#^58-5|Isa 58.5]]; [[Esther 04#^4-1|Esth 4.1]],[[Esther 04#^4-3|3]]; [[Daniel 09#^9-3|Dan 9.3]]. ^10-13-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-15|15]]:** *Capernaum*, see 4.23n. *Hades*, hell, comparable to Heb “Sheol” (Isa 14; [[LXX Tobit 03#^3-10|Tob 3.10]]; [[LXX Tobit 04#^4-19|4.19]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 01#^1-14|Wis 1.14]]; and elsewhere). By the first century ce, the concept of “hell” as a permanent place of damnation had developed. ^10-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-16|16]]:** See [[Matthew 10#^10-40|Mt 10.40]]. ^10-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-17|17]]:** *Seventy*, see 10.1n. ^10-17-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-18|18]]:** *Watched Satan fall*, visionary experience ([[John 12#^12-31|Jn 12.31]]; [[Revelation 12#^12-7|Rev 12.7–12]]; cf. [[Isaiah 14#^14-12|Isa 14.12]]). ^10-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-19|19]]:** *Snakes and scorpions*, see [[LXX Psalms 90#^90-13|Ps 90.13]]\[LXX\]. *Enemy*, Satan. ^10-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-20|20]]:** *Written in heaven*, an ancient Mesopotamian idea found in [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-32|Ex 32.32]]; [[Psalms 069#^69-29|Ps 69.28]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1]]; [[Revelation 03#^3-5|Rev 3.5]]; *1 En*. 47.3; 104.1; *Jub*. 19.9; etc., cf. the Rosh Ha-Shanah greeting: “May you be inscribed for a good year.” ^10-20

### 10.21–24: Knowledge of God

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-21|Text]], p. 136* · ([[Matthew 11#^11-25|Mt 11.25–27]]). ^10-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-21|21]]:** *In the Holy Spirit*, in communion with God, see 1.15n. *Father*, *Lord . . . ,* ([[LXX Tobit 07#^7-17|Tob 7.17]]; [[LXX Judith 09#^9-12|Jdt 9.12]]). ^10-21-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-22|22]]:** *Son . . . Father*, see [[John 03#^3-35|Jn 3.35]]; [[John 10#^10-15|10.15]]; [[John 17#^17-21|17.21]]. Knowledge of God is only fully known by and through Jesus. ^10-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-23|23–24]]:** See [[Matthew 13#^13-16|Mt 13.16–17]]. ^10-23

> [!note] Parable of the Good Samaritan ([[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25–37]]) *(p. 136)*
> The account opens with a lawyer (i.e., here, an expert in Torah) “testing” Jesus and thus depicting the lawyer as another of Jesus’ opponents. The term “test \[or trial\],” also translated “tempt,” appears in 11.4: “Do not bring us to the time of trial.” Further, by testing Jesus, the lawyer takes Satan’s role (4.12). His question is also misguided. “Eternal life” is not a commodity gained by a limited action; it is a gift freely given.
>
> Jesus responds with a question of his own: “What is written in the law?” (10.26). The lawyer responds by combining [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]], on love of God and [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], on love of neighbor—according to R. Akiva, Torah’s great (Heb *gadol*) teaching (*Sifra Kedoshim* \[ch 4\] on [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]). The two verses are combined elsewhere in Jewish thought (*T. Iss*. 5.2; *T. Dan* 5.3), and in [[Matthew 22#^22-37|Mt 22.37]] and [[Mark 12#^12-29|Mk 12.29–31]] they form Jesus’ “great commandment.” (See “The Concept of Neighbor,” p. 645.)
>
> Although some commentators depict the robbers as Jewish Robin Hoods displaced from their land by over-taxation and urbanization, and who protest their socio-economic disen-franchisement by taking from the rich and giving to the poor, the text says nothing of the sort. The term for “robber,” *lēstēs* (compare rabbinic Heb *listim*), connotes a violent criminal.
>
> Nor, contrary to one popular view, do the priest and the Levite bypass the injured man because of ritual purity concerns. Tobit (1.16–20) and Jose- phus (*Ag. Ap*. 2.30.211) demonstrate the strong Jewish concern for the respectful treatment of the dead. While Lev 21 forbids priests, from touching corpses save the bodies of immediate relatives; *m. Naz*. 7.1 insists even “A high priest or a Nazirite \[a person under utmost purity\] . . . may contract uncleanness because of a neglected corpse” (see also *y. Naz*. 56a; *b. Naz*. [[Nazir 43#43b|43b]]). Levites are not forbidden from contact with corpses. Further suggesting the issue is not purity: the priest is not going up to Jerusalem, where impurity would have prevented him from participating in the Temple service, but “down from” (Gk *katabainō*; 10.31) the city.
>
> Priest and Levite indicate not an interest in purity but a point about community. Jews generally then, and now, fit into one of three groups: priests (*kohanim*) descended from Aaron; Levites descended from Levi (Aaron’s ancestor), and Israelites, descended from children of Jacob other than Levi. Mention of the first two anticipates mention of the third. The parable shocks by making the third person not the expected Israelite but the unexpected Samaritan, the enemy of the Jews. It thus evokes [[II Chronicles 28#^28-8|2 Chr 28.8–15]], wherein enemy Samaritans care for Jewish victims, even as it reframes the lawyer’s question.
>
> The lawyer asked, “who is my neighbor?” Jesus reframes the question: more important than determining the difference between neighbor and stranger are the commandment to love both ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; 34) and, more, to act in that love.

### 10.25–28: Lawyer’s challenge

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-25|Text]], p. 137* · ([[Matthew 22#^22-34|Mt 22.34–40]]; [[Mark 12#^12-28|Mk 12.28–34]]; cf. [[Luke 18#^18-9|Lk 18.9–14]]). ^10-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-25|25]]:** *Lawyer*, Torah expert and a negative foil for Luke (see 7.30; 11.45–46,52; 14.3). *Test*, in the role of Satan, see 4.2; cf. 11.16. *Inherit eternal life*, see *m. Avot* 2.7, “\[The one who\] has gotten teachings of Torah, he has gotten himself life eternal.” ^10-25-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-26|26]]:** *Law*, Torah (2.22–24,27,39; 5.17; 16.16–17; 24.44). ^10-26

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-27|27]]:** *Love the Lord*, see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]] (cf. [[Joshua 22#^22-5|Josh 22.5]]). *Your neighbor as yourself*, see [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; R. Akiva, *Sifre* on [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], calls this the “greatest principle in the Law.” The Heb “meʾodekha,” usually translated “as yourself,” could also mean “who is a person like you” (i.e., a fellow human being). To Akiva’s comment, R. Ben Azzai replies by citing [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-1|Gen 5.1]] (creation in the image of God) “so that you should not say: ‘Since I despise myself, let my fellow be despised with me; since I am cursed, let my fellow be cursed with me.’ This is a more basic law” (see also *y. Ned.* 9.4; *Gen. Rab*. 24.7). On combining love of God and love of neighbor, see [[Matthew 22#^22-38|Mt 22.38]]**–**39; [[Mark 12#^12-30|Mk 12.30]]**–**33; *T. Dan* 5.3; *T. Iss*. 5.2. ^10-27

### 10.29–37: Parable of the good Samaritan

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-29|Text]], p. 137* · See “Parable of the Good Samaritan,” p. 136. ^10-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-29|29]]:** *Justify himself*, present himself in the right. ^10-29-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-30|30]]:** *Jerusalem to Jericho*, 17–18 miles, with a steep drop-off. *Robbers*, thieves, not freedom fighters (see 19.46; 22.52; [[John 10#^10-8|Jn 10.8]]; [[John 18#^18-40|18.40]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-26|2 Cor 11.26]]). ^10-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-31|31]]:** *Priest* (Heb “kohen”), such as Zechariah (see 1.5). *Going down*, not up to Jerusalem, where purity would have been a particular issue had he responsibilities in the Temple (see 1.9n.). ^10-31

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-32|32]]:** *Levite*, Temple functionary (see e.g., [[Torah 34 - Bamidbar#^1-50|Num 1.50]]). ^10-32

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-33|33]]:** *Samaritan*, see 9.52n. *Pity*, translated “compassion” in 7.13; 15.20. ^10-33

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-34|34]]:** *Oil*, see [[Isaiah 01#^1-6|Isa 1.6]]. *Wine*, as an an-tiseptic. ^10-34

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-35|35]]:** *Two denarii*, providing the cost of lodging. The full imagery evokes [[II Chronicles 28#^28-8|2 Chr 28.8–15]]. ^10-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-36|36]]:** *Was a neighbor*, Jesus changes the original question. ^10-36

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-37|37]]:** *The one*, the lawyer cannot say “Samaritan.” ^10-37

### 10.38–42: Martha and Mary

*[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-38|Text]], p. 137* · (see Jn 11–12). ^10-38

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-38|38]]:** *Her home*, Martha is a householder; Jesus criticizes her as he does other householders (see 5.29n.). ^10-38-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-39|39]]:** *Sat at the Lord’s feet*, see 1.17n.; women received, and gave, instruction in synagogues (13.11), homes, patronage capacities (see 8.3n.), and personal conversations. ^10-39

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-40|40]]:** *Many tasks*, Gk “pollēn diakonian,” lit., “much serving” or “much ministry” (4.39). ^10-40

**[[NRSVue Luke 10#^10-42|42]]:** *Better part*, instruction precedes service. ^10-42
