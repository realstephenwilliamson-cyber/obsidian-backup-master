### 5.1–7.29: The Sermon on the Mount

*[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 19* · See Luke’s “Sermon on the Plain” ([[Luke 06#^6-17|Lk 6.17–7.1]]). ^5-1

### 5.1–12: The Beatitudes

*[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 19* · “Beatitude” from Lat “beatus,” translation of Gk “makarios,” “blessed, fortunate” (see “The Beatitudes and Antitheses,” p. 18). Similar blessings with the introductory formula “happy are those” and citing similar themes such as the poor and humble appear in the Psalms (2.12; 32.1–2; 40.4; 41.1–2; 65.4; 84.4–5; 94.12; 112.1 etc.); see also [[Job 05#^5-17|Job 5.17]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-12|Dan 12.12]]; [[LXX Sirach 14#^14-1|Sir 14.1–2]]; 4Q525. ^5-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-1|1–2]]:** Like Moses, Jesus escapes death, enters Egypt, enters the water, goes into the wilderness, and ascends a mountain ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-15|Ex 2.15]]; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^5-1|5.1]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-26|14.26–29]]; [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-3|19.3]]). ^5-1-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-3|3]]:** *Blessed*, Gk “makarioi,” compare Heb “ʾashrei” (e.g., [[Psalms 001#^1-1|Ps 1.1]]). *Poor in spirit*, humble ([[Isaiah 61#^61-1|Isa 61.1]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-2|66.2]]; [[Zephaniah 2#^2-3|Zeph 2.3]]; 1QM 14.7 speaks of “the poor in spirit” whom God will save). ^5-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-4|4]]:** *Those who mourn* may imply more than grieving for the dead; it may include the suffering righteous, or those who grieve over the community’s sins, a Matthean (5.10–12; 7.15–22; 10.23; 13.53–58; 16.24–25; 19.28; 21.12–13; 24.9–10) and generally Jewish theme ([[Isaiah 61#^61-1|Isa 61.1–3]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-10|66.10]]; [[LXX Tobit 13#^13-14|Tob 13.14]]). ^5-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-5|5]]:** *Meek . . . inherit the earth*, based on [[Psalms 037#^37-11|Ps 37.11]] \[LXX\]; the Hebrew states that the meek will inherit the “land” (Heb “eretz”). *Meek*, people who do not take advantage of their position ([[Isaiah 49#^49-13|Isa 49.13]]; [[Psalms 022#^22-27|Ps 22.27]]; [[Proverbs 16#^16-19|Prov 16.19]]; *b. Shabb*. 30b; *b. Ned*. 38a; *Pesiq. Rab*. 36; Josephus, *Ant*. 19.330; Philo, *Life of Moses* 2.279). ^5-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-6|6]]:** See 1.19n. (cf. [[Isaiah 51#^51-1|Isa 51.1–5]]; *1 En*. 58.2–3). ^5-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-7|7]]:** *Merciful*, reflecting Heb “ḥesed,” a highly regarded attribute and one of the two primary aspects of God (along with justice; see [[Psalms 145#^145-9|Ps 145.9]]; *b. Shabb*. 151b; *Gen. Rab*. 33). ^5-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-8|8]]:** See [[Psalms 024#^24-3|Ps 24.3–4]]; *Lev. Rab.* 23.13. *Heart* represents the center of thought and conviction ([[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-47|Deut 28.47]]; [[Isaiah 35#^35-4|Isa 35.4]]; [[Proverbs 27#^27-11|Prov 27.11]];). ^5-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-9|9]]:** *Peacemakers*, the rabbis believed that those who are wise seek to create peace (*b. Ber.* 64a; *Pesiq. Rav Kah.* 18.6–9)**.** ^5-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-10|10–12]]:** The disciples will suffer together (see 10.23; 13.53–58; 16.24–25; 24.9–10; [[Romans 08#^8-17|Rom 8.17]]; [[Revelation 01#^1-9|Rev 1.9]]; [[Revelation 20#^20-4|20.4]]). ^5-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-10|10–11]]:** *Persecuted for righteousness*, Jesus’ followers faced hostility from both Jews and pagans (cf. [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-2|1 Thess 2.2]],[[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|14–15]]; [[1 Peter 03#^3-14|1 Pet 3.14]]; [[1 Peter 04#^4-14|4.14]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-8|5.8]]; see also [[Psalms 037#^37-11|Ps 37.11]]). Also, this verse could refer to Jews who choose death over committing grievous sins. In Heb, “kiddush ha-shem” (“sanctification of the name \[of God\]”) mandates that one suffer death rather than commit idolatry, unchastity, or murder (see *b. Sanh*. 74a). ^5-10-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-12|12]]:** See 23.30–37; [[Acts 07#^7-52|Acts 7.52]]. ^5-12

### 5.13–16: Salt and light

*[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-13|Text]], p. 19* · ([[Mark 09#^9-49|Mk 9.49–50]]; [[Luke 14#^14-34|Lk 14.34–35]]). ^5-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-13|13]]:** *Salt*, following ancient Mesopotamian notions, symbolizing purity and wisdom ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^30-35|Ex 30.35]]; [[II Kings 02#^2-19|2 Kings 2.19–22]]; [[Ezekiel 16#^16-4|Ezek 16.4]]; *m. Sot.* 9.15). ^5-13-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Light of the world*, [[Philippians 02#^2-15|Phil 2.15]]; [[John 08#^8-12|Jn 8.12]] applies the epithet to Jesus. See also [[Isaiah 42#^42-6|Isa 42.6]]; [[Isaiah 49#^49-6|49.6]]; [[Isaiah 51#^51-4|51.4–5]]; [[Proverbs 06#^6-23|Prov 6.23]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-3|Dan 12.3]]; [[LXX Sirach 31#^31-17|Sir 31.17]]; [[John 01#^1-4|Jn 1.4–5]]; *Midr. Tanh*. 2. The DSS speak of “the children of light” who are on the side of God (see 1QS 2.3; 3.3,19–21; 1QM 13.5–6,14–15). ^5-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-16|16]]:** *Good works*, Matthew insists that faith be accompanied by action (25.32–46). ^5-16

### 5.17–20: Views concerning the Torah

*[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-17|Text]], p. 20* ^5-17

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-17|17]]:** *Law*, Gk “nomos,” in LXX for Heb “torah.” Here the reference (accompanied by “the prophets,” Gk “prophētēs,”) is to the Torah and Neviʾim of the Tanakh (as opposed to the prophets themselves in v 12); it is possible that “the law and the prophets” was a technical term for the incipient Jewish canon. *Not to abolish*, Matthew’s Jesus upholds Torah. [[Acts 06#^6-14|Acts 6.14]] suggests that the view that Jesus abrogated Torah is a false claim. ^5-17-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-18|18]]:** [[Mark 13#^13-31|Mk 13.31]]. *Letter*, Gk “iota,” for Heb “yod,” the smallest letter (see translators’ note *a*). If the smallest letter cannot be altered, then surely the rest of the Torah must be upheld as well. *Not one stroke of a letter*, rabbinic teachings do not allow a letter of Torah to be altered (*y. Sanh*. 2.6; *b. Sanh.* 90a; *Ex. Rab.* 6.1, *Lev. Rab.* 19.2). The “stroke” is the smallest part of a letter that differentiates it from another letter. ^5-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-19|19]]:** *Least of these commandments*, all Torah commandments are to be followed, no matter how seemingly minor (see [[James 02#^2-10|Jas 2.10]]; *m. Avot* 2.1; 4.2; *m. Qidd*. 1.10; *b. Ned*. 39b; *b. Shabb*. 70b; *Avot de R. Natan* B 35). ^5-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-20|20]]:** *Exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees*, setting the bar high, as Pharisees were known as righteous. ^5-20

### 5.21–48: Antitheses

*[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-21|Text]], p. 20* · The common term “antitheses” (lit., “oppositions”) for these six teachings is inaccurate; some teachings proclaim not antithesis, but intensification (comparable to “making a fence around the Torah”; see *m. Avot* 1.1). ^5-21

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-21|21]]:** *You shall not murder*, [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.13]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-17|Deut 5.17]]. *Whoever murders . . .* , perhaps paraphrasing [[Torah 02 - Noach#^9-6|Gen 9.6]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-12|Ex 21.12]]. *Judgment*, a sentence for murder could only be ordered by a Jewish court ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-18|Deut 16.18]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-1|21.1]]**–**9). See *m. Sanh*. 1.4; *b. Sanh*. 35a; 72a-b; the rabbis discuss the procedures for conducting civil and criminal suits. ^5-21-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-22|22]]:** *Insult*, name-calling could be a legal offense (e.g., the Jewish court could excommunicate a person for insulting a teacher; see *b. Ber*. 19a; see also *b. B. Metz.* 58b). *Council* (Gk “sanhedrin”), in most general terms the word referred to the Jewish court system, although “Sanhedrin” could refer to the high court in Jerusalem (26.57). The reference to a local court is common, see *m. Makk*. 1.10; *m. Sanh*. 1.6; *t. Sanh*. 1.7). *Hell* (Gk “Gehenna”), based on Heb “Gehinnom,” a valley south of Jerusalem associated with child sacrifice ([[II Kings 23#^23-10|2 Kings 23.10]]; [[Jeremiah 07#^7-31|Jer 7.31]]; [[II Chronicles 28#^28-3|2 Chr 28.3]]; [[II Chronicles 33#^33-6|33.6]]). Its murky reputation resulted in Gehinnom, by the NT era, becoming associated with purgatory and/or hell, where the wicked, according to some traditions, are tortured after death (cf. 25.41; the rabbis mention Gehenna as a place of eternal fire where evildoers and enemies are punished; see *b. Eruv.* 19a; *b. Sanh*. 110b; *b. Pesah*. 54a; *b. Ber*. 8b). **23–24**: *At the altar*, Matthew presumes Jesus’ audience participated in Temple sacrifice; perhaps also an *cause* d Gk *say Raca to* (an obscure term of abuse) e Gk *a brother* f Gk *Gehenna* g Gk *your brother* h Gk lacks *to court* allusion to Gen 4 (Cain and Abel). ^5-22

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-24|24]]:** *Be reconciled*, Jews were required to seek peace with their neighbors before reconciliation with God was possible ([[Proverbs 06#^6-1|Prov 6.1]]**–**5; 16.7; *m. Avot* 3.10, “One who is pleasing to his fellow men, is pleasing to God”; see also *m. Yoma* 8.9; *Gen. Rab*. 93.1). ^5-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-25|25–26]]:** ([[Luke 12#^12-57|Lk 12.57–59]]), see [[Proverbs 06#^6-1|Prov 6.1–5]]. ^5-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-27|27]]:** *Adultery*, [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.14]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-10|Lev 20.10]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-17|Deut 5.18]]. ^5-27

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-28|28]]:** *Lust*, Jewish sources show a deep disdain for this offense (e.g., [[Job 31#^31-1|Job 31.1]],[[Job 31#^31-9|9]]; Sir 9.8; 23.35; 41.21; *b. Ber*. 16a; *Lev. Rab*. 23.12 \[on the adulterous eye\]; 11QSTemple 59.14). ^5-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-29|29–30]]:** ([[Mark 09#^9-43|Mk 9.43–48]]); repeated in [[Matthew 18#^18-8|Mt 18.8]]**–**9. *Cut it off*, the language is hyperbolic. ^5-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-31|31]]:** *Certificate of divorce* (Heb “sefer keritut,” [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-1|Deut 24.1–4]]); see 19.9. ^5-31

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-32|32]]:** *Unchastity* (Gk “porneia”; see 19.3–9; see also [[Mark 10#^10-2|Mk 10.2–12]]; [[Luke 16#^16-18|Lk 16.18]]; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-11|1 Cor 7.11–13]]), encompasses more than adultery, e.g., marriages viewed by Leviticus (18.6–18) as incestuous. Jesus’ sexual ethics are stricter than found in most branches of early Judaism, but see 11QS- Temple 57.17–19; CD 4.12–5.14 (which interprets Gen 2 as forbidding remarriage and polygamy). See also *m*. *Ned.* 11.12; *b. Sanh.* 22a, for a discussion on divorce and death, specifically as it relates to a first wife. ^5-32

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-33|33]]:** *Swear falsely*, paraphrase of [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-7|Ex 20.7]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-12|Lev 19.12]]; [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-3|Num 30.3–15]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-11|Deut 5.11]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-21|21.21–23]]. ^5-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-34|34]]:** *Do not swear at all*, see [[Ecclesiastes 05#^5-4|Eccl 5.5]] (“It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not fulfill it”) and [[James 05#^5-12|Jas 5.12]]. Some rabbis forbid certain oaths (e.g., *m. Ned*. 1.3 on binding vows; *m. Sanh*. 3.2 indicates that certain oaths can be retracted, in this case, regarding a debt), but Jesus goes farther. *Throne of God*, see 23.22; [[Hebrews 12#^12-2|Heb 12.2]]; [[Revelation 07#^7-15|Rev 7.15]]; [[Revelation 22#^22-1|22.1]],[[Revelation 22#^22-2|2]]. ^5-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-35|35]]:** [[Isaiah 66#^66-1|Isa 66.1]]. ^5-35

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-37|37]]:** See *b. B. Metz.* 49a (verbal pledges and contracts may be upheld as valid); *Ruth Rab.* 7.6. ^5-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-38|38]]:** *Eye for an eye*, [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-23|Ex 21.23–25]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-19|Lev 24.19–20]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-21|Deut 19.21]]; 11QSTemple 61.10–12; *Jub.* 4.31–32; *L. A. B.* 44.10; cf. [[Luke 06#^6-27|Lk 6.27–36]]). *M. B. Kamma* 8 states that such injuries should be compensated financially. ^5-38

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-39|39–40]]:** See [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-15|1 Thess 5.15]]. Jesus will be slapped and have his cloak removed (26.67; 27.35). *Right cheek*, presumes a back-handed slap. *Turn the other,* respond with neither violence nor abjection (see also [[Lamentations 3#^3-30|Lam 3.30]]). *Give your cloak*, most people owned only two garments; to strip naked would uncover the judiciary injustice. ^5-39

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-41|41]]:** *Second mile*, Roman soldiers could conscript locals to carry their gear for one mile; going the second is non-resistance. ^5-41

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-42|42]]:** *Give to everyone,* Torah requires almsgiving ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-24|Ex 22.25]]; [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-36|Lev 25.36–37]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-7|Deut 15.7–11]]). ^5-42

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-43|43]]:** *Love your neighbor*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; see “The Concept of Neighbor,” p. 645. *Hate your enemy*, no biblical text records this saying but see 1QS 9.21, which endorses hating those who are wicked. ^5-43

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-44|44]]:** *Love your enemies*, Jews were not to mistreat enemies. See [[Proverbs 24#^24-17|Prov 24.17]]; [[Proverbs 25#^25-21|25.21]]; Josephus, *Ag. Ap*. 2.211. ^5-44

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-45|45]]:** See [[Luke 06#^6-35|Lk 6.35]]; [[Luke 10#^10-6|10.6]]; [[John 08#^8-39|Jn 8.39]]. *Children of your father*, see [[John 01#^1-12|Jn 1.12]]; [[Romans 08#^8-14|Rom 8.14–15]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-26|Gal 3.26–27]]; [[Galatians 04#^4-5|4.5]]; [[Ephesians 01#^1-5|Eph 1.5]]. Jewish sources also portray God in parental terms ([[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Psalms 082#^82-6|Ps 82.6]]; [[I Chronicles 22#^22-9|1 Chr 22.9–10]]; *b. Qidd*. 36a teaches that Jews are to act appropriately as “sons of the Lord your God”; see also *Ex. Rab*. 46.4). ^5-45

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-46|46]]:** *Tax collectors*, agents of Rome; for Matthew, those needing evangelization (9.10–11; 11.19; 18.17; 21.31–32). ^5-46

**[[NRSVue Matthew 05#^5-48|48]]:** See [[Luke 06#^6-36|Lk 6.36]]; [[Romans 12#^12-2|Rom 12.2]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-13|Col 3.13]]; [[1 John 04#^4-19|1 Jn 4.19]]. *Be perfect* (Gk “teleios”), implying wisdom and maturity, see 19.21; [[James 01#^1-4|Jas 1.4]]; [[James 03#^3-2|3.2]]; see also [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-2|Lev 19.2]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-13|Deut 18.13]] (and Targums); 1QS 1.8–9,13; 2.1–2; 8.9–10; *Midr. Ps.* 119.3; [[Luke 06#^6-36|Lk 6.36]] demands disciples be merciful. In Jewish tradition, Heb “tamim” (“complete, sound”; see [[Torah 02 - Noach#^6-9|Gen 6.9]], where Noah is “blameless”) could indicate “completeness” with God, though not necessarily full moral perfection. See “Jesus in Rabbinic Tradition,” p. 734. ^5-48
