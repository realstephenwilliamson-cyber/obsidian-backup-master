# Zohar 1.147a

← [[Zohar 1.146b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 1.147b]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 2.06 - Parashat Va-Yetse#^z1-147a|Parashat Va-Yetse, at 1:147a]]

*Volume 1, folio 147a* · Vayetzei

*Passage Vayetzei 1:1 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.146b#^vayetzei-1-1|Vayetzei 1:1]].*

**Vayetzei 1:2** Observe that although the sun illumines all quarters of the world, yet he travels only in two directions, as we read: “He goeth toward the South, and turneth about unto the North” (Eccles. 1, 6), one being the right and the other the left. Every day, too, he emerges from the East, turns to the South, then to the North, then toward the Western side, and finally is gathered unto the West. As the sun emerges from the East, so Jacob went out from Beer-sheba, and as the sun turns toward the West, so Jacob went toward Haran.’ ^vayetzei-1-2

**Vayetzei 1:3** R. Simeon said that Jacob “went forth” from the ambit of the Land of Israel, and he “went into” another sphere, as is implied in the sentence, “and he went toward Haran” (lit. strange, alien). ^vayetzei-1-3

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayetzei 1:4.*

**Vayetzei 1:5** R. Hiya said: ‘When the sun goes down to the West, the West is called the place of the sun and his throne, the place in which he abides, and to which he gathers in all his radiance. ^vayetzei-1-5

**Vayetzei 1:6** This accords with the Rabbinic dictum that God puts on phylacteries, that is, He takes up all the supernal crowns, to wit the emblem of the supernal Father and the emblem of the supernal Mother (these being the phylactery worn on the head), and then He takes up the Right and the Left, thereby carrying the whole.’ ^vayetzei-1-6

**Vayetzei 1:7** R. Eleazar said: ‘The “Beauty of Israel” takes up the whole, and when the Community of Israel is drawn toward the world on high, it also carries the whole, the male world of the Holy One as well as the female world of the Holy One; for just as all the lights radiate from the one, so the other carries the whole, one world being a representation of the other. Hence Beer-sheba (lit. well of seven) signifies the Jubilee year, *be’er* (well) symbolising a Sabbatical year; and the sun shines only from the Jubilee year. Hence “Jacob went out from Beer-sheba and went unto Haran”, that is toward the West, which is identical with the Sabbatical year.’ ^vayetzei-1-7

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayetzei 2:15–5:31.*
