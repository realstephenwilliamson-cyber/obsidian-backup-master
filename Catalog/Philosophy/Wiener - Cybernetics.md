## About

Norbert Wiener's book on control and communication in machines and living things. It was the first public use of "cybernetics" for the study of self-regulating mechanisms, and it mixes technical mathematics with history, philosophy and biology.

- **Author:** Wiener, an American mathematician at MIT, was born on 26 November 1894 in Columbia, Missouri, and died on 18 March 1964 in Stockholm. Britannica credits him with establishing the science of cybernetics.
- **1948:** first published, in English, in Paris by Hermann et Cie. History of Information says John Wiley issued the American edition later that year, printed from the French sheets with their typographical errors; it had at least five printings in 1948. Wikipedia names Hermann and MIT Press as the publishers.
- **1961:** a second edition, with minor changes and two added chapters (Wikipedia); History of Information names Wiley and MIT Press and says it finally corrected the errors.
- **Contents:** Wikipedia lists ten chapters, from "Newtonian and Bergsonian Time" through feedback and oscillation, computing machines and the nervous system, psychopathology, and information, language and society, to "Brain Waves and Self-Organising Systems". The first edition ends with thoughts on a chess-playing machine.
- **Background:** Wikipedia traces the ideas to ten years of meetings at Harvard Medical School between physicians and mathematicians, physicists and engineers, and to Wiener's work with Vannevar Bush on the differential analyzer.
- **Reception:** an unexpected bestseller; Time reviewed it on 27 December 1948. Wiener followed it with *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950) for general readers.
- **Why it matters:** History of Information calls it the first conventionally published book to discuss electronic digital computing and traces words such as "cyberspace" to it. Wikipedia credits it with groundwork for servomechanisms, automatic navigation, artificial intelligence and neuroscience.
