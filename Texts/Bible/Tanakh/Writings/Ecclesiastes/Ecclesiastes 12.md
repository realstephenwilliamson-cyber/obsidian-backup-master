# Ecclesiastes 12

## Chapter 12

**12:1** So appreciate your vigor[^1] in the days of your youth, before those days of sorrow come and those years arrive of which you will say, “I have no pleasure in them”; ^12-1

**12:2** before sun and light and moon and stars grow dark, and the clouds come back again after the rain: ^12-2

**12:3** When the guards of the house[^2] become shaky,  
And the men of valor[^3] are bent,  
And the maids that grind,[^4] grown few, are idle,  
And the ladies that peer through the windows[^5] grow dim, ^12-3

**12:4** And the doors to the street[^6] are shut—  
With the noise of the hand mill growing fainter,  
And the song of the bird growing feebler,[^7]  
And all the strains of music dying down;[^8] ^12-4

**12:5** When one is afraid of heights  
And there is terror on the road.—  
For the almond tree may blossom,  
The grasshopper be burdened,[^9]  
And the caper bush may bud again;[^10]  
But man sets out for his eternal abode,  
With mourners all around in the street.— ^12-5

**12:6** Before the silver cord snaps  
And the golden bowl crashes,  
The jar is shattered at the spring,  
And the jug[^11] is smashed at the cistern.[^12] ^12-6

**12:7** And the dust returns to the ground  
As it was,  
And the lifebreath returns to God  
Who bestowed it. ^12-7

**12:8** Utter futility—said Koheleth—  
All is futile! ^12-8

**12:9** A further word: Because Koheleth was a sage, he continued to instruct the people. He listened to and tested the soundness[^13] of many maxims. ^12-9

**12:10** Koheleth sought to discover useful sayings and recorded[^14] genuinely truthful sayings. ^12-10

**12:11** The sayings of the wise are like goads, like nails fixed in prodding sticks.[^15] They were given by one Shepherd.[^16] ^12-11

**12:12** A further word: Against them,[^17] my son, be warned!  
The making of many books is without limit  
And much study[^18] is a wearying of the flesh. ^12-12

**12:13** The sum of the matter, when all is said and done: Revere God and observe His commandments! For this applies to all mankind: ^12-13

**12:14** that God will call every creature to account for everything unknown,[^19] be it good or bad.  
The sum of the matter, when all is said and done: Revere God and observe His commandments! For this applies to all mankind. ^12-14

[^1]: Cf. postbiblical bori; others “Remember thy Creator.”
[^2]: I.e., the arms.
[^3]: I.e., the legs.
[^4]: I.e., the teeth.
[^5]: I.e., the eyes.
[^6]: I.e., the ears.
[^7]: Exact meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^8]: Cf. 2 Sam. 19.36.
[^9]: Emendation yields “The squill (postbiblical Heb. ḥaṣab) resume its burden,” i.e., its blossom-stalk and its leaves.
[^10]: These plants, after seeming dead for part of the year, revive, unlike man; cf. Job 14.7–10.
[^11]: So in Punic; others “wheel.”
[^12]: Poetic figure for the end of life.
[^13]: A noun, like dibber (Jer. 5.13), which occurs in such postbiblical phrases as shanim kethiq(qe)nan, “normal years” (lit. “years according to their propriety”).
[^14]: Wekhathub is equivalent to wekhathob, an infinitive employed as in Esth. 9.16 and elsewhere.
[^15]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain. Others “are those that are composed in collections.”
[^16]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain. Emendation yields “They are accounted as a sharp ox goad” (post-biblical mardea‘).
[^17]: Emendation yields “Slow, there!” Cf. Arabic mah and mah mah; so also mah (meh) in Prov. 31.2.
[^18]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^19]: Emendation yields “all their conduct.”
