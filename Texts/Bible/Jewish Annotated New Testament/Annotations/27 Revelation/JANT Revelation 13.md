### 13.1–10: Beast from the sea

*[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 559* ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-1|1]]:** Dan 7 is the immediate inspiration for beasts arising from sea and land, but the tradition of such monsters derives from ancient Semitic legends of the primal chaos monsters Leviathan and Behemoth (see “Chaos Monsters,” p. 559). *Ten horns and seven heads*, see *stood* b Gk *the plague of its death* 12.3–4n. *Blasphemous names*, a perverse caricature of the holy names on the foreheads of the Lamb’s 144,000 followers (7.3; 9.4). ^13-1-2

> [!note] Chaos Monsters *(p. 559)*
> The bizarre polymorphic beasts that arise in ch 13 to threaten and delude the earth belong to the most archaic biblical traditions of creation and would have been easily recognized as such by early audiences. The idea that the God of Israel, like other Canaanite gods, defeated and bound Leviathan/Rahab, a sea-monster, as well as other monsters at the beginning of time, is invoked in such disparate biblical sources as Ps 74 and Job 41, and Isaiah calls for its reenactment to perfect the earth again (27.1; 51.9–10). While many sources from John’s own period refer to these primordial beasts by name (*1 En*. 60.7–9; *2 Bar*. 29.4; *4 Ezra* 6.49–52;), the book of Daniel had begun a tradition of imagining their anonymous reappearance in horrific forms that reflected the imperial powers of history (Dan 7), and it is this visionary tradition that John draws upon in ch 13. Even if he does not point it out here, the reader is supposed to recognize them as primordial chaos monsters.
>
> Other frightening beasts in Revelation draw on the same archaic traditions of chaos in creation. The seven-headed Satan-dragon (ch 12) owes much to early images of Leviathan (cf. [[Isaiah 27#^27-1|Isa 27.1]]), while its harassment of the woman and infant follow more closely the Greek myth of Leto and the child Apollo threatened by the monster Python. The Satan-dragon is said to serve God’s eschatological designs (17.17) before its destruction (20.9–10). So also the minor demon Death, a latter version of the ancient Canaanite drought-monster Mot, serves first as an agent of divine wrath (6.7–8) before its destruction (20.14). This paradoxical status, instrument of God and enemy of God, pertains to many demonic figures in ancient Jewish and Christian literature, from Satan (e.g., Job 1–2) and the serpent of Eden (Gen 3; *2 En.* 31) to the overseers of hell.

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-2|2]]:** As in [[Daniel 07#^7-4|Dan 7.4–6]], the beast’s appearance with multiple heads, horns, and animals indicates its monstrous nature. Demons in early Judaism and Christianity were often imagined as monstrous, with multiple animal forms. *Dragon*, Satan. *Authority* (see v 4) suggests that the beast’s danger stems not just from its primal threat of chaos but also from a larger evil, as if all dangers are linked. ^13-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Death-blow* (or mortal wound), usually taken as a reference to the emperor Nero; following his 68 CE suicide (Suetonius, *Nero*, 49.3–4), popular traditions in the eastern Mediterranean imagined his resurrection and reconquering of Rome (e.g., *Sib. Or.* 5.137–54,214–27; 8.68–72; Dio Chrys., *Or.* 21.10). Several “new Neros” appeared in the later first century. Jewish sources despised him (*Sib. Or.* 3.68–74). ^13-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-4|4–7]]:** The beast inspires worship, *blaspheming* holy things, and persecuting the saints (see 12.17; 13.9–10), all ways that John imagines emperor veneration whether officially prescribed or not. These views of the emperor cult recall [[Daniel 07#^7-25|Dan 7.25]]; [[Daniel 11#^11-36|11.36–39]], referring to Antiochus IV Epiphanes. *Forty-two months*, three and a half years; see 11.1–2n. *Dwelling* (lit., “tent, tabernacle”) recalls [[Daniel 11#^11-31|Dan 11.31–39]] and reflects contemporaneous fears that eschatological evil would involve a foreign ruler’s hubris in the Jerusalem Temple (see also [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-4|2 Thess 2.4]], see [[Isaiah 14#^14-13|Isa 14.13–14]]; [[Ezekiel 28#^28-2|Ezek 28.2]]). Using a distinctive editorial marker for clarification (*that is, . . .* ) a later editor has interpreted “dwelling” as the saints. ^13-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-8|8]]:** *Foundation of the world*, time of creation, implying that some are predestined to eternal life through inscription in a “book of life” (as Josephus observes \[ *J.W.* 2.119–66\], ancient Jews assiduously debated predestination in Roman times); the phrase can also apply to *slaughtered* (see translators’ note *b*), implying that the Lamb’s sacrifice was ordained (or even performed) at the beginning of creation. Many early Jewish texts imagine the foreordaining of later, even eschatological, events at the time of creation: e.g., *4 Ezra* 6.1–6; [[John 17#^17-24|Jn 17.24]]; *T. Moses* 1.12–14; *Gen. Rab.* 12.10). *Book of life*, see 3.5n. ^13-8

### 13:11–18: Beast from the earth

*[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-11|Text]], p. 560* · The land-monster, a version of the biblical Behemoth ([[Job 40#^40-15|Job 40.15–24]]; *1 En.* 60.7–11) resembles the Lamb (v 11) and mimics the miracles of the two prophets (vv 13–14; see 11.5–6), but it encourages idolatry (vv 14–15). Perhaps this beast is meant to represent some aspects of the Roman Empire. ^13-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Mortal wound*, see v 3n. ^13-12

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-15|15]]:** *Image of the beast*, the notion that images or representations, of a god could be infused with *breath* and thus *could even speak* refers to Greek and Roman “living statue” oracles, in which statues, ritually infused with the god’s spirit, “spoke” through sounds *slaughtered from the foundation of the world* c Gk *whose plague of its death* d Or *that had received the plague of the sword* and movements, and communities depended on them in crisis situations (see Lucian, *De Dea Syria*, 36). ^13-15

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-16|16–17]]:** The beast’s deceptive call for marking body parts parodies the marks on the 144,000 (see “Names Inscribed on the Body,” p. 561). ^13-16

**[[NRSVue Revelation 13#^13-18|18]]:** *Six hundred sixty-six*, represents a “gematria” (Jewish numerology) of the name Nero Caesar, using the numerical equivalents of the Heb letters underlying the Gk spelling of Nero’s name: “Kaisar Neron” spelled in Heb is “qof-samek-resh, nun-resh-vav-nun”; the numerical values are 100+60+200+50+200+6+50 = 666. (If the final “nun” is omitted, spelling “Nero” rather than “Neron,” the numbers add up to 616; see translators’ note *b*). John assumes that the audience is capable of decoding this number and understanding that the beast must be Nero returned from the dead. ^13-18
