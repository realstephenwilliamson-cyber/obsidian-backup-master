## About

A late dialogue of Plato in which a visitor from Elea, a follower of Parmenides, tries with the young mathematician Theaetetus to define the sophist and tell him apart from the statesman and the philosopher. The search leads into the problems of not-being and false statement.

- **People:** the Eleatic Stranger (or Visitor), who leads; Theaetetus; Theodorus, the elder mathematician; and Socrates, a mostly silent bystander. Other young mathematicians are present but do not speak.
- **Setting and dramatic date:** an Athenian gymnasium, the day after the conversation of the *Theaetetus*, which the SEP sets in 399 BC, shortly before Socrates' trial and death. The *Sophist* and *Statesman* take place on a single day and present themselves as the first two parts of a trilogy; the third, the *Philosopher*, was never written.
- **Composition:** Wikipedia gives 360 BC as most likely. The IEP puts it in the late group, c. 355–347 BC, and the SEP notes that the dating rests on stylistic likeness to the *Laws*. None of these sources doubts its authenticity.
- **Argument:** a series of definitions by division, modelled first on the angler; then the battle of gods and giants over the nature of being (245e–249d) and five greatest kinds: change, rest, being, sameness and difference. Against Parmenides, the Stranger argues that what is not is something different, not the opposite of what is, which makes false statement possible. Britannica notes the distinction between names and verbs, a statement needing both.
- **Place among the dialogues:** after the *Theaetetus* and before the *Statesman*. The IEP groups it with the *Statesman* and *Philebus* as the dialogues where the method of collection and division is most on display. Stephanus pages 216–268.
- **Why it matters:** the SEP says it arguably solves the problem of false statement, and that it and the *Statesman* show Plato's method and metaphysics after the criticisms of the theory of Forms in the *Parmenides*.
