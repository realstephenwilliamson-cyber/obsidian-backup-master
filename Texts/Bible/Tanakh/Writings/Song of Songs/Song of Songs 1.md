## Chapter 1

**1:1** The Song of Songs, by[^1] Solomon. ^1-1

**1:2** Oh, give me of the kisses of your mouth,[^2]  
For your love is more delightful than wine. ^1-2

**1:3** Your ointments yield a sweet fragrance,  
Your name is like finest[^3] oil—  
Therefore do maidens love you. ^1-3

**1:4** Draw me after you, let us run!  
The king has brought me to his chambers.[^4]  
Let us delight and rejoice in your love,  
Savoring it more than wine—  
Like new wine[^5] they love you! ^1-4

**1:5** I am dark, but comely,  
O daughters of Jerusalem—  
Like the tents of Kedar,  
Like the pavilions of Solomon. ^1-5

**1:6** Don’t stare at me because I am swarthy,  
Because the sun has gazed upon me.  
My mother’s sons quarreled with me,  
They made me guard the vineyards;  
My own vineyard I did not guard. ^1-6

**1:7** Tell me, you whom I love so well;  
Where do you pasture your sheep?  
Where do you rest them at noon?  
Let me not be as one who strays[^6]  
Beside the flocks of your fellows. ^1-7

**1:8** If you do not know, O fairest of women,  
Go follow the tracks of the sheep,  
And graze your kids[^7]  
By the tents of the shepherds. ^1-8

**1:9** I have likened you, my darling,  
To a mare in Pharaoh’s chariots: ^1-9

**1:10** Your cheeks are comely with plaited wreaths,  
Your neck with strings of jewels. ^1-10

**1:11** We will add wreaths of gold  
To your spangles of silver. ^1-11

**1:12** While the king was on his couch,  
My nard gave forth its fragrance. ^1-12

**1:13** My beloved to me is a bag of myrrh  
Lodged between my breasts. ^1-13

**1:14** My beloved to me is a spray of henna blooms  
From the vineyards of En-gedi. ^1-14

**1:15** Ah, you are fair, my darling,  
Ah, you are fair,  
With your dove-like eyes! ^1-15

**1:16** And you, my beloved, are handsome,  
Beautiful indeed!  
Our couch is in a bower; ^1-16

**1:17** Cedars are the beams of our house,  
Cypresses the rafters. ^1-17

[^1]: Or “concerning.”
[^2]: Heb. “Let him give me of the kisses of his mouth!”
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^4]: Emendation yields “Bring me, O king, to your chambers.”
[^5]: Understanding mesharim as related to tirosh; cf. Aramaic merath.
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^7]: As a pretext for coming.
