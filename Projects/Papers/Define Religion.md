My go to answer for the definition of religion is that of William James in his *Varieties of Religious Experience,* namely, “acts, and experiences of individual men in their solitude, so far as they apprehend themselves to stand in relation to whatever they may consider the divine.” It is important to note, however, that even James calls this definition (and possibly all definitions of religion) arbitrary and perhaps overly broad. I am inclined to agree with him on the first point but not the latter. I think if anything we should expand the category by replacing the word divine with the word supernatural. In doing so we encompass belief systems in which the word divine may be problematic due to the fact that they are not theistically focused yet nonetheless “religious” in all remaining aspects.

Granted this expansion on James’s definition seems to overly generalize what we mean by the word religion but I believe it to be vanity on the part of adherents of theistic traditions to deny that nontheistic systems can also be sacred and therefore ontologically religious. As James points out we must distinguish existential judgments from value judgments when we approach religion academically. Take as an example Haitian Voodoo. Here the existence of a demiurge may be taken as implicit but this is inconsequential. What is important are the actions and temperaments of the *loa*, spirits that are not considered divinities and are therefore more placated than worshipped. Surely we would not deny that this complex system of ritual and lifestyle classifies as religion.
