### 21.1–26.32: Paul in Jerusalem

*[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-1|Text]], p. 266* · While in Jerusalem, Paul appears before the Jewish council (22.30–23.11), Roman governor (24.1–23; 25.1–12), and Jewish king (25.23–26.32). This sequence parallels Jesus’ appearances before the Jewish council ([[Luke 22#^22-66|Lk 22.66–71]]), Pilate/Roman governor (23.1–5), and Antipas/Jewish king (23.6–12). The parallels suggest a significant amount of literary creativity on Luke’s part, thus casting doubt on the historical reliability of much of this material. ^21-1

### 21.1–16: Paul travels to Jerusalem

*[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-1|Text]], p. 267* ^21-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-1|1]]:** *We*, the first-person plural narrative resumes and continues until Paul’s arrival in Jerusalem (v 18). The route begins off the coast of southwest Asia Minor and proceeds eastward along the southern coast until the Phoenician coastal city of Tyre and then south to Caesarea and finally Jerusalem. *Cos*, island off the southwest coast of Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). *Rhodes*, larger island south and east of Cos. *Patara*, port on the southern coast. ^21-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-2|2]]:** *Phoenicia*, region at the Eastern end of the Mediterranean. ^21-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-3|3]]:** *Tyre,* major Phoenician coastal city (present-day Lebanon). ^21-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-4|4]]:** *Spirit*, the Spirit frequently directs the action (8.39; 10.19; 11.12; 13.2,4; 15.28; 16.6,7; 28.25). *Not to go on to Jerusalem,* this section of Acts begins by foreshadowing the numerous dangers Paul will face throughout his stay in Jerusalem; the warning is repeated in v 12. ^21-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-7|7]]:** *Ptolemais*, coastal city 22 mi south of Tyre (present-day Akko). ^21-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-8|8]]:** *Caesarea*, see 8.40; 10.1. *One of the seven*, see 6.1–6. ^21-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-9|9]]:** *Prophecy*, see 2.16; 3.18; 7.37; 10.43; 11.27; 13.1; 15.32; 19.6. *Daughters*, on women and prophetic powers, see [[II Kings 22#^22-14|2 Kings 22.14–20]]; T. Job 46–52; *Ex. Rab.* 1.1. ^21-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-10|10]]:** *Agabus*, see 11.28. ^21-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-12|12]]:** *Not to go up to Jerusalem,* see v 4. ^21-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-13|13]]:** *Die . . . for the name of the Lord Jesus*, see 5.41; 9.16. ^21-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-14|14]]:** *The Lord’s will be done*, [[Luke 22#^22-42|Lk 22.42]] attributes a similar statement to Jesus shortly before his arrest. Paul’s arrest and condemnation in Jerusalem parallel what happened to Jesus (Lk 22–23). ^21-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-16|16]]:** *Mnason*, otherwise unknown. On Christianity on Cyprus, see 4.36; 11.19–20; 13.4–12. ^21-16

### 21.17–26: Paul meets with James

*[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-17|Text]], p. 267* ^21-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-17|17]]:** *Welcomed us warmly*, Acts emphasizes the harmony among early Christians, especially between Paul and the Jerusalem church leaders. ^21-17-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-18|18]]:** *James*, see 12.17. *Elders*, see 11.30. ^21-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-19|19]]:** *Gentiles*, Acts often portrays Paul working among Gentiles, although his efforts result in converting Jews as well (13.43; 17.11; 18.8). ^21-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-20|20]]:** *Believers . . . among the Jews,* Acts portrays Jewish opposition to Paul extending even to Jewish believers. *Zealous for the law*, normally presented in Jewish literature as a positive quality ([[LXX 2 Maccabees 02#^2-27|2 Macc 2.27]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 16#^16-16|4 Macc 16.16]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 12.268; [[Sanhedrin 70#70b|*b. Sanh.* 70b]]), here suggesting a factor that motivates Jews against Paul. ^21-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-21|21]]:** *To forsake Moses,* Acts repeatedly defends Paul against this claim (16.3; 18.18; 20.16). ^21-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-23|23]]:** *Four men . . . under a vow*, Nazirite vow; [[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-1|Num 6.1–21]]. Paul had previously fulfilled such a vow (18.18). ^21-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-24|24]]:** *Pay for the shaving of their heads*, one of the rituals that fulfills the Nazirite vow ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-18|Num 6.18]]). Paul here demonstrates Torah-observance. It was possible for a person to assume payment for others who fulfilled the Nazirite vow, as Agrippa I did (Josephus, *Ant.* 19.294). ^21-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-25|25]]:** *Sent a letter*, see 15.23. *Gentiles . . . should abstain*, see 15.20,29. ^21-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-26|26]]:** *Having purified himself*, in order to enter the Temple’s precincts. Acts shows that Paul is scrupulous in his observance of Jewish Law and Temple practices. Portraying Paul as beyond reproach, Acts can focus on what is wrong with the Jews, namely their refusal to believe in Jesus and to accept that Gentiles need not observe the Law. ^21-26

### 21.27–36: Paul’s arrest in the Temple

*[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-27|Text]], p. 268* ^21-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-27|27]]:** *Seven days*, a period of purification (v 26) required of nazirites who had become ritually impure ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-9|Num 6.9]]). *Jews from Asia*, see 20.19. ^21-27-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-28|28]]:** *Against our people, our law*, Acts 21–26 is devoted to refuting these charges, which resemble those made against Stephen (6.13). *Brought Greeks into the temple*, Gentiles were permitted access onto the southern portion of the Temple Mount but prohibited from progressing beyond the Court of the Gentiles (Josephus, *J.W.* 5.193–94; 6.124–26; *Ant.* 15.417). A physical barrier existed at the edge of the court with a warning sign that Gentiles were not to proceed beyond that point (*CIJ* 2.1440). ^21-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-29|29]]:** *Trophimus*, see 20.4. ^21-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-30|30–31]]:** The riot recalls actions against Stephen (7.54–60) and Paul in Ephesus (19.28–32). ^21-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-31|31]]:** *Tribune*, later identified as Claudius Lysias (23.26). ^21-31

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-36|36]]:** *Away with him*, see [[Luke 23#^23-18|Lk 23.18]]. Luke contrasts the Jewish crowd, which is frenzied and violent, with the calm and orderly Romans. ^21-36

### 21.37–22.30: Paul’s defense and reaction

*[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-37|Text]], p. 268* ^21-37

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-38|38]]:** *Egyptian*, active sometime between 52–58, he attempted to wrest Jerusalem from Roman control (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.259–60; *Ant* 20.167–72); Josephus claims he had 30,000 followers (*J.W.* 2.261). *Out into the wilderness*, suggests that the Egyptian was motivated by eschatological beliefs and was possibly understood as a messianic leader. *Citizen*, see 16.37. ^21-38

**[[NRSVue Acts 21#^21-40|40]]:** *Hebrew language*, lit., “Hebrew dialect,” and thus possibly Aramaic, which was spoken more commonly than Hebrew (see translators’ note c); see also 22.2. *strangled* ^21-40
