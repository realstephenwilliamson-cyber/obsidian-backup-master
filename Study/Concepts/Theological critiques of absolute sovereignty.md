# Theological critiques of absolute sovereignty

> [!abstract] Definition
> Lines in Stephen's Leviathan writing that deny the Hobbesian sovereign its absoluteness: the Leviathan is a creature God formed to play with; the "artificial soul" has migrated to the program running the network; and a sovereign that protects by location fails Hobbes's own test. Each is set up in his fragments; none is yet argued through.

## Three lines in the fragments

**1. The Leviathan is a creature at play.** The 8 Jan 2024 fragment of [[Leviathan fragments 2018-2024|Leviathan fragments 2018-2024]] sets two texts side by side without comment: [[Psalms 104#^104-26|Psalm 104:26]] ("There go the ships, and Leviathan that you formed to sport with") and Srimad Bhagavatam 3.26.4b ("As Sport, God, the Great Preserver, is invested in the Nature of Reality"). The 2020 abstract titles the project "Playing with God's Monster and Hobbes's Metaphor" and lists the rabbinic Leviathan and लीला (divine play) among its materials; a 12 Oct 2020 fragment quotes Psalm 74 ([[The Cybernetic Leviathan|The Cybernetic Leviathan]]). If the namesake is a plaything, the sovereignty lodged in it is not absolute. Stephen has placed the texts; he has not written the argument.

**2. The sovereign is not where Hobbes put it.** cyberLevi2 (14 Jun 2018): "the Leviathan, in this tripartite manifestation, is itself the Sovereign", and "the social contract is a broken myth." Track 3 (12 Jun 2018): "the political authority is not the sovereign but the program that is running the machine is." A state claiming absolute sovereignty misdescribes its own position: it is a node, not the soul. See [[Algorithmic Governance as Sovereign]], [[Auctoritas vs Circulation]].

**3. The sovereign's one justification fails for the exploited.** Track 3 quotes Hobbes: the artificial man exists "for whose protection and defence it was intended." cyberLevi2 answers that "the war is still all against all, but the fear of violent death is sublimated to varying degrees usually simply depending on where your node on the network is located"; the 2020 abstract: "to those already exploited by its constituting unification, the greatest Leviathan is the Commonwealth of Humanity." A sovereign that protects by location fails Hobbes's own test.

## The question he flagged and left open

Track 5: "Is it not blasphemy both Philosophically and Religiously to consider the possibility that Hobbes Leviathan is the Communion of man and the Sovereign he describes no less than the Almighty himself?" and "Should I avoid the conclusion that Hobbes may be describing my relationship with God when he talks about the Sov?" This cuts two ways: either the absolute sovereign is a disguised God-construct, so that a state claiming it claims what belongs to God; or Hobbes unknowingly describes the relation between a person and God, and the fragments hold the beginnings of a theology of the Leviathan rather than a critique of it.

## Open questions

- Only the first line is theological on its face; the others become so only through Track 5. Theological critique, or political critique with a theological frame?
- Job 41 is not in the fragments, and "Leviathan in the rabbinic discussion" names no source; the AI report's rabbinic material is not confirmed by anything of his.
- Chapter 1 (12 Sep 2024) quotes the *Protagoras*: political wisdom "was in the keeping of Zeus", and justice and shame were given "to all". He quotes without comment. Does distribution to all stand against one artificial soul?
- The Lyotard note: "the problem that overshadows all others, including that of the contemporary state, is that of capital." Is the target the state, or capital wearing its name?
