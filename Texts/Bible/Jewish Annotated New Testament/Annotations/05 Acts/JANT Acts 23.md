### 23.1–11: Paul before the Jewish council

*[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-1|Text]], p. 270* ^23-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-1|1]]:** *Council*, the Sanhedrin. ^23-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-2|2]]:** *Ananias*, high priest ca. 48–59 CE, who remained influential until the revolt against Rome (66–73). ^23-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-3|3]]:** *Whitewashed wall*, metaphor for one who hides moral defects behind a seemingly proper demeanor or authority; see [[Ezekiel 13#^13-10|Ezek 13.10–16]]; [[Matthew 23#^23-27|Mt 23.27]]. Acts contrasts Paul and other believers in Jesus who follow the Law with Jews and Jewish authorities who do not. It is not clear what *law* the high priest supposedly violated. ^23-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-5|5]]:** Paul, citing [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-27|Ex 22.28]], affirms his obedience to Torah. ^23-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-6|6]]:** On Paul’s claim to be a Pharisee, see [[Philippians 03#^3-5|Phil 3.5]]. On Pharisees, see 5.34n. See “Pharisees,” p. 619. *Hope of the resurrection*, resurrection of Jesus is a central tenet for believers in Acts (2.32; 4.33; 5.30). Paul identifies this tenet as the essential difference between him and his opponents and the only legitimate claim they have against him (see 24.21). ^23-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-8|8]]:** Josephus notes that Sadducees rejected belief in bodily resurrection while Pharisees accepted it (*J.W.* 2.163–66; *Ant* 18.12–17). ^23-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-9|9]]:** *Scribes of the Pharisees*, their declaration of innocence presents an implicit censure of Jews who condemn Paul; Gamaliel had done much the same with regard to Peter (5.33–39). ^23-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-10|10]]:** *Tear Paul to pieces*, Acts suggests that even the Jewish legislature is violence-prone. ^23-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-11|11]]:** *Bear witness also in Rome*, see 19.21. Paul’s travels continue to be divinely guided (13.4; 16.6,7; 21.4). ^23-11

### 23.12–35: Paul transferred to Caesarea

*[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-12|Text]], p. 271* ^23-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-12|12–30]]:** The Jews’ plot against Paul prompts the tribune to transfer him to the custody of the Roman procurator. ^23-12-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-12|12]]:** *An oath*, Gk “anathematizein” often connotes complete destruction ([[Torah 39 - Chukat#^21-2|Num 21.2]]; [[I Samuel 15#^15-3|1 Sam 15.3]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 05#^5-5|2 Macc 5.5]]). By making the refusal to eat or drink part of the oath, Acts presents the Jews as bearing an intense hatred of Paul and proposing an act that violates biblical law and is punishable by death ([[Torah 43 - Masei#^35-20|Num 35.20–21]]). ^23-12-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-16|16]]:** *Son of Paul’s sister*, no other biblical source refers to Paul’s family. ^23-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-22|22]]:** *Tell no one*, so that he can remove Paul from the city before the conspirators find out about the change in venue. ^23-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-23|23]]:** *Caesarea*, residence of the Roman governor. The numbers depict a large force accompanying Paul. ^23-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-24|24]]:** *Felix*, Antonius Felix, a freedman, was procurator of Judea in the mid- to-late 50s. ^23-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-26|26–30]]:** The tribune’s letter describes previous events (21.27–23.23), omitting details such as his own hostile treatment of Paul (22.24). **29**: *Nothing deserving death*, several figures announce Paul’s innocence (25.25; 26.31); Pilate expresses a similar opinion about Jesus ([[Luke 23#^23-15|Lk 23.15]]). ^23-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-31|31]]:** *Antipatris*, city on the road to Caesarea. ^23-31

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-34|34]]:** *Cilicia*, region of southeast Asia Minor where Tarsus is located. ^23-34

**[[NRSVue Acts 23#^23-35|35]]:** *Herod’s headquarters*, palace built by Herod the Great and taken over by the Roman government. ^23-35
