Compare: [[NRSVue Hosea 12|NRSVue]] (Heb 12:1 = NRSVue 11:12; Heb 12:2–15 = NRSVue 12:1–14) · [[NRSVue Hosea 11#^11-12|NRSVue 11:12]]

## Chapter 12

**12:1** Ephraim surrounds Me with deceit,  
The House of Israel with guile.[^1]  
(But Judah stands firm with God  
And is faithful to the Holy One.)[^2] ^12-1

**12:2** Ephraim tends the wind  
And pursues the gale;  
He is forever adding  
Illusion to calamity.[^3]  
Now they make a covenant with Assyria,  
Now oil is carried to Egypt.[^4] ^12-2

**12:3** The LORD once indicted Judah,[^5]  
And punished Jacob for his conduct,  
Requited him for his deeds. ^12-3

**12:4** In the womb he tried to supplant his brother;  
Grown to manhood, he strove with a divine being,[^6] ^12-4

**12:5** He strove with an angel and prevailed—  
The other had to weep and implore him.  
At Bethel [Jacob] would meet him,  
There to commune with him.[^7] ^12-5

**12:6** Yet the LORD, the God of Hosts,  
Must be invoked as “LORD.”[^8] ^12-6

**12:7** You must return to your God!  
Practice goodness and justice,  
And constantly trust in your God. ^12-7

**12:8** A trader who uses false balances,  
Who loves to overreach, ^12-8

**12:9** Ephraim thinks,  
“Ah, I have become rich;  
I have gotten power!  
All my gains do not amount  
To an offense which is real guilt.”[^9] ^12-9

**12:10** I the LORD have been your God  
Ever since the land of Egypt.  
I will let you dwell in your tents[^10] again  
As in the days of old,[^11] ^12-10

**12:11** When I spoke to the prophets;  
For I granted many visions,  
[^12]And spoke parables through the prophets. ^12-11

**12:12** As for Gilead, it is worthless;  
And to no purpose have they  
Been sacrificing oxen in Gilgal:  
The altars of these are also  
Like stone heaps upon a plowed field.[^13] ^12-12

**12:13** Then Jacob had to flee[^14] to the land of Aram;  
There Israel served for a wife,  
For a wife he had to guard [sheep]. ^12-13

**12:14** But when the LORD  
Brought Israel up from Egypt,  
It was through a prophet;[^15]  
Through a prophet[^16] they were guarded. ^12-14

**12:15** [^17]Ephraim gave bitter offense,  
And his Lord cast his crimes upon him  
And requited him for his mockery. ^12-15

[^1]: I.e., the deceit and guile they practice on each other (below vv. 8–9) is constantly noted by the Lord.
[^2]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: Septuagint reads “futility.”
[^4]: I.e., they foolishly depend on alliances instead of on the Lord; cf. 5.13; 7.10–11.
[^5]: Presumably the patriarch Judah. Emendation would yield “Israel”; cf. next note.
[^6]: Cf. Gen. 25.26 and 32.29.
[^7]: Heb. “us.”
[^8]: I.e., one should not invoke any of the angelic hosts.
[^9]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^10]: I.e., securely; see 2 Kings 13.5.
[^11]: Lit. “fixed season.”
[^12]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^13]: I.e., the cults of Gilead and Gilgal are as worthless as that of Bethel.
[^14]: This is the punishment mentioned in 12.3.
[^15]: I.e., not through an angel.
[^16]: I.e., not through an angel.
[^17]: Meaning of 12.15–13.1 uncertain.
