### 4.1–25: The faithfulness of Abraham and his descendants

*[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 294* · The question arises: If Gentiles enjoy through Christ the same benefaction (1.5) as do Jews, without becoming members of Israel, why was Abraham circumcised? Paul argues that although Gentiles do not become children of Israel and thus under Torah, they do become children of Abraham, for Abraham was faithful and received the promise of children from many nations before he received circumcision as the seal of that faithfulness for himself and his ethnic descendants. His ethnic descendants know that they must remain faithful to inherit the promises made to Abraham. They have a special benefaction (1.5) before those from the other nations, but that does not warrant judging; instead, it involves responsibility to serve. Paul reasons that Gentiles who are faithful to the Christ, whom he regards as the descendant promised to Abraham to restore the world, are children of Abraham, on the same ideal terms of faithfulness. Now both groups of descendants are called to live faithfully. ^4-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-2|2]]:** *By works*, by \[performing\] rites; i.e., circumcision of himself and the male members of his household. *Boast about*, because he is marked as set apart to God. *But*, or albeit. ^4-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-3|3]]:** *Scripture*, [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]. *Abraham believed God*, Abraham was faithful to God. ^4-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-4|4]]:** Lit., “Now to the one performing \[rites\], payment is not regarded as a gift, but as what is owed.” ^4-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-5|5]]:** *But to one who without works*, lit., “But for the one not performing \[a rite\]; i.e., without circumcision.” ^4-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-6|6–8]]:** *David speaks*, [[Psalms 032#^32-1|Ps 32.1–2]]. *Works*, or rites. In this case, the psalmist does not offer sacrifice, but repentant confession of the faithful results in forgiveness. The psalm ends with praising an upright heart; see 3.23. *Iniquities*, lit., “lawless conduct.” *Sins are covered*, forgiven, as the parallel to *iniquities are forgiven*, indicates. ^4-6

> [!note] God Is One for All Humanity (ROM 3.30) *(p. 294)*
> Paul develops his argument around the oneness of God as upheld in the Shema (“Hear, O Israel: The Lord is our God, the Lord alone”; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4]]; see [[Romans 10#^10-12|Rom 10.12–13]]), to make his case for the creator God as the only God of all humankind. Paul insists that God is the God of Israel specifically (the Lord is *our* God), yet not “only” the God of Israel: if God is God alone (one), then Israel’s God is God of both Israel and of the other nations who turn to the one God, and Jesus Christ has been faithful to both Israel and the Gentiles simultaneously; thus they remain thereafter identified differently as Jews
>
> (circumcised), and members of the nations other than Israel (foreskinned). Later Jewish appeals to the Shema articulate similar eschatological expectations; e.g., Rashi, based on *Sifre Deut.* on [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4]], declared: “The Lord who is our God now, but not (yet) the God of the (other) nations, is destined to be the One Lord, as it is said, ‘For then will I give to the peoples a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the Lord, to serve Him with one consent’ ([[Zephaniah 3#^3-9|Zeph 3.9]]). And (likewise) it is said, ‘And the Lord shall be king over all the earth; on that day shall the Lord be One and His name One’ ([[Zechariah 14#^14-9|Zech 14.9]])”; cf. *Sifre* on [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4]] (piska 31).

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-9|9]]:** P*ronounced*, not in Gk. *Faith,* or faithfulness. *Faith was reckoned . . .* , see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]. ^4-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-11|11]]:** *Seal,* a confirmation and sign; *by faith*, or by faithfulness; *believe*, or are faithful. ^4-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-12|12]]:** Lit., “and father of circumcision not only for those from circumcision, but also for those walking in the footsteps of the one in foreskin, our faithful father Abraham.” ^4-12

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-13|13]]:** *Promise*, a new element in Paul’s argument, was made to Abraham in Gen 12 before the commandment to be circumcised in Gen 17. Cf. [[LXX Sirach 44#^44-19|Sir 44.19–21]]. *Inherit the world*, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-6|Gen 12.6–7]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|15.5]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-4|17.4–5]]; [[Isaiah 54#^54-1|Isa 54.1–3]]; Philo, *Praem.* 158. Paul answers the question posed in v 1. *Law* (“nomos”), refers here not to Torah but to the convention of circumcision. *Descendants*, lit., “seeds”; *through the law*, or through a convention; i.e., circumcision; no article in Gk; *faith*, or faithfulness. ^4-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-14|14]]:** Or “for if the heirs are (merely) from a law/convention (i.e., of circumcision).” ^4-14

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-15|15]]:** The God-given convention (of circumcision) brings contempt for those who flaunt it; but where no convention exists, neither does (the judgment) of sidestepping it (on the part of these non-Jews). ^4-15

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-16|16–17]]:** Those who follow Abraham’s example of trusting God’s word, i.e., who are faithful, are his promised descendants. Cf. [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-28|2 Macc 7.28]]. Here Paul works with the concept of the “covenants of the fathers” (see 11.28–29; [[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-40|Lev 26.40–45]]), that is, with Abraham and his descendants inheriting what was promised, which for Paul includes those from the other nations who are now turning to God in Christ as well as those who have been faithful to God as Israelites all along. ^4-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-16|16]]:** *It*, i.e., the promised inheritance; *faith*, or faithfulness; *descendants*, lit., “seed”; *not only to the adherents of the law,* or not only for the ones (deriving identity) from the convention (of circumcision); *adherents*, not in Gk. ^4-16-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-17|17]]:** *It is written*, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-5|Gen 17.5]]. *In whom he believed,* or to whom he was being faithful. *Who gives life to the dead*, not only the revivification of Sarah’s reproductive ability, but also the resurrection of Jesus and later of all. The phase resembles the second benediction in the *Amidah* (*Shemoneh Esrei* ) in praising God “who gives life to the dead” (Heb “miḥayeh ha-matim”). *Calls into existence,* see [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-28|2 Macc 7.28]] (“recognize that God did not make \[all things in heaven and earth\] out of things that existed,” cf. [[Isaiah 48#^48-13|Isa 48.13]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-2|2 Macc 7.2]]*.* ^4-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-18|18–19]]:** *Father of many nations . . . So numerous . . . be*, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|Gen 15.5]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-2|17.2–7]], and drawing from vv 15–27. Abraham behaved faithfully based upon hope that what was promised would take place; see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-1|Gen 21.1–8]]. ^4-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-20|20–21]]:** *Faith* (Gk “pistis”), the translation should highlight the meaning “faithfulness” rather than “faith,” because Paul is defining a continuum of decisions and concomitant actions based on trust in God’s promises. See [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-1|Gen 17.1]],[[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-17|17–22]]. Paul may be alluding to Abraham’s willingness to be circumcised as a fertility rite, as an act of faithfulness that he would be able to father this promised child. ^4-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-20|20]]:** *Waver,* or quarrel; *strong,* or able; *faith,* or faithfulness. ^4-20-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-22|22]]:** See [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]], referring back to [[Romans 04#^4-3|Rom 4.3]]. *Reckoned to him*, see 4.3–4. ^4-22

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-24|24]]:** *But for ours also*, or but also because of us, making the analogy between Abraham’s faith and that of Paul’s contemporaries among the followers of Jesus; *who believe in him,* or who are faithful to him \[God\]. ^4-24

**[[NRSVue Romans 04#^4-25|25]]:** *Handed over*, by God. *To death,* not in Gk. *For our trespasses*, in his faithfulness shown by his death, the Christ pays the penalty for human sin. ^4-25
