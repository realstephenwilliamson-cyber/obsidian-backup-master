# Zohar 1.157b

← [[Zohar 1.157a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 1.158a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 2.06 - Parashat Va-Yetse#^z1-157b|Parashat Va-Yetse, at 1:157b]]

*Volume 1, folio 157b* · Vayetzei

*Passage Vayetzei 23:225 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.157a#^vayetzei-23-225|Vayetzei 23:225]].*

**Vayetzei 23:226** God, however, desired that they should know also of that place which is the fitting counterpart of this earth, but they were not able to do so until they tasted bread from that place, to wit, heaven, as it says: “I will cause to rain bread from heaven for you” (Ex. 16, 4). It was only then that they attained to a knowledge and a vision of that realm. ^vayetzei-23-226

**Vayetzei 23:227** R. Jose came up to R. Hiya and kissed him, saying: ‘Assuredly this was the reflection of which God desired to remind us. We learn, then, that the preliminary to Israel’s knowledge was bread.’ They then arose and proceeded on their way. Whilst walking they noticed two Damascene plums, a male and a female, which led R. Jose to remark: ‘There is no species which is not divided into male and female. Further, whatever being exists on dry land has its counterpart in the sea.’ ^vayetzei-23-227

**Vayetzei 23:228** R. Jose discoursed on the verse: AND JACOB CAME FROM THE FIELD IN THE EVENING, AND LEAH WENT OUT TO MEET HIM. ‘According to tradition,’ he said, ‘she knew of his coming through the braying of an ass, and hence Scripture says: “Issachar is an ass large-boned” (Gen. 49, 14), where the word *garem* (large-boned) can also be read *garam* (he caused), signifying that the ass was a cause of his birth. Leah said to herself: I assuredly know that should Jacob once enter Rachel’s tent I shall not be able to get him out again. I will therefore await him here so that he may enter my tent. ^vayetzei-23-228

**Vayetzei 23:229** FOR I HAVE SURELY HIRED THEE WITH MY SON’S MANDRAKES. She mentioned the mandrakes to Jacob, because she thought this would predispose him in her favour, on account of their efficacy for childbirth. Jacob, however, knew that it did not depend on the mandrakes but on heaven. ^vayetzei-23-229

**Vayetzei 23:230** By the words “for I have surely hired thee”, Leah may have referred to the Torah, which Jacob embodied. Or she may have meant literally his own self, as much as to say: ”I have hired thee so that I may bear thy very image.” ^vayetzei-23-230
