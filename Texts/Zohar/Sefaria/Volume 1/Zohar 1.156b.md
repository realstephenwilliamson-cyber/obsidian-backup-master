# Zohar 1.156b

← [[Zohar 1.156a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 1.157a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 2.06 - Parashat Va-Yetse#^z1-156b|Parashat Va-Yetse, at 1:156b]]

*Volume 1, folio 156b* · Vayetzei

*Passage Vayetzei 23:193 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.156a#^vayetzei-23-193|Vayetzei 23:193]].*

**Vayetzei 23:194** Now it is that light without illumination which through its attachment to the upper world brought into being all those legions and hosts of existences, all the multitudinous species, of which it is written, “How manifold are thy works, etc.” ^vayetzei-23-194

**Vayetzei 23:195** And whatever is on earth has its counterpart on high, there being no object, however small, in this world but what is subordinate to its counterpart above which has charge over it; and so whenever the thing below bestirs itself, there is a simultaneous stimulation of its counterpart above, as the two realms form one interconnected whole. ^vayetzei-23-195

**Vayetzei 23:196** This may be illustrated from the verse: GIVE ME, I PRAY THEE, OF THY SON’S MANDRAKES. It was not the mandrakes that made Rachel bear children, but God used them as an instrument for procuring the birth of a child, Issachar, who should hold fast to the Torah more than all the other tribes. For Rachel at first held fast to Jacob and did not let him go to Leah, as it is written: “Is it a small matter that thou hast taken away my husband?” But afterwards Rachel said: “Therefore he shall lie with thee to-night for thy son’s mandrakes.” ^vayetzei-23-196

**Vayetzei 23:197** Thus the mandrakes were responsible for the birth of Issachar, through whom the fragrance of the Torah ascended to the presence of the Almighty, in harmony with the words: “The mandrakes give forth fragrance” (S. S. 7, 14); and thus it is further written: AND HE LAY WITH HER THAT (hu) NIGHT, where the term *hu* (he) points assuredly to Him of the supernal world, which, as already explained, is hidden absolutely. For the Torah came forth from the upper world, which is everywhere pointed to by the vocable *hu* (He), indicating a realm undisclosed. ^vayetzei-23-197

**Vayetzei 23:198** Now Issachar took hold of the Torah, which is called the tree of life, meaning life of the upper world, which is called *hu* (he) and not *attah* (thou). ^vayetzei-23-198

**Vayetzei 23:199** It is clear that it was not the mandrakes that opened Rachel’s womb, seeing that it is written “and God hearkened to her, and opened her womb”-God, and no other. For although the mandrakes are endowed with a certain power above, yet that power cannot influence the birth of children, inasmuch as children depend on fate (*mazzal*) and nothing else. ^vayetzei-23-199

**Vayetzei 23:200** However, the mandrakes also are a help to women who are slow in child-bearing but not barren, the latter being under the influence of *mazzal:* ^vayetzei-23-200
