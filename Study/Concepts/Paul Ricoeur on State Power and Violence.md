# Paul Ricoeur on State Power and Violence

> [!abstract] Definition
> Paul Ricoeur's hermeneutical critique of the state's monopolization of historical narrative through "commanded amnesia," pardon, and the ideological manipulation of collective memory to legitimize institutional violence.

## Narrative Manipulation of Memory
In *Memory, History, Forgetting* (2004) and *Lectures on Ideology and Utopia*:
- Ricoeur observes that sovereign power establishes its legitimacy by superimposing a canonical narrative upon historical events.
- By controlling the archive and commemorative public rituals, the state enforces an "excess of memory" regarding its own founding triumphs and an "excess of forgetting" regarding its victims.
- Through amnesty and political pardons, the state attempts to decree forgetting by fiat, institutionalizing an artificial social contract at the expense of historical justice.

## Digital Amplification
In contemporary informational crises (scrubbing public records, revising military histories, purging scientific data), state power merges with [[The Crisis of Curation|algorithmic curation]] to implement what Ricoeur called "devious forms of forgetting," stripping communities of their power to recount their own stories.
