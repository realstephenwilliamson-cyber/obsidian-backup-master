### 24.1–12: Women at the tomb

*[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-1|Text]], p. 165* · ([[Matthew 28#^28-1|Mt 28.1–8]]; [[Mark 16#^16-1|Mk 16.1–8]]; [[John 20#^20-1|Jn 20.1–13]]). ^24-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-1|1]]:** *Taking the spices*, see 23.55–56n. ^24-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-4|4]]:** *Dazzling clothes*, suggest angels (see v 23), as do sudden appearance and supernatural knowledge (vv 5–7). ^24-4

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-6|6]]:** *He told you*, see 9.22; 13.32–33. ^24-6

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-10|10]]:** See 8.1–3; [[John 19#^19-25|Jn 19.25]]; [[John 20#^20-2|20.2]]. ^24-10

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-11|11]]:** *They did not believe*, many commentators claim, incorrectly, that the men did not believe because Jews do not accept women’s testimony; to the contrary, they do not believe because they do not expect Jesus to rise ([[John 20#^20-25|Jn 20.25]]). *has risen* f Other ancient authorities lack verse 12 ^24-11

### 24.13–35: Emmaus incident

*[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-13|Text]], p. 166* ^24-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-13|13]]:** *Two of them*, Cleopas (v 18) and an unidentified follower, who may be a woman. *Emmaus*, location uncertain. *Seven miles*, lit., “sixty stadia”; a “stadion” is approximately 600 feet (180 m). ^24-13-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-16|16]]:** *Eyes were kept from recognizing*, see 9.35n.; 18.34. ^24-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-20|20]]:** *Chief priests and leaders*, Cleopas does not blame all Jews, although he ignores Roman involvement. ^24-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-21|21]]:** *Redeem* (Gk “lutroō,” “ransom”; see [[Matthew 20#^20-28|Mt 20.28]]; [[Mark 10#^10-45|Mk 10.45]]; [[1 Timothy 02#^2-6|1 Tim 2.6]]), bring about the messianic age (1.68). ^24-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-22|22]]:** *Women*, see 24.11n. ^24-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-25|25]]:** *Prophets have declared*, Jesus’ followers searched the scriptures for allusions to him. ^24-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-26|26]]:** *The Messiah should suffer*, not part of general Jewish messianic views. ^24-26

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-27|27]]:** *Moses and all the prophets*, see 16.16n.; 24.44; [[Acts 28#^28-23|Acts 28.23]]; Moses was regarded as having written the Torah. ^24-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-30|30]]:** *Took bread, blessed and broke*, see 9.16; 22.19. ^24-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-31|31]]:** *Their eyes were opened*, revelation is connected with the breaking of bread in a fellowship meal; faith comes through revelation, not Bible study or logic or even a vision of Jesus (see v 35; [[Acts 02#^2-42|Acts 2.42]]; [[Acts 20#^20-7|20.7]]; [[1 Corinthians 10#^10-16|1 Cor 10.16]]). The “recognition” scene (“anagnorisis”) was conventional in Greek and Roman literature as were sudden appearances and disappearances of the (presumed) dead (e.g., Plutarch describes how the \[dead\] Romulus appeared to a friend outside the city of Rome, announced “I will be your propitious deity, Quirinus,” and disappeared \[*Life of Romulus* 28.1–3\]). ^24-31

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-34|34]]:** *Appeared to Simon*, see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-5|1 Cor 15.5]]. *hundred sixty stadia* b Other ancient authorities read *walk along, looking sad?”* c Other ancient authorities read *Jesus the Nazorean* d Or *to set Israel free* e Or *the Christ* f Other ancient authorities lack *within us* ^24-34

### 24.36–43: Jesus’ appearance to his disciples

*[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-36|Text]], p. 167* · ([[John 20#^20-19|Jn 20.19–23]]). ^24-36

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-36|36]]:** *Peace be with you*, see 10.5n. ^24-36-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-37|37]]:** *Ghost*, the appearance of a dead person; here, a mistaken identification since Jesus is not dead (see vv 39–40 on Jesus’ corporeality); Luke stresses the reality of Jesus’ resurrection. ^24-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-42|42]]:** *Broiled fish* (see [[John 21#^21-9|Jn 21.9–13]]). ^24-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-43|43]]:** *He . . . ate*, ghosts, like angels, do not eat. ^24-43

### 24.44–53: Final teachings and ascension

*[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-44|Text]], p. 167* · (see [[Mark 16#^16-15|Mk 16.15]],[[Mark 16#^16-19|19]]). **44**: See vv 26–27; [[Acts 28#^28-23|Acts 28.23]]. *Written about me*, Luke continues to stress that Israel’s Scriptures predicted Jesus’ life, death, and resurrection (vv 25–26). The *Psalms* formed the opening and the longest part of the Ketuvim (“Writings”; see 16.16n.). ^24-44

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-45|45]]:** *Opened their minds*, without divine revelation, readers would not see the predictions about Jesus in the Scriptures (24.32). ^24-45

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-46|46]]:** *It is written*, no text explicitly states that “the Messiah” should suffer, and Luke cites no specific text. Isa 53 and [[Hosea 06#^6-2|Hos 6.2]] are sometimes claimed as proof texts (see also [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-3|1 Cor 15.3–4]]). ^24-46

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-47|47]]:** *Repentance and forgiveness of sins* ([[Acts 05#^5-31|Acts 5.31]]; [[Acts 10#^10-43|10.43]]; [[Acts 13#^13-38|13.38]]; [[Acts 26#^26-18|26.18]]), Jews believed God is always ready to forgive the repentant. *To all nations*, anticipates the Gentile mission ([[Acts 01#^1-8|Acts 1.8]]). ^24-47

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-49|49]]:** *What my Father promised*, the Holy Spirit ([[Acts 02#^2-1|Acts 2.1–21]]). ^24-49

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-50|50]]:** *Bethany*, see 19.29n. *Lifting up his hands*, see [[Torah 26 - Shmini#^9-22|Lev 9.22]]. ^24-50

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-51|51]]:** *Was carried up into heaven*, see [[Acts 01#^1-9|Acts 1.9–11]]. The verse explains why Jesus no longer appears. ^24-51

**[[NRSVue Luke 24#^24-53|53]]:** *Continually in the temple*, i.e, being in the Temple regularly; Luke thereby indicates that Jesus’ followers continue as faithful Jews (see [[Acts 02#^2-46|Acts 2.46]]). *“Peace be with you.”* b Other ancient authorities lack verse 40 c Or *the Christ* d Or *nations. Beginning from Jerusalem* 48*you are witnesses* e Other ancient authorities lack *and was carried up into heaven* f Other ancient authorities lack *worshiped him, and* g Other ancient authorities add *Amen* ^24-53
