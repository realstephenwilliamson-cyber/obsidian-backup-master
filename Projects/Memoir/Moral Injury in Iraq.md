Moral Injury in Iraq

Drescher and colleagues write that “In the context of war, moral injuries may stem from direct participation in acts of combat, such as killing or harming others, or indirect acts, such as witnessing death or dying, failing to prevent immoral acts of others, or giving or receiving orders that are perceived as gross moral violations” (Drescher, K. D., Foy, D. W., Kelly, C., Leshner, A., Schutz, A., & Litz, B.T., 2011)

Given this definition, as a rational human being it is difficult for me to discern any part of my experiences in Iraq as not morally injurious. But we must understand that war as a project is inherently morally injurious to everyone involved in its perpetration.
