## Mūla

प्रकाशत्वोपचारे तु किं बीजं यत्र सत्यता ।
सिद्धे हि चेतने युक्त उपचारः स हि स्फुटम् ॥ ^mula

prakāśatvopacāre tu kiṃ bījaṃ yatra satyatā |
siddhe hi cetane yukta upacāraḥ sa hi sphuṭam || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 10 - Chapter Ten, part 1#^pg344|p. 325]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=344>))
>
> The reason (bīja) why he is metaphorically called ‘fiery’ is (his) liveliness and the like, which is a real (reason). But (if something is unmanifest,) what could be a (similarly) real reason (bīja) for the metaphor (that ascribes to it) a luminous (manifest) state? (with 10.49cd–50ab)
>
> It is clear that a metaphor is logically possible (only) when (the subject of the metaphor is) proved to be conscious. This is because it is (a kind of) superimposition (adhyāropa) which is also based on the (conscious) unification (of elements perceived separately, as is a metaphor). But up to now (as you have presented your case), there is no conscious principle of any sort, even that cognition is not (in itself) luminous (aprakāśa) (as is consciousness). (If you were to say that a cognition) is illumined by another (cognition), then the same argument (would apply as before). (with 10.50cd–52ab)
