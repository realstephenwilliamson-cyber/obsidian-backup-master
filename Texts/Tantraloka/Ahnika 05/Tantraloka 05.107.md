## Mūla

आत्मन्यनात्मतानाशे महाव्याप्तिः प्रवर्तते ।
आनन्द उद्भवः कम्पो निद्रा घूर्णिश्च पञ्चकम् ॥ ^mula

ātmanyanātmatānāśe mahāvyāptiḥ pravartate |
ānanda udbhavaḥ kampo nidrā ghūrṇiśca pañcakam || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IV
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 04 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v04 06 - Chapter Five, part 4#^pg157|p. 142]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 04.pdf#page=157>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 5.107cd–108ab.
>
> The Great Pervasion comes about when first the (notion) of Self in what is not the Self has merged (into consciousness), once one has attained one’s own authentic identity, and (then the notion) of there being no Self (anātmatā) in the Self has been destroyed. (with 5.106cd–107ab)
>
> Therefore, it is said in the venerable Mālinīvijayottara that (the signs) are joy, an upward leap, trembling, sleep and rolling of the eyes (ghūrṇi), which is the fifth. (with 5.107cd–108ab)
