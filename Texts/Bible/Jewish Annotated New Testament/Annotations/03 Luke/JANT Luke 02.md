### 2.1–7: Jesus’ birth

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 113* · ([[Matthew 01#^1-18|Mt 1.18–25]]). ^2-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Augustus*, who ruled 27 BCE–14 CE, was called “god,” “son of god,” “savior,” and “father.” Luke’s readers therefore should recognize the political challenge: which “son of God” are they going to follow, Jesus or Caesar? *Registered*, this census is not externally attested. Rome, which registered residents primarily to determine taxes, did not undertake a single empire-wide census, and did not require people to return to their natal homes to register. No external evidence exists for a Roman census under Herod the Great, during whose lifetime Jesus was born. In Jewish thought, counting people directly is contrary to divine will ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^30-12|Ex 30.12]]; 2 Sam 24). [[Acts 05#^5-37|Acts 5.37]] dates the revolt of Judas the Galilean to the “time of the census” and thus contrasts Joseph and Mary’s obedience to Judas’s revolution. The scene may be a midrash on [[Psalms 087#^87-5|Ps 87.5–6]]\[LXX 86\]. ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Quirinius*, appointed “legatus” (military governor) to suppress the revolt by the Homonadensians in Cilicia; the actual governor was Varus. Josephus (*Ant*. 17.354; 18.1–2; see also *J.W*. 2.117; 7.253) reports a census under Quirinius in 6 CE, after Herod’s death. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-3|3–4]]:** *Their own towns*, the census explains how Jesus can be born in *Bethlehem*, the *city* where *David* was born (1 Sam 17; 20; [[Micah 5#^5-1|Mic 5.2]] predicts a ruler from Bethlehem; see [[Matthew 02#^2-5|Mt 2.5–6]]). Bethlehem is approximately five miles from Jerusalem, eighty-five miles from Nazareth. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Firstborn* (Gk “prototokos”; Heb “bekhor”), and thus dedicated to God ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-2|Ex 13.2]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-12|12]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-15|15]]; [[Torah 34 - Bamidbar#^3-12|Num 3.12–13]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-17|Deut 21.17]]); Israel is God’s “firstborn son” ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-22|Ex 4.22]]). *Manger*, feeding trough; the association of Jesus with the trough — a symbol of food — anticipates the Last Supper (22.19). The “manger scene” or crèche (in the USA), with both Magi (from Matthew 2) and shepherds (from Luke) was invented by Francis of Assisi in the thirteenth century. *Inn* (Gk “kataluma”), NRSV translates the same term in 22.11 as “guest room.” Luke gives no indication, contrary to one popular reading, that the innkeeper refused the poor family hospitality; rather, there was no room for the privacy needed for the birth. ^2-7

### 2.8–20: Annunciation to shepherds

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-8|Text]], p. 113* · ( [[Matthew 02#^2-1|Mt 2.1–12]]). ^2-8

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Shepherds*, suggesting the numerous positive im ages of shepherds (including Moses and David) in Israel’s Scriptures, and perhaps the connection of sheep with the sacrificial system (see Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.133,136). ^2-8-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-9|9]]:** *Glory of the Lord*, compare Heb “kavod” \[of YHWH\] ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-7|Ex 16.7]],[[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-10|10]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-16|24.16–17]]; [[Isaiah 40#^40-5|Isa 40.5]]; etc.). ^2-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Good news*, see 1.19n. *All the people* (Heb “kol haʿam”), would be heard by a Jewish audience as concerning the “people Israel.” ^2-10

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-11|11]]:** *City of David*, see 2.3–4n. *Messiah* (Gk “Christos”) *the Lord*, see 1.17n. *Pss. Sol*. 17.32 mentions “a Lord, the anointed” in reference to a Davidic king; see also the “Lord’s anointed” in [[I Samuel 24#^24-7|1 Sam 24.6]] (in reference to King Saul); [[Lamentations 4#^4-20|Lam 4.20]]. ^2-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Wrapped in bands*, KJV: “swaddling cloths”; see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-4|Wis 7.4–5]]. *Manger*, see 2.7n. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Heavenly host* ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-19|Deut 4.19]]; [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^33-2|33.2]]; [[I Kings 22#^22-19|1 Kings 22.19]]; [[Jeremiah 08#^8-2|Jer 8.2]]; [[Psalms 033#^33-6|Ps 33.6]]; etc.), the angels who serve God, and sing God’s praises ([[Isaiah 06#^6-3|Isa 6.3]]; Ps 29). ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-19|19]]:** See 2.51. ^2-19

### 2.21–24: Jesus’ circumcision and presentation

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-21|Text]], p. 114* ^2-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-21|21]]:** *Eight days had passed*, lit., “had been fulfilled”; ritual circumcision takes place eight days after birth; see 1.59n. *Called Jesus*, see 1.31n. ^2-21-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-22|22]]:** *Their purification*, no purification rites were required for fathers or newborns. Some scholars, attempting to prove the historicity of this irregular practice, suggest that Joseph had helped with the delivery of Jesus, and thus he too was ritually unclean, but the text usually cited (e.g., *m. Nidd.* 5.1) do not support the claim. For the mother’s purification (forty days after the birth of a son), see Lev 12. *Law of Moses*, the Gospel’s first of nine references to the Torah. *Jerusalem*, a Lukan focus. *Present him*, possible allusion to the “pidyon ha-ben,” “redemption of the firstborn” ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-2|Ex 13.2]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-12|12]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-15|15]]; [[Torah 38 - Korach#^18-15|Num 18.15–16]]; [[Nehemiah 10#^10-36|Neh 10.35–36]]); no law prescribes this presentation. Luke may be alluding to the presentation of Samuel ([[I Samuel 02#^2-24|1 Sam 2.24]]). ^2-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-23|23]]:** Paraphrasing [[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-2|Ex 13.2]]. ^2-23

### 2.25–38: Simeon and Anna

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-25|Text]], p. 114* ^2-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-25|25]]:** *Jerusalem*, see 2.22. *Simeon*, not otherwise attested. *Consolation* (Gk “paraklēsis”), here synonymous with redemption, see 1.68n.; 24.21n.; see also Isa 40\[LXX\]; *2 Bar*. 44.7. *Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n. ^2-25-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-27|27]]:** *What was customary*, see 2.22n. ^2-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-29|29]]:** *Now you are dismissing*, Lat “Nunc dimittis” the traditional name of the canticle. ^2-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-30|30–31]]:** *Your salvation*, see 3.6. *Presence of all peoples*, see [[Isaiah 52#^52-10|Isa 52.10]]; *among people* d Gk *Symeon* e Or *the Lord’s Christ* f Gk *In the Spirit, he* g Gk *he* h Gk *slave* [[Psalms 098#^98-2|Ps 98.2]]. ^2-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-32|32]]:** *Revelation to the Gentiles*, see [[Isaiah 49#^49-6|Isa 49.6]]; Judaism generally taught that salvation was for all people. *Glory to your people Israel*, see [[Isaiah 46#^46-13|Isa 46.13]]. ^2-32

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-33|33]]:** *Father*, Joseph (2.48; 3.23). ^2-33

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-34|34]]:** *Blessed them*, perhaps hinting at a priestly blessing ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-23|Num 6.23]]; [[I Samuel 02#^2-20|1 Sam 2.20]]). *Falling and the rising*, Jesus’ preaching, and the preaching about him, create division among Jews. ^2-34

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-35|35]]:** *Sword will pierce your own soul*, views of Mary’s righteousness, suffering, and intercession will develop in Christian thought. ^2-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-36|36]]:** *Prophet*, *Anna*, comparable to women prophets in Judaism (Miriam \[[[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-20|Ex 15.20]]\], Deborah \[[[Judges 04#^4-4|Judg 4.4]]\], Huldah \[[[II Kings 22#^22-14|2 Kings 22.14]]\], and possibly Isaiah’s wife \[[[Isaiah 08#^8-3|Isa 8.3]]\]). ^2-36

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-37|37]]:** *As a widow*, Luke favors celibacy. However, people did not reside in the Temple continually, despite [[Psalms 023#^23-6|Ps 23.6]]. ^2-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-38|38]]:** *Redemption of Jerusalem*, see 1.68n.; 24.21n. ^2-38

### 2.39–40: Jesus’ childhood

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-39|Text]], p. 115* ^2-39

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-39|39]]:** *Required by the law*, Luke emphasizes the family’s connection to Torah. ^2-39-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-40|40]]:** *Filled with wisdom* . . . , see [[Isaiah 11#^11-2|Isa 11.2]]. ^2-40

### 2.41–52: Jesus in the Temple

*[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-41|Text]], p. 115* ^2-41

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-41|41]]:** *Every year*, Luke continues the themes of Jerusalem, Temple, and Torah; the annual pilgrimage recollects Hannah ([[I Samuel 01#^1-3|1 Sam 1.3]]). *Passover*, one of three pilgrimage festivals (with Sukkot/Booths and Shavuot/Weeks), see [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-14|Ex 23.14–17]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-1|Deut 16.1–8]],[[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-16|16]]; see also [[Luke 22#^22-7|Lk 22.7–13]]. ^2-41-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-42|42]]:** *Twelve years old*, the story is not, contrary to popular teaching, Jesus’ bar mitzvah; for later texts about thirteen as the age of adult responsibility, see *m. Nidd*. 5.6; *m. Avot* 5.21. ^2-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-47|47]]:** Stories of heroes’ prodigious wisdom are conventional, e.g., Cyrus (Herodotus I, 114ff.), Alexander the Great (Plutarch, *Life of Alexander* 5), Moses (Philo, *Life of Moses* 1.21; Josephus, *Ant*. 2.230); Samuel (Josephus, *Ant.* 2.230); Josephus, *Life* 9, records: “When I was a child, about fourteen years of age, I was commended by all for the love I had of learning; on which account the high priests and principal men of the city frequently came to me together, to know my opinion about the accurate understanding of points of the law.” ^2-47

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-48|48]]:** *Father*, see 2.33n., anticipating the irony of Jesus’ response in 2.49. ^2-48

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-49|49]]:** *Father’s house*, the phrase is common in the Tanakh but never refers to the Temple. ^2-49

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-51|51]]:** See 2.19. ^2-51

**[[NRSVue Luke 02#^2-52|52]]:** See [[I Samuel 02#^2-26|1 Sam 2.26]]; [[Luke 01#^1-80|Lk 1.80]]; [[Luke 02#^2-40|2.40]]; similar terms describe Moses (Philo, *Life of Moses* 1.19; Josephus, *Ant*. 2.231); see also [[Proverbs 03#^3-4|Prov 3.4]]. ^2-52
