# John 19 (JANT notes)

[[JANT John 18|← John 18]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT John 20|John 20 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue John 19|NRSVue]] · [[John 19|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT John - Introduction]]

### 19.1–42: Condemnation, death, and burial

*[[NRSVue John 19#^19-1|Text]], p. 214* ^19-1

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-1|1]]:** *Flogged* ([[Luke 23#^23-22|Lk 23.22]]), common penalty ([[2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|2 Cor 11.23–25]]), here meant to weaken Jesus and speed his death. ^19-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-2|2]]:** *Crown . . . purple*, symbols of kingship used in mockery. ^19-2

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-5|5]]:** *Here is the man*, famous in its Latin version, “Ecce Homo.” ^19-5

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-6|6]]:** *Chief priests and police*, in John it is the Temple party, and not the people as a whole, who call for crucifixion (contrast [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]]). ^19-6

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-7|7]]:** *Jews*, here, the Temple functionaries. *Law*, probably a reference to the prohibition of blasphemy; see 10.30n. ^19-7

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-8|8]]:** *Afraid*, perhaps of making the wrong judgment. ^19-8

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-12|12]]:** *No friend of the emperor*, the Jews threaten to blackmail Pilate if he releases Jesus. ^19-12

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-13|13]]:** *Gabbatha*, an outdoor platform, perhaps meaning “elevated place.” Here Pilate actually renders judgment (previously he had been conducting an inquiry). ^19-13

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-14|14]]:** *Day of Preparation*, when the Passover lambs are slaughtered in the Temple. ^19-14

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-15|15]]:** When the chief priests affirm the emperor’s kingship, Pilate agrees to have Jesus crucified. ^19-15

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-16|16]]:** *Handed him over to them*, the absence of a new antecedent for “them” implies that the pronoun refers back to the “chief priests” in v 15. What follows, however, suggests that the referent should be the soldiers (v 23). ^19-16

### 19.17–37: The crucifixion

*[[NRSVue John 19#^19-17|Text]], p. 214* ^19-17

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-17|17]]:** *Cross*, the crossbeam rather than the entire cross. There is no mention that someone else (Simon of Cyrene, [[Mark 15#^15-21|Mk 15.21]]) carries the cross. *Golgotha*, likely just outside Jerusalem’s city walls. ^19-17-2

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-19|19–20]]:** *Inscription . . . in Hebrew, in Latin, and in Greek*, it is uncertain whether such inscriptions were usual. ^19-19

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-23|23]]:** *Tunic*, undergarment. This may reflect an actual weaving technique, but it may also be a symbolic reference to Jesus’ going to his death as a high priest, whose robes were also seamless (Jose- phus, *Ant*. 3.161; [[LXX Exodus 36#^36-35|Ex 36.35]]\[LXX\]; see also [[Revelation 01#^1-13|Rev 1.13]] and Hebrews passim). ^19-23

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-24|24]]:** *Scripture*, [[Psalms 022#^22-19|Ps 22.18]]. In [[Mark 15#^15-34|Mk 15.34]], Jesus quotes the first verse of this psalm, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” ^19-24

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-25|25]]:** *His mother*, see 2.1n. *Mary the wife of Clopas*, perhaps mother of James and Joses ([[Mark 15#^15-40|Mk 15.40]]). The number of women is unclear, but many commentators understand that four women were present: Jesus’ mother, his aunt, a woman named Mary who was the mother of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene. In the Synoptics, the women stand at a distance ([[Mark 15#^15-40|Mk 15.40]]). This is the first reference in this Gospel to Mary Magdalene, who comes to the fore in 20.1–18. ^19-25

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-26|26]]:** *Here is your son*, Jesus’ brothers disappear from the Gospel after 7.10. The reference to “brothers” in 20.17 is to the disciples. On a narrative level, the Gospel is showing that Jesus is concerned to care for the well-being of his mother, perhaps to counter-balance his rather gruff words to her in 2.4, the only other place that she appears. Jesus is also accentuating the role that the Beloved Disciple will have after his death, by giving him and his mother into their mutual care. The passage has been interpreted symbolically by viewing the mother and the disciple as representing, for example, Jewish Christianity and Gentile Christianity, with the former being entrusted to the care of the latter. The narrative interpretations, however, are more convincing. ^19-26

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-28|28]]:** *Scripture*, see [[Psalms 069#^69-22|Ps 69.21]]. *Thirsty*, only here does Jesus express thirst; his request to the Samaritan woman for water (4.7) is not an expression of thirst but rather a way of introducing the topic of “living water”; Jn 4 does not describe Jesus as drinking. ^19-28

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-29|29]]:** The only time in the Gospel that Jesus explicitly consumes food or drink, perhaps an allusion to his not-quite-human status. Biblical and post-biblical angels generally do not eat or drink, e.g., [[Judges 13#^13-16|Judg 13.16]]. *Hyssop*, used to mark the doorposts with the blood of lambs so the angel of death would “pass over” the Israelite firstborn sons ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-22|Ex 12.22]]). ^19-29

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-31|31]]:** *Day of Preparation*, see 19.14n. *Legs . . . broken*, to hasten death. That Jesus’ legs were not broken symbolizes his suitability as a Passover sacrifice given his unblemished nature, both physical and spiritual ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-46|Ex 12.46]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-14|Heb 9.14]]). ^19-31

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-34|34]]:** *Blood and water*, signifying both birth and the rituals of baptism and the Eucharist. ^19-34

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-35|35]]:** *He who saw*, the beloved disciple. ^19-35

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-36|36]]:** *Scripture*, [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-46|Ex 12.46]]; [[Psalms 034#^34-21|Ps 34.20]], read in the context of the previous verse, where the “righteous” is understood not generally, but as Jesus, the Passover lamb, dying when lambs were slaughtered for the Passover sacrifice. ^19-36

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-37|37]]:** *Scripture*, [[Zechariah 12#^12-10|Zech 12.10]] though John does not follow either the MT or the LXX verbatim. ^19-37

### 19.38–42: Burial

*[[NRSVue John 19#^19-38|Text]], p. 216* ^19-38

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-38|38]]:** *Joseph of Arimathea*, see [[Matthew 27#^27-57|Mt 27.57–60]]; [[Mark 15#^15-43|Mk 15.43]]; [[Luke 23#^23-50|Lk 23.50–53]]. ^19-38-2

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-39|39]]:** *Nicodemus*, 3.1–17; 7.50–52. *Myrrh*, resinous gum mixed with aloes, used for embalming; this may recall Jesus’ anointing (12.3). A hundred Roman pounds = 75 lbs. The overabundance of spices may reflect royal symbolism (see Josephus, *Ant*. 17.199 on the huge amount of spices at the burial of Herod the Great) or it may simply belong to the Johannine trope of abundance; see also the abundance of bread and fish in 6.13, and the miraculous catch of fish in 21.11. ^19-39

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-40|40]]:** *Custom of the Jews*, implies a non-Jewish audience. ^19-40

**[[NRSVue John 19#^19-41|41–42]]:** The *garden* is not literally *in the place where he was crucified*, but *nearby*. ^19-41
