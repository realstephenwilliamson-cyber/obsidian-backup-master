## About

A series of fifty volumes of English translations of the sacred texts of Asian religions, edited by Friedrich Max Müller (1823–1900) and published at Oxford from 1879 to 1910. It covers Hindu (Vedic and Brahmanic), Buddhist, Zoroastrian, Confucian, Taoist, Jain and Islamic texts.

- **Editor and publisher:** Max Müller supervised the series and died before its last volumes appeared (Encyclopaedia Iranica). Wikipedia names Oxford University Press as publisher; Iranica says the series was sponsored by Oxford University.
- **Plan and dates:** in the first volume (1879) Müller projected 24 volumes over eight years. His 1873 list had six canons; Jainism was added by 1884 (Iranica). Volume 50, a general index by Moriz Winternitz with a preface by A. A. Macdonell, appeared in 1910.
- **Shape:** by Iranica's count, 21 volumes Vedic-Brahmanic, 10 Buddhist, 8 Zoroastrian, 4 Confucian, 2 each Islamic, Jain and Taoist, and the index. Christianity and Judaism were left out; the Qur'an is the only Abrahamic text.
- **Translators:** twenty, Iranica says, among them James Legge (Confucian and Taoist texts), Georg Bühler (Dharmaśāstra), Julius Eggeling (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa), T. W. Rhys Davids and Hermann Oldenberg (Buddhist texts; Oldenberg also the Gṛhya-sūtras and Vedic hymns), E. W. West (Pahlavi texts), Hermann Jacobi (Jaina Sūtras), E. H. Palmer (Qur'an, vols 6 and 9) and K. T. Telang (Bhagavad Gītā, vol. 8). Müller translated the Upanishads (vols 1 and 15).
- **Assessment:** Iranica says the choice of texts reflects Müller's own interests and the culture of imperial Britain, with a strong focus on Indo-European faiths, and that some translations (the Avesta translation of Darmesteter and Mills, the Gāthās above all) are uneven and some introductions now dated. Arie Molendijk's study (Oxford University Press, 2016) treats the edition as an exceptionally ambitious editorial project.
- **Availability:** Wikipedia says all fifty volumes are in the public domain in the United States and are widely available online.
