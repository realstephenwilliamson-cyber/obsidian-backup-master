Ambiguum <sup>25</sup>

Gregory the Theologian, Or. 29.15 (SC 250:208, Il. 7-11). Gregory's point turns on a principle of Aristotelian logic (Cat 1a), i.e., whether or not an individual can belong to a species or class only if it shares all the characteristics of every member of that species or class. Aristotle found this to be a false conclusion, and Gregory agreed. Thus, conceding some differences in particulars (e.g., that in some ways the Father is superior to the Son) does not demand that the individuals in the species be reclassified. As Gregory states later in this sec tion of his oration, the Arian fallacy lies in arguing from the particular to the general, from a conditioned to an unconditioned term, which Gregory says “is like saying ‘X is a dead man,’ and then drawing the conclusion: ‘mankind without qualification is dead’; see Norris, Faith Gives Fullness to Reasoning, 150-51.
