### 12.1–11: Spiritual gifts

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 343* · This ch discusses how the spirit of God is manifest in the community of believers. ^12-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-1|1]]:** *Gifts* (Gk “pneumatika”), see translators’ note *a*, behaviors enabled by the Holy Spirit, such as speaking in tongues and prophesying. 1QS 4.3 attributes “prudence, insight, and wonderful wisdom” to God’s Spirit. Although later rabbinic literature considers prophecy to have ended with Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi due to the departure of the Holy Spirit from Israel (*b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 11#11a|11a]]), the Qumran Scrolls, Josephus, and Jewish apocalyptic literature consider it still occurring (1QpHab 7.4–6; Josephus, *Ant.* 13.299**–**300; *J.W.* 4.386; *2 Bar.* 1.1). The spiritual gifts of miracles and glossolalia were performed by some rabbinic sages and *hekhalot* (“palaces”) mystics (*Otsar ha-Geonim, Hagigah, Te-shuvot* 14–15; *b. Taʾan.* 24b; *b. Pesah.* [[Pesachim 112#112b|112b]]; *b. B. Kamma* 50a;). ^12-1-2

> [!note] Cursing Jesus *(p. 343)*
> *Let Jesus be cursed!* (12.3): Justin Martyr (ca. 160) claimed that Jews cursed Christ (*Dial.* 137.2), although no ancient Jewish sources attest this practice. Origen attributed this curse to Jews who blasphemed Jesus to avoid anti-Christian persecution (*Hom. Ps.* 37 \[36\]). Medieval Jewish apostates may have proven their return to the fold by publicly repudiating their former beliefs with such a curse (ninth-century Geonic responsum in Haim Moda*ʿ*i, ed., *Sefer shaʿarei tzedeq* 2: part 6
>
> \[no. 11\]; Agobard of Lyons, *On the Insolence of the Jews to Louis the Pious*). Alternatively, in the tradition of ancient malediction or curse formulae found in amulets and magical texts, the phrase could be translated, “May Jesus curse . . .,” which Paul cautions would only be efficacious if recited *by the Holy Spirit* (12.3) and not in the name of pagan deities. Paul advises the former pagan Corinthians to use the name “Jesus” in place of pagan divinities that are mere *idols* (12.2). Paul himself invokes a curse on opponents of *the Lord* (16.22).

> [!note] Spiritual Gifts (1 cor 12.8–10) *(p. 343)*
> Paul lists nine activities manifesting the Spirit’s work: (1) *utterance of wisdom* (Gk *sophia*): pronouncements of God’s eschatological wisdom, as revealed in the death and resurrection of Jesus (see 1.30; 2.7–10); (2) *utterance of knowledge* (Gk *gnō*sis): teachings, like those Paul has conveyed in this letter, and/or proclamations about the divine nature (see 1.5; 8.7,11; 13.2; [[Romans 15#^15-14|Rom 15.14]]), such as found in early Jewish mysticism (Ezek 1; *1 En*. 14.8; 4QShir Shabbb 4; *t. Meg.* 4.28; *b. Meg*. [[Megillah 24#24b|24b]]; *b. Hag*. [[Chagigah 12#12a|12a]]–14b); (3) *faith*: trustworthy words as opposed to deceitful utterances (see LXX [[LXX Proverbs 12#^12-17|Prov 12.17]]), a cornerstone of Jewish ethics ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.13]]; [[Psalms 120#^120-2|Ps 120.2]]; *m. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 2.1; *y. Demai* 24b; *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 4#4a|4a]]); (4) *healing*: Jewish traditions mention the healing powers of the patriarchs, Moses, Solomon, and various prophets and rabbis; the traditions also regarded healing as a manifestation of God’s power ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^20-17|Gen 20.17]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-13|Num 12.13]]; [[II Chronicles 30#^30-20|2 Chr 30.20]]; [[LXX Sirach 38#^38-4|Sir 38.4–8]]; [[LXX Sirach 48#^48-23|48.23]]; Philo, *On Agriculture* 95–8; Josephus, *Ant.* 8.42; *T. Reuben* 1.7; 4QPrayer of Nabonidus Aramaic \[4Q242\]; *b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 34#34b|34b]]); (5) *miracles*: attributed to the patriarchs, Solomon, various prophets and rabbis, e.g., ḤHoni the Circle Drawer who elicited rainfall \[*m. Taʾan.* 3.8\]; see “Jewish Miracle Workers,” p. 680; (6) *prophecy*: speech with eschatological, ethical, or mystical insight (see 12.1–11n.); (7) *discernment of spirits*: deciding whether a spirit manifested in gifts is demonic or angelic (5.12–6.11); (8) *tongues*: speaking in foreign languages, including the unintelligible language of angels, also found in Jewish literature (see 13.1,8; 14.2; [[Isaiah 28#^28-11|Isa 28.11]] \[LXX *xenolalia*\]; 2 Esd \[*4 Ezra*\] 14.40–41; [[Acts 02#^2-4|Acts 2.4]],[[Acts 02#^2-11|11]]; *Apoc. Abr*. 17; *T. Job* 48–50; *1 En.* 71.11; 10.46; *Gen. Rab.* 28.6; *b. Sukk.* [[Sukkah 28#28a|28a]]); (9) *interpretation of tongues*: found in Jewish, Greek, and Roman practice (*Mek. Bahodesh* 9 on [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-15|Ex 20.15]]; Plutarch, *Pyth. orac.* 24–25; Virgil, *Aeneid* 6.44–49, 97–99; Iambl., *Myst*. 1.7).

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-2|2]]:** *Idols that could not speak*, see 10.14; [[I Kings 18#^18-26|1 Kings 18.26–29]]; [[Habakkuk 2#^2-18|Hab 2.18–19]]; [[Psalms 115#^115-5|Ps 115.5]]; Bar 6.7; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 04#^4-16|3 Macc 4.16]]; *Jos. Asen.* 13.11. ^12-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-3|3]]:** *Spirit*, speech understood as breath flowing through the body. *By*, infused with. ^12-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-4|4–7]]:** Diverse *gifts,* (Gk “charismata,” “graced things”; see 1.7). *Services* and *activities,* reflect a common purpose for the church. ^12-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-11|11]]:** *Chooses*, gifts are allocated by the Spirit, and are not based on merit or skill. ^12-11

### 12.12–26: The body

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-12|Text]], p. 344* ^12-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-12|12]]:** Paul’s *body* metaphor inverts popular usage, where bodies of different social ranks were valued hierarchically (Ulpian, *Dig.* 48.19.8.3). For the body as a metaphor for the people Israel see e.g., [[Isaiah 01#^1-5|Isa 1.5–6]]; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 3.131. ^12-12-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-13|13]]:** Paul may be alluding to [[Isaiah 56#^56-6|Isa 56.6]], where Jerusalem’s Jewish remnant worship together with Gentiles (*1 En.* 108.12). ^12-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-15|15–25]]:** The body relies on each part fulfilling its proper role. ^12-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-26|26]]:** *All suffer*, see 11.23n.; 15.3n. Vicarious suffering and reward appears in some prophetic and later Jewish literature (Isa 53; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 17#^17-21|4 Macc 17.21]]; *Lev. Rab.* 4.6; *b. Shabb.* [[Shabbat 39#39a|39a]]). ^12-26

### 12.27–31: Distribution of gifts

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-27|Text]], p. 344* ^12-27

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-28|28]]:** *First . . . second . . . third*, in order of authority or perhaps chronology. ^12-28

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 12#^12-31|31]]:** *But strive*, better: “Yet you strive,” a challenge to overcome jealousy of others’ *gifts* and embrace the *more excellent way* of sustaining the community, as described in the next chapter (see 13.1–2). ^12-31
