# Rashi 40 - Balak

← [[Rashi 39 - Chukat]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 41 - Pinchas]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-2|Numbers 22:2–25:9]]* · Text: [[Torah 40 - Balak]]

## Chapter 22

### 22:2

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-2|Numbers 22:2]]

**Balak saw . . . all that Yisroel had done to the Emorite.** He said, “Those two kings whom we relied on could not withstand them, certainly we can not.” Therefore, “Moav became terrified.”

### 22:3

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-3|Numbers 22:3]]

**[Moav] became terrified.** The word denotes fear, as in “fear for yourselves.”

**Moav detested.** They detested their lives, similar to “I am disgusted with my life.” This is a shortened sentence.

### 22:4

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-4|Numbers 22:4]]

**To the elders of Midian.** But were they not ancient enemies, as it is said, “who attacked Midian in the field of Moav,” when Midian went to war against Moav? Still, because of their fear of Yisroel, they made peace. But why did Moav see fit to accept advice from Midian? When they saw the supernatural victories of Yisroel, they said, “Their leader was raised in Midian. Let us ask them what his character is.” They told them: “He has no power, except with the mouth.” They said, “We, too, will confront them with a man whose power is with his mouth.”

**Just as the ox laps up.** Whatever an ox laps up is no longer marked by blessing.

**At that time.** He was unsuited for royalty. He was one of the chieftains of Midian, and when Sichon died, he was appointed over them temporarily.

### 22:5

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-5|Numbers 22:5]]

**To Pesor.** Like the money changer, to whom everyone dispatches money. Similarly,  
all the monarchs dispatched their correspondence to him. According to the plain meaning of the passage, this was the name of the place.

**The land of his people.** Balak’s — he came from there. This person (Bil’am) prophesied, and said to him, “You are destined to be a king.” Should you ask, why did the Holy One, Blessed is He, visit His Divine Presence on an evil gentile? So that the nations might not extenuate themselves by saying, “If we had prophets, we would have repented.” He established prophets for them, and they breached a universal barrier; they had originally refrained from promiscuity, but this person (Bil’am) advised them to indulge freely in promiscuity.

**To call him.** The invitation was his, for his benefit, as he offered him a large sum of money.

**A nation came out of Egypt.** You may ask, “Why is this harmful to you”?

**Behold, they have covered the face of the earth.** Sichon and Og, who were our guardians, were killed by them.

**And is located across from me.** This is written incompletely. They are nearby, to cut me down, as in, “I cut them down.”

### 22:6

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-6|Numbers 22:6]]

**[Enable] us to strike at him.** My people and I will strike at them. Another interpretation: This term is found in the Mishnah, “He reduces the price for him” — to diminish them a little.

**For I know, etc.** Through the war of Sichon, whom you assisted in striking down Moav.

### 22:7

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-7|Numbers 22:7]]

**With magic chas in their hands.** All types of magic charms, so that he could not say, “My tools are not with me.” Another interpretation. The elders of Midian took up this mystical omen: They said, “If he comes with us this time, there is something to him; but if he rebuffs us, it is useless.” This is why, when he said to them, “Spend the night here,” they said, “It is hopeless.” They left him there and departed, as it is said, “the Moavite chieftains stayed with Bil’am,” but the elders of Midian departed.

### 22:8

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-8|Numbers 22:8]]

**Spend this night here.** The Sacred spirit visits him only at night. It is the same with all of the gentile prophets, and it was the same with Lavan — in a dream at night, as it is said, “God appeared to Lavan the Aramean in a dream that night,” like a man who visits his concubine secretly.

**According to what Adonoy tells me.** If He advises me to go with people like you, I will go with you. Perhaps it is beneath His dignity to allow me to go, unless it is with higher ranking officials than you.

**Remained (lit. sat)** The word denotes remaining.

### 22:9

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-9|Numbers 22:9]]

**Who are these men with you?** He gave him the opportunity for error. He (Bil’am) said, “At times, not everything is revealed to Him. He is not omniscient. So I shall divine a time when I can curse without his understanding.”

### 22:10

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-10|Numbers 22:10]]

**Balak son of Tzipor, etc.** Although I am not distinguished in Your eyes, I am distinguished in the eyes of kings.

### 22:11

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-11|Numbers 22:11]]

**Curse for me.** This [קָבָה לִּי] is harsher than אָרָה לִּי, as it is specific and clear.

**And banish him.** From the world. Balak only said, “I will banish them from the land,” I seek only to get them away from me. Bil’am hated them more than Balak.

### 22:12

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-12|Numbers 22:12]]

**Do not go with them.** He (Bil’am) said to Him, “if so, I will curse them here.” He (God) said to him, “You shall not curse the people.” He (Bil’am) said to Him, “If so, I will bless them.” He (God) said to him, “They do not need your blessing.” **For they are blessed.** [This follows] the proverb: One says to the wasp, “Neither your honey nor your sting.”

### 22:13

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-13|Numbers 22:13]]

**To go with you.** But with higher ranking officials than you. We learn that he was arrogant, and was unwilling to reveal that he was under God’s domination, except in insolent terms. Therefore, “Balak persisted. . .”

### 22:17

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-17|Numbers 22:17]]

**I will honor you greatly.** More than you have received in the past, I will give you.

### 22:18

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-18|Numbers 22:18]]

**Silver and gold enough to fill his house.** We learn that he was avaricious, and he coveted the money of others. He said, “He should rightfully give me all his gold and silver, since he would have to hire great armies, which may or may not succeed, whereas I will certainly succeed.”

**I cannot transgress.** He was forced to reveal that he was dominated by others. And he prophesied here that he would be unable to nullify the blesings which the patriarchs received by word of the Divine Presence.

### 22:19

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-19|Numbers 22:19]]

**You also.** His mouth ensnared him. You, too, will, in the end, leave in frustration, like the first ones.

**What more will [Adonoy tell me].** He will not alter his word from a blessing to a curse. Would that he does not add a blessing. Here, he prophesied that He (God) would bless them further through him (Bil’am).

### 22:20

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-20|Numbers 22:20]]

**If [the men have come] to call you.** If the invitation is for you, any you expect remuneration, then rise and go with them.

**However.** In spite of you, whatever I will say to you, you will do. Nevertheless, “Bil’am went.” He said, “perhaps I can convince him to agree.”

### 22:21

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-21|Numbers 22:21]]

**[He] saddled his donkey.** From here [we derive] that hatred breeds impropriety — he saddled it himself. The Holy One, Blessed is He, said, “Evil one, their patriarch Avrohom has already preceded you”! as it is said: “Avrohom rose early and saddled his donkey.”

**With the Moavite dignitaries.** His heart (intent) was exactly the same as theirs.

### 22:22

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-22|Numbers 22:22]]

**Because he went.** He saw that the thing was evil in the Omnipresent’s eyes, and yet he craved to go.

**To thwart him.** He was an angel of mercy, who wanted to prevent him from sinning, so that he would not sin and perish.

**Accompanied by his two attendants.** From here we derive that when a distinguished person embarks on a journey, he should bring along two people to attend him, who will, in turn, attend to one another.

### 22:23

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-23|Numbers 22:23]]

**The donkey saw.** But he [Bilam] did not see, for the Holy One, Blessed is He, granted an animal greater perception than a man, who, because of his intelligence, would become deranged from the perception of destructive spirits.

**With his sword drawn in his hand.** He said, ”This evil one abandoned the tools of his trade, for the weapon of the gentile nations is the sword, whereas he accosted them with his mouth, which is their calling. So I will take hold of his [tool], and accost him with his calling. This, too, is the way he came to his end — “and Bil’am the son of B’or, they killed with the sword.”

### 22:24

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-24|Numbers 22:24]]

**In a narrow path.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, בִּשְׁבִיל, “in the path.” Similarly, “If the dust of Shomron is sufficient for the paths . . .,” the dust which attaches itself to the soles of the feet when walking. Similarly, “Who measured the waters with his step”? with his feet, when walking.

**With a barrier at either side.** Unless specified otherwise, גָּדֵר, refers to a barrier of stone.

### 22:25

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-25|Numbers 22:25]]

**She was pressed.** She (the donkey), herself.

**And pressed.** Others — Bil’am’s foot.

### 22:26

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-26|Numbers 22:26]]

**Adonoy’s angel passed further ahead.** Passing further before him, proceeding to place himself before him in a different place, as in, “he passed before them.” There is a Midrash Aggadah in Tanchuma: Why did he see fit to stop in three places? He showed him the signs of the patriarchs.

### 22:28

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-28|Numbers 22:28]]

**These three times (lit. legs).** He hinted to him: “You seek to uproot a people which celebrates three pilgrimages a year?”

### 22:29

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-29|Numbers 22:29]]

**You ridiculed.** This follows Targum [Onkelos]. The word denotes insult and humiliation.

**Would that I had a sword in my hand.** This matter was extremely disparaging for him in the eyes of the dignitaries. This man is en route to kill an entire nation verbally, yet, for this donkey, he requires weapons of war!

### 22:30

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-30|Numbers 22:30]]

**Was it ever my habit.** This follows Targum [Onkelos]. Similarly, “does man benefit God by learning”? The Rabbis extrapolate this passage in the Talmud: They (the dignitaries) said to him, “Why are you not riding a horse”? He (Bil’am) said to them, ”I sent it out to pasture,” etc., as stated in Maseches Avodah Zarah.

### 22:32

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-32|Numbers 22:32]]

**Because your way is contrary to me.** Our Rabbis, the sages of the Mishnah, expound this as an acronym: “She was afraid [of me], she saw [me], and she turned, because the course is against me,” as if to say, “to arouse my vengefulness, and to anger me.” According to the plain interpretation, “because the course hurried against me.” The word [יָרַט] denotes hurrying. Because I saw that the traveler along the course (Bil’am) hastened and hurried on the course to anger and defy me. This is a shortened passage, similar to “Dovid longed.” Another interpretation: יָרַט denotes desire. Similarly, “through the wicked, He strives for my good will,” He attempts to appease and comfort me through the wicked, who only anger me.

### 22:33

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-33|Numbers 22:33]]

**Had she not turned aside.** Like לוּלֵא, “if not.” Sometimes אוּלַי, (lit. “perhaps”) is used to connote לוּלֵא, “if not.”

**I would surely have killed you.** This verse should be rearranged, to read “I would also have killed you.” As if to say, ”Not only would you have been restrained by me, but you also would have been killed.”

**And she, I would let live.** But now, because she spoke and admonished you, and you were unable to withstand her admonishment as it is written, “He said, ‘No”’ — I killed her, so that it should not be said, “this is the one that drove away Bil’am with her admonishment, and he was unable to reply.” For the Omnipresent is solicitous with human dignity. Similarly, “you shall kill the woman and the animal.” Similarly, “kill the animal.”

### 22:34

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-34|Numbers 22:34]]

**Because I did not know.** This, too, was disparaging, but he was forced to concede. He had boasted that he could divine the thoughts of Heaven, but his own mouth bore witness, “I did not know.”

**If it is wicked in your eyes, I shall return.** This reply was an attack against the Omnipresent. He (Bil’am) said to him, “He Himself commanded me to go, yet you, an angel, are nullifying His words. This is customary with Him — He says something, and an angel withdraws it. He said to Avrohom, ‘please take your son, etc.’ then nullified His words through an angel. I, too, if it is wicked in your eyes, must return.”

### 22:35

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-35|Numbers 22:35]]

**Go with the men.** A man is led along the path he wishes to follow.

**Go with the men.** Since your portion is with them — in the end you will perish from the earth.

**But.** In spite of your wishes, “the word that I will speak.”

**With Balak’s dignitaries.** He reveled in cursing them as much as they.

### 22:36

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-36|Numbers 22:36]]

**Balak heard.** He dispatched emissaries to inform him.

**To the city of Moav.** To his metropolis, his most prominent city, as if to say, “Look at what they are trying to uproot”!

### 22:37

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-37|Numbers 22:37]]

**Am I really incapable of honoring you?** He prophesied that he would, in the end, depart from him in disgrace.

### 22:39

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-39|Numbers 22:39]]

**Kiryas Chutzos** The city filled with market places, with men, women, and children in its outer areas. As if to say, “observe, and take pity, so that they will not be uprooted.”

### 22:40

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-40|Numbers 22:40]]

**Cattle and sheep.** A small amount — only one of the cattle, and one sheep.

### 22:41

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-41|Numbers 22:41]]

**Bamos Ba’al.** As Targum [Onklos] renders, “To the heights of his deity.” [Ba’al is] the name of an idol.

## Chapter 23

### 23:3

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-3|Numbers 23:3]]

**Perhaps, Adonoy will appear to me.** He does not usually speak with me by day.

**He went away alone.** As Targum [Onkelos] translates, “alone.” The word denotes placidity and quiet, as nothing but silence accompanied him.

### 23:4

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-4|Numbers 23:4]]

**Chanced to appear.** The word denotes a casual occurrence; it denotes disgrace; it denotes the uncleanness of seminal emission. As if to say, “reluctantly and in humiliation.” He appeared to him by day only in order to display the preciousness of Bnei Yisroel.’

**The seven altars.** “I have arranged seven altars” is not written here, but אֶת־שִׁבְעַת הַמִּזְבְּחֹת . He (Bil’am) said before Him: “Their patriarchs built seven altars before you, so I arranged [altars] to correspond with all of them. Avrohom built four: “He built an altar there to God, who had appeared to him.” “From there, he moved to the mountain, etc.,” “Avraham pitched his tent, etc.,” and one on Mount Moriah. Yitzchak built one: “He built an altar there, etc.” Ya’akov built two: One in Shechem, and one in Beis El.

**And I offered an ox and a ram on the altar.** But Avraham only offered up a single ram.

### 23:7

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-7|Numbers 23:7]]

**Curse Yaakov for me; Go, invoke wrath against Yisroel.** He instructed him to curse them with both names, in case one of them was not the principal one.

### 23:8

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-8|Numbers 23:8]]

**How can I curse, when God has not cursed.** When they deserved to have been cursed, they were not cursed. When their father mentioned their sin, “For they killed a man in their rage,” he cursed only their rage, as it is said, “Cursed is their rage.” When their father entered deceitfully to his father, he deserved to have been cursed. What is said there? “Let him also be blessed.” Of those who were to declare blessings it is said, “these [tribes] shall stand to bless the people.” Of those who were to declare curses, is not said, “these shall stand to curse the people,” but “these shall stand at the curse” — He did not want to invoke the word “curse” against them.

**If Adonoy has not been angry.** I have no power, except that I know how to pinpoint the moment when the Holy One, Blessed is He, is angry. But He was not angry during all those days that I came to you. This is why it is said, “My people, remember, now, what [Balak] advised, etc., and what Bil’am replied to him, etc., so that you may know God’s righteousness.”

### 23:9

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-9|Numbers 23:9]]

**For I view it from the mountain peaks (lit. head of the mountains).** I gaze at their origins, the beginnings of their roots, and I perceive that they are well founded and powerful, like the mountains and the hills, because of the patriarchs and the matriarchs.

**Behold! A people which shall dwell alone.** This was acquired for them by their forefathers — to dwell alone, as rendered by Targum [Onkelos].

**And will not be reckoned among the nations.** As Targum [Onkelos] explains, they will not be annihilated with the other nations, as it is said, “for I shall annihilate all the nations, etc.” They will not be reckoned with the rest. Another interpretation: When they rejoice, no nation rejoices with them, as it is said, “God shall give them repose alone.” But when the nations prosper, they partake with each of them, without debiting the balance of their account. This is [the meaning of] “and will not be reckoned among the nations.”

### 23:10

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-10|Numbers 23:10]]

**Who can count the dust of Yaakov.** As Targum [Onkelos] interprets, “the children of the house of Yaakov, etc., . . . of the four camps” — of the four banners. Another interpretation [of] the dust of Yaakov, One cannot reckon the commandments which they perform with dust. “Do not plow with an ox and a donkey,” “do not plant *kilayim,*” the dust of the [red] cow, the dust of the *sotah,* and similar ones.

**Or count the seed of Yisroel.** The seed which emanates during their intercourse.

**May my soul die the death of the righteous.** Among them.

### 23:13

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-13|Numbers 23:13]]

**Curse them for me.** The word denotes a command: “Curse them for me.”

### 23:14

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-14|Numbers 23:14]]

**Field of Tzofim.** This was an elevated area where a lookout was stationed to watch for forces which might approach the city.

**To the peak of the mountain.** Bil’am was not as great a sorcerer as Balak. Balak divined that Bnei Yisroel would suffer a severe breach there, for Moshe died there. He thought that the curse would descend upon them from there, that this is the breach that I divine.

### 23:15

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-15|Numbers 23:15]]

**I will be called here by chance.** By the Holy One, blessed is He. **I will be called by chance.** This denotes the reflexive usage.

### 23:16

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-16|Numbers 23:16]]

**And placed the word in his mouth.** What is this placing, and what would Scripture have been lacking if it had said, “return to Balak and say the following”? But when he heard that he was not permitted to curse, he said, “Why should I return to Balak merely to bring him grief”? So the Holy One, Blessed is He, placed a rein and a bit in his mouth, like a man who equips his animal with a bit to lead it wherever he wishes. He said to him, “You will be forced to return to Balak.”

### 23:17

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-17|Numbers 23:17]]

**Accompanied by the Moavite dignitaries.** Above it states “and *all* the Moavite dignitaries.” When they saw that it was hopeless, some departed, leaving only some others.

**What did Adonoy say?** This denotes derision, as if to say, “you are not your own master.”

### 23:18

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-18|Numbers 23:18]]

**Arise, Balak.** When he saw that he was ridiculing him, he intended to irritate him. “Stand on your feet! You are not permitted to remain seated while I serve as the Omnipresent’s emissary to you.”

**Son of (lit. his son) Tzipor.** This is a Scriptural usage, like “beasts (lit. his beasts) of the forest,” “beasts (lit. his beasts) of the earth,” “into a spring (lit. his spring) of water.”

### 23:19

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-19|Numbers 23:19]]

**God is not like man, etc.** He has already sworn to them to bring them and give them possession of the land of the seven nations, and you think that you can kill them in the wilderness?

**Would He say, etc.** The word denotes a question. Targum [Onkelos] translates “who retract and reconsider” — they retract, then reconsider whether to change their minds.

### 23:20

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-20|Numbers 23:20]]

**Behold I was commanded to bless.** You ask me, “what has God said”? I have received [instructions] from Him to bless them. **[Bless.** Similar “to bless.”]

**[When] He has blessed, I cannot reverse it.** He has blessed them, and I will not reverse his blessing.

**He has blessed.** As if it were וּבִרֵךְ . This is how the letter ר is conjugated, like אוֹיֵב חֵרֵף, as if it were חִרֵף . Similarly, וּבֹצֵעַ בֵּרֵךְ One who lauds and blesses the thief, saying “have no fear, you will not be punished, you will be at peace,” angers the Holy One, Blessed is He. But one cannot say that בֵּרֵךְ is a noun, for if so, it would be vocalized with a minor *patach* [*segol,* בֶּרֶךְ], with the accent above. However, because it connotes action, it is vocalized with a minor *komatz* [*tzayreh,* בֵּרֵךְ], with the accent below.

### 23:21

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-21|Numbers 23:21]]

**One does not see evildoers in Yaakov.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders. Another interpretation: By following its plain meaning, it may be expounded beautifully. The Holy One, Blessed is He, does not look at the evil in Yaakov. When they transgress His commandments, He does not pursue them exactingly by reflecting on the wickedness and wrongdoing of their violation of His doctrines.

**Transgression.** The word [עמל, (lit. tediousness)] denotes sin, as in “sin conceives,” “for you observe sinful and infuriating behaviour.” Because sinfulness is tedious before the Omnipresent.

**Adonoy, his God, is with him.** Even when they anger Him and are rebellious, He does not leave them.

**He has the King’s friendship.** The word denotes affection and friendship, like “David’s comrade” — one who loves David, “and he gave it to his fellow.” Similarly, Onkelos renders “the King’s Presence is among them.”

### 23:22

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-22|Numbers 23:22]]

**God Who has brought them out of Egypt.** You said, “behold, a nation came out of Egypt” — they did not leave by themselves, God delivered them.

**Has shown His great strength to them.** Like the power of His loftiness and elevation. Similarly, “and silver [shall be your] strength.” It denotes strength. Now I say that the word [תּוֹעֲפֹת] is from וְעוֹף יְעוֹפֵף. (“and let birds fly,”) someone who flies to a lofty elevation — this is a great application of power. וְתוֹעֲפֹת רְאֵם, lofty flight. Another interpretation [of] תוֹעֲפֹת רְאֵם, “the power of the *re’emim.*” The Rabbis explain that this refers to the demons and evil spirits.

### 23:23

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-23|Numbers 23:23]]

**For there is no sorcery in Yaakov.** For they deserve to be blessed, since there are no sorcerers or magicians among them.

**In due time, it will be said to Yaakov, etc.** There is destined to be another time such as this, when their preciousness will be revealed before everyone’s eyes, for they will be seated before Him studying the Torah from His mouth, and their partition will be further within, than the administering angels, who will ask them, “What has God done”? This is [the meaning of] what is said, “your eyes shall behold your master.” Another interpretation: יֵאָמֵר לְיַעֲקֹב does not refer to the future, but to the present. They have no need for magicians and sorcerers, for at any time when it is necessary that Yaakov and Yisroel be told what the Holy One, Blessed is He, has done, and what He has decreed on high, they do not perform magic or sorcery. Rather, they are told through their prophets what the Omnipresent’s decree is, or, the *Urim* and *Tummim* informs them. Onkelos, however, does not interpret this way.

### 23:24

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-24|Numbers 23:24]]

**Behold, a people that rises like a young lion, etc.** When they rise from their slumber in the morning, they are as vigorous as the young lion and the lion, to snatch the *mitzvos* wearing a *Tallis,* reading the *Shema,* and putting on the *Tefillin.*

**He does not lie down.** At night on his bed until he consumes and destroys every harmful spirit which comes to attack him. How is this? He reads the bedtime *Shema,* and entrusts his soul in the hand of the Omnipresent. If a corps or army comes to attack them, the Holy One, Blessed is He, protects them — and fights their battles, and strikes them (the attackers) dead. Another interpretation of “behold, a people rises like a lion, etc.,” is as Targum [Onkelos] renders.

**And drinks the blood of its kill.** He prophesied that Moshe would not die until he would strike the Midianite kings dead, and he (Bil’am) would be killed with them, as it is said, “and Bil’am the son of Beor, the sorcerer, the Bnei Yisroel killed by the sword, with the others whom they killed.”

### 23:25

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-25|Numbers 23:25]]

**Also do not curse them.** The first “also” is added to the second “also,” and the second “also” to the first “also.” Similarly, “also to me, also to you, let him not be.” Similarly, “also the youth, also the virgin.”

### 23:27

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-27|Numbers 23:27]]

**And you will curse them for me.** This is not a command, like “curse them,” but the future tense. “Perhaps it will be proper in His eyes, and you will curse them for me, from there.” “*Malduras lui*” (you will curse them), in Old French.

### 23:28

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-28|Numbers 23:28]]

**To the peak of Pe’or.** Balak was a great sorcerer, and he saw that they were destined to be smitten through Pe’or, but he didn’t know in what way. He said, “Perhaps the curse will descend on them from there.” It is similar with all the star gazers — they see, but do not know what they see.

## Chapter 24

### 24:1

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-1|Numbers 24:1]]

**Bil’am saw that it was favorable, etc.** He said, “it is no longer necessary for me to test the Holy One, Blessed is He, for He will never want to curse them.

**He did not approach, as at each time.** As he had done twice.

**Towards sorcery.** To divine whether God would perhaps chance to appear to him as he wished. He said, “Whether or not He wants to curse them, I will mention their sins, so that the curse will descend with the mention of their sins.”

**He set his face toward the wilderness.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders.

### 24:2

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-2|Numbers 24:2]]

**Bil’am raised his eyes.** He strove to induce the evil eye in them. Here you have his three characteristics, the evil eye, false vanity, and greed, as stated above.

**Encamped according to its tribes.** He saw each tribe dwelling by itself, without mingling. He saw that their entrances did not face each other, so that one should not look into his neighbor’s tent.

**And the spirit of God rested upon him.** It occurred to him not to curse them.

### 24:3

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-3|Numbers 24:3]]

**The son of (lit. his son was) Be’or.** Like לְמַעְיְנוֹ מָיִם, “into a spring of water.” According to Midrash Aggadah: Both were greater than their forefathers; “Balak, his son Tzipor” — his father was his son in royalty, and Bil’am was greater than his father in prophecy, he was a coin, the son of half a coin.

**Of the open-socketed eye.** His eye had been put out and removed, and its socket appeared open. The word [שְׁתֻם] is in the Mishnah; ”enough for it to have been opened, sealed, and dried.” The Rabbis remark, because he said, “He counts the seed of Yisroel” — that the Holy One, Blessed is He, sits and counts the seed of the Israelites, awaiting the drop from which a saint will be born, he thought, “the One who is Holy, and whose ministering angels are holy, looks at these things”? For this, Bil’am’s eye was blinded. However, some interpret שְׁתֻם הָעָיִן as “of the open eye,” as Targum [Onkelos] renders. And since he said “of the open eye,” but did not say “of the open eyes,” we learn that he was blind in one of his eyes.

### 24:4

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-4|Numbers 24:4]]

**Fallen, with opened eyes.** The plain interpretation is like Targum [Onkelos], that He (God) did not reveal Himself to him except at night, when he was lying down. According to Midrash, when He revealed Himself to him, he did not have the strength to stand on his feet, but fell to his face, because he was uncircumcised, and it was disgusting to be revealed to him when he stood upright before Him.

### 24:5

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-5|Numbers 24:5]]

**How goodly are your tents.** Because he saw that their entrances were not facing each other.

**Your dwelling places.** Your camps, as Targum [Onkelos] renders. Another interpretation of how goodly are your tents: How goodly is the tent of Shiloh and the Eternal Temple when they are inhabited, for sacrifices are offered in them to bring you forgiveness.

**Your dwelling places.** Even when they are desolate, since they are held as security for you, and their desolation brings atonement for your souls, as it is said, “Adonoy expended his fury” — and how did He expend it? “He ignited a fire in Tziyon.”

### 24:6

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-6|Numbers 24:6]]

**They stretch forth like streams.** Which extend and flow, stretching across the distance. The Rabbis remark, ‘from that wicked one’s blessings, we learn how he intended to curse them, when he decided to set his face towards the wilderness. When the Omnipresent overturned [the words of] his mouth, his blessings corresponded with his intended curses, etc.,’ as stated in “Chelek.”

**Like the aloe trees.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, meaning the same as “myrrh and aloes.”

**That Adonoy has planted.** In the garden of Eden. Another interpretation of כַּאֲהָלִים נָטַע ה', like the heavens stretched taut like a tent [as it is said, “He stretched them taut like a tent to be dwelt in”].

**That Adonoy has planted,** The expression נְטִיעָה is found with relation to tents, as it is said, “he stretched taut his palatial tents.”

### 24:7

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-7|Numbers 24:7]]

**From his buckets.** From his wells. The explanation is as Targum [Onkelos] interprets.

**And his seed will have an abundant flow.** This denotes success, like a seed planted near water.

**His king will be greater than Agag.** Their first king shall conquer Agag, king of Amalek.

**And his monarchy shall be uplifted.** Yaakov’s [monarchy] more and more, as he (Shaul) shall be followed by Dovid and Shlomo.

### 24:8

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-8|Numbers 24:8]]

**God Who has brought him out of Egypt.** Who gave them this eminence? God, who delivered them from Egypt with his power and exaltedness, shall consume the nations who oppress him.

**Their bones.** Of the oppressors.

**He shall crush.** Menachem interprets this as denoting breaking. Similarly, “[wolves in the evening] who did not break any bones in the morning.” Similarly, “you shall crush its earthen vessels.” I say that the meaning is “bone.” He strips the surrounding flesh with his teeth, and the marrow from inside, leaving the bone exposed.

**And dip His arrows [in their blood].** Onkelos interprets “the oppressors’ half,” their portion, like בַּעֲלֵי חִצִּים, [translated as] מָרֵי פַלְגּוּתָא (meaning portion, or half), יִמְחָץ is similar, meaning the same as “she split [his skull], passing through his forehead” — they shall apportion their land. Also, this may be interpreted as actually denoting arrows. The arrows of the Holy One, Blessed is He, will יִמְחַץ in the blood of the oppressors, He shall dip and stain with their blood, as in, “that your foot may wade through blood” This is not a departure from the connotation of “wound,” as in “I have wounded,” since someone stained with blood appears to have been injured and wounded.

### 24:9

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-9|Numbers 24:9]]

**He crouches and lies like a lion.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, they will settle in their land with strength and power.

### 24:10

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-10|Numbers 24:10]]

**[He] clapped his hands.** He struck one against the other.

### 24:13

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-13|Numbers 24:13]]

**Transgress the word of Adonoy.** Here “*my*” God is not stated, as it was originally, for he knew that he was loathed by the Holy One, Blessed is He, and that he had been repudiated.

### 24:14

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-14|Numbers 24:14]]

**Am returning to my people.** I am now like the rest of my people — the Holy One, Blessed is He, had forsaken him.

**Come, I will advise you.** Concerning the action you should take. What was the advice? The God of these people hates promiscuity, etc., as stated in “Chelek.” The proof that Bil’am instigated his advice so that they would be ensnared into immorality is from Scripture’s declaration, “they were involved with the Israelites through Bil’am’s advice.”

**What this people will do to your people.** This is a shortened passage; I will advise you about ensnaring them, and I will tell you how they are destined to destroy Moav at the end of days — “and smash the great men of Moav.” Targum [Onkelos] explains where the Hebrew falls short.

### 24:16

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-16|Numbers 24:16]]

**And knows the thoughts of the Most Exalted.** To pinpoint the moment of His anger.

### 24:17

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-17|Numbers 24:17]]

**I see it.** I see the praise and greatness of Yaakov. Not, however, now, but at a later time.

**A star has gone forth.** As Targum [Onkelos] interprets. דָּרַךְ means the same as דָּרַךְ קַשְׁתּוֹ, “He hurled with His bow,” as a star travels like an arrow. In Old French, “*destente*” (“hurl”). As if to say, “a constellation shall rise.”

**A staff has arisen.** A king who subdues and rules.

**Smash the corners of Moav.** This refers to David, of whom it is said, “he laid them on the ground and measured two strings to kill, etc.”

**And impale.** The meaning is to penetrate, like, “I have excavated,” “the pit ax which quarried you” “let the ravens of the river gouge it.” “*Forjier*” (“pierce”), in Old French.

**All of the sons of Sheis.** All the nations, since they were all descended from Sheis, the son of Adam.

### 24:18

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-18|Numbers 24:18]]

**Seir shall become the possession of his enemies.** His enemies, the Bnei Yisroel.

### 24:19

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-19|Numbers 24:19]]

**A ruler shall come from Yaakov.** Yet another ruler shall come from Yaakov.

**Who will annihilate the survivors of the city.** Of the most prominent city of Edom, Rome. He said this concerning King Mashiach, of whom it is said, “he shall rule from sea to sea,” “there will be no survivors of the house of Eisav.”

### 24:20

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-20|Numbers 24:20]]

**[Bil’am] Saw Amalek.** He gazed at Amalek’s punishment.

**Amalek was the first among nations.** He preceded them all in waging war against Israel. This, too, is Targum’s [Onkelos] interpretation. **And his end.** Will be to perish by their hand, as it is said, “you shall obliterate the memory of Amalek.”

### 24:21

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-21|Numbers 24:21]]

**He saw the Keinite.** Because the Keinite was encamped near Amalek, as in the incident of which it is said, “Shaul said to the Keinite, etc.,” he mentioned him after Amalek. He gazed at the greatness of Yisro’s descendants, of whom it is said, ”Tirasites, Shimasites, Sochasites.”

**How powerful is your dwelling place.** I wonder how you merited this. Were you not with me in counseling “come, let us deal shrewdly with them”? Yet now you have settled yourself in the power and strength of Israel.

### 24:22

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-22|Numbers 24:22]]

**For, if Kayin is to be uprooted, etc.** How fortunate you are to be encamped in this stronghold, so that you will no longer be driven from the world. For, even if you are destined to be exiled with the ten tribes, and will be uprooted from the place where you settled, what of it?

**Until where (lit. what) will Ashur take you captive.** Until where will he exile you? Perhaps to Chalach and Chavor? This is not considered being driven from the world, but being shifted from place to place, and you shall return with the other exiles.

### 24:23

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-23|Numbers 24:23]]

**He set forth his parable, etc.** Since he mentioned the captivity by Ashur, he said: Alas! who can survive from God’s devastation Who is capable of self-survival against His putting these — so that He shall not put them [into effect] against him — He who decrees (God) — these? Sancheriv shall arise and commingle all the nations. Furthermore, Warships will come from Kittim — Kitites, meaning Romans, shall go forth in great ships against Ashur.

### 24:24

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-24|Numbers 24:24]]

**And cause suffering to those at the other side.** Shall cause suffering for those at the other side of the river.

**They, too, shall be lost for eternity.** Similarly, Doniel explains, “until the animal was killed, and its body destroyed.”

**Warships.** Great ships, as it is written, וְצִי אַדִּיר, rendered by Targum as “great ships.”

## Chapter 25

### 25:1

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-1|Numbers 25:1]]

**In Shittim.** This was its name.

**Acting promiscuously with the daughters of Moav.** As a result of Bil’am’s advice, as stated in “Chelek.”

### 25:2

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-2|Numbers 25:2]]

**And prostrated themselves before their gods.** When he was overcome by lust, and asked her to submit to him, she drew a statue of Pe’or from her bosom toward him and said, ‘Prostrate yourself before this’!

### 25:3

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-3|Numbers 25:3]]

**Pe’or.** Because they bared, their buttocks before it and relieved themselves — this is the way it was worshipped.

**The rage of Adonoy flared against Yisroel.** He sent a plague on them.

### 25:4

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-4|Numbers 25:4]]

**Take all the leaders of the people.** To pass judgement on those who worshipped Pe’or.

**And hang them.** The worshippers.

**וְהוֹקַע .** This refers to hanging, as we find concerning the sons of Shaul, “and let us hang them for [the sake of] God.” and there, hanging is specified, idolatry is [punished] with stoning, and all those stoned are hung.

**Facing the sun.** In full public view. Midrash Aggadah: The sun identifies the sinners. The cloud rolls back from the area above him, and the sun shines on him.

### 25:5

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-5|Numbers 25:5]]

**Each of you must execute his men.** Each of the Israelite judges executed two men, and there were eighty eight thousand Israelite judges, as stated in Sanhedrin.

### 25:6

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-6|Numbers 25:6]]

**Behold, a man came from among Bnei Yisroel.** The tribe of Shimon gathered around Zimri, who was their leader, and said to him, “We are being sentenced to death, and you sit by, etc.,” as stated in “Eilu Hein Hanisrafin.”

**The Midianite woman.** Kozbi, daughter of Tzur.

**Before the eyes of Moshe.** They said to him, “Moshe, is she forbidden or permitted? If you say ‘forbidden,’ who permitted the daughter of Yisro to you? etc.” as stated there.

**Who were weeping.** The *halachah* was concealed from him [that anyone having relations with a gentile woman is to be executed by the zealous], so they all burst into tears. At the golden calf, Moshe stood against six hundred thousand, as it is said, “he ground it into powder, etc.,” yet here he was enfeebled? But this was in order that Pinchas might come and take what was appropriately his.

### 25:7

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-7|Numbers 25:7]]

**Pinchas. . .saw.** He saw the incident and remembered the *halachah.* He said to Moshe, “I received from you [the teaching] that anyone having relations with a gentile woman is to be executed by the zealous.” He replied to him, “Let the one who reads the correspondence serve as its executor.” Instantly, “he took a spear in his hand, etc.”

### 25:8

[[Torah 40 - Balak#^25-8|Numbers 25:8]]

**אֶל הַקֻבָּה .** Into the tent.

**Through her belly.** As in “the jaws and the uterus.” He struck precisely at Zimri’s male organ and her female organ, so everyone saw that he did not kill them needlessly. Many miracles were performed for him, etc., as stated there.
