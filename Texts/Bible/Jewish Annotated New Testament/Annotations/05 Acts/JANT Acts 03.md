# Acts 3 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Acts 02|← Acts 2]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Acts 04|Acts 4 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Acts 03|NRSVue]] · [[Acts 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Acts - Introduction]]

### 3.1–10: Peter heals a lame man

*[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 226* · An example of *wonders and signs* (2.43) and a continuation of the healings performed by Jesus ([[Luke 05#^5-17|Lk 5.17–26]]), presented as a sign of his messianic identity ([[Luke 07#^7-18|Lk 7.18–23]]). ^3-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Three o’clock*, the hour of the evening daily offerings ([[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^29-39|Ex 29.39]]; [[Torah 25 - Tzav#^6-13|Lev 6.20]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 14.65) and prayer ([[LXX Judith 09#^9-1|Jdt 9.1]]). ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Beautiful Gate*, otherwise unattested, possibly a reference to the impressive bronze Nicanor Gate (Josephus, *J.W.* 5.201; *m. Midd.* 1.4; 2.3). ^3-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-4|4]]:** *Intently*, typical practice during healing (13.9; 14.9). ^3-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-6|6]]:** *Name of Jesus Christ*, the power of Jesus, not the apostles, enables the miracle (3.16; 4.10,12,17,18,30; 16.18; 19.13). ^3-6

### 3.11–26: Peter’s speech in Solomon’s Portico

*[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-11|Text]], p. 227* · As with the giving of the Spirit (2.15), the crowd’s misperception prompts Peter’s teaching. ^3-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Solomon’s Portico*, eastern part of the Temple Mount (Josephus, *Ant.* 15.401; 20.221). ^3-11-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-13|13]]:** *God of Abraham . . . Isaac . . . Jacob*, see [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-6|Ex 3.6]],[[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-15|15]],[[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-16|16]]; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-5|4.5]]. The phrase, which begins the first benediction of the Amidah, here emphasizes that the God of the Jews has chosen Jesus. *Glorified his servant*, Jesus is identified as God’s servant (3.26; 4.27,30), possibly alluding to Isaiah’s suffering servant whom God vindicates and exalts ([[Isaiah 52#^52-13|Isa 52.13]]; [[Isaiah 53#^53-1|53.1–12]]); see “Scripture Fulfillment,” p. 727. *Handed over and rejected*, Jews are responsible for Jesus’ execution (2.23). *Pilate*, Roman prefect of Judea 26–36 CE. Luke-Acts regularly identifies Jews as those responsible for killing Jesus ([[Luke 23#^23-4|Lk 23.4]],[[Luke 23#^23-14|14–16]],[[Luke 23#^23-20|20–25]]; [[Acts 02#^2-22|Acts 2.22–23]],[[Acts 02#^2-36|36]]; [[Acts 03#^3-12|3.12–15]]; [[Acts 04#^4-8|4.8–10]]; [[Acts 05#^5-30|5.30]]; [[Acts 07#^7-51|7.51–52]]; [[Acts 10#^10-39|10.39]], but see [[Acts 04#^4-27|Acts 4.27]]). ^3-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-14|14]]:** *Righteous One*, attribute of Isaiah’s servant ([[Isaiah 53#^53-11|Isa 53.11]]), and possibly a messianic title (*1 En.* 38.2; 53.6). *Murderer*, Barabbas ([[Luke 23#^23-18|Lk 23.18–19]]). ^3-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Author*, Gk “archēgos,” often designating leaders of the people ([[Torah 14 - Vaera#^6-14|Ex 6.14]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^10-4|Num 10.4]]; [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^33-21|Deut 33.21]]), can also mean “founder” or “source” as here and elsewhere (5.31; see also [[Hebrews 02#^2-10|Heb 2.10]]; [[Hebrews 12#^12-2|12.2]]). *Witnesses*, see 1.8n. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-16|16]]:** *Faith in his name*, trust in his true being (“name” as communicating a person’s true nature). ^3-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Ignorance*, although ignorance can explain why Jews killed Jesus (7.60; 13.27), it can no longer justify their failure to recognize his messianic identity (17.30). ^3-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-18|18]]:** *Foretold through all the prophets*, this claim does not appear explicitly in the biblical prophets; Luke may be drawing on an interpretation of Isa 53 or [[Zechariah 12#^12-10|Zech 12.10]]; [[Zechariah 13#^13-7|13.7]]. *His Messiah would suffer*, Jesus’ death was part of divine plan ([[Luke 24#^24-25|Lk 24.25–27]],[[Luke 24#^24-46|46]]; [[Acts 17#^17-2|Acts 17.2–3]]; [[Acts 26#^26-22|26.22–23]]). ^3-18

> [!note] Jews and the Death of Jesus *(p. 227)*
> Luke-Acts presents Jews as primarily responsible for Jesus’ death. In Luke, the Roman governor Pilate had declared Jesus innocent ([[Luke 22#^22-4|Lk 22.4]]; [[Luke 23#^23-22|23.22]]) and was prepared to release him ([[Luke 23#^23-20|Lk 23.20]], [[Luke 23#^23-22|22]]), but Pilate acquiesced to the demands of the Jewish leaders and multitudes (23.18–25); this theme continues in Acts. At several points Peter accuses Jews of having killed Jesus (2.23,36; 31.5; 4.10; 5.30; 10.39), a charge repeated by Stephen (7.52)
>
> and Paul (13.27–29). At the same time, both the Gospel and Acts note that Jesus’ death happened according to divine plan (2.23; 3.18; 4.28; 13.28; see also [[Luke 24#^24-26|Lk 24.26]]) and that the Jews acted in ignorance (3.17; 13.27; see also [[Luke 23#^23-34|Luke 23.34]]). Neither claim is presented to exonerate the Jews or lessens their culpability. Their ignorance, rather than justify-ing their action, becomes inexcusable because they should have been able to discern God’s plan as set forth in the words of the prophets (13.27).

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-19|19]]:** *Repent . . . turn to God*, shown by accepting Jesus as Messiah (2.38). On the connection between repentance/turning and wiping out sins, see [[Isaiah 44#^44-22|Isa 44.22]]. ^3-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-20|20]]:** *Times of refreshing*, opportunity for renewal. ^3-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-21|21]]:** *Universal restoration*, Gk “apokatastasis”; reestablishing a previous, even original, condition (e.g., of the soul, Philo, *Heir* 293; of the land, Philo, *Decalogue* 164). *Through his holy prophets*, possible reference to [[Isaiah 56#^56-6|Isa 56.6–8]]. ^3-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-22|22–23]]:** *Prophet like me*, see [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15]],[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-18|18–19]]. By Luke’s time some Jews had come to interpret the “prophet-like-Moses” as a messianic figure (4Q175, and possibly 1QS 9.11). ^3-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Rooted out of the people*, [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-19|Deut 18.19]] states that any who reject the prophet-like-Moses will receive an unspecified judgment from God. The punishment indicated here has its closest parallel in [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-29|Lev 23.29]], but it is mentioned frequently as a response to transgressions (e.g., [[Torah 25 - Tzav#^7-20|Lev 7.20]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-29|18.29]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-3|20.3]],[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-18|18]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-29|23.29]]). Luke thus suggests that rejecting Jesus as Messiah results in exclusion from the people of God (see 4.12). ^3-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-24|24]]:** *Samuel*, recognized by some rabbis as the greatest of prophets (*y. Hag.* 2.27,77a). Samuel anointed David and thus inaugurated the Davidic line that stretches to Jesus (1 Sam 16.31). ^3-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-25|25]]:** *Descendants of the . . . covenant*, Jews are not the only covenantal partners since God’s blessings extend to “all the families of the earth” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Gen 12.3]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-18|18.18]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-18|22.18]]; [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^26-4|26.4]]). ^3-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 03#^3-26|26]]:** *First to you*, Jews will be followed by Gentiles in God’s plan for salvation. *Wicked ways,* from which Jews must repent (v 19). ^3-26
