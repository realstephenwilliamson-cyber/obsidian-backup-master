## About

A book built around a filmed conversation between Jacques Derrida and Bernard Stiegler about television and other teletechnologies, and what they do to memory, the archive, the event and political life.

- **Making:** Stiegler interviewed Derrida on 22 December 1993. Jean-Christophe Rosé filmed it under the auspices of the Institut National de l'Audiovisuel (INA). It was meant for television but never broadcast.
- **1996:** published in French by Éditions Galilée as *Échographies de la télévision. Entretiens filmés*.
- **2002:** English translation by Jennifer Bajorek, *Echographies of Television: Filmed Interviews*, Polity Press with Blackwell (Cambridge and Malden, MA). The ECU library catalogue gives viii + 174 pages; the MIT Press Bookstore listing gives 184.
- **Contents:** Wikipedia describes three parts: Derrida's "Artifactualities" (from a 1993 interview in *Passages*), the filmed interview (pp. 29–144), and Stiegler's essay "The Discrete Image" (first published in 1995). The ECU catalogue's contents note lists nine headings, from "Right of inspection" to "Vigilances of the unconscious", including "Spectrographies".
- **Ideas:** Derrida's "artifactuality": actuality, the news of the day, is always made through selection and editing. He asks for a critical culture of how actuality is produced. Stiegler argues that digital technology makes the image's discrete elements open to analysis, and calls for a culture of reception in which viewers analyze images rather than only consume them.
- **Why it matters:** the publisher stresses that the two improvised on camera, so the technology under discussion shaped the conversation itself. Wikipedia has no section on the book's reception.
