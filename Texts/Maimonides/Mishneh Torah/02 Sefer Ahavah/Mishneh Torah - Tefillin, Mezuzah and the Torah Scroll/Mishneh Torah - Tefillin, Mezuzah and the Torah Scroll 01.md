**1.** Four passages [of the Torah]:[^1] *Kadesh Li* and *V'hayah ki y'viacha Ado-nai* in the book of [[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-1|Exodus 13:1-10]] and[^2] 13:11-16) and *Shema* and *V'hayah im shamo'a* ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deuteronomy 6:4-9]]) and[^3] 11:13-21) should be written separately[^4] and covered with leather.[^5] They are called *tefillin*.[^6]  
They are placed on the head and tied[^7] on the arm.[^8] According to Torah law, even a mere point of one of the letters[^9] from these four passages prevents all of them from being acceptable. All four must be written in the proper manner.[^10] ^1-1

**2.** Similarly, if even one letter of the two passages[^11] contained in the *mezuzah*, *Shema* and *V'hayah im shamo'a* ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deuteronomy 6:4-9]] and 11:13-21), is lacking a point,[^12] it is not acceptable according to Torah law, which requires that they [each] be written in a perfect manner.[^13] Similarly, a Torah scroll which is lacking even one letter[^14] is unacceptable.[^15] ^1-2

**3.** There are ten requirements for *tefillin*.[^16] All of them are halachot transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai.[^17] It is necessary to fulfill them all. Therefore, if one made any changes with regard to them, the *tefillin* are not fit for use:[^18] Two of them involve their composition,[^19] and eight involve the coverings [placed around the passages] and the tying of their straps.[^20]  
These are the two that involve their composition:  
a) They must be written in ink;  
b)[^21][^22] They must be written on parchment. ^1-3

**4.** How is ink prepared? One collects the vapor of oils,[^23] of tar, of wax, or the like, [causes it to condense,][^24] and kneads it together with sap from a tree[^25] and a drop of honey. It is moistened extensively,[^26] crushed until it is formed into flat cakes, dried, and then stored.[^27]  
When one desires to write with it, one soaks [the cakes of ink][^28] in gallnut juice[^29] or the like and writes with it. Thus, if one attempts to rub it out, he would be able to.[^30]  
This is the ink with which it is most preferable to write scrolls, *tefillin*, and mezuzot.[^31] If, however, one wrote any of the three[^32] with gallnut juice[^33] or vitriol,[^34] which remains without being rubbed out, it is acceptable.[^35] ^1-4

**5.** If so,[^36] what was excluded by the halachah conveyed to Moses on Mount Sinai, which stated that it be written in ink?  
It excludes tints of other colors, such as red, green,[^37] and the like.[^38] If even one letter of a Torah scroll, *tefillin*, or mezuzot is in another tint or in gold,[^39] they are invalid.[^40] ^1-5

**6.** There are three types of parchment: *g'vil*,[^41] *k'laf*, and *duchsustos*.[^42]  
What is implied? The hide of a domesticated or wild animal is taken.[^43] First, the hair is removed from it.[^44] Afterwards, it is salted and then prepared with flour. Then resin and other substances which cause the skin to contract and become harder[^45] are applied to it. In this state, it is called *g'vil*.[^46] ^1-6

**7.** After the hair is removed, the hide may be taken and divided in half in the manner known to the parchment processors.[^47] Thus, there are two pieces of parchment: a thin one, which is on the side where the hair[^48] grew, and a thicker one, on the side of the flesh.  
After it has been processed using salt, then flour, and then resin and the like,[^49] the portion on the side where the hair grew is called *k'laf*[^50] and the portion on the side of the flesh is called *duchsustos*.[^51] ^1-7

**8.** It is a halachah transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai that a Torah scroll should be written on *g'vil*[^52] on the side on which the hair had grown.[^53] When *tefillin* are written on *k'laf*,[^54] they should be written on the side of the flesh.[^55] When a *mezuzah* is written on*duchsustos*,[^56] it should be written on the side of the hair.[^57]  
Whenever one writes on *k'laf* on the side of the hair[^58] or on *g'vil* or *duchsustos* on the side of the flesh,[^59] it is unacceptable.[^60] ^1-8

**9.** Although it is a halachah which was transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai, that if one wrote a Torah scroll on *k'laf*, it is acceptable.[^61] *G'vil* was mentioned only to exclude *duchsustos*. If a Torah scroll was written on the latter, it is not acceptable.[^62]  
Similarly, if a *mezuzah* was written on *k'laf* or on *g'vil*, it is acceptable.[^63] *Duchsustos* was mentioned only as a mitzvah.[^64] ^1-9

**10.** [Torah] scrolls, *tefillin*, and mezuzot[^65] may not be written on hide from a non-kosher animal, fowl, or wild animal.[^66] One may write on the hides of [all] kosher animals, wild beasts, and fowl.[^67] This applies even when these animals died without being ritually slaughtered or when they were killed by wild beasts.[^68]  
We may not write on the skin of a kosher fish because of the foul secretions, since the processing of the skin will not cause the foul secretions to cease.[^69] ^1-10

**11.** The *g'vil* for a Torah scroll and the *k'laf* for *tefillin* and for a Torah scroll must be processed with this purpose in mind.[^70] If they were not processed with this intent, they are not acceptable.[^71]  
Accordingly, if they were processed by a gentile, they are not acceptable. Even when [a Jew] instructed a gentile to process the parchment with the intent that it be used for a Torah scroll or for *tefillin*, it is not acceptable.[^72] The gentile follows his own intentions and not those of the person who hires him.[^73] Therefore,[^74] whenever an article must be made with a specific intent in mind,[^75] it is unacceptable if made by a gentile.[^76]  
[The parchment used for] a *mezuzah* need not be processed with this purpose in mind.[^77] ^1-11

**12.** It is a halachah transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai that a Torah scroll or *mezuzah* should be written only [on parchment] which has been ruled.[^78] [The parchment used for] *tefillin*, however, need not be ruled,[^79] because they are covered.[^80]  
It is permissible to write *tefillin* and mezuzot without [looking at] an existent text, because everyone is familiar with these passages.[^81] It is, however, forbidden to write even one letter of a Torah scroll without [looking at] an existent text.[^82] ^1-12

**13.** A Torah scroll, *tefillin*, or *mezuzah* written by an *apikoros*[^83] should be burned.[^84] If they were written by a gentile, an apostate Jew, a person who betrays [the Jews][^85] to a powerful person,[^86] a slave, a woman, or a minor,[^87] they are not acceptable[^88] and must be entombed,[^89] as [implied by [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-8|Deuteronomy 6:8-9]]]: "And you shall tie...[^90] and you shall write."[^91] [Our Sages[^92] explain that this includes only] those who are commanded to tie [*tefillin* on their arms][^93] and those who believe in what they write.[^94]  
[Sacred articles][^95] which are found in the possession of an *apikoros*, and it is not known who wrote them,[^96] should be entombed.[^97] Those which are found in the possession of a gentile[^98] are kosher. We should not, however, purchase Torah scrolls, *tefillin*, or mezuzot from gentiles for more than they are worth, so that they do not become accustomed to stealing them. ^1-13

**14.** A Torah scroll, *tefillin*, or *mezuzah* that was written on parchment from a non-kosher animal, beast, or fowl,[^99] or on parchment that was not processed [properly, is not acceptable].[^100] [Similarly,] a Torah scroll or *tefillin* that was written on parchment that was not processed with the intent to use it for these sacred purposes is not acceptable.[^101] ^1-14

**15.** When a person writes a Torah scroll, *tefillin*, or *mezuzah* without having [the proper] intention,[^102] should he write one of God's names without the desired intent,[^103] they are not acceptable.[^104]  
Therefore, when a person is writing God's name, he should not reply even if the king of Israel[^105] greets him.[^106] If he is writing two or three names,[^107] he may interrupt between them and reply.[^108] ^1-15

**16.** [When a scribe] dips his pen [in ink] to write God's name, he should not begin [writing] one of the letters of God's name.[^109] Rather, he should begin with the letter preceding [God's name].[^110]  
If [a scribe] forgot to write God's name in its entirety, he may insert it in between the lines.[^111] It is, however, unacceptable to have a portion of God's name on the line and a portion inserted [between the lines].[^112] With regard to other words, if one forgets, one may write half the word on the line and half above the line.[^113]  
When does the above apply? With regard to a Torah scroll.[^114] In contrast, with regard to *tefillin* and mezuzot, one should not insert even one letter[^115] [between the lines].[^116] Rather, if one forgets even one letter, one should entomb what one has written and write another one.[^117]  
It is permitted to write[^118] God's name on [parchment where letters] have been scraped off[^119] or rubbed out[^120] on all [of these sacred articles].[^121] ^1-16

**17.** Scribes who write Torah scrolls, *tefillin*, and mezuzot[^122] may not turn the parchment face down.[^123] Rather, they should spread a cloth over them or fold them.[^124] ^1-17

**18.** [The following rule applies when] a scribe who wrote a Torah scroll, *tefillin*, or *mezuzah* states: "I did not write the names of God with the proper intent."[^125] Once they have left his hand,[^126] his statements are not believed with regard to the disqualification of the scroll.[^127] They are, however, accepted to the extent that he must forfeit his entire wage.[^128]  
Why isn't he believed with regard to the disqualification of the scroll? Because it is possible that he wanted to cause a loss to the purchaser or to the person who hired him, thinking that[^129] with this statement all that he would be required to forfeit would be the payment for the names of God.[^130]  
Accordingly, were he to say that the parchment of this Torah scroll or *tefillin*[^131] was not processed with the proper intent in mind,[^132] his statements are accepted with regard to the disqualification of the sacred articles because, [by virtue of these statements,] he forfeits his entire wage.[^133] Everyone knows that if the parchments were not processed with the proper intent, he does not deserve any payment.[^134] ^1-18

**19.** Tefillin and mezuzot may be written only in Assyrian script.[^135] Permission was granted to write Torah scrolls[^136] in Greek as well.[^137] That Greek language has, however, been forgotten from the world. It has been confused and has sunk into oblivion.[^138] Therefore, at present, all three sacred articles may be written using Assyrian script alone.[^139]  
One must be precise while writing them, making sure that one letter does not become attached to another one, because any letter which is not surrounded by parchment[^140] on all four sides[^141] is unacceptable.[^142]  
Any letter that cannot be read by a child who is neither wise nor foolish[^143] is not acceptable.[^144] Therefore, one must be careful with regard to the form of the letters,[^145] so that a *yud* will not resemble a *vav*, nor a *vav* a *yud*;[^146] a *kaf* should not resemble a *beit*, nor a *beit* a *kaf*;[^147] a *dalet* should not resemble a *resh*, nor a *resh* a *dalet*.[^148]  
[The same applies in] other similar instances.[^149] [The text must be written in a manner] that a reader will be able to read without difficulty.[^150] ^1-19

**20.** [The following rules apply to] parchment which has holes: One should not write over a hole.[^151] If, however, ink passes over the hole [without seeping through],[^152] the presence of the hole is of no consequence, and one may write upon it.[^153] Accordingly, if the skin of a fowl[^154] has been processed, it is permissible to write upon it.[^155]  
[The following rules apply] when a parchment becomes perforated after[^156] it has been written on:[^157] If the perforation is within the inside of a letter[^158] - e.g., in the space inside a *heh*, inside a*mem*, or inside any of the other letters - it is acceptable.  
Despite the fact that a leg of a letter becomes perforated to the extent that it becomes separated [into two portions], it is acceptable if:  
a) [the length of[^159] the leg][^160] is equivalent to that of a small letter;[^161] and[^162]  
b) the letter's [present form][^163] does not resemble another letter.[^164]  
If [the length of[^165] the leg][^166] is not equivalent to that of a small letter, it is not acceptable. ^1-20
