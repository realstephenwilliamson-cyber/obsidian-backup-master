### 22.1–14: Parable of the wedding banquet

*[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-1|Text]], p. 51* · ([[Luke 14#^14-15|Lk 14.15–24]]). See 21.33–41; *Gos. Thom.* 64. ^22-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-2|2]]:** *Wedding banquet*, kingdom of heaven (25.1–13); in *b. Shabb.* 153a, those who are prepared for the king’s banquet will bask in its pleasures; those who are not will suffer. ^22-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-3|3]]:** *Slaves*, see 21.34n. ^22-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-5|5]]:** *Made light of it*, a gross insult to the host. ^22-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-6|6]]:** *Killed them*, the tenants move from insult to rebellion. ^22-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-7|7]]:** *Burned their city*, a reference to Jerusalem’s destruction; Israel’s prophets predicted the destruction of the First Temple ([[Isaiah 05#^5-24|Isa 5.24–25]]; [[Jeremiah 04#^4-5|Jer 4.5–8]]). ^22-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-10|10]]:** *Whom they found*, for Matthew, Jew and Gentile (see, e.g., 13.47). ^22-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-11|11–12]]:** *Wedding robe*, garment representing righteous deeds ([[Romans 13#^13-12|Rom 13.12]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-27|Gal 3.27]]; [[Revelation 03#^3-4|Rev 3.4–5]],[[Revelation 03#^3-18|18]]; [[Revelation 06#^6-11|6.11]]; [[Revelation 07#^7-13|7.13–14]]; [[Revelation 19#^19-8|19.8]]; [[Revelation 22#^22-14|22.14]]). ^22-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-13|13]]:** *Outer darkness*, 8.12; 25.30. ^22-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-14|14]]:** *Few are chosen*, not everyone will be saved (see *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 8.3, “Many have been created, but few will be saved.” See also *4 Ezra* 9.15; *b. Men*. 29b). ^22-14

### 22.15–22: Taxes to Caesar

*[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-15|Text]], p. 51* · ([[Mark 12#^12-13|Mk 12.13–17]]; [[Luke 20#^20-20|Lk 20.20–26]]). See *Gos. Thom*. 100; see also 17.24–27. ^22-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-16|16]]:** *Herodians*, political group supporting Rome’s client rulers. ^22-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-17|17]]:** *Pay taxes to the emperor*, Rome levied an annual census tax of one denarius, as well as other taxes ([[Luke 02#^2-1|Lk 2.1–2]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 1.154; 2.118; Tacitus, *Ann*. 2.42). Tax collectors and tax collecting were associated with sinfulness (9.10; [[Luke 03#^3-12|Lk 3.12–13]]; *m. Sanh*. 3.3; *b. Sanh*. 25b). ^22-17

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-18|18]]:** *Putting me to the test*, see 4.7; 16.1; 19.3; 22.35. *Hypocrites*, see 6.2,5,16; 15.7; 23 *passim*; 24.51. ^22-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-19|19]]:** *Denarius*, a day’s wage; see 20.2n. ^22-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-21|21]]:** *Give . . . to the emperor*, Jesus asks the interlocutors to determine for themselves what belongs to God (see 17.27n.; [[Romans 14#^14-13|Rom 14.13–23]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|1 Cor 9.19–23]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-17|1 Pet 2.17]]; contrast Rev, particularly ch 13). ^22-21

### 22.23–33: Questions about resurrection

*[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-23|Text]], p. 51* · ([[Mark 12#^12-18|Mk 12.18–27]]; [[Luke 20#^20-27|Lk 20.27–40]]). ^22-23

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-23|23]]:** *There is no resurrection*, Sadducees denied the concept of bodily resurrection ([[Acts 04#^4-1|Acts 4.1–2]]; [[Acts 23#^23-6|23.6–10]]). See 3.7n. and “Jewish Movements of the NT Period,” p. 614. ^22-23-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-25|25]]:** *Leaving the widow to his brother*, see 1.3n.; [[Torah 09 - Vayeshev#^38-8|Gen. 38.8]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-5|Deut 25.5–10]]; *no resurrection* Josephus, *Ant*. 4.254–56; *m. Yebam.* 2.2. ^22-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-28|28–30]]:** *Whose wife of the seven will she be,* the Sadducees attempt to point out the ridiculousness of bodily resurrection (see *2 Bar.* 51.10–11; *1 En*. 39.5; 69.11; *b. Ber*. 17a); Josephus, *J.W.* 3.374 states that the righteous dead in the world to come “dwell in chaste bodies.” ^22-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-32|32]]:** *God of Abraham . . . Jacob*, the Torah emphasizes the eternal nature of God’s relationship with Israel ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-6|Ex 3.6]],[[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-15|15–16]]; [[I Chronicles 29#^29-18|1 Chr 29.18]]; [[II Chronicles 30#^30-6|2 Chr 30.6]]; see also *2 En*. 22.3–14; 62.15–16; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-9|2 Macc 7.9]],[[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-36|36]]). ^22-32

### 22.34–40: The greatest commandment

*[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-34|Text]], p. 52* · ([[Mark 12#^12-28|Mk 12.28–34]]; [[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25–29]]). ^22-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-35|35]]:** *Lawyer*, Gk “nomikos”; the word occurs only here in Matthew, and in [[Luke 07#^7-30|Lk 7.30]]; [[Luke 10#^10-25|10.25]]; [[Luke 11#^11-45|11.45]],[[Luke 11#^11-46|46]],[[Luke 11#^11-52|52]]; [[Luke 14#^14-3|14.3]]; Titus 3.13. Except for Titus, where “Zenas the lawyer” seems to be a member of the community, lawyers appear only in the Gospels to test Jesus. *Test*, see 4.7n. ^22-35

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-36|36]]:** *Which commandment*, the traditional number of commandments in rabbinic literature is 613; some attempted to epitomize the Law (see, e.g., *b. Makk*. 23b; *b. Yebam*. 47b, citing [[Micah 6#^6-8|Mic 6.8]] and other verses). ^22-36

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-37|37]]:** *You shall love the Lord*, [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]]. [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4–5]] is the “Shema,” the basic affirmation of Jewish belief. ^22-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-39|39]]:** *Love your neighbor*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; *m. Avot* 1.2, “The world stands on three things: Torah, the service of God, and deeds of kindness”; see [[Romans 13#^13-9|Rom 13.9]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-14|Gal 5.14]]; [[James 02#^2-8|Jas 2.8]]. ^22-39

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-40|40]]:** *Hang all the law*, see 7.12n. Jesus combines two commandments (love of God, love for humanity) into one “greatest” commandment. This combination is found as well in earlier Jewish sources (e.g., *T. Iss*. 7.6, “I loved the Lord and every man with whole heart”; see also the later *T. Dan* 5.3). ^22-40

### 22.41–46: Jesus questions the Pharisees

*[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-41|Text]], p. 52* · ([[Mark 12#^12-35|Mk 12.35–37]]; [[Luke 20#^20-41|Lk 20.41–44]]). ^22-41

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-42|42]]:** *Son of David*, see 1.1n.; 9.27n. ^22-42

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-44|44]]:** See [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]; see also [[Acts 02#^2-34|Acts 2.34–35]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-25|1 Cor 15.25]]; [[Hebrews 01#^1-13|Heb 1.13]]; [[Hebrews 10#^10-12|10.12–13]]. *The Lord said to my Lord*, in Ps 110, the first usage refers to God, the second to David. ^22-44

**[[NRSVue Matthew 22#^22-45|45]]:** Jesus implicitly claims he is superior to David, since in his interpretation, David (the presumed author of the Psalm) calls the Messiah “Lord.” ^22-45
