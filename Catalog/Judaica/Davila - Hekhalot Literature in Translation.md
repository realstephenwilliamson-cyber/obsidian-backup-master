## About

Hekhalot (palaces) literature is a body of Jewish esoteric texts in Hebrew and Aramaic about ascent to the heavenly palaces and visions of the divine throne-chariot (merkavah) of Ezekiel 1. James R. Davila's volume translates its major texts into English.

- **Date and place:** Wikipedia says the texts were produced between late antiquity and the early Middle Ages; some date them to Talmudic times or earlier, and some scholars place them in Palestine. Davila's St Andrews record says late antique and early medieval. Britannica says merkavah mysticism began to flourish in 1st-century Palestine and had its centre in Babylonia from the 7th to the 11th century.
- **Main texts:** *Hekhalot Rabbati* (Greater Palaces), the ascent of Rabbi Ishmael; *Hekhalot Zutarti* (Lesser Palaces), Rabbi Akiva's ascent through the seven heavens; *Ma'aseh Merkavah*, hymns of those who go down to the chariot; *Merkavah Rabba*; and *3 Enoch*. Related works include *Shi'ur Qomah* and *Sefer HaRazim*. Britannica calls the Lesser and Greater texts the oldest literary sources of merkavah mysticism.
- **Features:** the Kedushah prayer and hymns built on it, perhaps used to induce trance (Wikipedia); a dangerous passage through seven heavenly dwellings past angelic gatekeepers, placated with magical seals (Britannica). Wikipedia calls the literature post-rabbinic but seeking continuity with rabbinic literature, hence its frequent pseudepigraphy.
- **Scholarship:** Wikipedia cites Gershom Scholem (1965), Peter Schäfer (1992) and Rachel Elior (1993), among others, and reports Davila's view that the merkavah mystics may have been a kind of shaman.
- **Davila's edition:** *Hekhalot Literature in Translation: Major Texts of Merkavah Mysticism* (Brill, Leiden, 2013; Supplements to the Journal of Jewish Thought and Philosophy 20; 443 pages). It translates Hekhalot Rabbati, Sar Torah, Hekhalot Zutarti, Ma'aseh Merkavah and Merkavah Rabba, with shorter texts and Geniza fragments.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says many motifs of later Kabbalah are based on these texts.
