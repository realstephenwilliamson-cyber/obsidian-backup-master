The fifth *āvaraṇa* (*panchamāvaraṇam*) consists of the outer 10 triangles (*bahirdasara*). It is the *sarvārthasādhaka cakra*, the “wheel that bestows all wealth.”

These triangles represent the five sensory and five motor organs of an individual, all of them avenues through which our minds can be disturbed. To attain yogic states, the mind must be decoupled from these disturbing sensory inputs.

The *bīja saḥ* means the perfume that maddens. This whole world that we see around us is created out of memories. It is only a vague reflection of the truth. It is imaginary. Just as a dream dissolves when we awaken from it, so too does this world dissolve when we awaken into a state called samadhi. The knowledge that the world is temporary, like a dream or a thought, and that being attached to it can only bring us misery, moves us up and out of the Mūlādhāra and Svādhiṣṭhāna Chakras. It is what we call *kulōttīrṇa*.<sup>419</sup> 419  *Lalitā Sahasranāma*, *Nāma* 

714\. “She who transcends all internal and external senses.”
