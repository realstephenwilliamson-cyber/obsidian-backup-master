# Crisis of Attestation

> [!abstract] Definition
> Paul Ricoeur’s concept of *attestation*—the fundamental self-trust and interpersonal credibility that "I can act, I can recount, I am trustworthy"—applied to collective institutional life, and its contemporary breakdown in digital information systems.

## Hermeneutics of Attestation
In *Oneself as Another* (1992), Ricoeur identifies attestation as the middle way between Cartesian certainty and Nietzschean suspicion: it is an assurance, a testimony of fidelity that cannot be mathematically proven but is maintained through witness and action.

## Collapse in Algorithmic Culture
In [[Cybernetic Anamnesis text 2025-11-05|Cybernetic Anamnesis]]:
- When cultural transmission shifts from bodily witness to black-box algorithmic feeds, citizens cannot extend attestation to systems whose selection rules are covert and proprietary.
- Suspicion radicalizes into total paranoia: every document may be a deepfake; every testimony may be bot-generated; every institution is suspected of bad faith.
- Without attestation, civil society collapses into endless epistemic civil war.
