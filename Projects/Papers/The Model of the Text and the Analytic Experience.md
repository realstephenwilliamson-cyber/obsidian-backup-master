The Model of the Text and the Analytic Experience

Χρεὼ δέ σε πάντα πυθέσθαι ἠµέν Ἀληθείης εὐκυκλέος ἀτρεµὲς ἦτορ ἠδὲ βροτῶν δόξας, ταῖς οὐκ ἔνι πίστις ἀληθής.

Ἀλλ΄ ἔµπης καὶ ταῦτα µαθήσεαι, ὡς τὰ δοκοῦντα χρῆν δοκίµως εἶναι διὰ παντὸς πάντα περῶντα.[^1]

1

In the lecture entitled *Narrative: Its Place in Psychoanalysis* before asking the question “What is it that distinguishes the analytic fact from all other facts?” Ricoeur (writes/says):

> Currently, I am trying to reinterpret psychoanalysis by taking as my starting point, not the theory, but what happens in analytic experience itself, that is, what happens in the relation between analysand and analyst, in particular in the transference phase.[^2]

Ricoeur goes on to offer an admission that “without having experienced the practice” he awaits “the corrections of those who do practice” and then proceeds to embark on a discussion intended to “reintroduce the narrative element into the structure of the analytic experience, by showing how it introduces itself into what I will call the criteriology of the analytic fact.”

So “What is it that distinguishes the analytic fact from all other facts?” A criteriology consisting of 4 elements. Analytic experience is brought to you by 1)presuppositions that “the deepest affects are not foreign to language,” 2)the dialogical structure of human desire, 3)a relation to reality and others constituted by an imaginary, deceptive illusion, 4)the narrative dimension.

But first, what are other facts? (Alternative Facts!?!)

Elsewhere Ricoeur writes, “strictly speaking, there are no facts in psychoanalysis, for the analyst does not observe, he interprets.”[^3] I think the key to the distinction between the analytic fact and all other facts lies in Ricoeur’s “fresh approach to the question of the relation between *erklaren* (explanation) and *verstehen* (understanding, comprehension) in the human sciences”[^4] offered by the Paradigm of Text-Interpretation. What are we to make of Dilthey’s “wrestling with this paradox” clarified “after having read Husserl's *Logical Investigations”* that:

> *Geisteswissenschaften* are sciences inasmuch as the expressions of life undergo a kind of objectification which makes possible a scientific approach somewhat similar to that of the natural sciences, in spite of the logical gap between *Natur* and *Geist*, factual knowledge and knowledge by signs. In this way the mediation offered by these objectifications appeared to be more important, for a scientific purpose, than the immediate meaningfulness of the expressions of life for everyday transactions.[^5]

I believe that which distinguishes this “logical gap between *Natur* and *Geist*, factual knowledge and knowledge by signs” is the same as that between “the analytic fact and all other facts.” In other words as the 4 structural elements of the analytic experience coalesce in the narrative dimension “the model of the text” shows how the experience itself constitutes “meaningful action.”

Let us now examine our criteriology beside the features of the paradigm of text interpretation mainly:

> \(1\) the fixation of the meaning, (2) its dissociation from the mental intention of the author, (3) the display of non-ostensive references, and (4) the universal range of its addresses.

Because he says “these four traits taken together constitute the ‘objectivity’ of the text. From this ‘objectivity’ derives a possibility of explaining which is not derived in any way from another field, that of natural events, but which is congenial to this kind of objectivity
