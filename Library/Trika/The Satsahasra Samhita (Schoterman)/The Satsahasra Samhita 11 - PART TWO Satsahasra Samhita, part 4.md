Iniroduçiion

The first thing to note with regard to the present chapter of the SatSS is its aclual number. In the captions of this chapter we have deliberately left this question open by denoting it as ‘patala 4/5. The chapter preceding the present chapter is unmistakably the third: this number is found in the colophons of al) the three MSS. as well as at rhe beginning {rrfīya patala arambkah) and the end (ii satsāhasre tritfyah patalal) of the T. With regard to the chapter following the present chapter we see the same: both the MSS and the T agree that it is the sixth pajaia. This leaves us with the question which number should be assigned lo the present chapter, four or five. Either way, one chapter is missing. The three MSS agree in their respective colophons that the present chaper is the fifth, thus amitting a fourth patala. On the other hand the T, which commentis upto line 150 of the text, begins ns commentary with carurrha patala ārambhah, and finishes with iti sarsāhasre caturtkah patalah. Consequently, according to the T the present chapter should be the fourth, thus omitting a fifth chapter.

Whereas the three MSS of the SatSS do not seem to bother about this obvious incongruity in the sequence of their chapters, the T seems to be aware of it. Thus the author of the T begins his commentary on the sixth chapter with the words: tatha pañcame pajale yāvat pihavyāptih sasthe patale yāthā, indicating that the fifth chapter begins after what is called the pṛhavyápti, ‘the pervasion \[of Devi) into the \[five\] Pithas\*. In this way the fifth chapter might begin after š/foka 132 of the text, which ends the description of the five Pithas. There seems to be a break in the text at this point: the names of all the guardians are repeated and they are connected with different parts of the body or with some phystcal phenomenon. H is noteworthy in this regard that the strict division of the guardians over the fve Pithas as we saw it before, is abandoned. Thus the Ñrst five of the six guardians belonging to the frst Pitha are taken together and identified with the fingers of the right hand; the last of them joins the first four of the second Pitha as the five fingers of the left hand ete. Another indication for the assumption that after 132 a new part begins could be found in the fact that line 132 is only a half-š/oka, which appears often at the end of a section or chapter in a text.

The present chapter of the ṢatSS is the first of this text which has a direct relation to the KmT. Its contents are based on the second chapter of the KmT, and quite a few lines are almost literally repeated. As we shall see in the notes, the ṢṢatSS has added much to the text of the KmT. The quintessence of the present chapler in the Ṭat5S and of the second chapter of the KmT are the same: Devi goes around in Bhārata Varsa, visiting various places where She obtains daughters, sons and guardians. At first sight it is a derivation from kuja, ‘a place overrun with plants’. a meaning which does not, however, make any sense within the context, Likewise a word kuñjikā can be safely ruled out. \[n the context of the fact that we ‘miss’ three paths, the most sensible thing to do is to divide this trisyllabic word into three paris: kuñ, ja, and ka. In this way ku may refer to bhūia or bhuvana {ku = earth); ja, derived from the root jan-, refers to bhâva or pada; ka finally refers to raudra of kal, being the first syllable of the latter. Consequently all Six Paths are mentioned in these lines.
