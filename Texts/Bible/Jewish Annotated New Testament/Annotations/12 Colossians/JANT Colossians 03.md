# Colossians 3 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Colossians 02|← Colossians 2]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Colossians 04|Colossians 4 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Colossians 03|NRSVue]] · [[Colossians 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Colossians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-1|3.1–4.6]]:** **Paraenesis** (moral exhortation). NT letters, like other Greek letters, typically include moral exhortation tied to the letter’s situation. The virtues Colossians promotes, and the vices it condemns, are not connected to the situation in Colossae but reflect general moral concerns. ^3-1

### 3.1–4: Baptism confers new moral life

*[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 414* · The paraenesis picks up the theme of 2.12–14, that baptism allows believers to start a new life free of the debt of sin. ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-1|1–3]]:** *Seated at the right hand*, possibly an echo of [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]], also a common NT motif, appearing twenty-five times ([[Mark 12#^12-36|Mk 12.36]]; [[Mark 14#^14-62|14.62]]; [[Acts 02#^2-33|Acts 2.33–34]]; [[Romans 08#^8-34|Rom 8.34]]; [[Ephesians 01#^1-20|Eph 1.20]]; [[Hebrews 10#^10-12|Heb 10.12]], etc.). *Died*, see 2.20n. *Hidden . . . revealed*, Colossians suggests the events set in motion by the Messiah’s death and resurrection are presently hidden from view but will be revealed with the Messiah’s return. See 1.26, where the mystery of God’s plan for humankind has been hidden (Gk “apokekrummenon”) but will now be revealed (Gk “ephanerōthē) to the saints; 2.3, where all the mysteries have been hidden (Gk “apokryphoi”). Paul uses more conventional apocalyptic language, where things presently hidden will be “unveiled” (Gk “apokalyptō,” as in [[Romans 08#^8-18|Rom 8.18]]). ^3-1-3

### 3.5–11: The old life

*[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-5|Text]], p. 415* ^3-5

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-5|5]]:** Pre-baptismal life is described in a vice-list, a common form of moral instruction ([[Romans 01#^1-29|Rom 1.29–32]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-10|1 Cor 5.10]],[[1 Corinthians 05#^5-11|11]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|6.9–11]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-19|Gal 5.19–21]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-32|Eph 4.32]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-9|1 Tim 1.9–11]]; [[2 Timothy 03#^3-2|2 Tim 3.2–5]]; [[Titus 03#^3-3|Titus 3.3]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-1|1 Pet 2.1]]; [[Mark 07#^7-21|Mk 7.21–23]] || [[Matthew 15#^15-19|Mt 15.19]]; [[Revelation 21#^21-8|Rev 21.8]]). See also 1QS 4.9–14; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.281; Chrysippus, *Stoicorum Veterum Fragmenta* 3.397). *Fornication*, prohibited sexual intercourse. *Idolatry*, connected to sexual vices in Paul’s own letters (e.g., [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-9|1 Cor 5.9–10]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|6.9–10]]; [[1 Corinthians 10#^10-7|10.7–8]]) and in Tanakh (e.g., [[Hosea 01#^1-2|Hos 1.2]]), but Colossians and [[Ephesians 05#^5-5|Eph 5.5]] emphatically connect it with greed. ^3-5-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-6|6]]:** *Wrath*, describing God’s final judgment (see [[Romans 01#^1-18|Rom 1.18]]; [[Romans 05#^5-9|5.9]]; etc., and [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-10|1 Thess 1.10]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-16|2.16]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-9|5.9]]). The idea of a day of wrath or “day of YHWH” is at least as old as Amos (cf. Am 5.18–20). ^3-6

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Anger . . . abusive language*, there is no hint that the vices mentioned relate to the situation in Colossae. ^3-8

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-9|9–10]]:** *Stripped off . . . clothed*, this metaphor may suggest baptism and/or indicate the new life brought by the gospel. Paul uses “enduo,” “dress,” in a baptismal context ([[Galatians 03#^3-27|Gal 3.27]]), but also of putting on armor ([[Romans 13#^13-12|Rom 13.12]],[[Romans 13#^13-14|14]]) and to describe believers dressed in their new resurrection bodies ([[1 Corinthians 15#^15-53|1 Cor 15.53–57]]). *Old self . . . new self . . . image of its creator*, the power of the gospel or of baptism, causes believers to become new creations; the last phrase is an allusion to [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-26|Gen 1.26–27]]. ^3-9

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised*, apparently (cf. 2.11n.) some (Gentile) *disobedient* (Gk *the children of disobedience*) b Or *living among such people* c Or *filthy* d Gk *its creator*, *11where* members of the Colossian church want to become circumcised or have already become so. *Barbarian*, a person who speaks no Greek. *Scythian*, in classical literature, the epitome of an uncivilized person (Cicero, *Nat. d.* 2.34.88). What the author means by contrasting Scythians and slaves is opaque. Some commentators take the two terms as synonymous, but that only compounds the difficulty. The pairs echo [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-19|1 Cor 7.19]] and [[Galatians 03#^3-28|Gal 3.28]] but serve here to introduce the household codes ([[Colossians 03#^3-18|Col 3.18–24]]) and so reinforce rather than mitigate social divisions. ^3-11

> [!note] There Is No Greek Nor Jew (col 3.11) *(p. 415)*
> In his own letters, Paul insists that the death and resurrection of the Messiah has leveled the social order: the natural divisions among people—ethnic, sexual, and social—are no more. In [[Galatians 03#^3-28|Gal 3.28]] he succinctly emphasizes this leveling, “There is no longer Jew or Greek, there is no longer slave or free, there is no longer male and female; for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.” The same idea occurs in others of Paul’s letters (cf. [[Romans 10#^10-12|Rom 10.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-13|1 Cor 12.13]]). Some scholars have plausibly concluded that [[Galatians 03#^3-28|Gal 3.28]] is a formula that Paul is quoting from even earlier liturgical, probably baptismal, use in the churches.
>
> Whether Paul borrowed the phrase or not, the author of Colossians is certainly borrowing it, as well as expanding it, and subtly changing its point. Paul uses three antitheses: Jew/Greek, slave/free person, and male/female. The author of Colossians removes the category of gender (as does Paul in [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-13|1 Cor 12.13]]), changes Paul’s order from Jews/Greeks to
>
> Greeks/Jews, and adds the antitheses circumcision/foreskin and barbarian/Scythian. As it is difficult to explain how barbarians are antithetical to Scythians, it might be wasted effort to attempt to explain the difference between the antitheses Greek/Jew and circumcised/foreskin; for most readers these antitheses are synonyms. If there is a difference, the author may be providing further evidence that some members of the Gentile Colossian church are enamored of the ritual laws of the Torah and have become circumcised; the antithesis in that case refers to circumcised and uncircumcised Gentiles, not the antithesis between Gentile and Jew. Colossians reflects a situation—real or imagined—in a church far removed from the concerns of the original Pauline communities. The categories important to Paul, Jew and Greek, female and male, slave and free, are no longer the important categories for the author of Colossians, who tries to adapt Pauline language to an era in the church that Paul never envisioned.

### 3.12–17: New life

*[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-12|Text]], p. 416* ^3-12

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-12|12]]:** *Chosen ones* (Gk “eklektoi”), the identification of Israel as God’s *chosen* people (Heb “bachir”), a common expression in Deutero-Isaiah and related texts (e.g., [[Isaiah 45#^45-4|Isa 45.4]]), becomes a label for Jesus’ followers (see [[Mark 13#^13-20|Mk 13.20]],[[Mark 13#^13-22|22]]; [[Luke 18#^18-7|Lk 18.7]]; [[Romans 08#^8-33|Rom 8.33]]; [[2 Timothy 02#^2-10|2 Tim 2.10]]; [[Titus 01#^1-1|Titus 1.1]]; [[Revelation 17#^17-14|Rev 17.14]]). The Qumran sectarians saw themselves as the chosen of the chosen (cf. CD 4.3–4: “they are the chosen of Israel, the ones called by name, the ones who stand in the Last Days”). Colossians insists that chosenness confers obligations, an idea that the rabbinic Judaism also promotes, as in *Mekhilta, Bachodesh, Yitro*, which recounts how all the other nations rejected the Torah because it forbade adultery, stealing, etc., “But when God came to Israel, they all said with one accord, ‘All that the Lord has said, we will do and we will obey’” \[citing [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-7|Ex 24.7]]\]. *Compassion . . . patience*, generic virtues balancing the vice list of 3.8 (cf. 1QS 3.25; *Did*. 1.1–6 || *Barn*. 18–19). ^3-12-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-13|13]]:** *Forgive*, perhaps echoing [[Matthew 06#^6-12|Mt 6.12]],[[Matthew 06#^6-14|14–15]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-1|1 Cor 6.1–8]]. Cf. [[Ephesians 04#^4-32|Eph 4.32]]. ^3-13

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-14|14–15]]:** *Clothe,* the metaphors “strip off” and “clothe” are Christian, influenced by the baptismal liturgy (cf. *Gos. Thom*. 37). The language suggests a baptismal situation for this section of Col. *Love* (Gk “agapē”), the highest virtue in which congregants should dress. See 2.2n.; 1 Cor 13. *Body*, see 1.18n. ^3-14

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-16|16]]:** *Word of Christ*, unique to Colossians, where it seems equivalent to “good news of the Christ.” *Teach and admonish*, cf. 1.28. *Sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs*, cf. [[Ephesians 05#^5-19|Eph 5.19]]. There is nothing to differentiate the three types of songs here; the author is not referring specifically to the biblical Psalms. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-17|17]]:** *God the Father*, see 1n. ^3-17

### 3.18–4.1: Household codes

*[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-18|Text]], p. 416* · “Haustafeln” (see Introduction to Colossians) governing the patriarchal family appear also in [[Ephesians 05#^5-22|Eph 5.22–6.9]] and [[1 Peter 02#^2-18|1 Pet 2.18–3.7]] and in Hellenistic Jewish sources (e.g., Josephus, *Ag. Ap*. 2.199–203; Philo, *Decalogue* 165–167). *Husbands . . . fathers . . . masters* are exhorted to behavior that undercuts the social leveling implied by Christ’s new rule (cf. 3.11n.). ^3-18

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-18|18–19]]:** *Be subject* (Gk “hypotasso”), accept the husband’s authority; see [[1 Corinthians 14#^14-34|1 Cor 14.34]], which strong manuscript indications—scribes in the “non-Western” manuscript tradition located these verses at various places within the epistle—suggest is an interpolation. Colossians names at least one woman with authority, Nympha (see 4.15n.). ^3-18-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-19|19]]:** *Love your wives*, of the 141 examples of Gk “agape” in the NT, only in Colossians and Ephesians, is the word used in regard to marital love (cf. [[Ephesians 05#^5-25|Eph 5.25]],[[Ephesians 05#^5-28|28]],[[Ephesians 05#^5-33|33]]). Elsewhere it describes the love of God or Jesus, or love among community members. In the LXX, “agapaō” (the verbal form of *Lord* c Other ancient authorities read *to the Lord* d In Greek the same word is used for *master* and *Lord* “agape”) describes marital love some thirty times (e.g., [[Torah 05 - Chayei Sara#^24-67|Gen 24.67]]). ^3-19

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-20|20–21]]:** See [[Ephesians 06#^6-1|Eph 6.1–4]] and annotations. The first readers of Colossians might have connected admonitions addressed to children with the fifth commandment ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-12|Ex 20.12]] || [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-16|Deut 5.16]]). ^3-20

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-22|3.22–4.1]]:** See [[Ephesians 06#^6-5|Eph 6.5–9]]. For Paul as for the author of Colossians, slave owners and slaves are both subject to the Christ, but neither author condemns slavery itself (cf. 3.11; see also Philemon). ^3-22

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Lord . . . masters*, slaves are to obey their masters as children are to obey their parents. ^3-23

**[[NRSVue Colossians 03#^3-25|25]]:** *Paid back . . . partiality*, Gk “prosōpolēmpsia” is a Hebraism, “to grasp the face,” as in [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-17|Deut 10.17]]; cf. [[Romans 02#^2-11|Rom 2.11]]; [[Ephesians 06#^6-9|Eph 6.9]]; [[James 02#^2-1|Jas 2.1]]. ^3-25
