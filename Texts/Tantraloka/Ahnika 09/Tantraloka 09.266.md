## Mūla

तत्रानुसंधिः पञ्चात्मा पञ्च कर्मेन्द्रियाण्यतः ।
त्यागायादानसंपत्त्यै द्वयाय द्वितयं विना ॥ ^mula

tatrānusaṃdhiḥ pañcātmā pañca karmendriyāṇyataḥ |
tyāgāyādānasaṃpattyai dvayāya dvitayaṃ vinā || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 09 - Chapter Nine, part 7#^pg267|p. 248]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=267>))
>
> Here (according to this teaching), action is one, (but) it is divided (and diversified) by the difference in the intention (to act) (anusaṁdhāna). There (in that case, this) intention (is basically) of five kinds, and so the organs of action are five. (with 9.265cd–266ab)
>
> The four external actions (are those done) in order to abandon, to grasp, to do both, and to rest within one’s own nature without the two. These (actions) correspond (respectively to those of) the excretory organs, the hands, the feet, and the genitals, that are four kinds of organs (of action). Inner action is based on the vital breath. Indeed, its organ is speech. (with 9.266cd–268ab)
