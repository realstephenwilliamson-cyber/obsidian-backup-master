## Chapter 13

**13:1** Saul was…[^1] years old when he became king, and he reigned over Israel two years. ^13-1

**13:2** Saul picked 3,000 Israelites, of whom 2,000 were with Saul in Michmas[^2] and in the hill country of Bethel, and 1,000 with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin; the rest of the troops he sent back to their homes. ^13-2

**13:3** Jonathan struck down the Philistine prefect in Geba;[^3] and the Philistines heard about it. Saul had the ram’s horn sounded throughout the land, saying, “Let the Hebrews hear.”[^4] ^13-3

**13:4** When all Israel heard that Saul had struck down the Philistine prefect, and that Israel had incurred the wrath of[^5] the Philistines, all the people rallied to Saul at Gilgal. ^13-4

**13:5** The Philistines, in turn, gathered to attack Israel: 30,000[^6] chariots and 6,000 horsemen, and troops as numerous as the sands of the seashore. They marched up and encamped at Michmas, east of Beth-aven. ^13-5

**13:6** [^7]When the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble—for the troops were hard pressed—the people hid in caves, among thorns, among rocks, in tunnels, and in cisterns. ^13-6

**13:7** Some Hebrews crossed the Jordan, [to] the territory of Gad and Gilead. Saul was still at Gilgal, and the rest of the people rallied to him in alarm. ^13-7

**13:8** He waited seven days, the time that Samuel [had set].[^8] But when Samuel failed to come to Gilgal, and the people began to scatter, ^13-8

**13:9** Saul said, “Bring me the burnt offering and the sacrifice of well-being”; and he presented the burnt offering. ^13-9

**13:10** He had just finished presenting the burnt offering when Samuel arrived; and Saul went out to meet him and welcome him. ^13-10

**13:11** But Samuel said, “What have you done?” Saul replied, “I saw the people leaving me and scattering; you had not come at the appointed time, and the Philistines had gathered at Michmas. ^13-11

**13:12** I thought the Philistines would march down against me at Gilgal before I had entreated the LORD, so I forced myself[^9] to present the burnt offering.” ^13-12

**13:13** Samuel answered Saul, “You acted foolishly in not keeping the commandments that the LORD your God laid upon you! Otherwise[^10] the LORD would have established your dynasty over Israel forever. ^13-13

**13:14** But now your dynasty will not endure. The LORD will seek out a man after His own heart, and the LORD will appoint him ruler over His people, because you did not abide by what the LORD had commanded you.” ^13-14

**13:15** Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal[^11] to Gibeah[^12] of Benjamin. Saul numbered the troops who remained with him—about 600 strong. ^13-15

**13:16** Saul and his son Jonathan, and the troops who remained with them, stayed in Geba of Benjamin, while the Philistines were encamped at Michmas. ^13-16

**13:17** The raiders came out of the Philistine camp in three columns: One column headed for the Ophrah road that leads to the district of Shual, ^13-17

**13:18** another column headed for the Beth-horon road, and the third column headed for the border[^13] road that overlooks the valley of Zeboim toward the desert. ^13-18

**13:19** No smith was to be found in all the land of Israel, for the Philistines were afraid that the Hebrews would make swords or spears. ^13-19

**13:20** So all the Israelites had to go down to the Philistines to have their plowshares, their mattocks, axes, and colters[^14] sharpened. ^13-20

**13:21** [^15]The charge for sharpening was a *pim*[^16] for plowshares, mattocks, three-pronged forks, and axes, and for setting the goads. ^13-21

**13:22** Thus on the day of the battle, no sword or spear was to be found in the possession of any of the troops with Saul and Jonathan; only Saul and Jonathan had them. ^13-22

**13:23** Now the Philistine garrison had marched out to the pass of Michmas. ^13-23

[^1]: The number is lacking in the Heb. text; also, the precise context of the “two years” is uncertain. The verse is lacking in the Septuagint.
[^2]: So in oldest mss.; other mss. and editions read “Michmash” throughout the chapter.
[^3]: Apparently identical with Gibeah in v. 2.
[^4]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^5]: Lit. “became malodorous to.”
[^6]: Septuagint and other versions read “three thousand.”
[^7]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^8]: So some Heb. mss.; other mss., Septuagint, and Targum read “said.” Cf. 10.8.
[^9]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^10]: Change of vocalization yields, “You acted foolishly. If you had kept the commandment the Lord your God laid upon you…”
[^11]: Septuagint reads here, “Samuel rose and left Gilgal and went his way. The rest of the people followed Saul to meet the soldiers, and they went from Gilgal.”
[^12]: Sometimes called Geba; cf. vv. 3, 16; 14.5.
[^13]: Septuagint reads “Geba.”
[^14]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain. Septuagint reads “sickle.”
[^15]: Meaning of several terms in this verse uncertain.
[^16]: I.e., two-thirds of a shekel.
