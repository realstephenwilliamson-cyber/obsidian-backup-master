### 26.1–28.20: Passion and resurrection

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-1|Text]], p. 58* · (16.21–23; 17.22–23; 20.17–19). The name “Passion” for the narrative of Jesus’ arrest, trial, and crucifixion comes from the Lat “passio,” “suffered.” ^26-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-1|26.1–2]]:** **Fourth Passion prediction.** See 16.21–23; 17.22–23; 20.17–19. ^26-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-2|2]]:** *Passover*, Pilgrimage festival celebrating the Israelites’ exodus from Egypt; the festival brought tens of thousands of pilgrims to Jerusalem where they offered the paschal lamb. See “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658. ^26-2

### 26.3–5: Jewish leaders conspire

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-3|Text]], p. 58* · ([[Mark 14#^14-1|Mk 14.1–2]]; [[Luke 22#^22-1|Lk 22.1–2]]; [[John 11#^11-47|Jn 11.47–53]]). ^26-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-3|3]]:** *High priest*, nominal head of the people absent a king (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.232–44; 6.300–309; *Ant*. 20.199–203). *Caiaphas*, high priest from 18–36 CE (Josephus, *Ant*. 18.35). ^26-3-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-4|4]]:** *They conspired*, priests and elders are Jesus’ primary opponents in the Passion narrative. ^26-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-5|5]]:** *Riot*, see 27.24. ^26-5

> [!note] Judas *(p. 58)*
> Although not mentioned in Paul, according to whom Jesus was “handed over” (Gk *paradidomi*) by God (see [[Romans 04#^4-25|Rom 4.25]]), Judas Iscariot is recalled here as the faithless disciple who betrayed Jesus for thirty pieces of silver. The motive of greed and the payment appear first not in Mark but in Matthew’s Gospel (26.14–16; 27.1–10; cf. [[Mark 14#^14-10|Mk 14.10–11]]; [[Mark 15#^15-1|15.1]]). These details begin to form the stereotype of the venal and disloyal Jew, a development aided by the fact that the name “Judas” means “Jewish man.” However, there is no indication that at the time of the New Testament’s composition, Judas was meant to represent all Jewish people. That interpretation would come later. It is likely that Matthew inserted the reference to thirty pieces of silver to link Judas’ ac- tion to [[Zechariah 11#^11-11|Zech 11.11–12]] and thus place Judas’ action within the realm of Hebrew prophecy.
>
> According to Jewish law, one guilty of bearing false witness must face the same penalty that was meant for the one betrayed ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-16|Deut 19.16–20]]; *b. Makk*. 5a; see also 11 QSTemple 61.7; Josephus, *Ant*. 4.219; [[Sanhedrin 9#9b|*b. Sanh*. 9b]]). Judas’ tragic choice to end his life by hanging — to hang on a cross or to hang from a tree are cognate terms in Greek — is also unique to Matthew (contrast [[Acts 01#^1-16|Acts 1.16–20]]). Judas’ death invokes the story of David’s servant Ahithophel, who hanged himself after betraying King David, after whom Jesus is patterned ([[II Samuel 17#^17-23|2 Sam 17.23–24]]). Rabbinic sources report that Ahithophel repented for his treachery and thereby interpret the suicide as a redemptive act that would have allowed him entrance into the world to come ([[Sanhedrin 104#104b|*b. Sanh*. 104b]]–105a).

### 26.6–13: Anointing woman

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-6|Text]], p. 59* ^26-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-6|6]]:** *Bethany*, see 21.17n. *Simon*, not otherwise known. ^26-6-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-7|7]]:** *Poured it on his head*, kings were anointed with oil (e.g., [[I Samuel 10#^10-1|1 Sam 10.1]]; [[I Samuel 16#^16-13|16.13]]; [[II Kings 09#^9-6|2 Kings 9.6]]). ^26-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-8|8–9]]:** *Money given to the poor*, rabbinic tradition mandates selling luxury items to provide for the poor (*b. Taʾan.* 20b). Passover was a time for giving charity (*m. Pesah* 9.10; 11.1). ^26-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-11|11]]:** *You always have the poor*, see [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-1|Deut 15.1–11]]. ^26-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-12|12]]:** *Prepared me for burial*, Jews anointed corpses in preparation for burial ([[John 19#^19-39|Jn 19.39–40]]; *T. Abr*. 20.11; Josephus, *Ant*. 17.199; *m. Sanh*. 23.5). See “The Burial of Jesus,” p. 677. ^26-12

### 26.14–16: Judas agrees to betray Jesus

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-14|Text]], p. 59* · ([[Mark 14#^14-10|Mk 14.10–11]]; [[Luke 22#^22-3|Lk 22.3–6]]). ^26-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-14|14]]:** *Judas Iscariot*, see 10.4n. To this point, Judas has been a faithful disciple. ^26-14-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-15|15]]:** *What will you give me*, Matthew offers greed as a motive. Luke (22.3–6) and John (13.2,27) link Judas to Satan. *Thirty pieces of silver*, [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-32|Ex 21.32]]: thirty pieces of silver was also the compensation to be paid for a slave gored by an ox; [[Zechariah 11#^11-12|Zech 11.12–13]] equates thirty pieces of silver to the wages of a shepherd. One silver coin in circulation was the Athenian tetradrachm (four drachmas), the rough equivalent of four denarii. A denarius was a day’s wage for a laborer, so the fee mentioned here is 120 days’ wages. ^26-15

### 26.17–29: The Last Supper

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-17|Text]], p. 59* · ([[Mark 14#^14-12|Mk 14.12–31]]; [[Luke 22#^22-7|Lk 22.7–34]]; [[John 13#^13-21|Jn 13.21–30]]). ^26-17

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-17|17]]:** *Unleavened bread*, Heb “matzah,” see 26.19n. *Eat the Passover*, the ritually sacrificed lamb ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-3|Ex 12.3]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-6|6]]; *m. Pesah*. 7.9; 10.3, 9; Josephus, *Ant*. 3.249; CD 11.18–21). ^26-17-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-19|19]]:** *Passover meal*, at the time a paschal lamb meal, but later would become the Heb “seder,” lit., “order,” where Jewish families recount the Israelites’ exodus from Egypt and eat special foods (matzah, paschal lamb, bitter herbs; see [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-18|Ex 12.18–27]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-4|Lev 23.4–8]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^9-1|Num 9.1–14]]; [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^28-17|28.17]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-1|Deut 16.1–8]]; cf. *m. Pesah.* ). The seder ritual practiced today, however, is largely a later, rabbinic development (e.g., [[Pesachim 95#95a|*b. Pesah*. 95a]],103a,114a; one tradition links the famous Rabbi Gamaliel the Younger to some of the earliest Passover celebrations in the rabbinic era \[see *t. Pesah*. 10.12\]). ^26-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-24|24]]:** *Son of Man*, see 8.20n. ^26-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-25|25]]:** *Rabbi*, see 23.7n. ^26-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-26|26–28]]:** *This is my body . . . this is my blood*, the origin of the Christian Eucharist, “communion,” diseases b Or *gospel* c Other ancient authorities add *disciples* or “Lord’s Supper,” a reenactment or remembrance of the Last Supper (see also [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-6|Ex 24.6–8]]; [[Isaiah 53#^53-11|Isa 53.11–12]]; [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|1 Cor 11.23–25]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-20|Heb 9.20]]). See “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674. *My blood of the covenant*, taken by some Christians to refer to Jesus’ “new” covenant ([[Jeremiah 31#^31-31|Jer 31.31]]; see also [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-8|Ex 24.8]]; [[Zechariah 09#^9-11|Zech 9.11]]; see translators’ note *a*). *For the forgiveness of sins*, Jesus’ blood, like that of sin offerings, has the power to forgive sins (12.6; see also 1.21; 9.2,3,8n.; 12.6; 20.28; 21.12–13). The Passover offering is not, however, a sin offering (see “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658). ^26-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-30|26.30–35]]:** **Predicting Peter’s denial.** ^26-30

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-30|30]]:** *Sung the hymn*, see 21.9n. *Mount of Olives*, see 21.1n.; 24.3n. ^26-30-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-31|31]]:** [[Zechariah 13#^13-7|Zech 13.7]]; [[John 16#^16-32|Jn 16.32]]. *Become deserters*, Gk “skandalisthēsesthe,” lit., “be made to stumble” (from “skandalon,” stumbling block; see 18.6n.). ^26-31

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-32|32]]:** *Galilee*, 4.12n.; 21.11; 28.7,10; Luke and John set resurrection appearances in Judea. ^26-32

### 26.36–56: Gethsemane

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-36|Text]], p. 60* · ([[Mark 14#^14-32|Mk 14.32–52]]; [[Luke 22#^22-39|Lk 22.39–54]]; [[John 18#^18-1|Jn 18.1–12]]). ^26-36

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-36|36]]:** *Gethsemane*, Heb “oil press” (“gat shemen”), near the Mount of Olives. ^26-36-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-37|37]]:** *Sons of Zebedee*, see 20.20n. ^26-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-38|38]]:** *Deeply grieved*, an inner struggle, see Ps 31; 42.5–6. *Stay awake*, see 24.42. ^26-38

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-39|39]]:** *Let this cup pass*, see 20.23n.; 26.27–28; see also [[Ezekiel 23#^23-31|Ezek 23.31–34]]. ^26-39

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-41|41]]:** *Time of trial*, see 6.13n. See also [[Romans 06#^6-19|Rom 6.19]]. ^26-41

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-42|42]]:** *My Father . . . your will be done,* see 6.9–13. ^26-42

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-48|48]]:** *Kiss*, greeting used by Jesus’ followers ([[Romans 16#^16-16|Rom 16.16]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-20|1 Cor 16.20]]; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-12|2 Cor 13.12]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-26|1 Thess 5.26]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-14|1 Pet 5.14]]), but also a sign of betrayal ([[II Samuel 20#^20-9|2 Sam 20.9]]). ^26-48

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-49|49]]:** *Rabbi*, see 23.7n.; 26.5. ^26-49

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-50|50]]:** *Friend*, see 20.13n. ^26-50

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-52|52]]:** *Perish by the sword*, see [[Torah 02 - Noach#^9-6|Gen 9.6]]; [[Jeremiah 15#^15-2|Jer 15.2]]; [[Revelation 13#^13-10|Rev 13.10]]. ^26-52

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-53|53]]:** *Legions*, Roman military cohorts totaling around 5,000–5,500 men (for a description of the Roman army, see Josephus, *J.W*. 3.59–109; 5.39–70. ^26-53

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-54|54]]:** *Scriptures*, Matthew cites none; no pre-Christian sources are adduced to predict the arrest, suffering, and crucifixion of a Messiah. ^26-54

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-55|55]]:** [[John 18#^18-19|Jn 18.19–21]]. *Scriptures of the prophets*, see 26.54n. ^26-55

### 26.57–68: Sanhedrin trial (

*[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-57|Text]], p. 61* · [[Mark 14#^14-53|Mk 14.53–72]]; [[Luke 22#^22-54|Lk 22.54–71]]; [[John 18#^18-13|Jn 18.13–14]]). ^26-57

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-57|57]]:** *Caiaphas*, see 26.3n. The historicity of this Sanhedrin trial, which is not attested in John’s Gospel, is highly questionable. It would have been illegal since hearings were forbidden on festivals (*m. Sanh*. 4.1; m. *Betzah* 5.2; *t. Betzah* 4.4). In addition, a court would not ordinarily convene in a high priest’s chamber (see [[Avodah Zarah 8#8b|*b. Avod. Zar.* 8b]]; [[Shabbat 15#15a|*b. Shabb*. 15a]]); nor could a criminal be condemned to death in such a speedy manner, since ordinarily, one full day must pass before a capital punishment is meted out (*m. Sanh*. 4.1). See “The Sanhedrin,” p. 602. ^26-57-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-59|59]]:** *Whole council*, the Sanhedrin (unlikely to be convened the first night of Passover). ^26-59

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-60|60]]:** *False witnesses*, at least two witnesses were needed to pronounce a death sentence (18.16n.; see also [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-6|Deut 17.6–7]]; [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-15|19.15]]; *t. Sanh*. 8.3). ^26-60

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-61|61]]:** See 24.2; 27.40; [[John 02#^2-19|Jn 2.19]]; *Gos. Thom*. 71. ^26-61

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-63|63]]:** *Silent*, perhaps alluding to [[Isaiah 53#^53-7|Isa 53.7]]. *Son of God*, see 14.33n. ^26-63

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-64|64]]:** *You have said so*, Matthew demurs from Mark’s “I am” ([[Mark 14#^14-62|Mk 14.62]]). *Son of Man . . . heaven,* [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13–14]]. ^26-64

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-65|65]]:** *Tore his clothes*, indicating mourning; Jewish custom required one to mourn upon hearing blasphemy (*m. Sanh*. 7.5; [[Sanhedrin 60#60a|*b. Sanh*. 60a]]). *He has blasphemed*, technically, Jesus does not blaspheme. *M. Sanh.* 7.4–5 defines blasphemy as uttering the Divine Name (see also [[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-10|Lev 24.10–16]]). The verdict is as trumped up as the trial (see 9.3n.; 11.31). ^26-65

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-66|66]]:** *He deserves death*, Jews may not have had the authority of capital punishment at this time ([[John 18#^18-31|Jn 18.31]]; [[Sanhedrin 41#41b|*b. Sanh*. 41b]] notes that the Sanhedrin ceased trying capital cases forty years before the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE). ^26-66

**[[NRSVue Matthew 26#^26-69|26.69–74]]:** **Peter’s denial.** *He swore an oath*, Peter violates Jesus’ teachings (5.33–37). ^26-69
