## About

A group of eight late, minor Upanishads of Shaktism, the tradition that worships the Goddess (Devi) as the Supreme Being. They present the feminine as the primal cause and equate her with Brahman and Atman, and they are a source for Devi worship and Tantra-related theology. A. G. Krishna Warrier translated all eight.

- **The eight (place in the Muktika canon of 108; Veda):** Sita (45, Atharva), Tripuratapini (80, Atharva), Devi (81, Atharva), Tripura (82, Rig), Bhavana (84, Atharva), Saubhagyalakshmi (105, Rig), Sarasvati-rahasya (106, Krishna Yajur), Bahvricha (107, Rig).
- **Date:** authors and dates are unknown. Wikipedia reports Denise Cush's dating to the 12th–15th centuries CE and Patrick Olivelle's view that sectarian Upanishads attached to the Atharvaveda were probably composed in the second millennium, up to about the 16th century. Brooks and Mahadevan count them among the most recent minor Upanishads.
- **Ideas:** June McDaniel argues that many of their premises combine Samkhya and Advaita Vedanta. Some survive in more than one version.
- **Classification:** they stand apart from the thirteen Principal Upanishads, as one of several groups of minor Upanishads alongside the Samanya, Sannyasa, Yoga, Shaiva and Vaishnava groups.
- **Warrier's translation (1967):** *The Śākta Upaniṣad-s, based mainly on the commentary of Upaniṣad-Brahmayogin*, Adyar Library Series 89, Madras, xxi + 95 pages. The Theosophical Society at Adyar calls the commentary Advaitic, and says the volume follows the *dakshinacara* or *samayacara* approach, sets aside the non-Vedic *vama-marga*, and brings out the Srividya mantra, the cakras and the worship of the Sricakra.
