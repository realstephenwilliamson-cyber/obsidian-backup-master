## Chapter Five

Then Arjuna said:

\[1\] “First you praise renunciation
and then you praise *Yoga*, Krishna.
Of these two, which is the better?
Give me a definite answer.”

The Blessed Lord answered:

\[2\] “Renunciation and *Karma* *Yoga*
both lead to highest happiness.
But of the two, *Karma* *Yoga*
is better than renouncing action.

\[3\] The perpetual renunciant
neither hates nor desires,
is indifferent to opposites,
and with ease is liberated.

\[4\] *Samkhya* and *Yoga* are different,
the childish say, but not the wise.
Either one correctly practiced
yields the fruit of both disciplines.

\[5\] Those who follow *Samkhya* arrive
at the same place as *yogins* do.
*Samkhya* and *Yoga* are one thing.
He who sees this is one who sees.

\[6\] Renunciation, Arjuna,
is difficult without *Yoga*.
The sage who is trained in *Yoga*
very quickly reaches Brahman.

[p. 34] \[7\] One who is trained in *Yoga*,
whose self, subdued, cleansed, and conquered,
has become all beings’ Self,
is not defiled even while acting.

\[8\] “I do not do anything.” Thus
thinks the *yogin* who knows the truth,
seeing, hearing, touching, smelling,
eating, walking, sleeping, breathing,

\[9\] Talking, excreting, taking hold,
eyes opening and eyes shutting,
certain that the senses abide
in the objects of the senses.

\[10\] Placing his actions onto Brahman,
having left behind all clinging,
his acts are unstained by evil
like a lotus unstained by water.

\[11\] With body, mind, and intellect,
even with the senses only,
*yogins* act without attachment
so as to purify the self.

\[12\] The trained *yogin*, abandoning
action’s fruit, attains final peace.
The untrained, attached to the fruit,
is bound by action based on craving.

\[13\] Renouncing all action in his mind,
the embodied soul sits happily
ruling the city of nine gates,
neither acting nor causing action.

\[14\] The Foremost One does not create
the world’s action or agency
or action’s union with its fruit.
Nature itself keeps doing this.

[p. 35] \[15\] Eternal Being does not receive
anyone’s good or evil deeds.
Wisdom is veiled by ignorance,
and that deludes all who are born.

\[16\] But for those whose ignorance of
*Atman* is destroyed by knowledge,
that very knowledge is like the sun,
illuminating the Supreme.

\[17\] And those whose minds and selves are fixed
on That as the highest object
will never be reborn again,
their evils shaken off by knowledge.

\[18\] The wise perceive the same *Atman*
in a cultivated *brahmin*,
in a cow or an elephant,
even in a dog or outcaste.

\[19\] Even on earth rebirth is conquered
by those whose minds are set in sameness.
*Brahman* is impartial, spotless,
and therefore they abide in *Brahman*.

\[20\] Not rejoicing at the desired
nor grieving at the undesired,
with mind firm and undeluded,
knowing *Brahman*, firm in *Brahman*.

\[21\] Without clinging to sensations,
finding happiness in the Self,
through *Yoga* at one with *Brahman*,
one attains lasting happiness.

\[22\] Pleasures born of sensory contact
are wombs of pain and suffering.
They start and stop, Son of Kunti.
The wise are not content with them.

[p. 36] \[23\] One who is able to endure,
before release from the body,
distress born of greed and anger
is a disciplined, happy man.

\[24\] A *yogin* with inner happiness
that comes from radiance within
is absorbed into *Brahman*
and attains *Brahman*’s *nirvana*.

\[25\] *Brahman*’s *nirvana* is attained
by seers whose evils are destroyed,
their doubts cut through, their selves restrained,
joyous in all beings’ welfare.

\[26\] For ascetics who have severed
desire and anger, thoughts controlled,
and who have knowledge of the Self
*Brahman*’s *nirvana* is everywhere.

\[27\] Banishing all outside contacts,
the gaze fixed between the two brows,
inhale and exhale equally,
breathing through the nostrils alone.

\[28\] With senses and the mind controlled,
the sage who aims to be released
from desire, fear, and anger
is forever liberated.

\[29\] By knowing Me, the Enjoyer
of sacrificial austerity,
the world’s great Lord, the Companion
of all beings, he comes to peace.”
