## Mūla

न शिखा ऋऋ ऌ ऌ च शिरोमाला थ मस्तकम् ।
नेत्राणि च ध वै नासा ई समुद्रे णुणू श्रुती ॥ ^mula

na śikhā ṛṚ ḷ Ḷ ca śiromālā tha mastakam |
netrāṇi ca dha vai nāsā ī samudre ṇuṇū śrutī || ^iast

*Muktabodha's e-text of the KSTS edition (M00160) has:* na śikhā ṝṛ ḷḹ ca śiromālā tha mastakam | netrāṇi ca dha vai nāsā ī samudre ṇuṇū śrutī ||

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, *Mālinī*, January 2023 – March 2023
> p. 46 ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Malinivijayottaratantra, chapters 1-5 (2022-2023).pdf#page=46>))
>
> [^27]N is the topknot, ṛ, Ṛ, ḷ, Ḷ, the garland of the head, TH the head, the eyes are Ca and Dha, the nose is Ī, the ears, with (their) ornaments, NU and NŪ.[^28] B the gutturals (K, KH, G, GH, Ṅ), I and A are, respectively, the face, teeth, tongue and speech.[^29] Va, BHa and Ya are on the throat, right and left shoulder, Ḍ and ḌH are on the arms, ṬH on the hands, JH and Ñ are the branches (śākhā), J, R and Ṭ[^30] are the staff and the skull, P the heart, CH and L, the breasts, Ā the milk, S along with emission Ḥ, the living being (jīva), after that is said to be the vital breath (H).[^31] Ṣ and KṢ are in the stomach and the navel (respectively), M, Ś, Ṁ and T[^32] are in the hips, the genitals and the two thighs, the letters E and AI are the knees, and the shanks (O and AU) come after them,[^33] D and PH the feet.[^34] (with 3.37–41ab)

[^27]: TĀ 15/121-125ab is MV 3/37-41ab with a few variants. Some of these may well be small changes Abhinavagupta himself made in order to clarify the meaning of the text.
[^28]: TĀ 15/121cd reads *netrāṇi cordhve dho ‘nye ī ghrāṇaṁ mudre ṇu ṇū śrutī.*- ‘the eyes are Ca, which isabove (as the third eye), and Dha the other two, Ī is the nose, Ṇa the ears, and U and Ū (their two) ornaments.’
[^29]: MV 3/38b reads *vaktradantajihvāsu vāci ca* — ‘are in the face, teeth, tongue and speech’ for *vaktradantajihvāgiri kramāt* — ‘are, respectively, the face, teeth, tongue and speech’.
[^30]: Both the TĀ here and MV 3/39b read ‘*jraṭau*’. It is dual, so either we should read JR and Ṭ, or emend to R and Ṭ, which do in fact correspond to the staff of the trident the goddess is holding in one of her two hands and the skull in the other. Otherwise, perhaps J should be taken to be an alternative for R.
[^31]: TĀ 15/124a reads *prāṇo havarṇaḥ kathitaḥ* — ‘the letter Ha is said to be the vital breath’.
[^32]: The edition of the MV reads *maśaṁtāḥ*, in which case there are four letters here, namely, Ma, Śa, Ṁ and Ta, which is correct. However, Jayaratha endorses the reading *maśāntā*, which omits Ṁ. See below.
[^33]: TĀ 15/125a reads *tatparau tu jaṅghe* — ‘those that come after them (O and AU) are the shanks’ — for MV 3/41ab, that reads *tathā jaṅghe tatparau* — ‘and the shanks (O and AU) come after them.’
[^34]: These are a quote from MV 3/37-41ab, with a few variants. These may well be simply differences in the MS Abhinavagupta was using. Unlike the Mātṛkā deposition, Jayaratha does not quote the original source in this case. He prefers the *Triśirobhairavatantra,* no doubt because the account there is more complete, including as it does the names of the Yoginīs who preside over the letters. We are thus led to suppose that Jayaratha may well not have been able to find an equivalent passage in which the names of the Mātṛkā presiding over the letters is given. However, a list of names and even descriptions of these Yoginīs is found in the Kubjikā Tantras. See Dyczkowski 2009 vol. 3, notes p. 239-268.
