## About

A Sanskrit text, one of the eighteen major Puranas, named after the sage Markandeya. It is one of the Puranas without a sectarian focus on a single god, and it contains the Devi Mahatmya, a central text of Shaktism.

- **Frame and contents:** Jaimini asks Markandeya about questions the Mahabharata left open. Markandeya sends him to four wise birds in the Vindhya range, whose answers fill chapters 4–45. Markandeya himself speaks in chapters 45–80 and 94–137, which scholars treat as the older core: genealogies, the manvantaras, geography and praise of Surya. Chapters 39–43 teach yoga.
- **Size:** 137 chapters in extant manuscripts. Tradition gives 9,000 verses; surviving manuscripts have about 6,900.
- **Devi Mahatmya:** chapters 81–93 (Wikipedia; the index of Pargiter's translation on Wisdom Library labels the same cantos). Wadekar's 2011 critical edition numbers it 76–88. Also called Saptasati or Chandipatha, it is recited during Durga Puja.
- **Date:** the Puranas are layered, so no single date applies. Wendy Doniger puts the main text about 250 CE and the Devi Mahatmya about 550 CE; others place parts in the 4th–6th centuries. An inscription of 608 CE quotes the Devi Mahatmya. A complete palm-leaf manuscript from Nepal is dated 998 CE.
- **Translations:** F. E. Pargiter's, for the Asiatic Society's Bibliotheca Indica, Calcutta; Princeton's catalogue dates it 1904 and records it as issued in nine parts, 1888–1905. Bibek Debroy's appeared from Penguin India in October 2019. Earlier English versions include C. C. Mukherjee (1893) and M. N. Dutt (1896).
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia calls the Devi Mahatmya the oldest known treatise on the Goddess as supreme truth and creator of the universe, and says some traditions rank it with the Bhagavad Gita. The Chandi Charitar Ukati Bilas in the Sikh Dasam Granth draws on the Purana.
