### 6.1–10: Summary of defense

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 362* ^6-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-2|2]]:** [[Isaiah 49#^49-8|Isa 49.8]], referring originally to the end of the Babylonian exile in 538 BCE with the edict of Cyrus of Persia permitting the Jews to return to Jerusalem. ^6-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-4|4–5]]:** *Servants of God*, see 1.8–9n. Catalogue of hardships; see 4.8–9n. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-6|6–7]]:** Catalogue of virtues; see [[Galatians 05#^5-22|Gal 5.22–23]]; [[Philippians 04#^4-8|Phil 4.8]]. ^6-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-8|8–10]]:** These seven contrasting pairs, *impostors, and yet are true . . . having nothing, and yet possessing everything*, are not paradoxes to show the imperturbability of an ideal Stoic sage but antitheses answering charges against Paul. This passage summarizes his self-defense begun in 2.14. *From a human point of view* (5.16), Paul and his co-workers might be accused of being *impostors, unknown*, etc., but in the context of the new creation (5.17), they are *true, known*, etc. ^6-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-11|6.11–7.4]]:** **Final appeal**. ^6-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-11|6.11–13]]:** *Frankly*, see 1.12n. ^6-11-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Children*, rabbis routinely referred to students or others who posed questions as children or sons, following the earlier model of biblical wisdom literature (e.g., [[Proverbs 05#^5-7|Prov 5.7]]). A well-known incident involving Hillel is at [[Shabbat 30#30b|*b. Shabb.* 30b]]-31a. See also, e.g., *b. Taʾan.* 21a. ^6-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-14|6.14–7.1]]:** An interruption of Paul’s appeal, since 7.2–4 better follows 6.11–13. This passage contains many words Paul nowhere else uses and also an uncharacteristically stark dualism. The section may be inserted here from a different letter. ^6-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-14|14]]:** *Mismatched*, lit., “misyoked,” only here in the NT. The term may play upon the Tanakh’s restriction against yoking different kinds of animals or creating unnatural mixtures ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-19|Lev 19.19]]). *Partnership* (only here in the NT), *fellowship*, and *share* (v 15), synonyms meaning “association.” *Righteousness and lawlessness*, see [[Romans 06#^6-19|Rom 6.19]], where the second word is translated “iniquity.” *Light and darkness* (see [[Romans 02#^2-19|Rom 2.19]]; [[Romans 13#^13-12|13.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-5|1 Cor 4.5]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-4|1 Thess 5.4–5]]) undergirds the imagery of 4.3–6, where the contrast between “believer” and *unbeliever* (v 15) also appears. Compare 1QM’s distinction between “sons of light” and “sons of darkness.” ^6-14-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Beliar*, Satan; see 2.11n. ^6-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-16|16]]:** *Temple of the living God*, see [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|1 Cor 3.16]]. *Living God*, see 3.2–3n. *We are the temple*, see [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|1 Cor 3.16]]. ^6-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 06#^6-16|16b–18]]:** A chain of citations: [[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-12|Lev 26.12]]; [[Ezekiel 37#^37-27|Ezek 37.27]]; [[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Isaiah 43#^43-6|Isa 43.6]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-11|52.11]], used by Paul to indicate separation from bodily and spiritual defilement. In their original settings, the verses all reflect God’s everlasting covenant with the Israelite nation. ^6-16-2
