### 5.1–20: Gerasene demoniac and the Legion of unclean spirits

*[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 79* · ([[Matthew 08#^8-28|Mt 8.28–34]]; [[Luke 08#^8-26|Lk 8.26–39]]). This exorcism in Gentile territory resembles the account in 1.21–28; in both scenes demons recognize Jesus, he expels them with commands, and onlookers quickly report the events. By his actions, Jesus again channels God’s holy spirit (1.24; 3.29) against the *unclean spirit* (v 2). ^5-1

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-1|1]]:** *Gerasenes*, Gerasa is forty miles from the Sea of Galilee, which makes the setting inconsistent with v 13. Gerasa may evoke Heb “gerash,” which means “expel”; the term appears in some biblical accounts of God expelling the nations from the land (e.g., *Gadarenes* [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-28|Ex 23.28]]), as here unclean animals are expelled (see also 6.6b–13n.). ^5-1-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-2|2]]:** *Unclean spirit*, see 1.22–24n. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-7|7]]:** The spirit recognizes Jesus as *Son of the Most High God* (Gk “hypsistos,” in LXX for “ ʿelyon,” “Most High,” an ancient name for God \[e.g., [[Psalms 009#^9-3|Ps 9.2]]\]). ^5-7

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-9|9]]:** *What is your name*, knowledge of the names of angels and demons was essential to enlist or control them. *Legion*, a Latin loan word, denotes a unit of 6,000 soldiers in the Roman army. This story opens several questions without providing answers: Do the swine represent the expulsion (“gerash”) of unclean animals or the Roman armies? Are the Gerasenes angry over the loss of their herds? What is the significance of a Gentile asking to follow Jesus? Why is there no messianic secret? ^5-9

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-20|20]]:** The *Decapolis*, a federation of ten predominantly Gentile cities east of the Sea of Galilee. ^5-20

### 5.21–43: Raising of Jairus’s daughter and healing a woman with a hemorrhage

*[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-21|Text]], p. 80* · ([[Matthew 09#^9-18|Mt 9.18–26]]; [[Luke 08#^8-40|Lk 8.40–56]]). Jesus quickly retraces his route back to the western, more Jewish shore. ^5-21

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-22|22]]:** *Leaders of the synagogue*, probably not a religious officer but rather a prominent person in the community. ^5-22

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-25|25]]:** *Hemorrhages*, Jewish law distinguished between a menstruating woman (“niddah”) and a woman with an irregular, continuous flow of blood (“zavah”; [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^15-19|Lev 15.19–30]]); Mark here refers to the latter. Lev 15 addresses normal and abnormal genital discharges. All cause ritual impurity, with different responses commanded. Whether such ritual impurity—an issue not mentioned in the text—would have mattered in a local village, where access to the Temple is not an issue, is not clear. ^5-25

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-25|25–34]]:** It is often assumed that this miracle depicts Jesus’ rejection of Jewish purity codes, which themselves were seen as particularly onerous for women, but it is more likely that the contrast is between sickness and healing based on faith, as the text twice states (vv 28–29,34). As in 1.40–45, where Jesus’ cleansing is emphasized, the kingdom of God is a time of liberation from impurity, not from purity laws ([[Zechariah 13#^13-1|Zech 13.1–2]]; [[Zechariah 14#^14-20|14.20–21]]). ^5-25-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-41|41]]:** *Talitha cum*, the Aramaic of Jesus’ words is likely a vestige of the stage of oral tradition and perhaps, for Mark’s Greek-speaking audience, suggestive of a magical formula. ^5-41

**[[NRSVue Mark 05#^5-42|42]]:** *Twelve years*, the girl’s age is the same as the period of the woman’s suffering from hemorrhage (v 25), suggesting a reference to the renewal of Israel as a whole, since “twelve” can stand for the twelve tribes, thereby indicating the whole Israelite people. It is not connected to the age of marriage. ^5-42
