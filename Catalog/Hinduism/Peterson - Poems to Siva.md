## About

The Tamil hymns to Shiva of three poet-saints, Sambandar, Appar and Sundarar. They make up the first seven of the twelve volumes of the Tirumurai, the canon of Tamil Shaivism.

- **Poets:** Sambandar (vols. I–III, 383 or 384 hymns), Appar (vols. IV–VI, 313 hymns) and Sundarar (vol. VII; 100 hymns per Prentiss). Princeton University Press dates the three to the sixth–eighth centuries; the EFEO says possibly the seventh and eighth. Wikipedia adds that Champakalakshmi gives the seventh–ninth, and that the saints' life stories are full of legend.
- **Size:** Wikipedia gives 796 hymns (patikams) with 8,284 stanzas, most hymns having ten or eleven verses. The EFEO describes the collection as 800 hymns.
- **Compilation:** in the 10th century, under the Chola king Rajaraja I, Nambiyandar Nambi collected and arranged the hymns. Legend says he found the palm-leaf manuscripts half eaten by white ants in a chamber of the Chidambaram Nataraja temple.
- **Music and place:** the hymns are set to 23 of the 103 modes (pan) of ancient Tamil music, and odhuvars still sing them in Tamil Nadu temples. The 275 temples they praise are known as the Paadal Petra Sthalams.
- **Translations:** Indira Viswanathan Peterson's *Poems to Siva: The Hymns of the Tamil Saints* (Princeton Library of Asian Translations, 1989; reissued 2014) renders a substantial portion, with an introduction and notes drawing on her research into performance and temple ritual. The CD-ROM *Digital Tēvāram* (IFP/EFEO, 2007) includes a complete English rendering by V. M. Subrahmanya Ayyar.
- **Why it matters:** Princeton calls it the primary scripture of Tamil Saivism, one of the first popular large-scale devotional movements in Hinduism. Wikipedia notes that Tamil scholars have called it a "Tamil Veda" and that its hymns document seventh-century temples and iconography.
