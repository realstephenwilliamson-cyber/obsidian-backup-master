### 15.1–20: Tradition of the elders

*[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-1|Text]], p. 39* · ([[Mark 07#^7-1|Mk 7.1–23]]). ^15-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-2|2]]:** *Do not wash*, see [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^30-17|Ex 30.17–21]], concerning priests; rabbinic legislation extends several Temple purity practices to the household (see *m. Yad*. 1.1–2.4; [[Berakhot 53#53b|*b. Ber*. 53b]]; [[Gittin 15#15b|*b. Git*. 15b]]; [[Pesachim 115#115a|*b. Pesah*. 115a]]-b; [[Sukkah 26#26b|*b. Sukk.* 26b]]; 27a). ^15-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-4|4]]:** *Honor your father and your mother*, [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-12|Ex 20.12]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-16|Deut 5.16]]. *Whoever speaks evil*, [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-17|Ex 21.17]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-9|Lev 20.9]]. ^15-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-5|5]]:** *Given to God,* the “*Korban*” offering dedicates property to the Temple (see *m. Ned.* 3.2; 5.6). ^15-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-8|8–9]]:** [[Isaiah 29#^29-13|Isa 29.13]] (following the word order of the LXX; cf. [[Mark 07#^7-6|Mk 7.6]]). ^15-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-11|11]]:** See *Gos. Thom*. 14. *Defiles*, spiritual purity is more important than bodily purity (5.19–20; see also [[Psalms 024#^24-3|Ps 24.3–4]]; 51; [[II Chronicles 30#^30-18|2 Chr 30.18–20]]). ^15-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-13|13]]:** See [[Isaiah 60#^60-21|Isa 60.21]]; [[Jeremiah 12#^12-12|Jer 12.12]]. ^15-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-14|14]]:** *Blind guides* ([[Luke 06#^6-39|Lk 6.39]]; see also *Gos. Thom*. 34), Matthew warns against following Pharisaic rather than church teachers, but see 23.2–3. *Fall into a pit*, suffer misfortune ([[Isaiah 24#^24-18|Isa 24.18]]; [[Jeremiah 48#^48-44|Jer 48.44]]; [[Psalms 007#^7-16|Ps 7.15]]; [[Proverbs 26#^26-27|Prov 26.27]]). ^15-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-15|15–16]]:** See 13.13n. *Still without understanding*, the disciples by now should not require explanations (13.16). ^15-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-18|18]]:** See 15.11n. ^15-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-20|20]]:** *To eat with unwashed hands does not defile*, Matthew omits Mark’s claim (7.19) that Jesus declared all foods clean; for Matthew, dietary laws remain in place, but some “traditions of the elders” are not followed. *commandment* e Other ancient authorities lack *of the blind* ^15-20

### 15.21–28: The Canaanite woman

*[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-21|Text]], p. 40* · ([[Mark 07#^7-24|Mk 7.24–30]]). See [[I Kings 17#^17-8|1 Kings 17.8–24]]. ^15-21

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-21|21]]:** *Tyre and Sidon*, see 11.21n. ^15-21-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-22|22]]:** *Canaanite*, Israel’s traditional enemies; the region was associated with Baal worship ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^9-25|Gen 9.25]]; [[Judges 02#^2-11|Judg 2.11–12]]; [[Judges 03#^3-7|3.7]]; [[Judges 08#^8-33|8.33]]; [[Hosea 02#^2-15|Hos 2.13]]). *Came out*, Jesus and the woman meet at the border; it is not clear that Jesus enters the Gentile district. *Son of David*, Jesus’ Jewish messianic title (see 1.1n.). The woman foreshadows the conversion of the Gentiles (see also 8.10n.; 18.6; 21.21; 24.13–14; 28.19). ^15-22

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-23|23]]:** *He did not answer*, his silence is explained in v 24. *Send her away*, Gk “apoluson” can alternatively mean “loose her,” i.e., perform the exorcism. ^15-23

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-24|24]]:** *Lost sheep*, see 10.6n. ^15-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-27|27]]:** *The dogs eat the crumbs*, the woman acknowledges her secondary position but still insists on her rights. ^15-27

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-28|28]]:** *Faith*, see 8.10n. Mark’s version lacks the reference to faith as the reason for the daughter’s healing. ^15-28

*[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-29|Text]], p. 40* · ([[Mark 07#^7-31|Mk 7.31–37]]). ^15-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-31|31]]:** *God of Israel*, see [[I Kings 01#^1-48|1 Kings 1.48]]; [[Psalms 041#^41-14|Ps 41.13]]; [[Psalms 068#^68-36|68.35]]; [[Psalms 069#^69-7|69.6]]; [[I Chronicles 16#^16-36|1 Chr 16.36]]; 1QM 13.2; 14.4; *t. ### 15.32–39: Feeding (more than) four thousand

*[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-32|Text]], p. 40* · ([[Mark 08#^8-1|Mk 8.1–10]]). Given the preceding story of the Canaanite woman, the story may suggest a Gentile setting; see 14.15–21. ^15-32

**[[NRSVue Matthew 15#^15-39|39]]:** *Magadan*, unknown city. However, there is no Gentile mission yet (28.19). ^15-39

> [!note] Peter *(p. 40)*
> Only in Matthew’s Gospel does Jesus tell the disciple named Simon son of Jonah: “You are Peter (Gk *petros*; Aram *kepha* \[the Greek transliteration of *kepha* — “Cephas” — appears in [[John 01#^1-42|Jn 1.42]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-12|1 Cor 1.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-5|15.5]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-11|Gal 2.11]],[[Galatians 02#^2-14|14]]\]), and on this rock (Gk *petra*; Aram *kepha*) I will build my church” (16.18). Only in Matthew does Jesus give Peter the “keys of the kingdom” (16.19). Peter is the leader of the disciples (16.19), is present at the Transfiguration (17.1–9), and despite his fear, follows Jesus after his arrest
>
> (26.58). He is also the only disciple to attempt to walk on water with Jesus (14.28–30). Roman Catholic tradition identifies Peter as the initial bishop of Rome, thereby making him the first pope (although the title was not officially used until almost two hundred years later; see Irenaeus, *Adv. Haer.* 3). Most Protestants note that the textual link between Peter the apostle and “the rock” of the Church is not definitive; it could be that the “rock” refers only to Peter’s proclamation “you are the Messiah” (16.13), not to Peter himself.
