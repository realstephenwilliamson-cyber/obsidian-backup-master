READING WORDS ON PRINTED PAGES (Dyczkowski's translation of the Tantraloka, 3 Nov 2026). Reading only: write nothing but your own
output files.

The images are pages of Mark Dyczkowski's English translation of Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka with Jayaratha's commentary. You are
checking what the print actually shows. Report the print exactly, even where the print itself looks wrong. Never fill in what a word
"should" be.

FILES (all under /home/claude/ill/)
- tb/third/<sheet>.png: each sheet stacks up to 9 strips. Each strip shows one or a few printed lines under a grey bar with its ID.
  Some strips have a red box around the words in question; others have no box.
- tb/third/task.txt lists, sheet by sheet, each ID with the text just BEFORE and just AFTER the words wanted, from a transcription
  that may contain errors. The words wanted are deliberately not given.

FOR EACH ID
1. Find the "before" words and the "after" words on the strip and transcribe exactly what stands BETWEEN them: nothing of the
   before or the after text itself. (Where there is a red box, it marks roughly the same place; trust the before/after text.)
2. Transcribe it exactly as printed, letter by letter, with every diacritic: ā ī ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ṅ ñ ṭ ḍ ṇ ś ṣ ḥ, capitals Ś Ā Ī Ū Ṛ Ṭ Ḍ, and
   the anusvāra as ṁ (this book prints m with a dot above). Look closely at the first and the last letter of each word: dot below
   or not (ṭ/t, ḍ/d, ṇ/n, ṣ/s), macron or not, curly quote opening ‘ or closing ’.
3. Include punctuation, quotation marks, brackets and verse bars printed against the word, exactly as printed: ( ) [ ] ‘ ’ “ ” , . ;
   : | || (write the bars with the ordinary vertical bar).
4. A small raised footnote number is not part of a word: write it as ^N (e.g. them),^22). If what stands between is only a footnote
   number, write just ^N.
5. If you cannot read a letter with confidence, write ? for it and set conf to "low". A dot or macron you cannot actually see is
   not there: mark it ? and low.
6. If you cannot find both the before and the after words on the strip, write printed "" with conf "low" and say so in note.

To look closer you may enlarge part of a sheet, e.g.
python3 -c "from PIL import Image; im=Image.open('/home/claude/ill/tb/third/V001.png'); im.crop((0,0,im.width,200)).resize((im.width*2,400)).save('/home/claude/ill/zoom/C-v001a.png')"
Then Read the zoomed file. Do this wherever a letter is in doubt; it is cheap.

OUTPUT: for each sheet, the file named in task.txt (read3/C/<sheet>.jsonl), one JSON object per line per ID:
{"id": "G0009", "printed": "...", "conf": "high" | "med" | "low", "note": ""}
Write each sheet's file as soon as that sheet is done. When all five are written, reply with one line: done, and the number of IDs.
