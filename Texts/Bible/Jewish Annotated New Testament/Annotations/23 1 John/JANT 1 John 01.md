# 1 John 1 (JANT notes)

### 1.1–4: Entering community fellowship

*[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 519* ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-1|1]]:** *From the beginning*, see 2.13,14; [[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1]]. *Touched with our hands*, the Gospels of Luke (24.39) and (especially) John (20.27) are the only ones to contain a post-resurrection event in which Jesus invites disciples to touch him; in [[Matthew 28#^28-9|Mt 28.9]] the women at the tomb grasp Jesus’ feet. *Concerning the word*, the prologue of John’s Gospel (1.1–14) introduces the concept of word (Gk “logos”; see “*Logos*,” p. 688). The phrase *from the beginning* as well as the *word* here suggest that the author of 1 John borrowed from the Gospel; the phrase *word of life* recalls Jesus’ statements in the Gospel of John about his bringing “eternal life” ([[John 05#^5-24|Jn 5.24]]) and being the “bread of life” (6.35). ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-2|2]]:** *We have seen it*, the author states three times in the first three verses that he was an eyewitness to Jesus’ activities. ^1-2

### 1.5–2.28: The core of belief

*[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-5|Text]], p. 519* ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-5|1.5]]:** The theme of *light* and *darkness* appears in [[John 01#^1-4|Jn 1.4]],[[John 01#^1-5|5]],[[John 01#^1-9|9]]; [[John 03#^3-19|3.19–21]]; [[John 08#^8-12|8.12]]; [[John 09#^9-5|9.5]]; [[John 12#^12-35|12.35–36]],[[John 12#^12-46|46]], and is common in the DSS as a means of distinguishing the behavior and identity of members of their own communities from those of their adversaries. ^1-5-2

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Fellowship* (Gk “koinonia”), the community of fellow-believers. One cannot participate in the community of light while *walking in darkness* (see [[Isaiah 09#^9-1|Isa 9.1]]). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Blood . . . cleanses*, a reference to sacrificial practice as a means of cleansing from sin; the blood of the “sin (or purification) offering” removed contamination from the Temple (see [[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^4-1|Lev 4.1–5.13]], [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-22|Num 15.22–31]]; see “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658). *Sin*, among the many Heb words for “sin” in the Tanakh, “ḥet” and its cognates are the most frequently used; etymologically it means falling short or missing a goal of a moral or cultic nature. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-8|8]]:** To deny one’s sin is to *deceive* oneself and deny God’s truth. ^1-8

**[[NRSVue 1 John 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Confess*, acknowledge the existence of. *Unrighteousness*, to be righteous or *just* is to be in accord with God’s will, which is *faithful*. *Forgive us our sins and cleanse us*, a combination of forgiveness (see e.g., [[I Kings 08#^8-34|1 Kings 8.34]]) and ritual cleansing language (see esp. [[Ezekiel 36#^36-25|Ezek 36.25]]). Judaism does not generally teach the concept of “original sin” as something inherited from Adam and Eve (but see [[Psalms 051#^51-7|Ps 51.5]] \[HB v 7\]); humans are not seen as born in a state of estrangement from God that must be repaired. Because humans sin, Judaism encourages confessing sin directly to God with a repentant attitude. Such confession often occurs in a communal setting, for example on Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) in a synagogue, but it can also be expressed in personal prayer, at anytime, anywhere. ^1-9
