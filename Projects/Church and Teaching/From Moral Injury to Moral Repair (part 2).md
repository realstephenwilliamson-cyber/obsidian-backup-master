From Moral Injury to Moral Repair\* (part 2)

\*"From Moral injury and moral repair in war veterans: A preliminary model and intervention strategy" (2009) *Clinical Psychology Review 29*
Brett T. Litz, Nathan Stein, Eileen Delaney, Leslie Lebowitz, William P. Nash, Caroline Silva, Shira Maguen

1. Connection: "an empathetic, trusting, and caring connection"

2. Preparation and education: "education about moral injury"

3. Modified exposure component: "emotion-focused full disclosure of events"

4. Examination and integration: "Exploration of the implications of the event for the person, including addressing 'maladaptive interpretations'"

5. "imaginal dialogue with a *benevolent* moral authority concerning the past and future"

--service member or veteran thinks of someone who has always had his or her back and who has been in her or his corner no matter what, to this person they can disclose the transgression, how they feel about themselves, and what should happen to them as a result: how does the moral authority respond?

--questions such as 'what was that like for you?' or 'what are you going to take from this?' are appropriate

--may be repeated in multiple sessions

6. Reparation and self-forgiveness "obviating self-condemnation and shame by turning it into self-benevolence"

--self-forgiveness as a "set of motivational changes where one becomes decreasingly motivated to avoid stimuli associated with the offense, decreasingly motivated to retaliate against the self (e.g., punish the self, engage in self-destructive behavior)"

-- self-forgiveness conceptually entails 1) acknowledging the event; 2) accepting responsibility for it; 3) experiencing the negative emotions associated with it; 4) devoting sufficient energy to heal; 5) committing to living differently in the future

7. Fostering reconnection: "social reconnection with various communities, including faith communities"

--seek positive relationships outside of therapy

--be a part of something greater

--importance of "an individual's understanding of, experience with, and connection to that which transcends the self"

--forgiveness within religious and spiritual frameworks is potentially instrumental in alleviating guilt, shame, and demoralization

8. Planning for the long haul: "future goals"

--extensive conversation about what the morally injured will take with them from the work they have done and their plans for the future

--it is important to plan for times when the person is at risk for being defined by the moral injury
