*[p. 1]* ^pg1

*V’ani sefliasi l’cha השם eis ratzon. Elohim b’rov chasdecha, aneini b’emes yishecha.* 

The eis ratzon (the moment of will that comes and passes) arrives as scheduled in the siddur. 

As is known, the Jewish calendar that hangs on the wall is not actually the real demarcator of judaic-time. A mere sketch, good for only this year. The luach is a better annual sketch. But we have a bigger map. 

Borges writes of the land obsessed with cartography to the point that they produce a map that matched the territory point for point, but due to its size became useless and neglected, the people forgetting the art of cartography. Not so the Jews. Our map is our landscape and it is called the Siddur. Its pages do not only flutter on the liminal outskirts of time. 

Imagine, if you would, the full set of volumes the actual Siddur would comprise. You can start by lining up all the Machzorim plus what is generally known as a Siddur, but you’re not coming close. All the little ticks and parentheticals, the Artscroll choose-your-own-adventure, pegged to the thrall of mundane clock-time. We must unfurl them. We need a full set of davening for every single day for a cycle of 19 consecutive years, including kriya and haftara, in order to fully write out the actual sequence of the full Siddur in unabbreviated form. 

That’s the real Jewish calendar. 

I should go back to the beginning. 

Moshe received the Torah at Sinai and from that moment, *lo b’shamayim hi* - it is not in heaven. The authority of the Torah was transferred from the domain of angels into human hands. The first mitzvah served as the vessel for the transfer of authority over time. (This is the pattern for all mitzvos: the relocation of the Torah implied a transfer of divine authority from angels to humans in 613 areas. As time is our present concern, we will defer a detailed discussion of the implications of the remaining 612. For the Time Being.) 

The beis din became empowered to declare months. This is not arbitrary, as if time does what it will in the abstract, and some panel of rabbis employed a convoluted system of cross examining witnesses who may or may not have seen the new moon before deciding to call a moon-timed time slice “Tishrei” or “Cheshvan.” No! The convolution served to wrest control away from arbitrary celestial looping and to the agency of the beis din. It actually was Tishrei. It actually was Cheshvan. There is no such thing as time in the abstract. 

For reasons that are sufficiently well known that I won’t belabor them here, Hillel II stands in mythopoetic disjunctive religioslation, spinning out a crystallization of human time authority that ultimately produced the mechanism of the 19 year cycle. The question this raises is, “to whom did the time authority now pass?” It was taken from the angels, held in trust by the beis din, but then reapportioned via theurgico-juridical processes.

There are three options regarding the recipient of the reapportionment: 

One view is that the Machine of the tick-toc calendar-bloc now has the time authority, that they put the authority into the time crystal and that’s where it just is now. 

One view is that it’s actually still with the molar aggregate of rabbis who could theoretically decide that things are different now. 

One view is that the authority is being held in trust for a Sanhedrin-in-potentia, whose future may have already been cashed in far before bond maturity. 

These views are all intolerable to the spirit of human freedom and the ownership of time by the molecular. Therefore, we need another view. 

Time authority is in accordance with the davener who takes flight from tic-toc-time into the 19 year cycle sequence of sidduric word-time flowing. This is because the rabbinical stream that flowed through Sura and Pumbedita and then to the rest of the discursive-rabbinic literary is not the only helix that spirals out of the ruins of Jerusalem. Rather the disbursive-paytanic liturgiture is in its own full sense the pure remaining sweet flow that turns its own helical spiral down the avenue of Jewish history. 

It is surely the rhythmic time flows of the latter that overcode the referential time flows of the former and in that particular mode, it is fully capable of scheduling the eis ratzon as it occurs by Shabbos mincha, yom tov kriya, etc. 

A power flow that can establish a mode of recurrence of the eis ratzon within its own field of mapped progress has the ability to indefinitely transform itself, essentially harnessing limitless energy to power that self transformation, so long as it is able bear holding a shape that allows for these eddies of will to form along the looping path while still giving sufficiently rich modes of difference in repetition to provide a satisfaction that always remains outside the reach of easy packaging, standing beyond the object of commodification. 

That’s why the actual siddur needs to be 19 years long, so we can be free in our own unenclosed commons with right-to-roam unabridged.
