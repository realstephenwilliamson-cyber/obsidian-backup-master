# Rashi 42 - Matot

← [[Rashi 41 - Pinchas]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 43 - Masei]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-2|Numbers 30:2–32:42]]* · Text: [[Torah 42 - Matot]]

## Chapter 30

### 30:2

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-2|Numbers 30:2]]

**The tribal leaders.** He bestowed honor on the leaders by teaching them first, and afterward, all of Bnei Yisroel. How do we know that the other discourses were so? The Torah therefore teaches, “. . . they returned to him— Aharon and all the leaders of the congregation— and Moshe spoke to them. Afterward, all of Bnei Yisroel approached.” Why, then, did he see fit to say this here? He taught that vows may be nullified by a single expert. When there is no single expert, nullification is by three laymen. But perhaps Moshe recited this chapter only to the leaders? “This is the word” is said here, and it is said concerning slaughtering outside [the Temple], “this is the word.” Just as that was said to Aharon and his sons, and all of Bnei Yisroel, as it is said, “speak to Aharon, etc.,” so was this, said to all of them.

**This is the word.** Moshe prophesied with “this is what Adonoy has said, about the time of midnight. . .,” and the [other] prophets prophesied with “this is what Adonoy has said:” Moshe surpassed them, as he prophesied with the phrase “this is the word.” Another interpretation: “This is the word” is exclusionary, teaching that the Sage [annuls] with the term “release,” and the husband with the term “nullification,” following the Scriptural language here. If they reverse the terminology, there is neither release nor nullification.

### 30:3

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-3|Numbers 30:3]]

**A vow.** By saying, ‘Let it be as a *konam* to me, that I will not eat, or I may not do a certain thing.’ But perhaps, even if he swears to eat the meat of an animal that was not ritually slaughtered, I read, concerning him, “all that he has verbalized, he must fulfill”? The Torah, therefore, teaches, “. . .to initiate a prohibition”— to prohibit what is permitted, but not to permit what is prohibited.

**He may not profane his word.** Like Gא יְחַלֵּל דְבָרוֹ, he may not secularize his word.

### 30:4

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-4|Numbers 30:4]]

**In her father’s home.** Under her father’s jurisdiction, even if she is not actually in his home.

**While still in her youth.** But not a minor, or a “*bogeres,*” as a minor’s vows are invalid, and a *bogeres* is no longer under her father’s jurisdiction for nullification of vows. When is she considered a minor? Our Rabbis say, at the age of eleven and a day, her vows should be examined. If she recognizes in Who’s Name she took the vow, or in Who’s Name she made the sanctification, then her vow is valid. At the age of twelve and a day, examination is no longer required.

### 30:6

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-6|Numbers 30:6]]

**But if her father restrained her.** He restrained her from the vow, in other words, he nullified it. I would not know what this הֲנָאָה means, but when it says, “but if, on the day her husband hears about it, יָנִיא אוֹתָהּ וְהֵפֵר, he restrains her and nullifies,” you may conclude that הֲנָאָה means nullification. Its plain meaning is to restrain and turn aside. Similarly, “why do you turn [the people’s hearts] away,” and “let the oil on my head not turn my head,” and “you will know My turning aside” [i.e. the punishment] for turning away from Me.”

**Adonoy will forgive her.** To whom does Scripture refer? To a woman who took the Nazirite vow. Her husband heard and nullified it, but she was not aware. She then violated her vow by drinking wine and defiling herself by contact with a corpse. She is in need of forgiveness, although it was nullified. Now, if those who[se vows] have been nullified require forgiveness, certainly those who[se vows] have not been nullified.

### 30:7

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-7|Numbers 30:7]]

**If she is [married] to a man.** This refers to an “*arusah.*” But perhaps to a “*nesuah?*” When it says, “and if she took the vow in her husband’s home,” a “*nesuah*” is mentioned, so this must mean an “*arusah.*” It is intended to distinguish her, in that her father and her husband annul her vow. If the father annulled, but not the husband, or the husband, but not the father, there is no annulment, and it goes without saying, if one of them endorsed it.

**And her vows were upon herself.** She took the vows in her father’s home, but her father did not hear them — they were neither annulled nor endorsed.

### 30:8

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-8|Numbers 30:8]]

**And her husband heard, etc.** Here you have [the ruling] that if the husband endorses, it stands.

### 30:9

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-9|Numbers 30:9]]

**He then annuls her vow which was upon her.** Perhaps even if the father did not annul? The Torah therefore teaches, “in her youth, while in her father’s house” — all through her youth, she is under her father’s jurisdiction.

### 30:10

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-10|Numbers 30:10]]

**Everything she prohibited for herself shall remain incumbent upon her.** Since she is neither under the jurisdiction of the father, nor the jurisdiction of the husband. Scripture refers here to a widow after “*nisuin,*” but a widow after “*erusin,*” when the husband dies, his jurisdiction transfers to the father.

### 30:11

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]]

**But if she took the vow in her husband’s home.** Scripture refers to a “*nesuah.*”

### 30:14

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-14|Numbers 30:14]]

**Every vow, and every oath of self-denial, etc.** Since it was said that the husband may annul, perhaps all vows are included; the Torah therefore teaches, “of self-denial.” He may only annul vows which are of self-denial They are defined in tractate Nedarim.

### 30:15

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-15|Numbers 30:15]]

**From one day to the next.** So that you should not say [he may annul] for twenty four hours — this is why “from day to day” is said, to teach that he may only annul until nightfall.

### 30:16

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-16|Numbers 30:16]]

**After having heard.** After he heard and endorsed, saying, “I approve,” he recanted and annuled it, even during the same day.

**He must bear her transgression.** He must take her place. We learn from here that one who causes another’s downfall, must bear full punishment in his place.

## Chapter 31

### 31:2

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-2|Numbers 31:2]]

**Against the Midianites.** But not against the Moavites, for the Moavites entered the matter out of fear. They were afraid of them, [fearing] that they would plunder them, as it only says, “do not provoke them with warfare.” The Midianites, however, were enraged over a dispute which was not theirs. Another interpretation: for the sake of two virtuous doves whom I must extract from them — Rus the Moavite, and Na’amah the Ammonite.

### 31:3

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-3|Numbers 31:3]]

**Moshe spoke, etc.** Although he heard that his demise depended on the matter, he joyfully implemented it, without delay.

**Arm.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, meaning the same as חֲלוּצֵי צָבָא, “armed.”

**Men.** Righteous men. Similarly, “choose men for us,” and, “men who are wise and renowned.”

**Revenge of Adonoy.** For, when anyone stands against Yisroel, it is as if he stands against the Holy One, Blessed is He.

### 31:4

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-4|Numbers 31:4]]

**For all of the tribes of Yisroel.** This includes the tribe of Levi.

### 31:5

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-5|Numbers 31:5]]

**They were handed over.** This informs you of the praiseworthiness of the shepherds of Yisroel, of how cherished they are by Yisroel. Before they heard about his death, what did he say? “a while more and they will stone me”! Yet, when they heard that the death of Moshe depended on the vengeance against Midian, they refused to go until they were handed over, against their wishes.

### 31:6

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-6|Numbers 31:6]]

**Them, and Pinchas.** This teaches that Pinchas was equivalent to all of them. But why did Pinchas go, while Elozor did not? The Holy One, Blessed is He, said, “The one who began the *mitzvah* by executing Kozbi, the daughter of Tzur, should complete it.” Another interpretation: He sought to avenge Yoseif, his maternal forefather, as it is said, “the Midianites sold him. . .” How is it derived that the mother of Pinchas was [descended] from Yoseif? Since it is said, “of the daughters of Putiel (פּוּטִיאֵל).” Of the children of Yisro, who fattened (פִּיטֵם) calves for idol worship, and of the children of Yoseif, who scorned (פִּטְפֵּט) his evil inclination. Another interpretation: He was annointed for warfare.

**With the sacred vessels.** This refers to the Ark and the Golden Headplate. Because Bil’am accompanied them, and caused the Midianite kings to fly through sorcery, he himself flying with them, he [Pinchas] displayed the Golden Headplate to them, in which the Divine Name was engraved, and they fell. This is why it is said, “. . .on those they had slain,” concerning the Midianite kings — they fell from the air on the slain ones. Similarly, concerning Bil’am, it is written, “. . .upon those they had slain” in the book of Yehoshua.

**In his hand.** In his possession. Similarly, “he took his entire country מִיָדוֹ, from his possession.”

### 31:8

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-8|Numbers 31:8]]

**[The] five kings of Midian.** Can I not see that Scripture enumerates five? Why, then, is it necessary to say “five”? Evidently, to teach that they all conspired equally, and they were all punished equally. Bil’am went there to receive the reward for the twenty-four thousand whom he felled from Yisroel, through his advice. He went from Midian to the Bnei Yisroel, and offered them evil advice. he said to them, “If when you were six hundred thousand you could not overcome them, and now you intend to fight them with twelve thousand?’ They gave him his reward, in full without stinting.

**With the sword.** He set upon Yisroel, exchanging his craft for theirs. For they are only victorious through verbal expression, through prayer and supplication. He came and took up their craft, to curse them verbally. So they confronted him as well by exchanging their craft for that of the gentiles, who come with the sword, as it is said, “you shall live by your sword.”

### 31:10

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-10|Numbers 31:10]]

**Their palaces.** The place of their guardians. The word (טִירֹתָם) denoting dwelling places for monks proficient in their laws. Another interpretation: The word denotes dwelling places for their ministers, as Targum renders “the P’lishtite ministers,” טוּרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתָּאֵי .

### 31:11

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-11|Numbers 31:11]]

**They took all the booty, etc.** This teaches that they were conscientious and righteous, beyond suspicion of theft through confiscating the booty without permission, as it is said, all the booty, etc. Concerning them, Scriptural tradition states explicitly, “your teeth are like a flock of sheep, etc.” — even the warriors among you are all righteous.

**Booty.** This refers to moveable property consisting of clothing and ornaments.

**Plunder.** [Refers to] plunder of moveable property, that is non-ornamental.

**All that had been seized.** Men and beasts. When “captives” is written next to “spoils,” then “captives” refers to men, and “spoils” to beasts.

### 31:13

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-13|Numbers 31:13]]

**Moshe and Elozor the kohein went out.** Because they saw the Israelite youths going out to seize some of the plunder.

### 31:14

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-14|Numbers 31:14]]

**Moshe was angry with the commanders of the army.** Who were appointed over the army. This teaches that the entire corruption of the generation is attributed to the great men, since they have the power to rebuke.

### 31:16

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-16|Numbers 31:16]]

**They were the very same ones.** This informs us that they recognized them — “this is the one through which so-and-so had his downfall.”

**On Bil’am’s advice.** He said to them, “Even if you gather together all the multitudes of the world, you will be unable to conquer them. Perhaps you are more numerous than the Egyptians, who had six hundred choice chariots? Come, I shall advise you. Their god despises promiscuity, etc.,’ as stated in “Chelek,” and in Sifri.

### 31:17

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-17|Numbers 31:17]]

**And every woman with intimate knowledge of a man.** Capable of intimate relations, even if she never actually had relations. They brought them before the Golden Headplate — and those capable of relations — their faces turned sickly green.

**You shall execute.** Why is this repeated? To separate the matter— these are R’ Yishmael’s remarks. For, when I read, “execute every male among the children, and every woman with intimate knowledge of a man, and all the children among the females, etc.,” I do not know [concerning every woman with intimate knowledge of a man] whether to execute [them] with the males, or allow them to live with the children. This is why “[you must] execute” is said.

### 31:19

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-19|Numbers 31:19]]

**Outside the camp.** So that they would not enter the Sanctuary courtyard.

**Whoever has killed a person.** R’ Meir said, “Scripture refers to a person who kills with an object capable of receiving contamination, and Scripture teaches that a utensil defiles a person when it is in contact with a corpse as if he had touched the corpse itself. Or, perhaps even if he shot an arrow at him and killed him? The Torah, therefore, teaches, “and whoever touched a corpse.” It compares one who kills with one who touches. Just as the one who touches (receives contamination) through contact, so does the one who kills (receives contamination) through contact.”

**Purify yourselves.** With sprinkling water, as prescribed for others who were defiled by contact with the dead. For, even those who rule that Gentile graves do not convey contamination in a tent, — as it is said, “you, my sheep, my pastoral sheep, you are man”; you are entitled “man,” he admits that gentiles convey contamination through touching and carrying. Since “man” was only said in the context of tent contamination, as it is said, “a person who dies in a tent.”

**You and your captives.** Not that the gentiles receive contamination and require sprinkling. Rather, just as you are members of the convenant, so with your captives, when they enter the covenant — should they become contaminated, they will require sprinkling.

### 31:20

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-20|Numbers 31:20]]

**Everything made of goat products.** This includes utensils made from the horns, hoofs, and bones.

### 31:21

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-21|Numbers 31:21]]

**Elozor the *kohein* said, etc.** Because Moshe fell into a state of anger, he fell into a state of error, so concealed from him, were the laws concerning purging the utensils of gentiles. You find a similarity on the eighth day of inauguration, as it is said, “he was angry with Elozor and with Isamar”— he fell into a state of anger, [and] he fell into a state or error. Similarly with “listen, now, rebels”! “he struck the rock.” Because of anger, he erred.

**Which Adonoy commanded, etc.** He credited the teaching to his master.

### 31:22

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-22|Numbers 31:22]]

**Only the gold, etc.** Although Moshe did not warn you except for the laws of ritual uncleanliness, you must be warned further, concerning the laws of purging. “Only” denotes exclusion, as if to say, ‘you are excluded from using the utensils, even after their purification from contamination by the dead, until they are purified from the absorption of forbidden “*neveilah*” meat.’ Our Rabbis remark that “only the gold, etc.” teaches that one must remove its rust before purging it. This is the connotation of the word “only” — there should be no rust there, only the metal, exactly as it is.

### 31:23

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-23|Numbers 31:23]]

**Anything placed on a fire.** In which to cook something.

**You must pass through fire.** It must be purged the way it was used. Something used with heated water, one purges with heated water; something used for roasting, like a spit or a grill, one brings to white heat with fire.

**However, it must be purified with sprinkling water.** According to the plain interpretation, this purification is to cleanse it from the contamination of the dead. He said to them, ‘The utensils require purging to purify them from prohibited food, and purification, to cleanse them from ritual contamination.’ And the Rabbis extrapolate here, that even for purification from prohibited [food contamination], metal utensils require immersion, and מֵי נִדָּה written here they expound as referring to water suitable for the immersion of the “*niddah.*” How much is this? Forty *seah.*

**But anything that was not placed on a fire.** Anything not used with fire, such as cups [and jugs], and flasks, which are used for cold food, and, therefore, did not absorb prohibited [food].

**You may pass through water.** Immerse them, and it is sufficient. This is only with metal utensils.

### 31:24

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-24|Numbers 31:24]]

**Into the camp.** To the camp of the *Shechinah* [only], for someone contaminated by the dead need not be banished from the camp of the Levites and the camp of the Israelites.

### 31:26

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-26|Numbers 31:26]]

**Take count (lit. count the heads).** Make an accounting.

### 31:27

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-27|Numbers 31:27]]

**Divide in half the spoils seized among those who took the war in hand, etc.** Half for these, and half for these.

### 31:32

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-32|Numbers 31:32]]

**The spoil was — in addition to the booty.** Because they were not commanded to contribute the tax from the moveable property, but from the spoil, the phrase was written this way. ‘the spoil, which would be included in the distribution and included in the tax, which remained after the plunder of personal property that the warriors plundered for themselves without distributing — [that spoil] was; the number of sheep, etc.’

### 31:42

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-42|Numbers 31:42]]

**From the half [designated] for Bnei Yisroel that Moshe divided.** For the community, by appropriating it for them from the military men.

### 31:43

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-43|Numbers 31:43]]

**The community’s half consisted of.** So much and so much.

### 31:47

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-47|Numbers 31:47]]

**Moshe took, etc.**

### 31:48

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-48|Numbers 31:48]]

**The officers.** Those who were appointed.

### 31:49

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-49|Numbers 31:49]]

**וְלֹא־נִפְקַד . Not [a man is] missing.** Targum [Onkelos] renders לֹא שְׁגָא . This (שְׁגָא), too, denotes, in Aramaic, “missing,” like “I [will suffer] its loss,” rendered by Targum, דַּהֲוָת שָׁגְיָא מִמִּנְיָנָא, “whatever is missing from the tally.” Similarly, “for your seat will be missing” — your seating place will be missing, the one who usually sits there. Similarly, “ וַיִפָּקֵד מְקוֹם דָּוִד,” his place was missing — and no one was sitting there.

### 31:50

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^31-50|Numbers 31:50]]

**Anklet.** This refers to leg bands.

**Bracelet.** On the arm.

**Ring.** Ear rings.

**Clasp.** Shaped around the crotch, to atone for their sinful thoughts concerning the daughters of Midian.

## Chapter 32

### 32:3

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-3|Numbers 32:3]]

**Ataros, Divon, etc.** They were part of the land of Sichon and Og.

### 32:6

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-6|Numbers 32:6]]

**Should your brothers.** This denotes a question.

### 32:7

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-7|Numbers 32:7]]

**Why do you dishearten.** Turn aside and restrain their hearts from crossing, for they will think that you are afraid to cross because of the war, and the strength of the cities and the people.

### 32:8

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-8|Numbers 32:8]]

**From Kadeish Barnei’a.** This was its name. There were two places named Kadeish.

### 32:12

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-12|Numbers 32:12]]

**The Kenizite.** He was Kaleiv’s stepfather, to whom Kaleiv’s mother bore Osniel.

### 32:13

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-13|Numbers 32:13]]

**He made them wander.** He moved them about, as in “a wanderer and vagrant”

### 32:14

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-14|Numbers 32:14]]

**To add.** As in “add a year to a year,” “add your burnt-offerings, etc.” The word denotes addition.

### 32:16

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-16|Numbers 32:16]]

**We will build [enclosures] for our flocks here.** They were more concerned over their property than [over] their sons and daughters, for they gave precedence to their flocks over their children. Moshe said to them, ‘not so! Treat what is of major importance as such, and what is secondary as such. First build yourselves cities for your children, and afterward pens for your flocks.’

### 32:17

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-17|Numbers 32:17]]

**We will then arm ourselves rapidly.** We will arm ourselves quickly, as in “quicken the spoil, hasten the plunder,” “let him hurry, hasten [his actions].”

**Before Bnei Yisroel.** At the head of the armies, since they were great warriors, as it is said concerning Gad, “he shall tear arms, skulls as well.” Moshe, too, reiterated specifically to them, in “These are the words”: “I commanded you at that time, etc., go forth armed before your brothers, Bnei Yisroel, all [you] great warriors.” And at Yericho it is written, “the armed force went before them” — they were [the sons of] Reuven and Gad, who fulfilled their condition.

**Our children will dwell.** When we are with our brothers.

**In fortified cities.** Which we shall build now.

### 32:19

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-19|Numbers 32:19]]

**Across the Yardein and beyond.** On the western bank.

**For, our inheritance came to us.** We have already received it, on the eastern bank.

### 32:24

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-24|Numbers 32:24]]

**For your sheep.** This word is conjugated from 'צֹנֶה', “all the sheep and cattle.” It does not have the “ אָלֶ"ף “ intervening between the “ נוּ"ן “ and “ צָדִ"י “ and the “ אָלֶ"ף “ which appears here after the “ נּוּ"ן “ is instead of the “ הֵ"א “ of צֹנֶה . I learned this from R’ Moshe Hadarshan’s commentary.

**But what you have expressed verbally,** you must fulfill. For the sake of Heaven, as you pledged to cross and do battle until the conquest and apportionment [of the land]. Moshe had only requested of them, “the land will be conquered, etc. and after you may return” but they pledged “until [Bnei Yisroel] have inherited” — so they added that they would remain for the seven years of apportionment, and they did so.

### 32:25

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-25|Numbers 32:25]]

**The tribe of Gad said.** All of them as one man.

### 32:28

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-28|Numbers 32:28]]

**[Moshe] commanded, to them.** Like עֲלֵיהֶם, concerning them and their stipulation, he appointed Elozor and Yehoshua, as in “God will fight for you.”

### 32:32

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-32|Numbers 32:32]]

**And we shall [then] have our permanent inheritance.** As if to say, ‘in our hands, in our possession, will be our hereditary territory on this side.’

### 32:36

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-36|Numbers 32:36]]

**Fortified cities and sheep pens.** The end of this passage is related to the begning ofhe subject — “the sons of Gad built” these cities, to serve as fortified cities and sheep pens.

### 32:38

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-38|Numbers 32:38]]

**Nevo, Ba’al Me’on, their names having been changed.** Nevo and Ba’al Meon were names of idols. The Emorites would name their cities after their idols, so the sons of Reuven changed their names in favor of other names. This is the meaning of “their names having been changed” — Nevo and Ba’al Me’on were turned aside in favor of a different name.

**And Sivmah.** They built Sivmah. This is Sevam mentioned above.

### 32:39

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-39|Numbers 32:39]]

**And drove out.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, וְתָרִיךְ, “and ousted,” for the word יָרֹשׁ may be used in two ways, to denote inheritance, or to denote ousting, meaning driving out, expelling.

### 32:41

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-41|Numbers 32:41]]

**Villages.** כַּפְרְנֵיהוֹן, their villages.

**And [he] called them Chavos Yair.** Because he had no children, he called them by his name, as a memorial.

### 32:42

[[Torah 42 - Matot#^32-42|Numbers 32:42]]

**And [he] called it Novach.** לָה does not have a dot in its ה . I saw in R’ Moshe Hadarshan’s commentary: Because this name did not endure, it is therefore silent, implying that it is to be expounded as לָא, “not” [Novach]. But I wonder how he (R’ Moshe) would expound two similar words, וַיֹאמֶר 'לָה' בֹעַז, “Boaz said to her,” לִבְנוֹת לָה בַיִת, “to build her a house.”
