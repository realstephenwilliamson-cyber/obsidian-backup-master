### 13.1–25: Final exhortations

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 487* ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Mutual love*, Gk “philadelphia,” most commonly used of affection between siblings. ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-2|2]]:** *Hospitality to strangers* (Gk “philoxenia”) was a virtue throughout the ancient Mediterranean; see e.g., [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-1|Gen 19.1–3]]; [[Judges 19#^19-19|Judg 19.19–21]]; [[1 Peter 04#^4-9|1 Pet 4.9]]; *Did.* 12.1–2. *Entertained angels*, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-1|Gen 18.1–15]] and *Jub*. 6.11–24; 13. ^13-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-4|4–5]]:** *Marriage . . . money,* commonplace moralisms among Jews and Christians; see [[Matthew 19#^19-4|Mt 19.4–5]]; [[Mark 10#^10-6|Mk 10.6–8]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-22|Eph 5.22–23]]; Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.199–203. *Never . . . forsake*, see [[Torah 52 - Vayeilech#^31-6|Deut 31.6]],[[Torah 52 - Vayeilech#^31-8|8]]; [[Joshua 01#^1-5|Josh 1.5]]. ^13-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-6|6]]:** See [[Psalms 118#^118-6|Ps 118.6]]. ^13-6

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-7|7]]:** *Leaders,* never named. *Imitate*, resembles Paul’s language; see e.g., [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-16|1 Cor 4.16]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-17|Phil 3.17]]; [[Philippians 04#^4-9|4.9]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-6|1 Thess 1.6–7]]. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-8|8]]:** *Same . . . forever*, intended as a reassurance that Christ’s redeeming power would never wane. ^13-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-9|9]]:** *Strange teachings*, see [[Ephesians 04#^4-14|Eph 4.14]]; [[Colossians 02#^2-8|Col 2.8]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-3|1 Tim 1.3–7]]. *Regulations about food*, vague language, perhaps a reference to Jewish dietary laws or to the issue of meat offered to idols (see Acts 15; 1 Cor 8); for the author, food regulations do not define this community (see 9.9–10). ^13-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-10|10]]:** *Altar*, site of Christ’s sacrifice in the heavenly Temple. ^13-10

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-11|11]]:** The Yom Kippur and red heifer sacrifices were *burned outside the camp*; see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-27|Lev 16.27]]; [[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-2|Num 19.2–3]]. ^13-11

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Outside the city gate*, see [[John 19#^19-17|Jn 19.17–20]]. The analogy between animal sacrifice and Jesus as human sacrifice is explicit. Hebrews does not mention either Pilate or the Sanhedrin as responsible for Jesus’ crucifixion. *Sanctify the people*, see 9.13–14; 10.10,14,29. ^13-12

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Bear the abuse he endured*, share in his sufferings by our own witness (including the possibility of death). ^13-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-14|14]]:** *No lasting city*, see 11.13–16; 12.22–29. *City that is to come*, the heavenly Jerusalem; see 11.10,16; 12.22. ^13-14

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-15|15–16]]:** Prayer, hymns, and good works are described as forms of sacrifice in [[Psalms 027#^27-6|Ps 27.6]]; [[Psalms 050#^50-14|50.14]],[[Psalms 050#^50-23|23]]; [[Psalms 107#^107-22|107.22]]; Tob 4.11; [[LXX Sirach 03#^3-3|Sir 3.3]],[[LXX Sirach 03#^3-20|20]]; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.253,271–72; *Avot de R. Natan* 4. In some rabbinic writings, the daily prayers replace the daily sacrifices; see 5.3n.; 9.22n. ^13-15

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-17|17]]:** *Obey your leaders*, see 13.7. ^13-17

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-18|18]]:** *Clear conscience*, see 10.22. ^13-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-19|19–25]]:** The closing includes typical elements of letters: mention of travel plans, a benediction, final exhortations, and final greetings; cf. Rom 16; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-13|1 Cor 16.13–24]]. ^13-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-19|19]]:** *Restored to you*, reunited. ^13-19-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-20|20–21]]:** Concluding benediction. *God of peace*, similar language is found in Paul; see e.g., [[Romans 15#^15-33|Rom 15.33]]; [[Romans 16#^16-20|16.20]]; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-11|2 Cor 13.11]]; [[Philippians 04#^4-9|Phil 4.9]]. ^13-20

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-20|20]]:** *Great shepherd*, following ancient Mesopotamian and Jewish models, a popular epithet for Jesus as the ideal leader; see [[Matthew 26#^26-31|Mt 26.31]]; [[John 10#^10-11|Jn 10.11]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-4|1 Pet 5.4]]; [[Revelation 07#^7-17|Rev 7.17]], building upon the idea that the Davidic king and God are called “shepherd” in the Tanakh (e.g., Ezek 34; Ps 23). *Eternal covenant*, God’s covenant with Israel is described as eternal; see [[II Samuel 07#^7-13|2 Sam 7.13]],[[II Samuel 07#^7-16|16]]; [[Isaiah 55#^55-13|Isa 55.13]]. ^13-20-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-23|23]]:** *Timothy*, Paul’s companion, see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|1 Cor 4.17]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|2 Cor 1.1]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]; also the recipient of two disputed Pauline letters, 1 and 2 Timothy. [[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1–3]] reports that his mother was Jewish*.* He is the only person mentioned by name in Hebrews (besides Jesus or figures from the Tanakh). *Set free*, presumably from prison (see e.g., [[Philippians 02#^2-19|Phil 2.19–24]], although no NT text details Timothy’s imprisonment). ^13-23

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 13#^13-24|24]]:** *Saints*, see 3.1; 6.10. *Those from Italy*, most likely Italians living abroad. Paul’s Letter to the Romans points to a sizable community of Jesus-followers in Rome by the mid-first century. This verse can suggest that the Romans were the original addressees; see Introduction. ^13-24
