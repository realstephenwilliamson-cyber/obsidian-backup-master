## About

A dialogue by Plato on the correctness of names. Hermogenes holds that names rest on custom and convention; Cratylus, that they are correct by nature. Socrates examines both, and more than half the dialogue is given to his etymologies of Greek words.

- **People:** Socrates; Hermogenes, a member of Socrates' circle, apparently left without an inheritance and present at Socrates' death (*Phaedo* 59b); and Cratylus, a radical Heraclitean whom Aristotle names as a teacher of the young Plato (SEP).
- **Dramatic date:** Wikipedia gives none. The SEP lists Colin Smith's 2022 case for 399 BC without discussing it.
- **Composition:** Wikipedia says most modern scholars place it mostly in the middle period. The IEP groups it with the *Menexenus* and *Meno*, at the end of the early group or the start of the middle, c. 387–380 BC. The SEP reports Ross, Luce and Levin placing it with the *Phaedo*, while Kirk, Mackenzie and Sedley date it otherwise. None of these sources questions its authenticity.
- **Shape (Stephanus pages, after the SEP):** conventionalism and an argument against Protagoras 383a–386e; names as tools with a natural standard 386e–390e; correctness and etymological meaning 390e–394e; the etymologies, many of them Heraclitean, 394e–421c; names as imitations, down to single letters, 421c–427d; criticism of Cratylus' naturalism 427d–435d; a closing argument for forms against universal flux 435d–440e.
- **Outcome:** Socrates allows that convention contributes to the correctness of names (435b) and ends by ranking the study of things above the study of names (Wikipedia). Scholars still disagree whether the dialogue favours convention, a refined naturalism, or something else (SEP).
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia calls Cratylus Plato's first known intellectual influence. Karl Bühler based his 1934 organon model of language on the dialogue, and Gérard Genette (1976) drew on it.
