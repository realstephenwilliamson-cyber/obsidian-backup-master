Background

It is to be noted that though Rāmakaṇtha is arguing against Buddhist positions throughout this last section, he conducts his whole defense of the validity of verbal cognition by appeal to Buddhist notions of what constitutes valid knowledge. He quotes Dharmakīrti three times, each time to support his own paksa, not as part of the pūrvapaksa. Hė takes examples of verbal cognition that the Buddhist may take to be merely mental constructions (vikalpas), and shows that according to Dharmak<īrti’s strictures, they should in fact count as genuine means of knowledge. For example in arguing for the validity of verbal cognitions that arise from scripture, it is the Buddhist criterion of pratibandha that Rāmakaṇtha claims they meet. This illustrates a phenomenon that is repeated elsewhere in Indian Philosophy, namely, the spread of an idea to an opposing tradition as a result of the attempts of the tradition adopting the idea to overcome other views held by its originator.
