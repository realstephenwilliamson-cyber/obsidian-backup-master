**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-1|11.1]]:** *Imitators*, see 4.16n. The verse concludes this section, where Paul discusses his own behavior (10.30,33); imitating teachers’ mundane habits pervades rabbinic thought (*b. Ber.* 62a). ^11-1

### 11.2–16: Gender roles

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-2|Text]], p. 340* ^11-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-2|2]]:** *Handed them*, Jews construed the transmission of tradition as a chain of authority (Josephus, *Ant.* 13.297; *m. Avot* 1.1; see 1.13n.). ^11-2-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Head*, authority or source, referring to Paul’s image of believers as the body of Christ (3.23) or to the order suggested by [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-18|Gen 2.18–25]]; [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-16|3.16]] (see 11.8n.). Philo, citing Plato, understands the head to be the body’s most divine part, its master (*On the Creation* 119; see Plato, *Tim.* 45a; *Leg.* 12.942e). ^11-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-4|4]]:** *Disgraces his head*, see “Head Covering,” p. 340. ^11-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-5|5]]:** The Talmud counts seven female prophets (Sarah, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Huldah, and Esther \[*b*. *Meg.* 14a\]); [[Nehemiah 06#^6-14|Neh 6.14]] mentions a (false) female prophet, Noadiah. *Shaved*, signaling shame (Aristophanes, *Thesm.* 837; Tacitus, *Germ.* 19; *T. Job* 23.7–10; 24.10) or pagan cultic practices. Biblical and Second Temple period Jewish texts show women praying in diverse contexts ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-20|Ex 15.20–21]]; Judg 5; 1 Sam 2; LXX [[DRB Esther 14#^14-3|Esth 14.3–19]]; [[LXX Tobit 03#^3-11|Tob 3.11–15]]; [[LXX Judith 09#^9-2|Jdt 9.2–14]]; Sus 13.35, 42–43; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 03#^3-19|2 Macc 3.19–20]]; Philo, *Cont*. 66; 80; 87–89; *Jub.* 25.15-23; *L. A. B.* 31.5; 33.4–5, 40.5–7; 42.2). ^11-5

> [!note] Head Covering *(p. 340)*
> The evidence of veiling in the biblical period is uncertain. Roman women covered their hair in public (except during mourning, weddings, and certain festivals) as a sign of modesty and to indicate their respectable status as protection against solicitation. Such veiling was also normative for Jewish married women (LXX \[Theodotion\] Dan 13.32; *Avot de R. Natan B* 9.25; 42.117; *m. Ketub.* 2.1; 7.4; *m. Shabb*. 6.6; *b. Ketub.* 72a-b). The veil is prominent in pagan and Jewish marriages, where the groom’s veiling of the bride signified her change of status, and his private unveiling of her symbolized consummation ([[Torah 05 - Chayei Sara#^24-65|Gen 24.65–67]]; Poll., *Onom.* 3.36; Catullus, *Carm.* 61.8; Claud., *Fesc*. 4.2–3; *Epithal.* 10.282–85; Ovid *Metam*. 10.1–10; *Ex. Rab.* 41.6; *Tanh.* Ki Tissa 112 *b. Sot.* 49a). Uncovering or shaving a woman’s head were forms of shaming, punishment, or mourning in biblical and later culture ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^5-18|Num 5.18]]; [[Isaiah 03#^3-17|Isa 3.17–24]]; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 04#^4-6|3 Macc 4.6]]; *m. Sot.* 1.5).
>
> Paul recommends customs consonant with both Jewish and Roman social norms: what is appropriate for women is inappropriate for men and vice versa (11.14–15). Roman and Jewish priests traditionally covered their heads when in the divine presence ([[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^28-36|Ex 28.36–40]]; [[Ezekiel 44#^44-18|Ezek 44.18–20]]; Plutarch, *Quaest. rom*. 10; *Mor*. 266C). However, non-priests did not cover their heads when viewing (or, in the case of Romans, performing) sacrifices. Paul mandated this non-priestly practice for all males (11.4), perhaps to preserve a sense of unity or to avoid pagan ritual associations. Rabbinic tradition describes male head covering (Heb *sudaraʾ*) as a “crown of glory” and as signifying the “fear of heaven” (*b. Ber.* 60b, quoting [[Psalms 008#^8-6|Ps 8.5]]; *b. Shabb.* 156b; see also *b. Qidd.* 31a). Widespread wearing of head coverings by Jewish men is a post-Talmudic custom. Some Jewish women began wearing wigs instead of head coverings in the sixteenth century (*Shulchan Arukh*, *Orech Chaim* 75, 2).

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-6|6]]:** *Cut off her hair*, to deter sexual attraction, particularly from angels (see 11.10n.). *Disgraceful*, in Germany and Cyprus, adulteresses were shorn (Tacitus, *Germ.* 19; Dio Chrys., 2 *Fort.* \[*Or.* 64\] 3). ^11-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-7|7]]:** *Image*, [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-1|Gen 5.1]] describes Adam but not Eve as a physical likeness of God (see 11.8n.; contrast [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-26|Gen 1.26–27]])*. Reflection*, lit., “glory” (Gk “doxa,” meaning “\[good\] repute,” in LXX for Heb “kavod,” “glory” \[e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-7|Ex 16.7]]\]). The image of God’s “glory” resting on men’s heads like a crown, commonly represented in art as a nimbus or halo, appears in Jewish writings ([[Psalms 008#^8-6|Ps 8.5]]\[LXX 8.6\]; [[LXX Baruch 05#^5-2|Bar 5.2]]; 4Q491 frag. 11; *1 En.* 62.15-16; *b. Ber.* 17b; *b. Sanh.* 111b; *b. Meg.* 15b; see 15.40). ^11-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-8|8]]:** [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-21|Gen 2.21–23]]. As God was the prototype for Adam, Adam was the prototype for Eve (Philo, *QG* 1; 4; 27–28). Paul, like some of his contemporaries, regards Adam as the perfect original and Eve as derivative (Philo, following Plato, *On the Creation* 46 \[134\]; *Leg. all.* 1.12 \[31–33\]; 2.13 \[44\]; *QG* 1,25,43; see also *Gen. Rab.* 8.9,12; 17.4; 18.2; *b. Nidd.* 31b). ^11-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-9|9]]:** *For the sake of*, [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-18|Gen 2.18]],[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-20|20]],[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-24|24]]. ^11-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-10|10]]:** *Authority*, concealing her derivative glory allowed her to approach God in worship, either to reposition her dependent status from her husband to God, or to avoid the lust of *angels* participating in and assisting worship ([[Psalms 138#^138-1|Ps 138.1]] LXX; lustful angels, like the “watchers,” are mentioned in 1 *En.* 6–11; *T. Naph.* 3.5; *Tg. Ps. -J.* to [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-2|Gen 6.2]]). ^11-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-11|11]]:** *Man*, or husband (Gk “anēr”)*. Woman,* or wife (Gk “gynē”), see 7.3-4. ^11-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-12|12]]:** *Comes through*, birthed by. ^11-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-14|14–15]]:** *Nature*, or sexual characteristic (Sophocles, *Trach*. 1062; Plato, *Leg.* 770d), creation’s established order to distinguish male and female hair styles, corresponding to Roman custom (see 11.3n.; Ovid, *Ars* 1.505,518; Cicero, *Cat*. 2.22–24; Seneca the Elder, *Controversiae.* 2, preface 2; Martial, *Epigrams* 10.65.8). *Degrading*, see Philo, *Spec. Laws* 3.37–38; Pseudo-Phocylides, *Sent.* 210–12. ^11-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-15|15]]:** *Covering*, long enough to cover her bosom (see *Tg. Ps. -J.* to [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-2|Gen 6.2]]; *m*. *Sot.* 1.5). ^11-15

### 11.17–34: The Lord’s Supper

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-17|Text]], p. 341* ^11-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-17|17]]:** *Come together,* to share the Lord’s Supper. See “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674, and “Food and Table Fellowship,” p. 650. ^11-17-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-18|18]]:** *Divisions*, for the Lord’s Supper, Paul disapproves of following Roman and Jewish dining customs, which reserved the best seats and the finest food and drink for the most esteemed guests; the poorest and least influential received inferior treatment (Horace *Sat*. 8; Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.1.297–700; *t. Ber*. 4.8; 5.5–8; *y. Ber*. 8.2). ^11-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-19|19]]:** *Genuine* (dokimos), proved righteous. ^11-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|23]]:** *Received* . . . *handed on*, see 11.2n. *Betrayed* (Gk “paradidōmi”), lit., “handed over.” Paul refers to God’s handing over Jesus to death ([[Romans 04#^4-25|Rom 4.25]]; [[Romans 08#^8-32|8.32]]) rather than attributing the act to Judas ([[Matthew 10#^10-4|Mt 10.4]]; [[Matthew 27#^27-3|27.3]]; [[Mark 03#^3-19|Mk 3.19]]; [[Mark 14#^14-10|14.10]]; [[Luke 06#^6-16|Lk 6.16]]; [[John 12#^12-4|Jn 12.4]]). In all of Paul’s uses of the term, the primary referent is divine action. Jewish writings sometimes interpret vicarious suffering or death as redemptive (Isa 53; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 06#^6-27|4 Macc 6.27–29]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 17#^17-20|17.20–22]]; *T. Benj.* 3.8; 1QS 5.6; 8.3; 9.4; *Mek. Pisḥa* 1 on [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-1|Ex 12.1]]; *Lev. Rab.* 20.12; *b. Moʾed Qat.* 28a; compare Heb “sekharan shel tzaddiqim” \[“reward of the righteous”\] in *Gen. Rab.* 62.2; see also *b. Shabb*. 33b; *b. Ketub*. 8b; *b. Qidd.* 40b). ^11-23

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-24|24–25]]:** *Thanks*, blessing before the meal (see [[Luke 22#^22-19|Lk 22.19]]). In Jewish practice, blessing before the meal (Heb “birkat ha-motzi,” “benediction on bringing forth \[bread\]”) is complemented by the blessing after the meal, *after supper* (see 10.17n.). *This is my body*, Jesus’ words at the last supper (see 5.7n.; 10.3,16–17nn.; [[Matthew 26#^26-26|Mt 26.26]] || [[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22]] || [[Luke 22#^22-19|Lk 22.19]]; see also [[John 06#^6-51|Jn 6.51]],[[John 06#^6-54|54–55]]). *New covenant*, see [[Jeremiah 31#^31-31|Jer 31.31]]; LXX 38.31; in [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-6|2 Cor 3.6]] Paul uses the phrase to contrast the “letter” of the Law vs. the “spirit” (see also [[Luke 22#^22-20|Lk 22.20]]). Covenants were sometimes sealed with blood sacrifices (see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-10|Gen 15.10–18]]; [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^31-54|31.54]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-8|Ex 24.8]]; [[Psalms 050#^50-5|Ps 50.5]]). Rabbinic literature links the redemptive aspect of the blood of circumcision to that of the blood of the paschal lamb based on a reinterpretation of [[Ezekiel 16#^16-6|Ezek 16.6]], “in your blood . . . live!” recited during circumcision ceremonies as well as Passover seders (*Tg. Neb.* [[Ezekiel 16#^16-6|Ezek 16.6]]; *b. Eruv.* 19a ). *In remembrance of me*, see [[Luke 22#^22-19|Lk 22.19]], compare Heb “lezikaron” (“reminder”), used in [[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-9|Ex 13.9]] of “tefillin,” phylacteries. ^11-24

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-27|27]]:** *Answerable*, those who partake of the Lord’s Supper in an *unworthy manner* of the moral standards of the gospel will be held accountable at the Final Judgment. ^11-27

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-28|28]]:** *Examine*, in the legal sense of determining the truth (see 3.13; 16.3; [[2 Corinthians 08#^8-8|2 Cor 8.8]]). ^11-28

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-29|29]]:** *Discerning*, distinguishing between regular eating and the Lord’s Supper. ^11-29

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-30|30]]:** *Reason*, improper observance incurs sickness and death. ^11-30

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-31|31]]:** *Judged ourselves*, see 5.12–6.11. ^11-31

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 11#^11-32|32]]:** *Disciplined*, prodded to repent. *Condemned*, anyone who denies both God and God’s saving action will receive eternal punishment ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-5|Wis 3.5–6]]). *manner,* c Other ancient authorities read *the Lord’s body* d Gk *fallen asleep* e Or *When we are judged, we are being disciplined by the Lord* f Gk *brothers* ^11-32
