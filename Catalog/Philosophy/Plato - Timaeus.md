## About

A late dialogue of Plato. After Critias tells the story of ancient Athens and Atlantis, Timaeus gives a long account of how the cosmos, the soul and the human body were made.

- **Characters and setting:** Socrates, Timaeus (whom Wikipedia links to Timaeus of Locri), Critias and Hermocrates; a fourth person from the day before is absent. It is set the day after Socrates' account of an ideal state like the *Republic*'s, at the Panathenaic festival (SEP). No dramatic date is given.
- **Date of composition:** Wikipedia gives c. 360 BC; the IEP puts it in the late group, c. 355–347 BC. G. E. L. Owen argued for a middle-period date, before the *Theaetetus* and *Parmenides*; H. F. Cherniss defended the late date, which the SEP says later stylometric work has largely supported. No source consulted questions its authenticity.
- **Contents (17a–92c):** an opening conversation, then Timaeus' discourse in three parts: the works of intellect, the effects of necessity, and their cooperation in making human beings (SEP). A good craftsman (the Demiurge) orders preexisting chaos after an eternal model; the world soul is made of Sameness, Difference and Being; the receptacle (*chôra*) is a third kind beside forms and their copies; the four elements are built from triangles as regular solids. The account claims only to be likely (*eikôs*).
- **Sequel:** the *Critias*, unfinished. A third work, *Hermocrates*, may have been planned.
- **Place among the dialogues:** Thrasyllus put it in his eighth tetralogy, subtitled "On Nature", with the *Clitophon*, *Republic* and *Critias* (Diogenes Laertius 3.60). The IEP calls it Plato's most detailed work on natural science; the Atlantis myth first appears here.
- **Why it matters:** Aristotle criticized it for implying a beginning of time. Cicero translated part of it (c. 45 BC); Calcidius' 4th-century Latin version made it, the SEP says, the only Plato available in the Latin West until the twelfth century. Thierry of Chartres and William of Conches commented on it, and Kepler drew on it.
