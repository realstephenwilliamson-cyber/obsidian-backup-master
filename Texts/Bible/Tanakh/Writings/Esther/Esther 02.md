## Chapter 2

**2:1** Some time afterward, when the anger of King Ahasuerus subsided, he thought of Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her. ^2-1

**2:2** The king’s servants who attended him said, “Let beautiful young virgins be sought out for Your Majesty. ^2-2

**2:3** Let Your Majesty appoint officers in every province of your realm to assemble all the beautiful young virgins at the fortress Shushan, in the harem under the supervision of Hege, the king’s eunuch, guardian of the women. Let them be provided with their cosmetics. ^2-3

**2:4** And let the maiden who pleases Your Majesty be queen instead of Vashti.” The proposal pleased the king, and he acted upon it. ^2-4

**2:5** In the fortress Shushan lived a Jew by the name of Mordecai, son of Jair son of Shimei son of Kish, a Benjaminite. ^2-5

**2:6** [Kish] had been exiled from Jerusalem in the group that was carried into exile along with King Jeconiah of Judah, which had been driven into exile by King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon. ^2-6

**2:7** He was foster father to Hadassah—that is, Esther—his uncle’s daughter, for she had neither father nor mother. The maiden was shapely and beautiful; and when her father and mother died, Mordecai adopted her as his own daughter. ^2-7

**2:8** When the king’s order and edict was proclaimed, and when many girls were assembled in the fortress Shushan under the supervision of Hegai,[^1] Esther too was taken into the king’s palace under the supervision of Hegai, guardian of the women. ^2-8

**2:9** The girl pleased him and won his favor, and he hastened to furnish her with her cosmetics and her rations, as well as with the seven maids who were her due from the king’s palace; and he treated her and her maids with special kindness in the harem. ^2-9

**2:10** Esther did not reveal her people or her kindred, for Mordecai had told her not to reveal it. ^2-10

**2:11** Every single day Mordecai would walk about in front of the court of the harem, to learn how Esther was faring and what was happening to her. ^2-11

**2:12** When each girl’s turn came to go to King Ahasuerus at the end of the twelve months’ treatment prescribed for women (for that was the period spent on beautifying them: six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and women’s cosmetics, ^2-12

**2:13** and it was after that that the girl would go to the king), whatever she asked for would be given her to take with her from the harem to the king’s palace. ^2-13

**2:14** She would go in the evening and leave in the morning for a second harem in charge of Shaashgaz, the king’s eunuch, guardian of the concubines. She would not go again to the king unless the king wanted her, when she would be summoned by name. ^2-14

**2:15** When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail—the uncle of Mordecai, who had adopted her as his own daughter—to go to the king, she did not ask for anything but what Hegai, the king’s eunuch, guardian of the women, advised. Yet Esther won the admiration of all who saw her. ^2-15

**2:16** Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, in his royal palace, in the tenth month, which is the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign. ^2-16

**2:17** The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she won his grace and favor more than all the virgins. So he set a royal diadem on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti. ^2-17

**2:18** The king gave a great banquet for all his officials and courtiers, “the banquet of Esther.” He proclaimed a remission of taxes[^2] for the provinces and distributed gifts as befits a king. ^2-18

**2:19** [^3]When the virgins were assembled a second time, Mordecai sat in the palace gate. ^2-19

**2:20** But Esther still did not reveal her kindred or her people, as Mordecai had instructed her; for Esther obeyed Mordecai’s bidding, as she had done when she was under his tutelage. ^2-20

**2:21** At that time, when Mordecai was sitting in the palace gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king’s eunuchs who guarded the threshold, became angry, and plotted to do away with King Ahasuerus. ^2-21

**2:22** Mordecai learned of it and told it to Queen Esther, and Esther reported it to the king in Mordecai’s name. ^2-22

**2:23** The matter was investigated and found to be so, and the two were impaled on stakes. This was recorded in the book of annals at the instance of the king. ^2-23

[^1]: Identical with Hege in v. 3.
[^2]: Or “an amnesty.”
[^3]: Meaning of verse uncertain.
