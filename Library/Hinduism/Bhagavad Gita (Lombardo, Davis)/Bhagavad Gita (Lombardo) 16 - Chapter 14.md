## Chapter Fourteen

The Blessed Lord spoke:

\[1\] “I will now go on to declare
the highest of all knowledges,
acquiring which all the sages
have attained utmost perfection.

\[2\] Taking refuge in this knowledge,
attaining identity with Me,
they are not born even at creation
and do not tremble when all dissolves.

\[3\] Great *Brahman* is for Me the womb
in which I place the embryo.
The origin of all beings
arises there, O Arjuna.

\[4\] Whatever forms, Son of Kunti,
issue forth from any womb,
all have great *Brahman* as their womb,
and I am their seeding father.

\[5\] *Sattva*, *rajas*, *tamas—gunas*
material nature has produced—
bind down *Atman* in the body,
though it is imperishable.

\[6\] Of these, *sattva*, pure, untainted,
luminous, free from malady,
binds by attachment to happiness
and to knowledge, O Sinless One.

[p. 81] \[7\] Know that *rajas* is marked by passion
that arises from desire.
This binds down embodied *Atman*
by attachment to activity.

\[8\] *Tamas* is born of ignorance,
confusing all embodied beings,
binding them down with laziness
and torpor, Scion of Bharata.

\[9\] *Sattva*, then, causes attachment
to happiness; *rajas* to action;
and *tamas*, obscuring knowledge,
causes attachment to indolence.

\[10\] *Sattva* arises by subduing
*rajas* and *tamas*; *rajas* by subduing
*sattva* and *tamas*; and *tamas* by
subduing *sattva* and *rajas*.

\[11\] When through all the body’s portals
the light of wisdom starts to shine,
that is when it should be known
*sattva* has become dominant.

\[12\] Avarice and activity,
restlessness and desire
arise in the ascendancy
of *rajas*, Bull of the Bharatas.

\[13\] Gloominess, lack of exertion,
negligence, and confusion
arise in the ascendancy
of *tamas*, O Scion of Kuru.

\[14\] If an embodied being dies
when *sattva* is predominant,
[p. 82] it goes to the pure, stainless worlds
of those with the highest wisdom.

\[15\] One who dies in *rajas* is born
among those attached to action.
Dying while *tamas* is ascendant,
one is reborn from deluded wombs.

\[16\] They say the fruit of good action
is *sattvic* without any stain.
The fruit of *rajas* is suffering,
the fruit of *tamas* ignorance.

\[17\] From *sattva* arises knowledge,
and from *rajas* avarice;
sloth and delusion come from *tamas*
as does ignorance as well.

\[18\] Those established in *sattva* rise;
the *rajasic* stay in the middle;
to the lowest state of *gunas*
far below go the *tamasic*.

\[19\] When the seer perceives the doers
as none other than the *gunas*
and also knows what is higher,
he comes to attain My being.

\[20\] Going beyond these three *gunas*,
which produce the body, freed from
the pain of birth, old age, and death,
*Atman* attains immortality.”

Then Arjuna asked:

\[21\] “One gone beyond these three *gunas*,
by what marks is he recognized?
[p. 83] How does he act? And how, Krishna,
does he transcend these three *gunas*?”

The Blessed Lord answered:

\[22\] “He neither hates nor desires
either the presence or the absence
of brightness or activity
or of delusion, Son of Pandu.

\[23\] Seated apart, dispassionate,
undisturbed by the *gunas*,
calmly watching *gunas* working,
standing firm, never wavering,

\[24\] Self-contained, to whom pain and pleasure,
a clod, a stone, a lump of gold,
loved, and unloved are all equal,
steadfast whether blamed or praised,

\[25\] The same in honor and disgrace,
impartial to friend and enemy,
renouncing all undertakings—
he is said to transcend the *gunas*.

\[26\] And one who serves me steadily
by practicing *Bhakti Yoga*
and transcendence of the *gunas*
soon will be absorbed in *Brahman*.

\[27\] I am indeed the abode of *Brahman*,
of the immortal and the eternal,
and of everlasting *dharma*,
and of bliss that is absolute.”
