**1.** It is natural for a man's character and actions to be influenced by his friends and associates and for him to follow the local norms of behavior. Therefore, he should associate with the righteous and be constantly in the company of the wise, so as to learn from their deeds. Conversely, he should keep away from the wicked who walk in darkness, so as not to learn from their deeds.  
This is [implied by] Solomon's statement ([[Proverbs 13#^13-20|Proverbs 13:20]]): "He who walks with the wise will become wise, while one who associates with fools will suffer." Similarly, \[[[Psalms 001#^1-1|Psalms 1:1]]] states: "Happy is the man who has not followed the advice of the wicked."  
A person who lives in a place where the norms of behavior are evil and the inhabitants do not follow the straight path should move to a place where the people are righteous and follow the ways of the good.  
If all the places with which he is familiar and of which he hears reports follow improper paths, as in our times, or if he is unable to move to a place where the patterns of behavior are proper, because of [the presence of] bands of raiding troops, or for health reasons, he should remain alone in seclusion as [Eichah 3:28] states: "Let him sit alone and be silent."  
If they are wicked and sinful and do not allow him to reside there unless he mingle with them and follow their evil behavior, he should go out to caves, thickets, and deserts [rather than] follow the paths of sinners as \[[[Jeremiah 09#^9-1|Jeremiah 9:1]]] states: "Who will give me a lodging place for wayfarers, in the desert." ^6-1

**2.** It is a positive commandment[^1] to cleave unto the wise and their disciples in order to learn from their deeds[^2] as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-20|Deuteronomy 10:20]]] states: "and you will cling to Him."  
Our Sages[^3] [questioned the nature of this command for] is it possible for man to cling to the Divine Presence?[^4] They [resolved the difficulty,] explaining this commandment to mean: Cleave unto the wise and their disciples.[^5]  
Therefore,[^6] one should try to marry the daughter of a Torah Sage[^7] and marry his daughter to a Torah Sage, eat and drink with Sages,[^8] do business on behalf of Sages,[^9] and associate with them in all possible ways as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-22|Deuteronomy 11:22]]] states: "to cling to Him."  
Similarly, our Sages[^10] have directed [us], saying: "Sit in the dust of their feet and drink in their words thirstily." ^6-2

**3.** Each man is commanded to love each and every one of Israel as himself[^11] as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Leviticus 19:18]]] states: "Love your neighbor as yourself."  
Therefore,[^12] one should speak the praises of [others] and show concern for their money just as he is concerned with his own money[^13] and seeks his own honor.[^14]  
Whoever gains honor through the degradation of a colleague[^15] does not have a share in the world to come.[^16] ^6-3

**4.** Loving a convert[^17] who has come to nestle under the wings of the *Shechinah*[^18] [fulfills] two positive commandments: one for he is [also] included among the "neighbors"[^19] [whom we are commanded to love][^20] and one because he is a convert and the Torah ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-19|Deuteronomy 10:19]]) states: "and you shall love the converts."  
[Thus,[^21] God] has commanded us concerning the love of a convert just as He has commanded us concerning loving Himself as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-1|Deuteronomy 11:1]]] states: "and you shall love God, your Lord."[^22] The Holy One, blessed be He, Himself, loves converts as \[[[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-18|Deuteronomy 10:18]]] states: "and He loves converts."[^23] ^6-4

**5.** Whoever hates a [fellow] Jew in his heart transgresses a Torah prohibition as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Leviticus 19:17]]] states: "Do not hate your brother in your heart."[^24] One is not [liable for] lashes[^25] for violating this prohibition because no deed is involved.[^26]  
The Torah only warns [us] against hating in [our] hearts.[^27] However, a person who beats a colleague or insults him,[^28] although he is not permitted to do so,[^29] does not violate [the prohibition,] "you shall not hate."[^30] ^6-5

**6.** When one person wrongs another, the latter should not remain silent and despise him[^31] as \[[[II Samuel 13#^13-22|II Samuel 13:22]]] states concerning the wicked: "And Avshalom did not speak to Amnon neither good, nor bad for Avshalom hated Amnon."  
Rather,[^32] he is commanded to make the matter known and ask him: "Why did you do this to me?", "Why did you wrong me regarding that matter?" as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Leviticus 19:17]]] states: "You shall surely admonish your colleague."  
If,[^33] afterwards, [the person who committed the wrong] asks [his colleague] to forgive him, he must do so.[^34] A person should not be cruel when forgiving [as implied by [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^20-17|Genesis 20:17]]]: "And Abraham prayed to God..."[^35] ^6-6

**7.** It is a mitzvah[^36] for a person who sees that his fellow Jew[^37] has sinned or is following an improper path[^38] [to attempt] to correct his behavior and to inform him that he is causing himself a loss[^39] by his evil deeds as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Leviticus 19:17]]] states: "You shall surely admonish your colleague."  
A[^40] person who rebukes a colleague - whether because of a [wrong committed] against him or because of a matter between his colleague and God[^41] - should rebuke him privately.[^42] He should speak to him patiently and gently,[^43] informing him that he is only making these statements for his colleague's own welfare,[^44] to allow him to merit the life of the world to come.[^45]  
If he[^46] accepts [the rebuke], it is good; if not,[^47] he should rebuke him a second and third time.[^48] Indeed, one is obligated to rebuke a colleague who does wrong until the latter strikes him[^49] and tells him: "I will not listen."  
Whoever[^50] has the possibility of rebuking [sinners] and fails to do so is considered responsible for that sin,[^51] for he had the opportunity to rebuke the [sinners]. ^6-7

**8.** At first,[^52] a person who admonishes a colleague should not speak to him harshly until he becomes embarrassed[^53] as \[[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Leviticus 19:17]]] states: "[You should]... not bear a sin because of him."[^54] This is what our Sages said:[^55] Should you rebuke him to the point that his face changes [color]? The Torah states:[^56] "[You should]... not bear a sin because of[^57] him."  
From this, [we learn that] it is forbidden for a person to embarrass a [fellow] Jew.[^58] How much more so [is it forbidden to embarrass him] in public.[^59] Even though a person who embarrasses a colleague is not [liable for] lashes[^60] on account of him, it is a great sin.[^61] Our Sages said:[^62] "A person who embarrasses a colleague in public does not have a share in the world to come."  
Therefore,[^63] a person should be careful not to embarrass a colleague - whether of great or lesser stature - in public, and not to call him a name which embarrasses him[^64] or to relate a matter that brings him shame in his presence.[^65]  
When does the above[^66] apply? In regard to matters between one man and another. However, in regard to spiritual matters, if [a transgressor] does not repent [after being admonished] in private,[^67] he may be put to shame in public and his sin may be publicized. He may be subjected to abuse, scorn, and curses until he repents, as was the practice of all the prophets of Israel. ^6-8

**9.** It is pious behavior if a person who was wronged by a colleague would rather not admonish him or mention the matter at all because the person who wronged him was very boorish or because he was mentally disturbed,[^68] [provided] he forgives him totally without bearing any feelings of hate or admonishing him.[^69] The Torah is concerned only with those who carry feelings of hate.[^70] ^6-9

**10.** A person is obligated to show great care for orphans and widows because their spirits are very low and their feelings are depressed.[^71] This applies even if they are wealthy. We are commanded to [show this attention] even to a king's widow and his orphans[^72] as [implied by [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-21|Exodus 22:21]]]: "Do not mistreat any widow or orphan."  
How[^73] should one deal with them? One should only speak to them gently and treat them only with honor. One should not cause pain to their persons with [overbearing] work or aggravate their feelings with harsh words and [one should] show more consideration for their financial interests than for one's own.[^74] Anyone who vexes or angers them, hurts their feelings, oppresses them, or causes them financial loss transgresses this prohibition.[^75] Surely this applies if one beats them or curses them.[^76]  
Even though [a person who violates] this prohibition is not [liable for] lashes,[^77] the retribution one suffers for its [violation] is explicitly stated in the Torah (*ibid.* 22:23): "I will display My anger and slay you with the sword."[^78] There is a covenant between them and He who spoke and created the world[^79] that whenever they cry out because they have been wronged, they will be answered as [*ibid.*:22] states: "When they cry out to Me, I will surely hear their[^80] cry."  
When does the above apply? When one causes them suffering for one's own purposes. However, it is permitted for a teacher to cause them suffering while teaching them Torah, or a craft, or in order to train them in proper behavior. Nevertheless,[^81] he should not treat them in the same manner as he treats others, but rather make a distinction with regard to them and treat them with gentility, great mercy, and honor for \[[[Proverbs 22#^22-22|Proverbs 22:22]]] states: "For God will take up their cause."  
This[^82] applies to both those orphaned from their father and those orphaned from their mother.[^83] Until when are they considered orphans in the context [of this mitzvah]? Until they no longer need a mature individual to support, instruct,[^84] and care for them and are able to see to all their own needs by themselves, like other adults.[^85] ^6-10

[^33]: [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Leviticus 19:17-18]] states:  
    (17) Do not hate your brother in your heart. You must surely admonish your neighbor and not bear a sin because of him. (18) Do not seek revenge or bear a grudge against the children of your people. Love your neighbor as yourself.  
    Each of the clauses in these two verses is halachically significant. The first verse informs us that hatred of a fellow Jew is prohibited (Halachah 5). Then, it explains how one should respond if negative feelings towards a colleague arise (Halachah 6, here). Afterwards, it teaches that one's negative feelings must be expressed in a manner that does not embarrass one's fellow Jew (Halachah 8). Thus, the verse can be rendered: Do not bear hate in your heart, instead, inform your fellow Jew of your complaint, but do so in a manner that is not sinful. (Note the Ramban's commentary on the verse.)  
    The second verse teaches that vengeance or bearing a grudge is forbidden (Chapter 7, Halachot 7-8) and then, concludes with a positive statement obligating us to love our fellow Jew (Halachah 3).
