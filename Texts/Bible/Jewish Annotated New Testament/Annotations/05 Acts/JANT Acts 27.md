### 27.1–44: Sea voyage to Rome

*[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-1|Text]], p. 276* · Paul’s sea voyage to Rome contains many elements common in ancient literature (e.g., Homer, *Od.* ): detailed itineraries, violent storms, shipwrecks, the crew’s flight. Neither human resistance nor forces of nature can prevent Paul from reaching his divinely appointed end (19.21; 23.11). ^27-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-1|1]]:** *We*, the first-person plural, which first appeared at 16.10, resumes and continues through 28.16. *Augustan Cohort*, a military unit stationed in Syria. ^27-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-2|2]]:** *Adramyttium*, port city in northwest Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). *Aristarchus*, see 19.29. ^27-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-3|3–5]]:** The ship sailed north and west along *have seen* d Or *the Christ* e Or *Quickly you will persuade me to play the Christian* the coast of Asia Minor. ^27-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-6|6]]:** *Alexandrian ship*, sailing from the Egyptian city of Alexandria whose cargo was primarily grain (27.38). The city of Rome relied for much of its grain on shipments from Egypt. ^27-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-7|7]]:** *Cnidus*, southwest Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). *Salmone*, the northeast coast of Crete. ^27-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-8|8]]:** *Fair Havens* and *Lasea*, the southern coast of Crete. ^27-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-9|9]]:** *Fast*, Yom Kippur or the Day of Atonement, in late September/early October. Although not explicitly required by biblical law ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-27|Lev 23.27]]), fasting had become a widely practiced ritual performed on this day (Josephus, *Ant.* 17.163; *m. Yoma* 8.1), such that Jews might refer to the day as “the fast” (Philo, *Life of Moses* 2.23). From this time of year until early spring, sailing in open waters was considered dangerous because of the unfavorable prevailing winds. ^27-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-16|16]]:** *Cauda*, close to the southern shore of Crete. ^27-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-17|17]]:** *Syrtis*, waters along the north coast of Africa, between Cyrene and Carthage, known as dangerous for shipping. ^27-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-18|18]]:** *Throw the cargo overboard*, a standard practice to lighten a ship during violent storms; see Jon 1.5; Josephus, *J.W.* 1.280. ^27-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-21|21]]:** *Listened to me*, see v 10. ^27-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-23|23]]:** *Angel of the God*, angels often mark decisive moments in the narrative (5.19; 8.26; 10.3) and offer help to persons in danger (12.7–11). ^27-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-24|24]]:** *Stand before the emperor*, see 9.15; 19.21; 23.11. ^27-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-26|26]]:** *Have to run aground*, Paul’s prediction is fulfilled; see v 41. ^27-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-27|27]]:** *Sea of Adria*, Adriatic Sea. ^27-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-32|32]]:** *Cut away the ropes*, a common method to become unmoored. ^27-32

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-35|35]]:** *Took bread . . . giving thanks*, Paul’s actions mirror those of Jesus, who approaching his death gave thanks over bread ([[Luke 22#^22-19|Lk 22.19]]). The model may be Jewish rituals of reciting blessings before eating bread or meals (see *Ep. Arist.* 185; *m. Ber.* 6.1) and would thus contribute to the portrayal of Paul as an observant Jew; see 22.3; 24.14–16. ^27-35

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-37|37]]:** *Two hundred seventy-six persons,* Alexandrian ships were quite large (Lucian, *Navigium* 5–6; Seneca, *Letters* 77.1). ^27-37

**[[NRSVue Acts 27#^27-41|41]]:** See v 26. 20° 30° 25° 35° **Chs 27–28:** Paul’s journey to Rome. ^27-41
