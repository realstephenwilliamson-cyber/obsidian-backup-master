# James 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT James 02|James 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue James 01|NRSVue]] · [[James 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT James - Introduction]]

### 1.1–8: Importance of faith

*[[NRSVue James 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 490* ^1-1

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-1|1]]:** *James*, Heb “Ya‘aqov,” Gk “Iakobos” which elides to Lat “Iacomus” to Old French “Gemmes” to Middle English “James.” This is a result of a tendency within Greek dialects in transcribing foreign names to drop K sounds and to assimilate B to M (or M to B); coupled with this is the complicated assimilation to European languages where Lat “IA” becomes “G” or “J.” For another example, Heb “Yavneh” elides to Gk “Iamneia” (English: Jamnia). The NRSV tends to translate the Gk “Iakobos” as “Jacob” when referring to “Old Testament” figures, but as “James” when referring to Jesus-followers. *Servant* (lit., “slave”) *of God*, a title, e.g., [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-5|Deut 34.5]]. *Sifre Deut.* 3.24 identifies those who (presumptuously) gave themselves the title (e.g., Solomon in [[I Kings 03#^3-9|1 Kings 3.9]]) and those who received the title from God (e.g., Job in [[Job 02#^2-3|Job 2.3]]). *Lord* (Gk “kyrios”), meaning master, like the Heb “Adonai,” substituted for the tetragrammaton. The received text of James uses a standardized opening formula identifying author and addressee(s); see, e.g., [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]. *Lord Jesus Christ*, joining “Jesus Christ” to “the Lord” breaches Jewish ideas of monotheism (see *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 63#63a|63a]]) by partnering God’s name with “another.” However, this may not, according at least to later Jewish sources, constitute forbidden forms of idolatry for Gentiles *(Tosafot* to *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 63#63b|63b]]). James approves the belief that “God is one” (2.19). *Twelve tribes*, Jesus’ followers, appropriating imagery from Israelite sacred history ([[Ezekiel 37#^37-19|Ezek 37.19–24]] and Josephus, *Ant*. 11.133), pictured the messianic ideal of the return of the exiled tribes being accomplished in the present ([[Acts 15#^15-15|Acts 15.15–21]]), as opposed to (unredeemed) Israel whose tribes remain in captivity. *Dispersion*, Gk “diaspora,” Heb “galut,” “golah,” Jewish communities in Gentile lands. *Greetings* (lit., “rejoice,” [[Philippians 03#^3-1|Phil 3.1]]), a frequent letter salutation (see [[Acts 15#^15-23|Acts 15.23]]); see also Gamaliel’s epistles in *t. Sanh*. 2.6 and *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 11#11b|11b]]: “To our brothers, inhabitants of the Dispersion (*Golah*) . . . Great be your peace always.” ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-2|2]]:** *My brothers*, the opening address of a moral sermon as in *m. Taʾan*. 2.1. *Trials*, see [[Matthew 05#^5-10|Mt 5.10–12]]. ^1-2

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Testing . . . endurance*, Some later rabbinic texts such as *Tanh. Num. “*behaʿalotekha” name four righteous ancestors (Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph) who endured various trials to test their faith. This midrash ends by stating that God “tests the righteous” ([[Psalms 011#^11-5|Ps 11.5]]). ^1-3

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Let endurance*, [[Psalms 034#^34-20|Ps 34.19]] says, “Many are the afflictions of the righteous but the Lord delivers him out of all of them.” [[Proverbs 03#^3-12|Prov 3.12]] notes how God disciplines those whom God loves. *Endurance*, Jewish Wisdom literature praises the themes of endurance and steadfastness as tantamount to one’s love of God: *Pss. Sol.* 14.1 says, “God is faithful to those who truthfully love him, to those who endure his discipline.” [[Psalms 017#^17-3|Ps 17.3]] notes constancy of faith of the righteous when tried. *Be . . . complete*, *Gen. Rab*. 79.5 states that “‘Complete’ means in body, in children, in property . . . in wisdom.” ^1-4

> [!note] Implanted Word *(p. 490)*
> This phrase (v 21) may be a reference to [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-14|Deut 30.14]], “The word is very near to you; it is in your mouth and in your heart for you to observe.” Paul interprets [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-14|Deut 30.14]]: “‘The word is near you, on your lips and in your heart’ . . . because if you confess with your lips that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved” ([[Romans 10#^10-8|Rom 10.8–9]]). Paul understands the
>
> “word” to refer to “Lord Jesus.” “Heart,” for him, refers to “belief.” For some, strong conviction brings about such changes for believers that they will naturally behave in ways in accordance with those convictions ([[Romans 04#^4-21|Rom 4.21]]; [[Hebrews 11#^11-19|Heb 11.19]]); for others, action is required to complete belief ([[James 01#^1-22|Jas 1.22]]). By “implanted word” James means God’s natural moral law described by Scripture as in the “mouth and heart,” the law that needs to be observed (see v 22).

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Wisdom . . . given you*, *y. Ber*. 4.4, “Grace us with wisdom” (followed by) “You have graced us with wisdom.” *Gives to all generously*, [[Proverbs 16#^16-33|Prov 16.33]] sees all things coming from God. The “Amidah” or “standing” prayer praises “God on high \[who\] fully gives tender goodness” (“’El ʾelyon gomel ḥasadim tovim”) and then further requests from God discernment, understanding, and wisdom (“de*ʾ*ah, binah vehaskel”). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Like . . . sea*, an image of inconstancy (cf. Philo, *Migr.* 148, “irresolute . . . like a boat tossed by the wind”). *Midr. Ps*. 119.46 describes those tossed around “who grasp the rope by both ends” looking for salvation, like those wavering between God and Baal ([[I Kings 18#^18-21|1 Kings 18.21]]). See [[Psalms 034#^34-5|Ps 34.4–6]] (HB vv 5–7) for the power of faith. **7–8**: *Double-minded*, *Midr. Mishle* 12.20 refers to two minds (lit., “two kidneys”) that will bend a person toward good but also away from it; two hearts are both good and evil advisers ([[Ecclesiastes 10#^10-12|Eccl 10.12]]; [[Psalms 032#^32-10|Ps 32.10]] \[HB v 11\]). James extols those who have steadfast love, which comes from single-hearted integrity. ^1-6

### 1.9–11: Poor and rich

*[[NRSVue James 01#^1-9|Text]], p. 491* · Jewish texts vary in their attitudes toward wealth. Qumran texts such as the *Damascus Document* 4.17–18, and the *Hodayot* (1QH 18.22–35) and [[Proverbs 01#^1-13|Prov 1.13]] disparage those who gather (ill-gotten) gain (Heb “hon”*)* while [[Proverbs 03#^3-9|Prov 3.9–10]] praises abundance as a reward for giving up one’s first-fruits to God and serving God with acquired wealth (“hon”). See also [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-10|Deut 5.10]]; [[Isaiah 01#^1-17|Isa 1.17–19]]; [[Proverbs 19#^19-17|Prov 19.17]]. While rabbinic literature sees wealth as anxiety producing (*m. Avot* 2.8) and notes that Torah scholars must devote their efforts to Torah study even suffering deprivation (*m. Avot* 6.4 and *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 5#5a|5a]]), it also sees wealth as fitting for righteous scholars (see *Avot* 6.8; *b. Eruv.* [[Eruvin 86#86a|86a]]). In sum, Judaism has no general philosophy of wealth and poverty. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Lowly . . . up* (exaltation), *Num. Rab*. 22 states that God judges fairly, lowering one and raising another ([[Psalms 075#^75-8|Ps 75.7]] \[HB v 8\]). In this world, the true social order of those deserving praise is often reversed (*b. Pesah*. [[Pesachim 50#50a|50a]]). ^1-9-2

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-10|10–11]]:** *And the rich . . . low* (humiliation), see [[I Samuel 02#^2-7|1 Sam 2.7–8]] and for one of many rabbinic examples, see *Tanh. Vay*. 10, citing [[Psalms 035#^35-5|Ps 35.5]]. *Because the rich will disappear*, *Sifre Deut. piska* 31 (on [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-15|Deut 32.15]]: “and Jeshurun waxed fat and kicked . . . forsook God”) is the “locus classicus*”* for the rabbis to show cases where abundance led to sin and perdition, as was said to be the case in the generation of Noah. ^1-10

### 1.12–18: Temptation and good deeds

*[[NRSVue James 01#^1-12|Text]], p. 491* ^1-12

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Blessed . . . temptation*, here as elsewhere this terminology is based on Hebrew usage; e.g., see [[Psalms 001#^1-1|Ps 1.1]], “happy \[or “favored are”\] those” (Heb “ʾashrei haʾish,” Gk “makarios anēr”). “Ashrei” in the sense of being fortunate or blessed commonly appears in biblical (e.g., [[I Kings 10#^10-8|1 Kings 10.8]]) and in Talmudic (e.g., *m. Yoma* 8.9) literature, in DSS (1QS 525.1–4); “makarios,” its equivalent in the LXX, appears in the Gospels (e.g., [[Matthew 05#^5-3|Mt 5.3–12]]). *Temptation* (Gk “peirasmos”) is used in [[James 01#^1-2|Jas 1.2]] (NRSV translates “trials”); see [[Matthew 06#^6-13|Mt 6.13]]; [[Matthew 26#^26-41|26.41]], and elsewhere. *Midr. Tanh*. to [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-15|Deut 23.15]] shows how self-control in the face of temptation leads to eternal life. *Endures temptation*, God testing the righteous is the theme of *Gen. Rab.* 32.32 as well as the prologue to Job. *Crown of life*, according so some rabbinic texts, the righteous wear “crowns” in the next world (signifying spiritual accomplishment) and are able to “see God” (*b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 17#17a|17a]]). See also [[Luke 01#^1-52|Lk 1.52–53]]; [[Luke 06#^6-20|6.20–25]]. *Crown*, wreath given to athletes, soldiers, and leaders. ^1-12-2

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-13|13–14]]:** *No one . . . should say . . . tempted by one’s own desire*, [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-39|Num 15.39–40]] warns against following personal lusts, while the commandments of God are meant to prevent sin. **15**: *Desire . . . sin . . . death*, *Kallah Rabbati* 2.6 remarks that lustful thoughts lead to sinful actions and sin kills (also, *b. Ber.* [[Berakhot 33#33a|33a]]). Rabbinic literature contains many references to “temptation” and the “evil desire.” For example, *b. Sukk*. [[Sukkah 52#52a|52a]] compares the righteous winning the struggle against temptation to one who conquers a mountain; *b. Sukk.* [[Sukkah 52#52b|52b]] advises that one can melt and smash the evil urge by engaging in Torah study, suggesting that God will help defeat the evil urge. *B. B. Bat.* 16b equates the “evil urge,” “satan,” and “the angel of death.” ^1-13

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Do not be deceived* anticipates the preacher’s style of admonition in the name of God, *Midr. Tanh*. *Vayetze* 4: “al titeʾu.” ^1-16

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Father of lights*, in the DSS the title “prince” (Heb “sar”) of lights seems to allude to the angel Michael (1QM 13.10; 1QS 3.20; cf. CD A 5.18), but James uses the image of light to allude to the Creator of physical and spiritual lights by which one sees true paths and avoids dangerous obstacles (see also Philo, Dreams 1.112–13). *No variation*, although James may have in mind [[Malachi 3#^3-6|Mal 3.6]], which speaks of God’s unchanging faithfulness to the children of Israel, because of the reference to *shadow*, it is more likely that this language reflects the Platonic notion (adopted by Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.327–329) that eternal forms do not change. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-18|18]]:** *Birth . . . truth*, *Gen. Rab*. 8 (to [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-26|Gen 1.26]]) says that at Creation (sometimes called the “birth” of the world—a phrase in the Jewish New Year liturgy) God convened a committee initially comprised of Truth, Mercy, Righteousness, and Peace, to oversee humanity’s formation. Truth and Peace, foreseeing humanity’s true nature, objected to the creation of humans, and Truth was for a period cast out from the divine council (see [[Psalms 085#^85-11|Ps 85.10–11]] \[HB vv 11–12\]). *First fruits*, [[Jeremiah 02#^2-3|Jer 2.3]] metaphorically defines Israel as God’s first fruits (“reshit”), the choicest of the nations, and *Lev. Rab*. 36.4 reads the first word of [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-1|Gen 1.1]] (Heb “bereshit,” “in the beginning”) with this in mind, implying that the world was created for the sake of Israel. ^1-18

### 1.19–27: Ears, eyes, hands, mouths

*[[NRSVue James 01#^1-19|Text]], p. 492* ^1-19

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Let everyone . . . speak*, see [[LXX Sirach 05#^5-11|Sir. 5.11]]; see *m. Avot* 1.15, “say little and do much.” *Slow to anger*, *m. Avot* 5.11 stresses, “be slow to anger; quick to pacify.” ^1-19-2

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Implanted word*, see “Implanted Word” in side-bar above. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Doers of the word*, for Jews, “doers” refers to performing Torah. Some rabbis (*b. Shabb*. [[Shabbat 88#88a|88a]], see Philo, *Rewards* 79) linked “we will do and we will be obedient” at Sinai ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-7|Ex 24.7]]) to “doers of his word” and “hearers of his word” (cf. [[Psalms 103#^103-18|Ps 103.18–21]]). James restricts the “implanted word’s” imperative to the law of showing mercy, particularly toward widows and orphans, and in general keeping the soul from impurity and iniquity (see further v 27). ^1-22

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-23|23–24]]:** *Mirror . . . immediately forget*, according to *Avot de R. Natan* A 22.2, the saintly Hanina ben Dosa counseled that if one’s thoughts were of greater focus than one’s deeds, the thoughts would eventually perish. ^1-23

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-25|25]]:** *But those who look . . . perfect law . . . liberty*, here to *look* is to meditate on moral laws. *B. Men*. 99b finds great reward for those who constantly meditate on Torah. [[Psalms 019#^19-8|Ps 19.7]] (HB v 8) states: “The law of the Lord is perfect, reviving the soul.” On the description of revealed law (i.e., Torah) in relation to interior laws of the heart, cf. the twelfth-century commentary of Abraham ibn Ezra to [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-17|Deut 30.17–19]]. He sums up the Torah by noting its core tenet: all proper observance depends on the heart and one’s days can be lengthened to enable love. Likewise the law of liberty (2.12) means that the way individuals freely choose to act toward others impacts how God acts toward them (*m. Sot*. 1.7). People are at liberty to choose their path and so to reap punishment or reward (see [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-16|Deut 30.16–17]]). *B. Eruv*. 54a, referring to [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-16|Ex 32.16]] describing the tab- *shadow of turning* b Gk *my beloved brothers* c Gk *at the face of his birth* lets of the Law as God’s writing “engraved,” reads “engraved” \[Heb “ḥarut”\] law as if it said “freedom” \[Heb “ḥeirut”\] law, suggesting that the Torah itself is a law of freedom. ^1-25

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-26|26]]:** *Bridle their tongues*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-16|Lev 19.16]] commands, “You shall not go around as a slanderer among your people.” [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-8|Num 12.8–10]] shows Miriam speaking against Moses and being struck by leprosy. Many Rabbis also noted the ill effects the tongue can cause. For instance, one Rabbi reported in *b. Ber* 32b that the sin of unguarded speech creates ills within the community and even with one’s relationship with God. Typical is *Deut. Rab*. 6.14, which explains that the Temple was destroyed for this sin. Traditional Jews have recited for 1500 years a private petition asking God to guard one’s tongue; the prayer is still recited three times a day at the close of the Amidah. [[Psalms 039#^39-2|Ps 39.2–4]] requires muzzling the mouth so as not to sin with that organ. See [[Proverbs 11#^11-13|Prov 11.13]] for the danger of gossip; see also [[James 03#^3-5|Jas 3.5–6]], and many wisdom texts from the Tanakh for the value of keeping silent. ^1-26

**[[NRSVue James 01#^1-27|27]]:** *Religion*, here, ethical piety as seen by James’s use of “aspilos” or pureness of heart. *God, the Father* (lit., “God and father”), implying that God’s nation of Israel is God’s son; Jews would commonly address God as “our Father” as, e.g., in *b. Taʾan.* 25b (see also [[Matthew 06#^6-9|Mt 6.9]]). *Care for orphans . . . distress*, James restricts the implanted word to the laws like [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-21|Ex 22.22]] mandating care for orphans and widows. [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-19|Deut 27.19]] proclaims “‘Cursed be anyone who deprives the alien, the orphan, and the widow of justice.’ And all the people shall say, ‘Amen!’” Rabbis preached such good works toward others as for example in *m. Taʾan*. 2.1: “God saw their works” (Jon 3.10)—not their mourning rituals. Likewise for James, without acts of lovingkindness, confessing belief is worthless. See e.g., [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-18|Deut 10.18]]. *Unstained by the world*, freedom from worldly entrapments stemming from common desires and jealousies; see 4.4. James may also be troubled by exposure to “pagan” mores and assimilation as are *b. Mo*ʾ*ed Qat*. 16b and *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 59#59a|59a]], where God weeps over God’s children assimilating among the pagan nations. ^1-27
