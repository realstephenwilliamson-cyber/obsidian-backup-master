# Rashi 48 - Shoftim

← [[Rashi 47 - Re'eh]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 49 - Ki Teitzei]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-18|Deuteronomy 16:18–21:9]]* · Text: [[Torah 48 - Shoftim]]

## Chapter 16

### 16:18

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-18|Deuteronomy 16:18]]

**Judges and police officers.** Judges: Magistrates who render legal decisions. Police officers — who compel the people to abide by their instructions by administering corporal punishment with cudgels and whips, until they accept the judge’s decision.

**In all of your cities.** In each and every city.

**For your tribes.** This continues from ‘You shall appoint for yourself’ — “Judges and police officers you shall appoint for yourself for your tribes, in all of your cities which Adonoy, your God, is giving you.”

**For your tribes.** This teaches that judges are to be seated for each and every tribe and in each and every city.

**Who will judge the people, etc.** Appoint competent, righteous judges, to judge righteously.

### 16:19

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-19|Deuteronomy 16:19]]

**Do not pervert justice.** This follows the obvious interpretation.

**Do not display favoritism.** Not even during the arguments. This prohibits the judge from being gentle with one but harsh with the other, or from requiring one to stand while the other is seated; because when one notices the judge deferring to his fellow, his arguments are repressed.

**Do not accept bribery.** Even to adjudicate fairly.

**For bribery blinds.** Once he has accepted his bribe, it is impossible for him not to be partial, and subvert the verdict in his favor.

**Words that are just.** Words that are justified, true justice.

### 16:20

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-20|Deuteronomy 16:20]]

**Pursue absolute justice.** Follow after the pre-eminent tribunal.

**So that you may live and inherit.** There is adequate merit in the appointment of ethical judges to bring life to the Israelites, and settle them on their land.

### 16:21

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-21|Deuteronomy 16:21]]

**Do not plant an Asherah tree for yourself.** This makes him liable from the time that it was planted. Even if he never worshipped it, he transgresses a negative commandment for the very planting.

Do not plant an *Asherah* tree for yourself; any tree near the altar of Adonoy, your God. This prohibits planting a tree or building a structure on the Temple mount.

### 16:22

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-22|Deuteronomy 16:22]]

**Do not erect for yourself a monument.** A single-stone monument for sacrificial offerings, even for the sake of Heaven.

**That [God] detests.** An altar made of stones or an earthen altar is what He commanded, but He detests this, because it was in the canon of the Canaanites. Although He cherished it during the times of the patriarchs, He now detests it, since they included it in the idolatrous ritual.

## Chapter 17

### 17:1

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-1|Deuteronomy 17:1]]

**Do not sacrifice, etc., anything bad.** This prohibits causing the *pigul* disqualification of the sacred offerings through a pernicious statement. Additional derivations are extrapolated from this in the tractate dealing with the slaughter of the sacred offerings.

### 17:2

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-2|Deuteronomy 17:2]]

**By violating His covenant.** Which He established with them prohibiting idolatry.

### 17:3

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-3|Deuteronomy 17:3]]

**That I did not command.** To worship them.

### 17:4

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-4|Deuteronomy 17:4]]

**Authenticated.** The testimony was consistent.

### 17:5

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-5|Deuteronomy 17:5]]

**You are to take out that man . . . to your city, etc.** Anyone who renders אֶל שְׁעָרֶיךָ as “to the gates of your tribunal” is mistaken, for we have learned the following: “to your gates” — this refers to the gate where he worshipped. But perhaps it refers to the gate in which he was tried? ”Your gates” is said below, and “your gates” is said above. Just as “your gates” above refers to the gate in which he worshipped, so does “your gates” below refer to the gate in which he worshipped. Targum [Onkelos] renders “to your city.”

### 17:6

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-6|Deuteronomy 17:6]]

**Two witnesses or three.** If testimony is valid with two, why is three specified? To compare three to two. Just as two comprise a single entity, so three comprise a single entity — hence, none are liable as “conspiring witnesses” unless all are convicted as conspirators.

### 17:8

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-8|Deuteronomy 17:8]]

**If . . . is too abstruse.** הַפְלָאָה always denotes separation, or setting apart — the matter is separated, concealed from you.

**Between blood and blood.** Between ritually unclean blood and clean blood.

**Between decision and decision.** Between a ‘not guilty’ decision, and a ‘guilty’ decision.

**Or between leprosy and leprosy.** Between ritually unclean leprosy, and clean leprosy.

**Matters under dispute.** When the wise men of the city disagree over the matter, one finding that it is unclean, the other that it is clean; one ruling ‘guilty,’ the other ‘not guilty.’

**You shall rise and ascend.** This teaches that the Beis Hamikdosh was loftier than all other places.

### 17:9

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-9|Deuteronomy 17:9]]

**The *kohanim* — the Levites —** The *kohanim,* who are descended from the tribe of Levi.

**And the judge officiating during those days.** Even if he is not comparable to the other judges who preceded him, you must obey him — you have no one but the judge in your day.

### 17:11

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-11|Deuteronomy 17:11]]

**Neither right or left.** Even if he tells you that right is left or left is right; certainly, if he tells you that right is right and left is left.

### 17:13

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-13|Deuteronomy 17:13]]

**Let all the people hear.** From here it is derived that we wait with him until the festival, then execute him during the festival.

### 17:16

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-16|Deuteronomy 17:16]]

**He may not acquire an abundance of horses for himself.** Except as required for his riding entourage, so that he does not send the people back to Egypt, the source of supply for horses, as it is said concerning Shlomo, “Chariots were brought up out of Egypt for six hundred silver pieces, and horses for one hundred fifty.”

### 17:17

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-17|Deuteronomy 17:17]]

**He may not acquire an abundance of wives.** No more than eighteen, as we find that Dovid had six wives, and it was said to him, “If this is insufficient, then I shall add, for you, as many, and again.”

**And silver and gold he may not accumulate for himself in great abundance.** Except as required to maintain his retinue.

### 17:18

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-18|Deuteronomy 17:18]]

**It shall be, that when he occupies.** If he fulfills this, then he is worthy of his kingdom enduring.

**A duplicate of [this] Torah.** Two Torah scrolls, one to be stored in his treasure chamber, and one to accompany him on his arrivals and departures. Onkelos, however, translates “text,” rendering מִשְׁנֵה as “teaching,” or “word.”

### 17:19

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-19|Deuteronomy 17:19]]

**Every word of this Torah.** This follows the plain meaning.

### 17:20

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-20|Deuteronomy 17:20]]

**And he does not stray from the commandment.** Even an easily fulfilled directive by a prophet.

**So that the days of his reign are lengthy.** Implicit in the positive is the negative. We find this with Shaul, whom Shmuel told, “Wait seven days, until I come to you, etc.,” to offer *olah*-sacrifices. And it is written, “He waited seven days,” but did not keep his commitment to wait the entire day. He had hardly completed the *olah*-offering, when Shmuel arrived, and told him, “You have been foolish! You have not kept. etc., so now your reign will not endure.” Thus, you learn that, over an easily fulfilled directive by a prophet, he was punished.

**He and his sons.** This teaches that if his son qualifies as a regent, he takes precedence over all other candidates.

## Chapter 18

### 18:1

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-1|Deuteronomy 18:1]]

**The entire tribe of Levi.** Whether physically sound or blemished.

**A portion.** In the spoils.

**Or an inheritance.** In the land.

**Adonoy’s fire offerings.** The sacred offerings at the Sanctuary.

**And His inheritance.** This refers to the extra-Sanctuary sacred items — the *terumah*-gifts and the tithes. However, they did not receive a complete inheritance among their brethren. Sifrei expounds: **But he will have no territory** — this refers to the inheritance of the remaining ones. ‘Among his brothers’ — this refers to the inheritance of five. I do not know what this means. But it seems to me that the Canaanite territory across the Yardein and onward is entitled ‘The land of five nations;’ that of Sichon and Og, ‘Two nations,’ the Emorites and Canaanites; and ‘the inheritance of the remaining ones’ adds that of the Keinites, Kenizites, and Kadmonites. A similar exposition is made in the portion of the Torah concerning gifts disclosed to Aharon, “Therefore, Levi did not have, etc.” — this prohibits [the territory of] the Keini, Kenizi, and Kadmoni. Afterward, I found in R’ Klonimus’ commentary, that the text of Sifrei is as follows: “‘But he will have no territory’ — this refers to the inheritance of five. **Among his brothers.** This refers to the inheritance of seven.” The inheritance of five tribes, and the inheritance of seven tribes. Because Moshe and Yehoshua did not appropriate and inheritance except that of the five tribes alone — for Moshe appropriated the portions of Reuvein, Gad and half the tribe of Menashe. Yehoshua appropriated the portions of Yehudah, Efrayim, and half the tribe of Menashe, and the seven others took possession by themselves, after Yehoshua’s demise — because of this, he mentions five separately, and seven separately.

### 18:2

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-2|Deuteronomy 18:2]]

**As He said to him.** “You shall not inherit their land, etc., I am your portion.”

### 18:3

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-3|Deuteronomy 18:3]]

**From the people.** But not from the *kohanim.*

**Whether an ox or a sheep.** This excludes wild animals.

**The foreleg.** From the carpus to the shoulder blade, *espaldon* in Old French.

**The jaw.** Including the tongue. Those expounding Scriptural selections remarked that the foreleg represents the hand, as it is said, ‘He grasped a spear in his hand’; the jaw represents prayer, as it is said, ‘Pinchas stood and prayed;’ and the [fourth] stomach represents ‘[He pierced] . . . the woman in her genital area.’

### 18:4

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-4|Deuteronomy 18:4]]

**The first portion of your grain.** This refers to the *terumah*-gift. No amount is specified. The Rabbis, however, established specific amounts. The generous gift: one fortieth of the total crop. The minimal gift: one sixtieth. The standard gift: one fiftieth. They found Scriptural support for the minimal amount of one sixtieth, as it is said, “Give one sixth of an *eiphah* from a *chomer* of barley.” Now one sixth of an *eiphah* is half a *se’ah.* If one gives half a *se’ah* from a *kur*, the resulting amount is one sixtieth, since the *kur* is comprised of thirty *se’ahs.*

**And the first of the shearing of your sheep.** When you shear your sheep every year, give the first of it to the *kohein.* No amount is specified, but the Rabbis established a specific amount: one sixtieth. How many sheep cause the first shearing obligation? Five ewes, as it is said, “Five sheep which cause [the first shearing obligation].” R’ Akiva said, “The first shearing” — two. “of your sheep” — four. “you shall give him” — five.

### 18:5

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-5|Deuteronomy 18:5]]

**To stand and perform the service.** This teaches that the service may be performed only while standing.

### 18:6

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-6|Deuteronomy 18:6]]

**If a Levite should come.** Perhaps it is literally to the Levite that Scripture refers? The Torah therefore teaches “He shall perform the service” this excludes the Levites, who are not permitted to perform the services.

**Coming with all his soul’s desire. He shall perform the service.** This teaches, concerning the *kohein,* that he is permitted to come and offer up his personal voluntary or obligatory sacrifices, even during a watch which is not his own. Another interpretation. This teaches, further, that the *kohanim* who came during the festival [were permitted] to bring offerings with the ministering watch, by performing the sacrificial services with the offerings which were brought because of the festival obligation — for example, the festival *musaf*-offerings — although the watch was not their own.

### 18:8

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-8|Deuteronomy 18:8]]

**They shall eat equal portions.** This teaches that they share in the skins, and in the flesh of the sin-offering goats. Perhaps even in the offerings unrelated to the festival — for example, the constant *tamid* and Shabbos *musaf*-offerings, and the endowment and gift-offerings? The Torah therefore teaches, except for what the families sold [to one another], with the exception of the matters [included] in the ancestral exchange, during the time of Dovid and Shmuel, when the watches were established. They exchanged with each other — ‘Take your Shabbos, and I will take my Shabbos.’

### 18:9

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-9|Deuteronomy 18:9]]

**Do not learn to perpetrate.** ‘But you may learn to understand and instruct.’ This means, to understand how corrupt their activities are, and to instruct your children, ‘Do not do this, as this is the custom of the Gentiles.’

### 18:10

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-10|Deuteronomy 18:10]]

**Anyone who passes his son or daughter through fire.** This is the way “Molech” was worshipped. They would build a fire on either side, and then pass the child between both of them.

**Who practices the kosem-occult.** What is the *kosem*? One who grasps his divining rod and asks, ‘Shall I go, or not’? Concerning this it is said, “My people petition their sticks, their rods counsel them”!

**Who practices time-frame- occult.** R’ Akiva said that this refers to those who divine the times, declaring that certain times are propitous for beginnings. The Rabbis said, that this refers to sleight of hand magicians.

**Who divines portentuous events.** [As when] bread drops from his mouth, a deer crosses his path, or his staff falls from his hand.

### 18:11

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-11|Deuteronomy 18:11]]

**Or a snake charmer.** Who gathers flocks of snakes and scorpions or other wild animals into one area.

**One who invokes the spirit of Ov.** This refers to sorcery entitled “Pisom”: A voice emanates from the armpit, and summons the dead onto the armpit.

**Or yidoni.** He places the bone of an animal called *yedoa into his mouth, and the bone speaks through magical powers.*

**Or communicates with the dead.** For example, someone who conjures up [the dead] onto his male organ, or communicates with a skull.

### 18:12

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-12|Deuteronomy 18:12]]

**Anyone perpetrating this.** ‘One perpetrating all this’ is not said, but ‘anyone perpetrating this’ — even one of them.

### 18:13

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-13|Deuteronomy 18:13]]

**Walk in perfect trust with Adonoy, your God.** Walk with Him in utter trust, in anticipation of Him. Do not explore the future, rather, whatever befalls you accept with perfect trust. Then, you will be with Him, [He will take you] as His portion.

### 18:14

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-14|Deuteronomy 18:14]]

**Adonoy, your God, has not given you their status.** Whereby they listen to augurers or sorcerers, for He visited the Divine Presence on the prophets, and the *urim vetumim* oracle.

### 18:15

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deuteronomy 18:15]]

**From your midst, of your brethren, like me.** Just as I am in your midst and of your brethren. He shall ordain [a prophet] for you in place of me. Similarly, from prophet to prophet.

### 18:20

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-20|Deuteronomy 18:20]]

**Which I did not instruct him to say.** However, I did instruct his fellow.

**Which he says in the name of foreign gods.** Even if he is halachically correct, by prohibiting what is actually prohibited, and permitting what is permitted.

**He must die.** By asphyxiation. With three, execution is carried out by mortals: with someone who fabricates a prophetic message he did not hear; one who delivers a prophecy which was not revealed to him, but to his fellow; and one who prophesies in the name of an idol. However, one who withholds his prophecy, violates a prophet’s instructions, or violates his own prophecy is punished by Heavenly execution, as it is said, “I shall prosecute [the matter] with him.”

### 18:21

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-21|Deuteronomy 18:21]]

**If you should say to yourself.** You are destined to say this, when Chananiah ben Azor will appear and prophesy, “Behold, the Sanctuary vessels will be returned from Babylon now, immediately,” while Yirmiyahu will stand and cry out, ”Concerning the columns and the basin, etc., concerning the remaining vessels” which were not exiled with Yechaniah — “they shall be transported to Babylon” with Zidkiyahu’s exile!

### 18:22

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-22|Deuteronomy 18:22]]

**Should the prophet speak.** And say, ‘The following is destined to befall them,’ and you observe that it does not occur — **This is something Adonoy did not say,** and you must kill him. But you may ask, this applies with a prophet who foretells the future. What if he appears and says ‘You must do the following. I speak by the word of God!’? They have already been instructed: If he attempts to divert you from any one of the commandments, “Do not listen to him”; unless he has been established in your estimation as a perfect saint, as, for example, Eliyahu on Mount Carmel, who offered a sacrifice on an extra-Sanctuary altar when such altars were prohibited, in order to create a restrictive enclosure around Yisroel — strictly as befitting the need of the moment, so that the breach [in Torah observance] may be contained. This is why it is stated, “You shall obey him.”

**Do not fear him.** Do not refrain from finding him guilty. Do not fear punishment because of him.

## Chapter 19

### 19:3

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-3|Deuteronomy 19:3]]

**Ready the route.** Refuge! Refuge! was emblazoned at every fork in the road.

**Divide the borders of your land into three sections.** The distance from the border until the first of the refuge cities should be equivalent to the travelling distance between that city and the second; so, from the second to the third; and so from the third to the opposite border of Eretz Yisroel.

### 19:5

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-5|Deuteronomy 19:5]]

**And as his hand swung. . .downward.** As he lowered the axe over the tree. Targum [Onkelos] renders וְתִתְמְרֵיג יְדֵהּ, meaning “his hand was lowered” to strike a downward blow with the axe onto the tree. “For the cattle lowered it,” Yonasan renders, אֲרֵי מְרַגוּהִי תּוֹרַיָּא .

**The iron flew off the wooden handle.** Some Rabbis say that the iron flew off the handle. But some say that the iron loosened a chip from the tree being chipped. This flew off, and caused the death.

### 19:6

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-6|Deuteronomy 19:6]]

**Lest the blood-redeemer pursue.** This is why I am instructing you to ready the route, and [why there are] numerous refuge cites.

### 19:8

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-8|Deuteronomy 19:8]]

**When [God] expands . . .** in accordance with His oath. To give you the land of the Keini, Kenizi, and Kadmoni.

### 19:9

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-9|Deuteronomy 19:9]]

**You shall add three more.** Making nine — three across the Yardein, three in the land of Canaan, and three in the time destined to come.

### 19:11

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-11|Deuteronomy 19:11]]

**If there is a man who hates his neighbor.** Because of his hatred, he will eventually stalk him. From here it is derived that if a man violates a minor offense, he will ultimately commit a grave transgression. Because he violated “Do not hate [your brother],” he ultimately committed murder. This is why it says, “If there is a man who hates his neighbor, etc.” Otherwise, it should have written, “If a man rises up, stalks his neighbor, and wounds him mortally.”

### 19:13

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-13|Deuteronomy 19:13]]

**Do not view him with compassion.** Do not say, the first one has already been killed. Why kill this one, and have two dead Jews?

### 19:14

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-14|Deuteronomy 19:14]]

**Do not move back the boundary.** The word means the same as “They have retreated rearward.” When one moves the boundary marker rearward, into his neighbor’s property, in order to increase his own. But doesn’t it already say, “Do not steal”? Why, then, does the Torah say, “Do not move back...”? This teaches that someone who overturns his neighbor’s boundary violates two negative commandments. Perhaps this applies outside the land [of Eretz Yisroel] as well? The Torah therefore teaches, “in your hereditary property which you inherit, etc.” In Eretz Yisroel one violates two commandments, but outside [Eretz Yisroel,] one violates only “Do not steal.”

### 19:15

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-15|Deuteronomy 19:15]]

**One witness.** This establishes a source construct: Wherever “witness” is mentioned in the Torah, the reference is to two, unless specified as one.

**For any sin or transgression.** To have his fellow penalized by his testimony, whether by corporal or monetary punishment. However, he may rise up to require him to take an oath. If someone says to his fellow, ‘Repay that which I lent you,’ he replies, ‘I owe you nothing,’ and a single witness testifies that he owes [the money,] he is obligated to take an oath.

**By the word of two witnesses.** But they cannot write their testimony in a letter and send it to the court; nor may a translator stand between the witnesses and the justices.

### 19:16

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-16|Deuteronomy 19:16]]

**And bears fallacious testimony against him.** The story is utterly untenable, as the witness was dissociated from the entire testimony. How is this? When other witnesses said to them, ‘But weren’t you with us on that day at another location?!’

### 19:17

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-17|Deuteronomy 19:17]]

**The two men shall stand.** Our passage refers to the witnesses. This teaches that women cannot testify, and that the witnesses must testify while standing.

**Who are involved in the dispute.** This refers to the litigants.

**Before Adonoy.** It should seem to them, as if they stand before the Omnipresent, as it is said, “He [God] judges in the midst of judges.”

**Who are in those days.** Yiftach, in his generation, is like Shmuel in his generation — you must treat him reverently.

### 19:18

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-18|Deuteronomy 19:18]]

**The judges shall investigate thoroughly.** This is directed at those asserting that the witnesses were conspirators. They shall investigate and interrogate those accusing them of conspiracy, by the prescribed method of investigation and interrogation.

**Behold, the witness testified falsely.** Wherever “witness” is mentioned, Scripture refers to two.

### 19:19

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-19|Deuteronomy 19:19]]

**As he conspired.** But not as he did. From here it is derived that if the execution was carried out, the witnesses are not executed.

**To do to his brother.** What does the Torah teach with “to his brother”? It teaches that witnesses who conspired against the daughter of a *kohein* who was married are not executed by burning, but by the method of execution of the adulterer, which is asphyxiation, as it is said, “She . . . shall be burned by fire” — she, but not the adulterer. This is why “to his brother” is said here — “as he conspired to do to his brother,” but not as he conspired to do to his sister. However, with all the other executions, the Torah likens women to men, and conspirators against a woman are executed as are conspirators against a man. For example, if they testified that she committed murder or desecrated Shabbos, they are executed by the same method as she would have been. For “his sister” is not excluded here except where the punishment for conspiracy may be fulfilled with the adulter’s [type of] execution.

### 19:20

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-20|Deuteronomy 19:20]]

**Will hear and be fearful.** From here we derive that there must be a proclamation, ‘So and so were executed for conspiratorial testimony in court.’

### 19:21

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-21|Deuteronomy 19:21]]

**Eye for eye.** Monetary payment. Similarly, “tooth for tooth, etc.”

## Chapter 20

### 20:1

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-1|Deuteronomy 20:1]]

**When you go to war.** Scripture positioned going to war here to teach that anyone lacking a body member is not qualified to go to war. Another interpretation. To teach that if you judge righteously, you will be assured when you go to war, that you will be victorious. Similarly, Dovid said, “I have acted justly and righteously — Do not abandon me to those who would dispossess me.”

**Against your enemy.** Consider them enemies. Do not pity them, for they will not pity you.

**Horse and chariot.** In My eyes, they are all like a single horse. Similarly, it says, “You shall strike down Midian as one man.” Similarly, it says, “When Pharoah’s horse approached.”

**People who outnumber you.** In your eyes they are numerous, but in My eyes, they are not numerous.

### 20:2

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-2|Deuteronomy 20:2]]

**As you near the battle.** Near your departure from the border settlement at your country’s boundary.

**The kohein shall approach.** The one annointed for this purpose. He was entitled “The one annointed for warfare.”

**And speak to the people.** In the sacred Hebrew tongue.

### 20:3

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-3|Deuteronomy 20:3]]

**Hear, Yisroel.** Even if you have no merit other than the reading of the Shema alone, you are worthy of being saved.

**Against your enemies.** These are not your brothers. If you fall into their hands, they will be merciless towards you. This is not the same as the war between Yehudah and Yisroel, where it is said, “The men whose names were specified arose and took hold of the captives; All those who were naked, they clothed from the spoils. They clothed them, gave them shoes, fed them, gave them drinks, anointed them with oil, and led on donkeys those who were weak. They brought them to Yereicho, the city of dates, near their brothers. Then they returned to Shomron.” Rather, you are proceeding towards your enemies. Therefore, be strong for the battle.

**Do not be faint hearted or intimidated; do not panic or be crushed.** Four prohibitions, corresponding with the four things the Gentile monarchs do. They fasten their armor plates so that they strike each other and produce a clamor which terrorizes their opponents into fleeing; they tramp heavily with their horses and goad them into a frenzy so that the pounding of the horses’ hoofbeats is heard; they utter screams and blow horns and other noise makers.

**Do not be faint hearted.** From the horses’ neighing.

**Do not be intimidated.** From the fastening of the armor.

**Do not panic.** From the noise of the horns.

**Do not be crushed.** From the sounds of screaming.

### 20:4

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-4|Deuteronomy 20:4]]

**For Adonoy your God.** They advance by mortal triumph, whereas you advance by the triumph of the Omnipresent. The Plishtim advanced by the triumph of Golias — what was his end? He fell, and they fell with him.

**Marches with you.** This refers to the camp of the sacred ark.

### 20:5

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-5|Deuteronomy 20:5]]

**And did not inaugurate it as a dwelling.** Did not live in it. חִנּוּךְ denotes inauguration.

**And another man will inaugurate it.** This is a matter of great anguish.

### 20:6

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-6|Deuteronomy 20:6]]

**And did not redeem it.** Did not redeem it during the fourth year, when the fruit must be eaten in Jerusalem, or redeemed with money, and the money spent for food eaten in Jerusalem.

### 20:7

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-7|Deuteronomy 20:7]]

**Lest he die in battle.** He should return lest he die, for if he does not follow the instructions of the *kohein,* he deserves to die.

### 20:8

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-8|Deuteronomy 20:8]]

**The officers will further.** Why is “further” mentioned here? Because this is to be added to the words of the *kohein.* The *kohein* delivers the address and announces from “Hear Yisroel” until “to save you.” “Is there a man,” then the second, and then the third, the *kohein* addresses and an officer announces. With our passage, an officer addresses and another officer announces.

**Who is afraid, or faint hearted.** R’ Akiva said, as the plain meaning indicates — he cannot maintain himself in the battle mode, where he must face the menacing sword. R’ Yosi Hagalili said, one who is afraid because of his transgressions. The reason the Torah provides the pretext of returning because of one’s house or vineyard or wife is to protect those who return because of their transgressions — so that no one realizes that they are transgressors. Those who notice their return will say, ‘Perhaps he built a house, or planted a vineyard, or took a wife.’

### 20:9

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-9|Deuteronomy 20:9]]

**Army commanders.** To station disciplinary officers at the front and to the rear, carrying iron spears. If anyone attempts to retreat, they are empowered to cut off their legs. “Disciplinary officers,” lit. “those who lift up” — men who are stationed at the edge of the battlefield to “lift up” those who falter, and encourage them by saying, ‘Get back to the battle, do not run away! Running away is the beginning of downfall’!

### 20:10

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-10|Deuteronomy 20:10]]

**When you near a city.** Scripture refers to a discretionary war, as stated explicitly below, “You shall deal this way with all the distant cities, etc.”

### 20:11

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-11|Deuteronomy 20:11]]

**All the people found in it.** Even if you should find in it members of the seven nations you are required to annihilate, you may allow them to survive.

**Payers of tribute and your servants.** Provided they accept the tribute payment and the servitude.

### 20:12

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-12|Deuteronomy 20:12]]

**If it does not settle for peace with you, but engages in war against you.** Scripture informs you that if it does not make peace with you, it will ultimately engage in war against you if you withdraw and depart.

**You shall lay siege to it.** Even if this causes famine and thirst, and death from pestilence.

### 20:13

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-13|Deuteronomy 20:13]]

**Adonoy your God will deliver it into your hand.** If you fulfill everything mentioned here, God will ultimately deliver it into your hand.

### 20:14

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-14|Deuteronomy 20:14]]

**And children.** Even the male children. How, then, is “Strike down all its males” fulfilled? With the adults.

### 20:17

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-17|Deuteronomy 20:17]]

**As [God] commanded you.** This adds the Girgashites.

### 20:18

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-18|Deuteronomy 20:18]]

**In order that they do not teach you.** This infers that if they repent and convert, you may accept them.

### 20:19

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-19|Deuteronomy 20:19]]

**Days.** Two.

**Many.** Three. From here it is derived that we do not lay siege to the Gentile cities fewer than three days before Shabbos. And it teaches that the peace overture is tendered for two or three days. Similarly, it is said, “David remained in Ziklag for two days.” Scripture refers to discretionary warfare.

**For, is the tree in the field a man?** Here, כִּי is understood as “perhaps.” ‘Perhaps the tree in the field is a man, to be included in your siege, and suffer famine and thirst along with the city’s inhabitants? Why must you destroy it?’

### 20:20

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^20-20|Deuteronomy 20:20]]

**Until it is conquered.** The meaning is “conquest.” Until it is subjugated to you.

## Chapter 21

### 21:2

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-2|Deuteronomy 21:2]]

**Your elders shall go out.** The most distinguished of your elders. This refers to the supreme Sanhedrin.

**And measure.** From the spot where the corpse is lying.

**In the direction of the cities around the corpse.** In every direction, in order to determine which is closest.

### 21:4

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-4|Deuteronomy 21:4]]

**To a stony valley.** Hard, which cannot be cultivated.

**They shall decapitate.** By severing the nape with an axe. The Holy One, may He be blessed, said, ‘Let a yearling calf, which never bore fruit, come and be decapitated in a place which cannot bear fruit, as atonement for the murder of the one who was not permitted to bear fruit.

### 21:7

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-7|Deuteronomy 21:7]]

**Our hands have not spilled.** Is it possible to imagine that the elders of the court committed murder? Rather, ‘We did not observe him and dismiss him without food and escort.’ The *kohanim* then say:

### 21:8

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-8|Deuteronomy 21:8]]

**Forgive your people Yisroel: The blood shall thus be atoned for in their behalf.** Scripture informs them that once they have fulfilled this, the sin will be absolved.

### 21:9

[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^21-9|Deuteronomy 21:9]]

**Still, you must eradicate.** This teaches that if the murderer is apprehended after the decapitation of the heifer, he must be executed. This is righteous in the eyes of God.
