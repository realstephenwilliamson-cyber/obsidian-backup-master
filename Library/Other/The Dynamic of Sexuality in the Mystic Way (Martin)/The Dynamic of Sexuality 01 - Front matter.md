Abstract 

Mystical experience is an enduring element of the world’s religious traditions. Defined as a personal encounter and present awareness of God, or the Absolute, it figures prominently within the Christian and Hindu traditions. The transcultural similarity of allusive and metaphorical language which is used to describe the experiences in the two traditions is the broad focus of this thesis. I shall appropriate the research method of phenomenography in order to analyse the parameters of this special language. The particular mystical traditions under examination are those found among the mediaeval Beguine community and Śākta Tantrism. It is contended that both systems formulate an understanding of the divine based on an eroticized projection of the sexual self as other. An exploration of the texts, *The Flowing Light of the Godhead*, written by Mechthild of Magdeburg (ca. 1208–ca. 1282), and *Saundaryalaharī*, written by Śaṅkara (trad., 788–820), will be used to engage with the rich symbolisms generated in accounts of mystical experience in each tradition. A separate chapter is devoted to elucidating points of comparison and contrast. It is argued firstly that the two traditions exalt and exemplify the mystical quality of Love, but from different angles: for Mechthild it is a ‘love of love’, and for Śaṅkara it is a ‘love of beauty’. Secondly, both are exemplars of ‘sensational mysticism’, or ‘bodily spiritualities’, if only in an arrested form for Mechthild. That is, they both tend to express the transcendent in the immanent.
