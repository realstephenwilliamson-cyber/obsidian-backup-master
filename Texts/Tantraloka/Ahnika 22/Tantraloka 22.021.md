## Mūla

तेषां मध्यादेकतमं मन्त्रमस्मै समर्पयेत् ।
सोऽप्यहोरात्रमेवैनं जपेदल्पभुगप्यभुक् ॥ ^mula

teṣāṃ madhyādekatamaṃ mantramasmai samarpayet |
so'pyahorātramevainaṃ japedalpabhugapyabhuk || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 13 - Chapter Twenty-Two#^pg303|p. 283]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=303>))
>
> 1) OM, 2) Mātrkā, 3) Māyā, 4) Vyomavyāpin (the Pervader of the Sky), 5) the six-syllabled Mantra, 6) Bahurūpa (the Pluriform), and 7) the one called Netra (the Eye) — these are the seven (universal) common Mantras. (The teacher) should offer one Mantra amongst them (to his disciple), who should repeat it day and night, eating little or nothing (at all). Now, once (the teacher) has presented the Mantra (to his disciple) and offered (tarpita) that Mantra (with oblations etc.) into the fire that has been purified by means of the common (basic) procedure, he should (proceed) to purify (his disciple of the obligation to observe his previous) vow (vrataśuddhi). (with 22.20–22)
