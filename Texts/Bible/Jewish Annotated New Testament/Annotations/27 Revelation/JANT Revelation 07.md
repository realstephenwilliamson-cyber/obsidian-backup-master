# Revelation 7 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Revelation 06|← Revelation 6]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Revelation 08|Revelation 8 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Revelation 07|NRSVue]] · [[Revelation 07|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Revelation - Introduction]]

### 7.1–8: The 144,000 sealed

*[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-1|Text]], p. 551* · The visionary sequence alternates between cosmic cataclysm and spectacular glory. ^7-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-1|1–3]]:** *Four corners of the earth*, apocalyptic literature often envisioned the cosmos and heavens as organized in four directions (see *1 En*. 18–36). *Angels . . . the four winds* (see *1 En*. 18.1–5; 72) and an angel who rises with *the sun* declare a temporary reprieve until the sealing of righteous is completed. *Marked the . . . foreheads*, following the scene in Ezek 9, the righteous are sealed on the forehead, reflecting magical uses of texts, similar to the placement of “tefillin” ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-8|Deut 6.8]]; see [[Revelation 13#^13-16|Rev 13.16–17]]; [[Ezekiel 09#^9-4|Ezek 9.4]]). ^7-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-4|4–8]]:** The twelve *tribe*\[s\] of Israel, beginning with *Judah* (v 5), the source of the Davidic Messiah (but not including Dan, replaced by *Manasseh* \[v 6\]) are here multiplied by *twelve thousand*. This “census” of the righteous resembles military lists like those in 1QM. John’s eschatology revolves around the restoration of the tribes of Israel, as in [[Ezekiel 37#^37-15|Ezek 37.15–22]]; see [[LXX Tobit 13#^13-13|Tob 13.13]]; *4 Ezra* 39–47; *2 Bar.* 78.4–7, affirming the centrality of ancient Jewish tribal identities (see 7.4b), even for those who arrive from *every nation* (7.9). In 14.1–5 the *one hundred forty-four thousand* are credited with a special degree of purity that involved celibacy, whether out of priestly tradition or reflecting rules for holy war ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-15|Ex 19.15]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-1|Lev 22.1–9]]; [[I Samuel 21#^21-5|1 Sam 21.4]]). ^7-4

### 7.9–12: Universal worship

*[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-9|Text]], p. 552* ^7-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-9|9]]:** *Great multitude*, Gentiles who have devoted themselves to purity (white robes) and to the God and Messiah of Judaism. Some biblical and early Jewish texts imagined the nations of the world congregating before the God of Israel at the end of time, honoring him alone, embracing some Jewish practices, and thus implicitly gaining salvation ([[Isaiah 66#^66-18|Isa 66.18–23]]; [[LXX Tobit 13#^13-11|Tob 13.11]]; see also [[Revelation 15#^15-4|Rev 15.4]]); early rabbinic sources likewise regarded righteous Gentiles as having a place in the world to come (*t. Sanh*. 13.2). *Palm branches*, expressions of victory celebration (as in [[LXX 1 Maccabees 13#^13-51|1 Macc 13.51]]; [[Matthew 21#^21-18|Mt 21.18]]; [[Mark 11#^11-8|Mk 11.8]]). ^7-9-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-11|11]]:** *Elders and . . . living creatures*, similar images of the performance of praise (see 4.4n.; 4.6n.) punctuate the liturgical structure of Revelation. ^7-11

### 7.13–17: Reward for fidelity

*[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-13|Text]], p. 552* · The idea of purification by means of martyrdom may be inspired by [[Daniel 11#^11-35|Dan 11.35]]; the idea of a great tribulation (Gk “thlipsis”) was common among early Jesus-believers ([[Mark 13#^13-19|Mk 13.19]],[[Mark 13#^13-24|24]]; 2 Thess 1; etc.). Gentiles thus sanctified become like priests, servants before the heav- enly throne and within the heavenly Temple. ^7-13

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-14|14]]:** *You are the one that knows*, see [[Zechariah 04#^4-5|Zech 4.5]]. *Blood of the Lamb*, see 5.6–7n., and “Names Inscribed on the Body,” p. 561. ^7-14

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-16|16]]:** John refers to [[Isaiah 49#^49-10|Isa 49.10]], which originally concerned the return from Babylonian exile, in depicting the blessed status of the sanctified Gentiles. ^7-16

**[[NRSVue Revelation 07#^7-17|17]]:** The Lamb’s location *at the center of the throne* does not imply a merging with God but rather anticipates its eschatological position on Mt. Zion (14.1) and at the center of the new Jerusalem (21.22–23); the continuation of the verse cites [[Isaiah 25#^25-8|Isa 25.8]]. *Will be their shepherd . . . guide them to springs*, the acclamation of the Lamb’s eschatological blessings recalls Ps 23, perhaps through the prophetic language of Isaiah (49.10) and Ezekiel (34.23). ^7-17
