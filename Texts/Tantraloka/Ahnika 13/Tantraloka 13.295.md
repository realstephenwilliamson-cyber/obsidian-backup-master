## Mūla

शक्तिः पतन्ती सापेक्षा न क्वापीति सुविस्तरात् ।
एवं विचित्रेऽप्येतस्मिञ्छक्तिपाते स्थिते सति ॥ ^mula

śaktiḥ patantī sāpekṣā na kvāpīti suvistarāt |
evaṃ vicitre'pyetasmiñchaktipāte sthite sati || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VII
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 07 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v07 09 - Chapter Thirteen, part 5#^pg327|p. 305]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 07.pdf#page=327>))
>
> The venerable Aniruddha has also described (the Lord’s) independence at length in (his commentary on) the Mataṅgatantra, (in the course of) explaining (the nature of) the Lord’s unfolding power (unmīlinī). (There he writes:) ‘the power of God, which discloses the essential nature (of all things), descends down even to immobile beings (such as plants). Nowhere (in any circumstance) (kvāpi) is it dependent (on anything).’ (with 13.293cd–295ab)
>
> 4) Finally, the teacher (guru) possesses (all) types of worldly benefits and liberation. He has completely accomplished all that needs to be done, and abides in the (divine) nature that has (the power to accomplish) the five (divine) tasks (of creation, persistence, destruction, grace and obscuration). The four grades, beginning with the regular (initiate) and the rest, (may be bestowed) sequentially or otherwise, all together or separately; (such is the) wonderful variety of the descent of the power (of grace). (with 13.295cd–300ab)
