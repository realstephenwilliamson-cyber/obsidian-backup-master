# Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 070

← [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 069]] · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh]] · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 071]] →

*Chapter 70* · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh]]

**1.** The [beginning] of the *Kerias Shema* period of the *Maariv* prayer is when three small stars become visible. On a cloudy day one should wait until "the doubt leaves his heart."[^1] Nowadays it is customary for the congregation to pray *Maariv* immediately after *Minchah*, even it is not yet night in order not to burden the congregation, for it is a burden for the congregation to gather a second time. This should not be done before *pelag haminchah*,[^2] for then, even already having done so,[^3] they will not have fulfilled their obligation.[^4] Fortunate is one who prays *Maariv* with the congregation after nightfall; and between *Minchah* and *Maariv* engages in the study of Torah, so that he joins night and day with Torah. According to *Kabbalah*[^5] this is a significant matter. At any rate, it is fitting for any God fearing person, who prayed with the congregation before nightfall, not to eat [a meal] before nightfall. He should wait and immediately after the stars appear he should read the three chapters of *Kerias Shema* and then eat his meal. One who does not pray with the congregation is forbidden to pray *Maariv* before the stars appear.[^6] ^70-1

**2.** It is preferable to pray *Maariv* immediately when the stars appear. It is forbidden to start a meal or do anything, even to study [Torah], one-half hour before the stars appear, the same as it is forbidden before *Minchah ketanah*.[^7] If you have no time, for instance, if you teach a group [at that time], you should, at least, not delay [praying *Maariv*] beyond midnight. Even if you prayed after midnight, although it is after the fact,[^8] as long as it is before daybreak, you have fulfilled your obligation. ^70-2

**3.** If you arrive at the synagogue for the *Maariv* prayer, and find the congregation about to begin the *Shemoneh Esrei*, even if it is before nightfall but after *pelag haminchah*, you should pray *Shemoneh Esrei*, along with them, and afterwards—after nightfall, recite *Kerias Shema* with the berachos. If the congregation is holding in mid-*Kerias Shema* and its berachos, and you have sufficient time to recite *Kerias Shema* and [its] berachos until *Shomeir amo Yisrael la'ad*, before they reach *Shemoneh Esrei*, you should do so and omit *Baruch Hashem le'olam* etc. and you are not required to say it after the prayer.[^9] If you have not yet prayed *Minchah*,[^10] you should pray the *Shemoneh Esrei* prayer of *Minchah* while the congregation is reciting *Kerias Shema* with the berachos, and wait,[^11] at least the time required to walk four *amos* and afterward pray the *Maariv Shemoneh Esrei* with the congregation. Afterwards—after nightfall you should recite *Kerias Shema* with the berachos. ^70-3

**4.** *Baruch Hashem le'olam* until *Yiru eineinu* should be said preferably while seated.[^12] It is forbidden to interrupt from the beginning of *Vehu rachum* until after the *Shemoneh Esrei*. The *Shamosh'*s announcements of *Ya'aleh veyavo!* and *Tal umator!* are not considered an interruption because they are needed for the prayer. ^70-4

**5.** If one person remains praying *Maariv* at the synagogue at night his friend must wait until he completes his prayers so that he not be distracted.[^13] [However,] if he began to pray at a time which would not enable him to complete his prayer together with the congregation, then one is not obligated to wait for him, because it is apparent that he knowingly began this prayers[^14] because he is not afraid. ^70-5

[^1]: I.e., he is absolutely certain that it is past nightfall.
[^2]: See Chapter 69 para 2.
[^3]: I.e., having prayed.
[^4]: Of praying *Maariv*.
[^5]: *Magein Avraham* 1: 1.
[^6]: Since it is preferable to pray *Maariv* after nightfall, it is only because of the merit of *tefillah betzibur* (congregational prayer) that one may pray earlier.
[^7]: See Chapter 69 para. 3.
[^8]: I.e., you had not prayed by midnight.
[^9]: *Baruch Hashem le’olam* was instituted so that those who arrive late be given the opportunity to complete their prayers together with everyone (*Tosafos*). It is, therefore, not necessary to make it up after *Maariv*.
[^10]: And you arrive when the congregation is in mid-*Kerias shema* and its berachos.
[^11]: Between *Shemoneh Esrei*.
[^12]: Others hold that standing is preferable (*Rema* 236).
[^13]: The concern of being left alone may hinder his concentration.
[^14]: I.e., being aware that he will be the last person remaining in the synagogue.
