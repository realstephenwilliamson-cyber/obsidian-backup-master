# Krama and the Twelve Kālīs

> [!abstract] Definition
> The esoteric doctrine of the Krama (step-by-step) lineage incorporated by Abhinavagupta into *Tantrāloka* (Āhnika 4), mapping the cyclical process of cognition, manifestation, and withdrawal through twelve modalities of the Goddess Kālī (*Dvādaśa-Kālī*).

## The Fourfold Cognitive Cycle
Each movement of reality passes through four moments (*Caturartha*):
1. **Sṛṣṭi (Creation / Objective Emanation):** Sṛṣṭi-kālī, Raktā-kālī, Sthitināśā-kālī.
2. **Sthiti (Maintenance / Sustained Perception):** Yama-kālī, Saṃhāra-kālī, Mṛtyu-kālī.
3. **Saṃhāra (Dissolution / Retraction into Subject):** Bhadra-kālī, Mārtaṇḍa-kālī, Paramārka-kālī.
4. **Anākhya (The Ineffable / Transcendent Ground):** Kālāgnirudra-kālī, Mahākāla-kālī, Mahābhairavaghoracaṇḍa-kālī.

## Philosophical Meaning
The twelve Kālīs do not denote separate mythological beings; they are the pulsating moments (*spanda*) of subjective perception as consciousness projects objects outward, experiences them, dissolves them back into the knower, and rests in the transcendent void (*mahāvyoma*).
