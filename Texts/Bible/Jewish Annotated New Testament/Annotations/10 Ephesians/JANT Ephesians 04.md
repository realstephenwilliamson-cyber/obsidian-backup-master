# Ephesians 4 (JANT notes)

Text: [[NRSVue Ephesians 04|NRSVue]] · [[Ephesians 04|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Ephesians - Introduction]]

### 4.1–13: Unity amid diversity of believers

*[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 393* ^4-1

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-1|1]]:** *Prisoner*, see 3.1n. *Lead a life worthy of the calling*, followed by a detailed elaboration of what constitutes this life. ^4-1-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-2|2]]:** *Humility and gentleness*, particularly praiseworthy qualities in the Tanakh; see, e.g., [[Proverbs 16#^16-19|Prov 16.19]]. Behavior undermining these qualities is forbidden in the pietistic rules of the DSS; see 1QS v, 25-vi, 1 and 26. ^4-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-4|4–6]]:** *One body . . . one spirit . . . one God and Father of all*, seven unities are enumerated. The text clearly plays on [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deut 6.4]] (“Hear, O Israel . . . the Lord alone”), which became in the same period the central rabbinic statement of faith (*m. Ber*. 1). This repetitive formulation is reminiscent of the received rabbinic Sabbath afternoon prayer: “You are one and Your name is one, and who is like Your people Israel, one \[= unique\] nation on earth?” ^4-4

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Above all and through all and in all*, if God is one and not subject to division, then God is omnipresent. *Christ* d Gk *fatherhood* e Other ancient authorities add *first* ^4-6

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-8|8]]:** *It is said*, quoting [[Psalms 068#^68-19|Ps 68.18]], though different from the MT and LXX (the quoted version employs third person instead of second, and tribute taken is here gifts given) to interpret the psalm as referring to Jesus. ^4-8

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-11|11–12]]:** *The gifts he gave*, Christ’s gift (see v 7), referenced in the psalm. *Evangelists,* messengers bearing the good news; *pastors*, lit., “shepherds.” *In building up the body of Christ* (v 12), the church requires leaders with different gifts. ^4-11

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-13|13–16]]:** What mature faith must leave behind. ^4-13

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-14|14]]:** *Blown about by every wind . . . ,* immature faith is insecure and easily misled. ^4-14

### 4.17–5.20: Worthy conduct and qualities

*[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-17|Text]], p. 394* ^4-17

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-17|17]]:** *As the Gentiles live*, having erased the boundary between Jews and Gentiles (2.13**–**14; 3.6), the author affirms another boundary. ^4-17-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-18|18]]:** *Darkened in their understanding . . . their ignorance*, for the association of ignorance and darkness, see [[Ecclesiastes 02#^2-13|Eccl 2.13-14]]. *Hardness of heart*, see [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^7-13|Ex 7.13]] and elsewhere. ^4-18

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-19|19]]:** *Licentiousness . . . impurity*, priority is given, in the condemnation of pagan practice, to sexual sin. The equation of sexual sin and impurity, suggested by their juxtaposition in this verse, is widespread in the Tanakh; see, e.g., Lev 18. Smearing the foreigner with such frequently false accusations (for the rabbinic example, see *m. Avod. Zar*. 2.1) is commonplace. ^4-19

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-22|22–24]]:** *Your old self . . . the new self*, lit., “old person . . . new person.” The human inclination toward evil is so powerful that leaving sin behind might require a new self; see [[Jeremiah 31#^31-3|Jer 31.3]]**–**32; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-26|Ezek 36.26]]**–**27. ^4-22

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-24|24]]:** *Likeness of God*, “likeness,” absent in the original, is added based upon [[Colossians 03#^3-10|Col 3.10]]. ^4-24

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-25|25]]:** *Putting away falsehood*, see v 22; falsehood is the next of the sins to be put away. ^4-25

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-26|26]]:** *Be angry but do not sin*, [[Psalms 004#^4-5|Ps 4.4]]\[LXX\]; cf. [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Lev 19.17]]**–**18, where loving one’s neighbor requires reproving. *Do not let the sun go down*, similarly at 1QS V, 26. ^4-26

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-27|27]]:** *Devil*, “satan,” as per the Septuagint’s translation; see [[Job 01#^1-6|Job 1.6]]**–**7 and elsewhere. Postbiblical Jewish writings are replete with references to Satan (known by various names) and other rebellious or evil divine beings; see, e.g., *1 En*. 6.7 and 8.1**–**3; *Jub*. 17.16; *t. Avod. Zar.* 1.18. ^4-27

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-28|28]]:** S*omething to share with the needy*, and not merely to support themselves or acquire riches. See [[James 02#^2-14|Jas 2.14]]**–**17, where James argues that works are crucial for salvation and the needy must be supported. The rabbis, too, insisted that a person should have honest labor and support the poor; see, e.g*. , m. Avot* 1.2 and 3.17. ^4-28

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-29|29–31]]:** *Evil talk . . . wrangling and slander*, 1QS 7.2**–**9 condemns a similar list; the rabbis condemn what they call “leshon ha-raʿ,” “the tongue of evil”; see, e.g., *Lev. Rab*. 16 and [[Arakhin 15#15b|*b. Arak*. 15b]]. ^4-29

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 04#^4-30|30]]:** *Seal*, see 1.13n. ^4-30
