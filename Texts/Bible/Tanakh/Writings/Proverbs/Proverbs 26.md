# Proverbs 26

Compare: [[NRSVue Proverbs 26|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 26

**26:1** Like snow in summer and rain at harvesttime,  
So honor is not fitting for a dullard. ^26-1

**26:2** As a sparrow must flit and a swallow fly,  
So a gratuitous curse must backfire.[^1] ^26-2

**26:3** A whip for a horse and a bridle for a donkey,  
And a rod for the back of dullards. ^26-3

**26:4** Do not answer a dullard in accord with his folly,  
Else you will become like him. ^26-4

**26:5** Answer a dullard in accord with his folly,  
Else he will think himself wise. ^26-5

**26:6** He who sends a message by a dullard  
Will wear out legs and must put up with[^2] lawlessness. ^26-6

**26:7** As legs hang limp on a cripple,  
So is a proverb in the mouth of dullards. ^26-7

**26:8** Like a pebble in a sling,  
So is paying honor to a dullard. ^26-8

**26:9** As a thorn comes to the hand of a drunkard,  
So a proverb to the mouth of a dullard. ^26-9

**26:10** A master can produce anything,  
But he who hires a dullard is as one who hires transients.[^3] ^26-10

**26:11** As a dog returns to his vomit,  
So a dullard repeats his folly. ^26-11

**26:12** If you see a man who thinks himself wise,  
There is more hope for a dullard than for him. ^26-12

**26:13** A lazy man says,  
“There’s a cub on the road, a lion in the squares.” ^26-13

**26:14** The door turns on its hinge,  
And the lazy man on his bed. ^26-14

**26:15** The lazy man buries his hand in the bowl;  
He will not even bring it to his mouth. ^26-15

**26:16** The lazy man thinks himself wiser  
Than seven men who give good advice. ^26-16

**26:17** A passerby who gets embroiled in someone else’s quarrel  
Is like one who seizes a dog by its ears. ^26-17

**26:18** Like a madman[^4] scattering deadly firebrands, arrows, ^26-18

**26:19** Is one who cheats his fellow and says, “I was only joking.” ^26-19

**26:20** For lack of wood a fire goes out,  
And without a querulous man contention is stilled. ^26-20

**26:21** Charcoal for embers and wood for a fire  
And a contentious man for kindling strife. ^26-21

**26:22** The words of a querulous man are bruising;[^5]  
They penetrate one’s inmost parts. ^26-22

**26:23** Base silver laid over earthenware  
Are ardent lips with an evil mind. ^26-23

**26:24** An enemy dissembles with his speech,  
Inwardly he harbors deceit. ^26-24

**26:25** Though he be fair-spoken do not trust him,  
For seven abominations are in his mind. ^26-25

**26:26** His hatred may be concealed by dissimulation,  
But his evil will be exposed to public view. ^26-26

**26:27** He who digs a pit will fall in it,  
And whoever rolls a stone, it will roll back on him. ^26-27

**26:28** A lying tongue hates those crushed by it;[^6]  
Smooth speech throws one down. ^26-28

[^1]: Kethib, “fail.”
[^2]: Lit. “drink.”
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^4]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^5]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
