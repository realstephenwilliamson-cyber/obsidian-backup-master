### 13.1–37: Prediction of the destruction of the Temple and the end-time

*[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 97* · ([[Matthew 24#^24-1|Mt 24.1–36]]; [[Luke 21#^21-5|Lk 21.5–36]]). The main themes of the so-called Markan Apocalypse are that many apocalyptic messengers are deceitful and that discerning people will watch and wait for the real end (vv 32–33). ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Mount of Olives*, see 11.1n. *Privately*, see 4.10–12n. ^13-3

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-5|5]]:** *Beware*, implies both resisting the false prophets and readiness for the true events to unfold (vv 26–27). ^13-5

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-6|6]]:** *In my name*, suggests that other followers of Jesus would have varying understandings of the last days; cf. 9.38–41. *I am he,* God’s self-identification to Moses in [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-13|Ex 3.13–15]] is very similar, and the audience would have noted the irony that *many will come*, using this phrase about themselves, as indeed Jesus is elsewhere reported to have done ([[John 08#^8-24|Jn 8.24]],[[John 08#^8-28|28]],[[John 08#^8-58|58]]; [[John 13#^13-19|13.19]]). ^13-6

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-7|7–8]]:** In contrast to texts such as Dan 8–12, the events of the end cannot be treated as orderly predictions (cf. [[Isaiah 19#^19-2|Isa 19.2]]; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 13.31–32; Rev). Some phrases here echo prophetic passages about the invasions of Jerusalem (e.g., [[Ezekiel 07#^7-21|Ezek 7.21–27]]), and repeat the general notion of great cataclysmic battles before a new peaceful era dawns. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-9|9]]:** Although Mark may condemn specific *councils* and *synagogues*, they still have authority over Jesus’ Jewish followers (cf. [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-24|2 Cor 11.24–25]]). ^13-9

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-10|10]]:** This verse may be Mark’s insertion. Jesus himself probably did not emphasize a mission to the Gentiles. ^13-10

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-14|14]]:** *Desolating sacrilege*, from [[Daniel 11#^11-31|Dan 11.31]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-11|12.11]] (Heb “shiqquts meshomem”), the image of Zeus the Seleucids placed in the Temple, spurring the outbreak of the Maccabean Revolt in 167 BCE. Mark may be referring to Caligula’s image of himself that he intended to erect in the Temple; his assassination in 40 CE removed the threat. ^13-14

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-19|19–32]]:** Apocalyptic motifs (Dan 12; *1 En.* 8.2; 83.1) accompanied by warnings to be *alert* (v 23). ^13-19

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-19|19]]:** Mark alters the prediction in [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1]] (LXX from “until that day”)—looking into the future—to *until now* (cf. [[Mark 10#^10-30|Mk 10.30]]). ^13-19-2

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-22|22]]:** *Signs and omens*, see 8.11n. *Elect*, those predestined to be saved. ^13-22

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-24|24–25]]:** The quotation reflects a combination of various passages: [[Isaiah 13#^13-10|Isa 13.10]]; [[Ezekiel 32#^32-7|Ezek 32.7–8]]; [[Joel 2#^2-10|Joel 2.10]],[[Joel 3#^3-4|31]]; [[Joel 4#^4-15|3.15]]. ^13-24

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-26|26]]:** [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]; see “Son of Man,” p. 74. ^13-26

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-27|27]]:** See 3.7–12n. ^13-27

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-30|30]]:** *Generation*, see 8.38n. ^13-30

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-31|31]]:** [[Isaiah 51#^51-6|Isa 51.6]]; [[Isaiah 54#^54-10|54.10]]. ^13-31

**[[NRSVue Mark 13#^13-32|32–35]]:** *No one knows*, is not a rejection of apocalyptic reasoning but a further reason to *keep awake*; cf. 2 Esd 4.52; [[Matthew 25#^25-13|Mt 25.13]]. Contemporaneous and later Jewish texts state that the identity of the messiah, or the time of his coming, is hidden until the end (*4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.28; *1 En.* 62.7; *b. Pesah.* [[Pesachim 54#54b|54b]]; *b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 97#97a|97a]]). ^13-32
