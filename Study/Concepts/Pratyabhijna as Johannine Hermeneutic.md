# Pratyabhijñā as Johannine Hermeneutic

> [!abstract] Definition
> Applying the Kashmir Shaiva doctrine of *Pratyabhijñā* (recognition, प्रत्यभिज्ञा)—as articulated by Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta—to the Fourth Gospel, interpreting salvation not as a juridical transaction or future state, but as the immediate, ecstatic awakening to the divine identity that has never been absent.

## The Kashmir Shaiva Framework
In the *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* and *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam*:
- Recognition is illustrated by the lover who does not recognize her beloved standing right before her until his identity is revealed. The beloved was physically present all along, but the joy of union required *recognition*.
- Similarly, Consciousness (*Śiva*) is already the essence of all reality, contracted through self-limitation (*māyā*). Realization is the sudden recognition of what one already is.

## The Johannine Application
In [[The Gospel of Recognition|The Gospel of Recognition]]:
- The Johannine Prologue: *"He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him"* ([[John 01#^1-10|John 1:10]]).
- The Resurrection appearances: Mary Magdalene outside the tomb weeping, thinking Jesus is the gardener, until he speaks her name: *"Mary"*—and instant recognition occurs: *"Rabboni!"* ([[John 20#^20-16|John 20:16]]).
- Johannine discipleship is structured as *Pratyabhijñā*: divine life does not come from across a spatial chasm; it is recognized as already dwelling within (*"I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you"*, [[John 14#^14-20|John 14:20]]).
