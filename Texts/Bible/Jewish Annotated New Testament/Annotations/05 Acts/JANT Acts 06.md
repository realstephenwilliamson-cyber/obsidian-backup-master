### 6.1–7: Hellenists and Hebrews

*[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 233* · Problems threatening to divide the community are amicably resolved. ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Hellenists . . . Hebrews*, the division appears to be based largely on linguistic differences between Aramaic and Greek speakers. *Widows*, the Tanakh often identifies widows as persons deserving of special assistance ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-17|Deut 24.17–21]]; [[Isaiah 10#^10-1|Isa 10.1–3]]; [[Zechariah 07#^7-10|Zech 7.10]]). Neglecting widows, who represent the poor in general, is contrary to the ideal of communal assistance (4.32–37). ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-2|2]]:** *Wait*, not table service per se, but providing service to the community, especially its poor (which may be financial help, as in translators’ note *c*); see 11.29; 12.25. The actions described are not a reference to the Lord’s Supper or Eucharist. ^6-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-3|3–4]]:** The proposed division of labor does not hold; none of the seven serves food, and two of them, Stephen and Philip, pray and preach, tasks assigned to the apostles. ^6-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-5|5]]:** *Pleased the whole community*, the community is again united. Only Stephen (6.8–8.1) and Philip (8.5**–**40; 21.8) reappear. ^6-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-6|6]]:** *Laid their hands*, sign of appointment for divine service ([[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^27-18|Num 27.18]]; [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-9|Deut 34.9]]; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-14|1 Tim 4.14]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-22|5.22]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-6|2 Tim 1.6]]). ^6-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-7|7]]:** *Priests*, formerly characterized as opponents of the apostles (5.17), join the believers (2.41; 4.4; 5.14). ^6-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-8|6.8–8.1]]:** **Stephen’s arrest, speech, and martyrdom.** Luke models the story of Stephen’s trial and death on that of Jesus, including the quotation from [[Psalms 031#^31-6|Ps 31.5]] ([[Luke 23#^23-46|Lk 23.46]] || [[Acts 07#^7-59|Acts 7.59]]) and a plea for forgiveness for his persecutors ([[Luke 23#^23-34|Lk 23.34]],[[Luke 23#^23-46|46]] || [[Acts 07#^7-60|Acts 7.60]]). The similar portrayals, reflecting Luke’s literary method, may also draw attention to the hostility Jews exhibit not just against Jesus, but against his followers as well. ^6-8

### 6.8–15: Stephen’s arrest

*[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-8|Text]], p. 233* ^6-8-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-8|8]]:** *Wonders and signs*, see 2.43. ^6-8-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-9|9]]:** *Freedmen*, Gk “libertinoi”; enslaved Jews who were manumitted (e.g., Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 155; Tacitus, *Ann.* 2.85); a community of such Jews, perhaps originally from Rome, may have formed a synagogue in Jerusalem, in part to provide assistance for fellow Jews (*CIJ* 2.1404). *Cyrenians, Alexandrians*, from North Africa. *Cilicia and Asia*, Roman provinces in Asia Minor. All four locations had well-established Jewish communities. The opposition comes from Diaspora Jews, who would be linguistically if not culturally similar to Stephen, a Hellenist (i.e., Greek-speaking) Jew. ^6-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-11|11]]:** *Blasphemous words*, might warrant death ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-16|Lev 24.16]]; *m. Sanh.* 7.5) or expulsion from the community (*m. Ker.* 1.1–2). *Against Moses and God*, an ironic statement since Stephen’s speech and the rest of Acts claim that Jews, who reject Jesus, are against God and “Moses,” i.e., Torah (7.53; 11.18). ^6-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-13|13]]:** *False witnesses*, also used against Jesus ([[Matthew 26#^26-59|Mt 26.59–61]]; [[Mark 14#^14-55|Mk 14.55–58]], though not mentioned in Lk). ^6-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-14|14]]:** *Will destroy this place*, according to the Gospels, Jesus speaks about the destruction of the Temple ([[Matthew 26#^26-61|Mt 26.61]]; [[Luke 21#^21-6|Lk 21.6]]; [[John 02#^2-19|Jn 2.19]]) and is accused of seeking its destruction ([[Mark 14#^14-58|Mk 14.58]]). Such declarations were viewed as hostile, as in the case of Jesus the son of Ananias whose prediction of the destruction of the Temple and city caused Jewish officials to restrain him and ultimately present him to the Roman procurator, Albinus, who scourged him in an attempt to make him desist (Josephus, *J.W.* 6.301–9). *Change the customs*, Acts often states that this accusation is false (15.21; 16.3; 21.26). ^6-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Face of an angel*, divine illumination indicates that Stephen speaks with wisdom and authority ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-29|Ex 34.29]]; [[I Samuel 29#^29-9|1 Sam 29.9]]; [[II Samuel 14#^14-17|2 Sam 14.17]]; [[II Samuel 19#^19-28|19.27]]). ^6-15
