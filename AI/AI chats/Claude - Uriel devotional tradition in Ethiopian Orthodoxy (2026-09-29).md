> Tell me about the Uriel devotional tradition in Ethiopian Orthodoxy

Uriel (Ge'ez ʿUraʾel) is one of the most honored angels in Ethiopian Orthodox piety, and what makes the cult distinctive is its textual base. Its foundation is 4 Ezra and 1 Enoch, both canonical in the Ethiopian church, and it presents Uriel as the angelus interpres who explains prophecies to Enoch and Ezra and helps them. So he is above all the angel of revealed understanding: the one who opens hidden things to the seer.

**Feasts.** He is commemorated on the 22nd of each month. The two annual feasts are tied to specific stories. Mahibere Kidusan, the church's Sunday School department, gives the reasons. Tir 22 (late January) marks God appointing him over thunder, flood, and lightning. Hamle 22 (late July) commemorates the fiery cup Ezra drank to receive wisdom in 2 Esdras 14:39. That makes a feast day out of an exegetical moment. The Ethiopic tradition reads the angel as the giver of the cup, though the text itself is less explicit. The earliest homily was set to be read on 21 Hamle.

**Texts and history.** The textual tradition goes back to Emperor Zärʾa Yaʿǝqob (1434–1468). Its earliest source is an angelological homiliary from Däbrä Ḥayq (EMML 1835). As the cult grew, churches were dedicated to him, and later homilies and miracles were gathered into the Dǝrsanä Uraʾel, which reached a standard form under Menilek II. The long recension assigns a homily and a miracle to each month, and some manuscripts add a mälkǝʾ ("image" hymn) of Uriel. Amsalu Tefera's article in *Aethiopica* 21 (2018) is the scholarly starting point.

**The blood legend.** According to the Dǝrsanä, Uriel dipped his wing in the blood and water from Christ's side at the crucifixion and filled a cup. He then carried it to Ethiopia and sprinkled it on places, sanctifying them for churches. This is why Ethiopian icons often show him holding a chalice. The places run roughly north to south, including Däbrä Damo, Aksum, and Lake Ḥayq, and the sprinkling marks them as future religious centers. This is a late layer, not early tradition. My own reading is that it does the same work as the Kebra Nagast's Ark story: it maps sacred history onto Ethiopian soil.

**The Western contrast.** The Latin Church suppressed his cult in 745 under Pope Zachary. Eastern Orthodoxy honors him only within the general Synaxis of the archangels on November 8. Ethiopia is where a full independent cult survived, with its own homiliary, feasts, and churches. That fits its keeping of Enoch: the cult lasted where the book that gives Uriel his fullest role stayed canonical.
