# Hebrews 9 (JANT notes)

### 9.1–10: Inadequacies of the first covenant

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-1|Text]], p. 476* ^9-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-1|1–5]]:** The description is based on God’s instructions in [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-8|Ex 25.8–31.11]] (also [[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^36-1|Ex 36.1–40.38]]) regarding the building of the tabernacle, the tented sanctuary (which precedes the Temple), in which dwelt God’s presence. See “The Jerusalem Temple,” p. 658. ^9-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-2|2–3]]:** *Tent*, can also be rendered “tabernacle” (Gk “skēnē,” Heb “mishkan”). *First one*, meaning the outer sanctuary . Although the Tanakh is not always consistent in its terminology, the tabernacle was comprised of two parts, an outer sanctuary, sometimes known as the *Holy Place* (plural in Gk, “places”), and an inner one, known as the *Holy of Holies.* The outer sanctuary contained the objects mentioned**:** *Lampstand* (a seven-branched lamp, not to be confused with a nine-branched Hanukkah menorah, which is associated with Jewish practices developed much later); *bread of the Presence*, set upon the *table*; its name derives from its being located in the sanctuary wherein lies God’s presence. The Mishnah contains a lengthy description of the preparation, placement, and presentation of this holy bread (*m. Men.* 11). *Second curtain*, the curtain that constituted the entrance to the Holy of Holies (see 6.19n). ^9-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-4|4]]:** *Golden altar of incense*, [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^30-1|Ex 30.1–9]]. *Ark of the covenant*, [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-10|Ex 25.10–16]]; [[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^37-1|37.1–5]]. The ark (Heb “aron”) is the mostly holy box that contains the covenant God made with the Israelites at Mt. Sinai. In Exodus the Hebrew term translated as covenant is “edut,” but Hebrews identifies the contents of the ark as the *tablets of the covenant* (Gk “plakes tēs diathēkēs”). In synagogues, the “holy ark” (Heb “aron hakodesh”) is where Torah scrolls are kept. *Urn holding the manna*, [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-33|Ex 16.33–34]]; *Aaron’s rod*, [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-25|Num 17.25]]. *Tablets*, see [[Torah 23 - Pekudei#^40-20|Ex 40.20]]; [[I Kings 08#^8-9|1 Kings 8.9]]. ^9-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-5|5]]:** *Cherubim*, composite creatures with features from eagles, lions, etc.; they served as God’s throne in the Temple ([[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-20|Ex 25.20]]). *Mercy seat*, Gk “hilasterion” in LXX for obscure Heb “kapporet,” the cover on top of the ark of the covenant (see [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-17|Ex 25.17–22]]; [[Torah 35 - Nasso#^7-89|Num 7.89]]). The phrase “mercy seat” originated with Martin Luther’s German Bible translation, and derives from the etymology of “kapporet,” from “kipper,” to affect atonement.” The term occurs elsewhere in NT only in [[Romans 03#^3-25|Rom 3.25]] (“sacrifice of atonement”), and in [[LXX 4 Maccabees 17#^17-22|4 Macc 17.22]] (“atoning sacrifice”). For rabbinic uses, see e.g., *m. Midd*. 1.1; 5.1. ^9-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-7|7]]:** *Once a year*, on Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement; see Lev 16. *Committed unintentionally*, lit., “unknowingly,” though meaning here is ambiguous. Possibly the author is con-flating prescriptions for the Yom Kippur ritual (in which all sins are being atoned) with prescriptions for the various sin-offerings listed in the early chs of Lev, which include offerings designed to atone for sins “unintentionally” committed; see [[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^4-27|Lev 4.27]]; [[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^5-14|5.14]]. It could mean that the priest’s offering effects his own atonement and only sins of which the people are collectively or individually unaware, since people do not seek forgiveness for wrongs they do not know they committed. Alternatively, “sins committed unintentionally” could refer to any sin committed by anyone who is genuinely repentant; in other words, the person did not act in order to sin, but rather the action taken resulted in a wrong being done. The Mishnah teaches that a person who sins knowingly and who believes that Yom Kippur will bestow atonement will not be forgiven (*m. Yoma* 8.9; see [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-2|Sir 23.2]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 13#^13-39|1 Macc 13.39]]). ^9-7

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-8|8]]:** *Holy Spirit*, Hebrews sometimes speaks of the *Holy Spirit* as the speaker of Scripture; see 3.7. *Has not yet been disclosed,* although the high priest has access to the inner sanctuary, the focus here is not on access but on the exclusion of others from that space. *First tent*, see 9.2n. ^9-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-9|9]]:** *Conscience of the worshiper*, relief from the burden of guilt because of sin; cf. [[Psalms 051#^51-3|Ps 51.3–5]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 17#^17-10|Wis 17.10]]. ^9-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-10|10]]:** *Food and drink*, see 13.9n. At best the sacrifices deal with minor matters of purification. See 10.1–4nn. In Jewish tradition, sacrifice has many functions, including worship, commemoration, inauguration, purification, as well as expiation; see “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658. *Set things right*, lit., “time of correction”; although vague, the author appears to be contrasting the “present time” in v 9 with this “time of correction,” presumably a temporal description of the coming world mentioned in ch 1. ^9-10

### 9.11–28: Christ’s better covenant

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-11|Text]], p. 477* ^9-11

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-11|11]]:** *Good things that have come* may refer to the new covenant; the phrase could be translated “good things to come” and so refer to the promise of salvation Jesus’ followers inherit; see ch 4; 5.9; 6.5. *Perfect tent*, meaning the heavenly tabernacle, which is *not made with hands*; see [[Acts 07#^7-48|Acts 7.48]]; [[Acts 17#^17-24|17.24]]. ^9-11-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-12|12]]:** *Holy Place*, in this case equivalent to the Holy of Holies (see 9.2). It is unlikely Hebrews is describing events assumed to have happened during Jesus’ lifetime (i.e., his anointing as high priest and entry into the earthly Holy of Holies). Rather, Hebrews is interpreting Jesus’ crucifixion and resurrection in terms of their salvific efficacy. His death is analogous to the Yom Kippur sacrifice, and his resurrection and ascent to the heavenly realm are analogous to the high priest’s entry to the Holy of Holies. (See vv 23–28.) *Eternal redemption,* see 7.27–28. ^9-12

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-13|13]]:** *Ashes of a heifer*, an allusion to the burning of the red heifer in Num 19, a ritual distinct from Yom Kippur, though sometimes in rabbinic literature, the two sacrifices were paired together (see *m. Zeb.* 14.1). *Sanctifies . . . defiled*, people who are impure. Hebrews does not distinguish between ritual and moral purity (see “The Law,” p. 655). ^9-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-14|14]]:** *How much more* introduces a rabbinic “qal vahomer” (similar to the argument “a fortiori,” “how much more so”) argument from the minor to the major. *Blood of Christ*, see 9.25–28. *Eternal spirit*, lit., “spirit of the age.” The referent is unclear, though it is evidently not the Holy Spirit; it is possibly a paraphrastic expression for divine power. *Without blemish*, sacrificial animals were required to be without blemish (e.g., [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-20|Lev 22.20–21]]). Here it means that Christ was without sin, as was expected of biblical priests who officiated ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-16|Lev 21.16–21]]). *Purify our conscience*, Hebrews sometimes uses the language of purification to describe forgiveness of sin; cf. [[Psalms 051#^51-9|Ps 51.7–17]]. *Dead works*, see 6.1. *Living God*, see 3.12n. ^9-14

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-15|15–22]]:** The author’s logic depends on the double meaning of the Greek “diathēkē” as either “covenant” or “will” in the sense of a last will and testament. Both a “will” and a “covenant” require a death to take effect (a blood sacrifice was part of the ritual of making a covenant). Blood was an intrinsic element of ratifying the Mosaic covenant in [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-4|Ex 24.4–8]]; Christ’s blood is similarly necessary to inaugurate the *new covenant*. Paul also makes use of the double meaning of “diathēkē” in [[Galatians 03#^3-15|Gal 3.15–18]]. ^9-15

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-15|15]]:** *New covenant*, see 8.6–7; 10.9–10. *Eternal inheritance*, salvation; see 1.14; 6.12,17; also [[Romans 08#^8-15|Rom 8.15–17]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-29|Gal 3.29]]; [[Galatians 04#^4-6|4.6–7]]. *Death has occurred*, Jesus’ crucifixion, see v 12n. *Transgressions under the first covenant*, sins as defined by the Tanakh. ^9-15-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-18|18]]:** *Blood*, see [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-4|Ex 24.4–8]]. ^9-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-19|19–21]]:** Although evoking [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-3|Ex 24.3–8]], Hebrews conflates different rituals and the objects they require. See also [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^14-2|Lev 14.2–6]]; [[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-9|Num 19.9]],[[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-18|18]],[[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-20|20]]. For example, the *water and scarlet wool and hyssop* (v 19) are not part of Ex 24**.**3–8. ^9-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-19|19]]:** *Sprinkled both the scroll itself*, an addition to what Exodus describes; the scroll (Gk “biblion,” “book”) probably here refers to the Torah. ^9-19-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-20|20]]:** See [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-8|Ex 24.8]]. ^9-20

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-21|21]]:** *Sprinkled with the blood*, in [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-8|Ex 24.8]] Moses sprinkles the people, not the tent, though in [[Torah 25 - Tzav#^8-15|Lev 8.15]],[[Torah 25 - Tzav#^8-19|19]] Moses sprinkles blood on the altar when ordaining Aaron. ^9-21

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-22|22]]:** *Purified with blood*, in keeping with the logic of Leviticus, blood acts as a kind of ritual detergent (see “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658). *Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins*, Second Temple and rabbinic Judaism see various replacements, from supporting the poor (Tob 4.8–11) to prayer, charity and repentance (*y. Taʾan*. 2.1) to the death of innocent schoolchildren or the righteous (*b. Shabb*. 33b), to the claim in *Avot de R. Natan* A 4 that “a sage sits and expounds to the congregation, Scripture accounts it to him as though he had offered up fat and blood on the altar.” ^9-22

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-23|23]]:** *Sketches of the heavenly things*, a shorthand way to refer to the earthy tabernacle and its accouterments (see vv 1–5). *Sketches* might be better translated “copies” or “patterns.” The author relies on Plato’s idea of ideal forms and imperfect copies (see 10.1n.). *Heavenly things*, meaning unclear; perhaps the world is cleansed from the cosmic force of sin, or that the consciences of worshippers have been purified (see 9.14). ^9-23

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-24|24]]:** *Human hands*, see 9.11. *On our behalf*, see 7.25. ^9-24

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-25|25–26]]:** See 10.1–18n. ^9-25

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-27|27]]:** *Judgment*, views of a collective judgment at the end of time were and *will* b Other ancient authorities lack *and goats* c Or *tabernacle* common among Jews of antiquity; see [[Revelation 20#^20-12|Rev 20.12]]; *Jub.* 5.10; 23.11; *1 En.* 10.4–16; *Mek.* “Beshallah” 4; *Mek.* “Shirah” 6. In the Mishnah, God judges people annually at the new year festival of Rosh Ha-Shanah (*m. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 1.2). ^9-27

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 09#^9-28|28]]:** *Bear the sins of many* is from [[Isaiah 53#^53-12|Isa 53.12]], concerning the suffering servant; it is alluded to in [[Mark 10#^10-45|Mk 10.45]], and a different portion of the passage is quoted in [[Acts 08#^8-32|Acts 8.32–35]]. *Second time*, see [[Mark 13#^13-24|Mk 13.24–27]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-13|1 Thess 3.13]]; [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-13|4.13–17]]. Jewish tradition combines the coming of the Messiah with the messianic age; it requires no “second coming.” *Not to deal with sin*, the crucifixion was a one-time event; Christ’s return does not involve a second sacrifice (see vv 24–26). ^9-28
