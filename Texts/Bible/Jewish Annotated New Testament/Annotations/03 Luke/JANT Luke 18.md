### 18.1–8: Parable of the unjust judge and persistent widow

*[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-1|Text]], p. 152* ^18-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-1|1]]:** *Need to pray*, Luke draws a moral from the parable (see 8.9n.; 15.7). For Luke’s interest in prayer, see 6.28; 11.1–2; 18.1–14; 22.40,46. ^18-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-2|2]]:** *Neither feared . . . nor had respect*, negative traits rather than praise for objectivity ;see [[LXX Sirach 35#^35-14|Sir 35.14–26]]; Josephus (*Ant.* 10.83) describes King Jehoiakim as “neither religious towards God, nor good-natured towards men.” ^18-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-3|3]]:** *Grant me justice*, lit., “avenge me”; biblical tradition offers images of widows ranging from poor, under divine protection, and requiring community support (e.g., [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-19|Deut 27.19]]), to wealthy, stealthy, and deathly (e.g., Abigail, Judith, perhaps Jael). *Opponent*, his side is not mentioned; the woman is not a model of loving enemy (6.35) or avoiding courts (5.25). ^18-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-5|5]]:** *Wear me out*, boxing term suggesting “punch in the eye” (translators’ note *c*). ^18-5

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-7|7]]:** *Chosen ones*, here, followers of Jesus ([[Matthew 24#^24-22|Mt 24.22]],[[Matthew 24#^24-24|24]],[[Matthew 24#^24-31|31]]; [[Mark 13#^13-20|Mk 13.20]],[[Mark 13#^13-22|22]],[[Mark 13#^13-27|27]]; [[Romans 08#^8-33|Rom 8.33]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-12|Col 3.12]]; [[Titus 01#^1-1|Titus 1.1]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-9|1 Pet 2.9]]; [[Revelation 17#^17-14|Rev 17.14]]); a “qal vahomer” argument (see 13.15n.; if the unjust judge responds, how much more so will the just judge). ^18-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-8|8]]:** *Comes*, to judge (17.22–37; [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13–14]]). Luke extends the parable’s interpretation: fidelity should not wane given the delay of divine justice. *be in the field; one will be taken and the other left.”* b Gk *he* c Or *so that she may not finally come and slap me in the face* ^18-8

> [!note] Parable of the Pharisee and the Tax Collector ([[Luke 18#^18-9|Lk 18.9–14]]) *(p. 152)*
> Some readers dismiss the Pharisee as hypocritical, sanctimonious, and legalistic, and in turn identify with the tax collector, the repentant, humble, and justified sinner. This approach is not surprising, given that Luke has presented numerous maleficent Pharisees as well as several admirable tax collectors. However, once readers choose to identify with the tax collector and reject the Pharisee, the parable traps them: to conclude in effect (following 18.11), “God, I thank you that I am not like this Pharisee . . . ” places readers in the very position they condemn. Moreover, this interpretation overlooks the Pharisee’s numerous excellent qualities: tithing, fasting, giving thanks without asking for something in return.
>
> Other readers presume that the tax collector stands “far off” (18.13) because other worshippers ostracize him, believing him to be ritually impure. The parable says nothing about either ostracism or impurity; to the contrary, to enter the Temple a person must be ritually pure. Even were he ostracized, the cause would not be impurity but employment: he works for Rome, the occupation government.
>
> Still other readers perceive the Temple to have become an elitist, xenophobic, misogynist, fully corrupt “domination system” that Jesus rejects. Again, the parable thwarts this stereotype, since it is in the Temple that repentance and reconciliation occur.
>
> We might rather see the Pharisee as helping the tax collector. Just as the sin of one person impacts the community (e.g., “forgive us our sins” \[11.4\] rather than “forgive me my sins”), so the merits of the righteous can benefit the community (see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-24|Gen 18.24–33]]; hence one view of the cross: the sacrifice of one can save the many). Jews who first heard this parable (especially if 18.14b, a line that appears in various places in the Gospel, was not original to the parable; see 14.11; [[Matthew 23#^23-12|Matt 23.12]]) may well have understood the Pharisee’s merit to have impacted the tax collector. This would be the parable’s shock: not only that the agent of Rome is justified but that the Pharisee’s own good works helped in that justification.

### 18.9–14: Parable of the Pharisee and tax collector

*[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-9|Text]], p. 153* · See “Parable of the Pharisee and the Tax Collector,” p. 152. ^18-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-9|9]]:** *Righteous*, see 1.16–17; 5.32; 15.7; 23.50. ^18-9-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-10|10]]:** *Tax collector*, see 3.12n. ^18-10

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-12|12]]:** *Fast twice a week . . . give a tenth of all*, supererogatory practice. For Jewish fasting practices, see 5.33n.; *Pss. Sol*. 3.9; *Did*. 8; Tacitus, *Hist*. 5.4; Suetonius, *Aug*. 76.3; *Meg. Taʾan*. ^18-12

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-13|13]]:** *Beating his breast*, here indicating contrition (as Jews do on Yom Kippur when reciting the “ ʾal ḥet,” the litany of sins) and not mourning (e.g., [[Nahum 2#^2-8|Nah 2.7]]). ^18-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-14|14]]:** *Justified*, restored to a right relationship with God. *Rather than*, the Greek could be translated “alongside,” indicating both were justified. *All who exalt*, see 13.30; 14.11; [[Matthew 23#^23-12|Mt 23.12]]. ^18-14

### 18.15–17: Little children

*[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-15|Text]], p. 153* · ([[Matthew 19#^19-13|Mt 19.13–15]]; [[Mark 10#^10-13|Mk 10.13–16]]). ^18-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-15|15]]:** *Bringing even infants*, see 9.42n. *Touch*, for a blessing and/or healing. *Sternly ordered*, Christian commentators who suggest that Jews do not care about children and here have the disciples representing Jewish culture miss the point that it is the Jewish parents and caregivers who are bringing their children to Jesus. ^18-15-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-17|17]]:** *As a little child*, with dependence, not pride. ^18-17

### 18.18–30: The rich ruler

*[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-18|Text]], p. 153* · ([[Matthew 19#^19-16|Mt 19.16–30]]; [[Mark 10#^10-17|Mk 10.17–31]]). ^18-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-18|18]]:** *Good Teacher*, respectful address. *Inherit eternal life*, see 10.25n. ^18-18-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-19|19]]:** Jesus distinguishes himself from God; see 1.17n.; 4.8; 5.25; 8.39. ^18-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-20|20]]:** *Commandments*, see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-12|Ex 20.12–16]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-16|Deut 5.16–20]], following the Septuagint’s order. The listing is not intended to be limited only to these commandments. ^18-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-22|22]]:** *Sell all that you own*, see 6.30n.; 14.33. *Treasure in heaven*, see 12.33–34. ^18-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-23|23]]:** *Very rich*, see 12.16n. ^18-23

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-24|24]]:** *Those who have wealth*, see 16.14n. ^18-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-25|25]]:** *Eye of a needle*, contrary to popular legend, Jerusalem had no “needle gate” through which camels passed with difficulty. ^18-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-27|27]]:** *Possible for God*, see 1.37; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-14|Gen 18.14]]; [[Jeremiah 32#^32-17|Jer 32.17]]; [[Job 42#^42-2|Job 42.2]]. ^18-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-28|28]]:** *Left our homes*, see 5.1–11. ^18-28

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-29|29]]:** *Wife*, an addition to [[Matthew 19#^19-29|Matt 19.29]]; continuing the theme of familial disruption (see 12.51n.). ^18-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-30|30]]:** *Age to come*, the “olam ha-ba,” see 11.2; *m. Avot* 4.17: “Better is one hour of repentance and good works in this world than the whole life in the world to come, and better is one hour of bliss in the world to come than the whole life of this world.” ^18-30

### 18.31–34: Third Passion prediction

*[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-31|Text]], p. 154* · ([[Matthew 20#^20-17|Mt 20.17–19]]; [[Mark 10#^10-32|Mk 10.32–34]]). See 9.22,44–45; 17.25. ^18-31

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-31|31]]:** The passages usually cited as *written about . . . by the prophets* (e.g., Isa 53; [[Zechariah 13#^13-7|Zech 13.7]]; Ps 22, understood here as a prophecy) do not use the phrase “Son of Man.” Most of these passages receive messianic interpretation only in Christian sources. *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. ^18-31-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-34|34]]:** *Was hidden from them*, see 24.16. ^18-34

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-35|18.35–43]]:** **Healing a blind man** ([[Matthew 20#^20-29|Mt 20.29–34]]; [[Mark 10#^10-46|Mk 10.46–52]]). ^18-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-35|35]]:** *Jericho*, see 10.30n.; 19.1. ^18-35-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-38|38]]:** *Son of David*, see 1.27n.; 3.31; 20.41–44n.; see also [[Matthew 22#^22-42|Mt 22.42]]; [[Mark 12#^12-35|Mk 12.35]]. ^18-38

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-42|42]]:** *Faith has saved you*, see 7.50n.; 8.48; 17.19. ^18-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 18#^18-43|43]]:** *All the people* (Gk “pas ho laos”; see 2.10; [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]]), Jesus retains the support of the Jewish population. ^18-43
