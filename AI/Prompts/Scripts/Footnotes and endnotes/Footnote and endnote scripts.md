# Footnote and endnote scripts

Three tested ways of giving a converted book its footnotes or endnotes, from 15 Oct 2026 (decisions D31c and D31d). The scripts ran in a Claude cloud workspace; paths inside them (`/home/claude/...`, `/mnt/user-data/...`) must be adapted. The reports are kept as `.txt` so that their example links are not read as vault links.

**Which one to use.** First look at the PDF's text layer for one page with notes (PyMuPDF `get_text('dict')`: fonts, sizes, positions) and at the same page in the converted notes.

- **Re-convert with footnote zones** (`goodall/`): when the PDF is born-digital and the converter can be taught where the notes zone starts and how the note calls are set. Used for Goodall (ed.), *Śaivism and the Tantric Traditions* (Brill: raised digits drawn as special glyphs, margin note numbers apart from their text, a short rule above the notes): 1,124 footnotes. `conv.py` is the converter of 9 Oct with every change behind a job option (`conv.diff` shows them); `merge.py` carries the current notes' later corrections and links into the new conversion; `check.py` compares notes per page with the PDF's margin numbers. Read `REPORT-goodall.txt`. On Bang's thesis the same options gave fewer footnotes than the first conversion, so it was not applied.
- **Restructure the existing notes from the PDF's text layer** (`finn/`): when the notes were made by a fresh OCR (good words, no structure) and the PDF still carries an older text layer with positions and fonts. Used for Finn's typescript: italic notes at the foot of the page, margin numbers for verses and notes, small raised note calls. `layer.py` dumps the layer; `pdfside.py` finds the notes zone (best roman/italic split), margin labels and raised numbers; `mdpages.py` splits the notes at their page markers; `transform.py` aligns the layer's words with the notes' words (difflib), puts each note call where the layer has it (removing the OCR's misread digits, never letters unless they are a misread digit of that note's number), turns each note into a Markdown footnote, restores verse numbers that the OCR had read apart from their verses, and splits notes the OCR had run together; `runall.py` and `assemble.py` build the new notes and check them (page markers unchanged, every reference paired with one definition, the words unchanged apart from the removed digits). `finnfn-apply.js` is the runner job that wrote them (SHA-256 check of each note first). Pages that already had footnotes were left alone. For a book whose notes are smaller type rather than italic, change the zone test in `pdfside.py` to font size. Read `REPORT-first-look.txt` for the layout analysis.
- **Restructure from the text layer and the page images** (`torzsok/`, 18 Oct 2026): for a skewed typescript scan whose OCR misreads or drops the raised calls and the note numbers (Törzsök's thesis: 120 footnotes). Calls are confirmed on the page image (ink above the baseline) and their digits read from the image by shapes learnt from the book's own correctly read calls; a footnote is written only where the call's digits and the note's own number at both ends are read. Read `REPORT-torzsok.txt`, including *What went wrong first*: a version that guessed note starts from indentation attached definitions to the wrong notes, which the word and pairing checks did not catch.
- **Text-only notes** (`rastogi/`, 18 Oct 2026): for a book whose notes are partly images (Rastogi's Devanagari quotations): only notes that are wholly text in the layer, not followed by an image and not running on, become footnotes (108). Read `REPORT-rastogi.txt`.
- **Section notes from the text layer** (`dhvanyaloka/`, 18 Oct 2026): for a book whose notes follow each block of text and restart at 1 (the Dhvanyāloka's K/A/L blocks: 886 footnotes). Blocks, note labels and call digits come from the ABBYY layer; a footnote is written only where the call's digits and the note's own number at both ends are read. Quote marks next to a call are never removed there: neither OCR tells them from a misread number. Read `REPORT-dhvanyaloka.txt`.
- **Endnote calls read from the page images** (`deshpande/`, 18 Oct 2026): for a low-resolution scan where neither OCR has the raised numbers (Deshpande's *Abhinavagupta*: 81 calls linked to the appendix). Subagents read the numbers from zoomed bands of every page; the script checks each chapter's sequence, finds the words before each call in the note, and links it to a block id on the endnote, placed from the PDF layer's labels. Read `REPORT-deshpande.txt`.
- **Footnotes placed by page readers** (`kubjika/`, 26 Oct 2026): for a fresh OCR whose calls and note numbers came out as stray marks or not at all, with notes that run over page breaks (Dyczkowski, *Kubjikā, Kālī, Tripurā and Trika*: 162 footnotes). Readers (agents) go through every page image and write an op list (`reader-brief.txt` says how); `apply_ops.py` applies it, joins carried-over notes to their footnote, and checks one call and one definition per number. A third reader placed a sample blind.
- **Link endnote calls to their endnotes** (`endnotes/`): for books whose notes are endnotes in a Notes section (Ricoeur, *Politics, Economy, and Society*: 123; Stiegler, *Nanjing Lectures*: 452). Each endnote paragraph gets a block id, each call becomes a link with the superscript digits as its label. Read `REPORT-endnotes.txt`.

**Checks every time:** page-marker lines identical and in order (each followed by a blank line, so its `^pg` id registers); headings and frontmatter unchanged apart from `text_source`; the words of each note unchanged once links, footnote markers and removed OCR digits are set aside; every `[^n]` paired with exactly one `[^n]:`; no link inside frontmatter; look at five or six pages against the page images, including the pages the scripts found hardest, **before** applying (since 18 Oct: the definitions' text against the printed notes, not only where the references sit); every paragraph without a footnote is an original paragraph or a piece of one (`torzsok/layoutcheck.py`).
