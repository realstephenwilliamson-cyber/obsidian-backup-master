### 26.1–29: Paul’s final defense

*[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-1|Text]], p. 275* · (cf. 22.1–21; 24.10–21), in which he responds to the Jews’ charges (vv 2–8), describes his conversion (vv 9–18), summarizes his preaching (vv 19–23), and appeals to Agrippa II (vv 24–29). ^26-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-1|1]]:** *Stretched out his hand*, common gesture calling for quiet (12.17; 13.16; 21.40). ^26-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-2|2–4]]:** Paul opens with standard words of praise; see 24.2–4. ^26-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-3|3]]:** *Customs . . . of the Jews*, Paul signals that the charges against him are based on violation of Jewish (23.29) and not Roman law (17.7; 24.5). ^26-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-4|4–5]]:** Acts represents Paul as an observant Jew; see 22.3; 24.15–16. *Pharisee*, Paul identifies himself as a Pharisee, see [[Philippians 03#^3-5|Phil 3.5]]. ^26-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-6|6–8]]:** *Promise made by God to our ancestors*, standard promises made by God to the ancestors had to do with progeny and land (e.g., [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|Gen 15.5]]). Here it seems that the promise refers to Jesus’ resurrection (v 8) that inaugurates the end of days ([[Isaiah 02#^2-2|Isa 2.2]]; [[Hosea 03#^3-5|Hos 3.5]]; [[Micah 4#^4-1|Mic 4.1]]), including the revival of God’s people ([[Isaiah 26#^26-19|Isa 26.19]]). ^26-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-9|9–18]]:** Paul’s conversion, see also 9.1–19; 22.3–21. ^26-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-9|9–11]]:** Unlike previous reports (9.1–2; 22.4–5), Paul describes his actions against believers in Jesus as widespread and aggressive. ^26-9-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-10|10]]:** *Saints*, see 9.13n. ^26-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-14|14]]:** *In the Hebrew language*, a detail not mentioned previously. *It hurts you to kick against the goads*, not previously quoted, depicting Paul as resisting the pressure of God’s plan by comparing him to a draft animal kicking the stick used to move it in the desired direction. The saying is frequently attested in Greek literature (e.g., Euripides, *Bacc.* 795). ^26-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-18|18]]:** *From darkness to light*, a transformation that occurs when one acknowledges God and in this context Jesus as well (13.47; [[Isaiah 42#^42-16|Isa 42.16]]; [[Luke 01#^1-79|Lk 1.79]]; [[Luke 02#^2-32|2.32]]). *Satan*, conversion marks the moving away from the powers of evil; the implication may be that Jews who resist conversion are under Satan’s control. *Forgiveness of sins*, occurs with belief in Jesus (2.38; 5.31; 10.43; 13.38). ^26-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-20|20]]:** *Repent*, Paul calls for all to repent (17.30; 20.21). ^26-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-21|21]]:** See 21.27–36. ^26-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-22|22]]:** Paul’s claim that his instruction comes from the prophets and Moses authenticates his status as a Jew (24.14–16) and establishes the Christian message as consistent with biblical teaching. On *prophets and Moses*, see 24.14n. ^26-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-23|23]]:** *Messiah must suffer*, Christian understanding of messianic identity; see 3.18,21–25; 8.32–35; 9.16; 17.3; [[Luke 02#^2-49|Lk 2.49]]; [[Luke 24#^24-7|24.7]],[[Luke 24#^24-26|26]],[[Luke 24#^24-46|46]]). *Light*, see v 18. ^26-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-24|24]]:** Festus confirms that Paul’s views are based on knowledge, but that their results, or possibly Paul’s zeal for them, show them to be insane. ^26-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-27|27]]:** *Do you believe*, Paul’s statement implies that anyone who believes in the prophets should also believe in Jesus. ^26-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-31|31]]:** *Doing nothing to deserve death*, Paul’s innocence is confirmed by Roman and Jewish authorities; see 23.29; 25.25. ^26-31

**[[NRSVue Acts 26#^26-32|32]]:** *Appealed to the emperor*, see 25.11. Paul will appear before the highest human authority. A judgment by the emperor, however, cannot contravene the divine approval of Paul’s mission. ^26-32
