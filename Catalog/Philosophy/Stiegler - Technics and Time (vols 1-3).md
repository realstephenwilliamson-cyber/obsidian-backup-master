## About

Bernard Stiegler's three-volume philosophy of technology. Wikipedia summarises its thesis: technics forms the horizon of human existence, the history of philosophy has suppressed this by working from the split between *epistēmē* and *technē*, and the genesis of technics is the genesis of the human and of temporality.

- **Author:** French philosopher (1 April 1952 – 5 August 2020). He turned to philosophy in prison (1978–83), studying by correspondence with Gérard Granel, and took his doctorate at the EHESS in 1993 under Jacques Derrida.
- **Volume 1:** *La technique et le temps, 1: La faute d'Épiméthée* (Galilée, 1994); English *The Fault of Epimetheus*, translated by Richard Beardsworth and George Collins (Stanford University Press, 1998). Part I reads Leroi-Gourhan, Simondon and Bertrand Gille on the history of technology and hominisation and finds the relation between the human *who* and the technical *what* undecidable. Part II reads Heidegger, with the myth of Prometheus at its centre.
- **Volume 2:** *La désorientation* (1996); English *Disorientation*, translated by Stephen Barker (Stanford). Wikipedia's article on the book gives 2008; its article on Stiegler gives 2009; Stanford University Press's own page gives October 2008.
- **Volume 3:** *Le temps du cinéma et la question du mal-être* (2001); English *Cinematic Time and the Question of Malaise*, translated by Stephen Barker (Stanford, 2010).
- **Key ideas:** technics, as organised inorganic matter, is essentially a form of memory and constitutive of human temporality; humans are prosthetic creatures. The series also reads Husserl and Kant, and offers what Wikipedia calls a cinematic constitution of consciousness.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia calls the series the fullest systematic statement of Stiegler's philosophy.
