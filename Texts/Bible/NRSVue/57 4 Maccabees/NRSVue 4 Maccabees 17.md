# 4 Maccabees 17 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 16|← 4 Maccabees 16]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 18|4 Maccabees 18 →]]

**17:1** Some of the guards said that when she also was about to be seized and put to death she threw herself into the flames so that no one might touch her body. ^17-1

**17:2** O mother, who with your seven sons nullified the violence of the tyrant, frustrated his evil designs, and showed the nobility of your faith! ^17-2

**17:3** Nobly set like a roof on the pillars of your sons, you held firm and unswerving against the earthquake of the tortures. ^17-3

**17:4** Take courage, therefore, O holy-minded mother, maintaining firm an enduring hope in God. ^17-4

**17:5** The moon in heaven with the stars is not so majestic as you, who, after lighting the way of your star-like seven sons to piety, stand in honor before God and are firmly set in heaven with them. ^17-5

**17:6** For your children were true descendants of father Abraham.^[Gk *For your childbearing was from Abraham the father*; other ancient authorities read *For . . . Abraham the servant*] ^17-6

## The effect of the martyrdoms

**17:7** If it were possible for us to paint the history of your piety as an artist might, would not those who beheld it shudder as they saw the mother of the seven children enduring their varied tortures to death for the sake of piety? ^17-7

**17:8** Indeed, it would be proper to inscribe on their tomb these words as a reminder to the people of our nation:^[Or *as a memorial to the heroes of our people*] ^17-8

**17:9** “Here lie buried an aged priest and an aged woman and seven children because of the violence of the tyrant who wished to destroy the way of life of the Hebrews. ^17-9

**17:10** They vindicated their nation, looking to God and enduring torture even to death.” ^17-10

**17:11** Truly the contest in which they were engaged was divine, ^17-11

**17:12** for on that day virtue gave the awards and tested them for their endurance. The prize was immortality in endless life. ^17-12

**17:13** Eleazar was the first contestant, the mother of the seven sons entered the competition, and the brothers contended. ^17-13

**17:14** The tyrant was the antagonist, and the world and the human race were the spectators. ^17-14

**17:15** Reverence for God was victor and gave the crown to its own athletes. ^17-15

**17:16** Who did not admire the athletes of the divine^[Other ancient authorities read *true*] legislation? Who were not amazed? ^17-16

**17:17** The tyrant himself and all his council marveled at their endurance, ^17-17

**17:18** because of which they now stand before the divine throne and live the life of eternal blessedness. ^17-18

**17:19** For Moses says, “All who are consecrated are under your hands.” ^17-19

**17:20** These, then, who have been consecrated for the sake of God are honored not only with this honor but also by the fact that because of them our enemies did not rule over our nation, ^17-20

**17:21** the tyrant was punished, and the homeland purified—they having become, as it were, a ransom for the sin of our nation. ^17-21

**17:22** And through the blood of those pious ones and their death as an atoning sacrifice, divine Providence preserved Israel that previously had been mistreated. ^17-22

**17:23** For the tyrant Antiochus, when he saw the courage of their virtue and their endurance under the tortures, proclaimed their endurance to his soldiers as an example, ^17-23

**17:24** and this made them high-minded and courageous for infantry battle and siege, and he ravaged and conquered all his enemies. ^17-24
