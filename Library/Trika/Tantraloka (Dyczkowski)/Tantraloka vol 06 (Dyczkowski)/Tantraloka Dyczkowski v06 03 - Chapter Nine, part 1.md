#### Viveka on 9.1

। The word ‘tattva’ is a neuter noun which is derived from the pronoun ‘rat’, meaning ‘that’, to which is added the suffix ‘-tva’, that corresponds to the suffix ‘-ness’ added at the end of some abstract nouns in English. Thus, tattva literally means ‘that-ness’. The Monier-Williams dictionary translates the word in a broad general sense as ʻa true or real state, truth, reality; an element or elementary property, the essence or substance of anything’. This translation is derived from the definition given to the term by the empiricist, realist Nyāya school. We read in the introduction of the Nyāyabhāṣya: ‘what then is rattva? (It is) the true state of being of what exists, and the absence of a true state of being of what does not exist. Accepting the true existence of what truly exists just as it is, 15 zattva, it is not false (aviparīta ~ lit. ‘not reversed’). According to Somadeva (2004: 191-2): ‘Abhinava understands a rattva to be similar to the common property (sāmānya) present in all members of a superset (mahājāti).
