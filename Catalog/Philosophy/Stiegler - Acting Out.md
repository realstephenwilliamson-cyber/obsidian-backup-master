## About

An English volume joining two short books by the French philosopher Bernard Stiegler (1952–2020), published by Stanford University Press in 2009 in a translation by David Barison, Daniel Ross and Patrick Crogan. The first is autobiographical; the second concerns desire and its disorders in consumer society.

- **French originals:** both 2003: *Passer à l'acte* and *Aimer, s'aimer, nous aimer: Du 11 septembre au 21 avril*.
- **Author:** born at Villebon-sur-Yvette. Wikipedia says he was imprisoned from 1978 to 1983 for armed robbery, first at Saint-Michel prison in Toulouse and then at Muret, and began studying philosophy by correspondence with Gérard Granel. *Radical Philosophy* says he was caught on his fourth bank robbery and served five years. He later took a doctorate at the EHESS under Jacques Derrida (1993), directed IRCAM and founded the Institut de recherche et d'innovation at the Centre Pompidou (2006).
- **"How I Became a Philosopher":** first given as a talk in 2003 and framed as a confession after Rousseau. *Radical Philosophy* says it was where he first made his imprisonment public. He describes prison as a quasi-phenomenological rediscovery of the world, drawing on Husserl, Aristotle and Epictetus.
- **"To Love, to Love Me, to Love Us: From September 11 to April 21":** argues that a consumer society run on television advertising wears away the feeling of existing and what Stiegler calls primordial narcissism, producing individual and collective pathologies. His examples include the September 11 attacks, the National Front's electoral success and Richard Durn's killings at Nanterre.
- **Ideas:** Wikipedia ties the book to Stiegler's theory of individuation, partly drawn from Gilbert Simondon, and to his account of synchronic and diachronic processes. *Radical Philosophy* lists his wider themes as technics as constitutive of the human, proletarianisation and the pharmakon.
