### 11:1–14: Interlude—measurement of the current Temple and the eschatological acts of Moses and Elijah

*[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 556* ^11-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-1|1–2]]:** As in Ezekiel’s vision of the new Temple (Ezek 40–42), measurement denotes divine perfection, although here the seer, not an angel, measures (see [[Ezekiel 40#^40-3|Ezek 40.3]]; [[Revelation 21#^21-9|Rev 21.9–21]]). That only the outer court of Gentiles is consigned to desecration could mean the vision originated before 70 CE, since after 70 an author would have realized the whole Temple had been destroyed. *Forty-two months*, three and a half years or 1,260 days, a lengthy time, inspired by [[Daniel 07#^7-25|Dan 7.25]]; [[Daniel 12#^12-7|12.7]]. ^11-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Two witnesses*, typically identified as Moses and Elijah for their miraculous powers (11.5–6; see Ex 7–12; 1 Kings 17–21; 2 Kings 1). *Sackcloth*, rough fabric (often made from goat or camel hair) similar to burlap, worn next to the skin as a sign of mourning and repentance ([[Isaiah 50#^50-3|Isa 50.3]]; Jon 3.5–6). ^11-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-4|4]]:** *Olive trees*, source for oil for Temple lamps ([[Zechariah 04#^4-3|Zech 4.3–14]]). *Lampstands*, see 1.12n. ^11-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-6|6]]:** *Shut the sky*, bring a drought as Elijah did ([[I Kings 17#^17-1|1 Kings 17.1]]). *Blood . . . plague*, as in the Exodus tradition (see 8.7–9nn.). ^11-6

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-7|7]]:** *Beast*, see 13.1; 17.8. A monster from the Abyss (cf. 9.2–3), symbolizing evil. ^11-7

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-8|8]]:** *Great city*, Jerusalem, as indicated by reference to Jesus’ crucifixion. This is one of Revelation’s few references to the historical Jesus. *Sodom*, is analogized to Jerusalem in [[Ezekiel 16#^16-46|Ezek 16.46–56]] (see [[Isaiah 01#^1-10|Isa 1.10]]; [[Jeremiah 23#^23-14|Jer 23.14]]), an analogy to the city of violence and fornication of Gen 19. *Egypt*, designates Jerusalem negatively, continuing a biblical typology of “evil” places. ^11-8

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-9|9]]:** *Three and a half days*, see 11.1–2n*. Peoples and tribes*, Jerusalem seen as overrun with Gentiles and with little care for dead bodies (see [[Psalms 079#^79-1|Ps 79.1–3]]; Tob 1–2). ^11-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-10|10]]:** *Exchange presents*, parody of celebration. ^11-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-11|11]]:** *Breath of life from God*, see [[Ezekiel 37#^37-5|Ezek 37.5]],[[Ezekiel 37#^37-10|10]], following [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]], but here designating resurrection rather than initial vivification. ^11-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-12|12]]:** *Come up here*, an ascension. *Cloud*, traditional means of heavenly transport (see [[Ezekiel 01#^1-4|Ezek 1.4]]; [[Psalms 018#^18-12|Ps 18.11–12]]; [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]; *4 Ezra* 13.3; [[Mark 13#^13-26|Mk 13.26]]). ^11-12

### 11.15–19: The seventh trumpet

*[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-15|Text]], p. 557* · The seventh trumpet initiates a sequence of liturgical acts—songs of announcement, prostration, songs of thanksgiving—that brings about a shift in *kingdom*\[s\], from worldly to divine dominion. Verb tenses describe God’s dominion as a completed, victorious event. ^11-15

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-15|15]]:** *Kingdom of the world*, the earthly rulers. *He will reign forever and ever*, [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-18|Ex 15.18]]. While *of his Messiah* indicates the Messiah’s subordination to God, it is ambiguous whether it is *our Lord* or *his Messiah* who will *reign* (in the singular) *forever and ever.* ^11-15-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 11#^11-19|19]]:** This victory song seems to be inspired by Ps 2 (see vv 1,5,9–10,12). *Temple in heaven*, see 4.1–11 and “The Heavenly Temple Cult,” p. 553. At the conclusion of this thanksgiving song the reader is granted visual access to the very inner part of the heavenly Temple, where stands the ark. *Flashes of lightning*, as in Ezekiel (1.4,14,24), the vision of the interior furniture of the Temple is likened to a storm theophany (see 4.5; 8.5; 10.3). ^11-19
