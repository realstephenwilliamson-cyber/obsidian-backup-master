### 2.1–10: Paul meets with the apostles

*[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 376* · Acts 15 gives a different account of these events, omitting the confrontation between Paul and the leaders. ^2-1

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-1|1]]:** *After fourteen years*, the date from which this is to be reckoned is not clear; see 1.18n. Paul again claims apostolic independence. *Jerusalem*, 1.17n. *Barnabas*, Paul’s Jewish companion ([[Acts 04#^4-36|Acts 4.36]]; [[Acts 09#^9-27|9.27]]; [[Acts 11#^11-22|11.22–30]]; [[Acts 12#^12-25|12.25]]; [[Acts 13#^13-1|13.1–15.39]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-6|1 Cor 9.6]]). *Titus*, Paul brings Titus (2.3) to test the Jerusalem church’s acceptance of uncircumcised Gentiles. ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Revelation,* either his original revelation (1.12) or a subsequent one; Paul clarifies that he was not summoned by the Jerusalem church. *I laid before them*, according to this translation Paul had only one meeting, that with the leaders of the community. Alternatively, *I laid before them* \[the whole community\] *the gospel that I proclaim among the Gentiles*, and again later, *in a private meeting with the acknowledged leaders*, which suggests that Paul had two meetings, one public and one private. *Acknowledged leaders*, lit., “those who seem,” “those who are esteemed,” James, Cephas, and John, see 2.6,9. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-3|3]]:** *Titus . . .  was not compelled*, vindicating Paul’s insistence that Gentile believers need not become Jews. *Greek*, Gentile. See 3.28. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-4|4]]:** *False believers*, lit., “false brothers” (see [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-26|2 Cor 11.26]]) who insisted that Gentile believers must be circumcised. *Secretly brought in*, lit., “intrusive, alien.” The Gk implies not stealth but inappropriateness or foreignness. *Slipped in*, lit., “came in alongside,” not necessarily implying stealth (see [[Romans 05#^5-20|Rom 5.20]]). *Freedom*, enjoyed by believers is a major theme here (5.1) and elsewhere ([[1 Corinthians 10#^10-29|1 Cor 10.29]]; [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-17|2 Cor 3.17]]). *Enslave*, slavery (see 5.1; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-22|1 Cor 7.22]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|9.19]]) entails following Torah commandments. Rabbinic texts recognize that the observance of the commandments is a “yoke” (e.g., *m. Ber.* 2.2) but argue that true freedom is gained by voluntary subordination to God’s will (e.g., *m. Avot* 2.4 and 6.2). See 5.1n. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-5|5]]:** *We did not submit*, some versions omit “not”; perhaps this reading was generated to explain Paul’s circumcision of Timothy ([[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1–3]]) and the rumor Paul denies at 5.11. An example of anacoluthon, in which the speaker changes direction and syntax midsentence. The meaning is “Because of the presence of false brethren, the leaders of the Jerusalem community tried to persuade me to circumcise Titus, but I did not yield to them.” ^2-5

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-6|6]]:** Another anacoluthon. The meaning is “And from those who were supposed to be acknowledged leaders . *. .* I received nothing.” *Shows no partiality*, lit., “does not take the face of a person,” a biblical idiom describing the ideal judge, mortal or divine (e.g., [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-15|Lev 19.15]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-17|Deut 10.17]]; [[II Chronicles 19#^19-7|2 Chr 19.7]]; see also [[Romans 02#^2-11|Rom 2.11]]). *Contributed nothing*, perhaps “did not confer with me” (same word as in 1.16). ^2-6

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Had been entrusted*, by God (2.8). *The uncircumcised*, lit., “the foreskinhood,” those who have a foreskin, Gentiles (see 5.6). *The circumcised*, lit., “the circumcision,” Jews. See [[Ephesians 02#^2-11|Eph 2.11]], “Gentiles by birth, called \[the foreskin\] by those who are called ‘the circumcision.’” *M. Ned.* 3.11 attests the same usage, “‘the foreskin’ is a name that applies only to Gentiles.” In contrast, Acts 10 depicts Peter as bringing the gospel to Gentiles (see [[Matthew 28#^28-19|Mt 28.19]]), and Acts 13 depicts Paul as first evangelizing Jews. ^2-7

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-9|9]]:** *John*, the son of Zebedee ([[Mark 01#^1-19|Mk 1.19]]), Peter’s associate (Acts 3–4), not elsewhere mentioned by Paul. *Pillars*, a metaphor also found in rabbinic literature ([[Berakhot 28#28b|*b. Ber.* 28b]]). *Right hand of fellowship*, our “shaking hands,” a gesture attested in [[II Kings 10#^10-15|2 Kings 10.15]]; [[Ezra 10#^10-19|Ezra 10.19]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 18.328. ^2-9

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Asked only one thing*, the great compromise of [[Acts 15#^15-20|Acts 15.20]],[[Acts 15#^15-29|29]] is conspicuously missing. *Remember the poor*, Paul raises money for the Jerusalem “poor” ([[Romans 15#^15-25|Rom 15.25–29]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-1|1 Cor 16.1–3]]; 2 Cor 8–9), the church’s self-designation. The use of the plural term “the poor” as a self-designation may have its origins in the Psalms; see, e.g., [[Psalms 034#^34-3|Ps 34.2]]; 147.6; 149.4. Giving money to the poor and raising money for the poor are paramount virtues in rabbinic piety (e.g., *t. Peʾah* 4.18–21, “charity and deeds of kindness equal all the other commandments of the Torah”; and *Lev. Rab.* 5.4). ^2-10

### 2.11–14: Paul confronts Peter at Antioch

*[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-11|Text]], p. 378* ^2-11

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-12|12]]:** *James*, 1.19n. *Eat with the Gentiles,* on table fellowship, see “Food and Table Fellowship,” p. 650. *Fear of the circumcision faction*, lit., “fear of those from the circumcised,” that is, Jews. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Other Jews*, most likely, Jewish believers of the church in Antioch. *Barnabas*, see 2.1n. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Cephas*, see 1.18n. *Live like a Gentile*, there are no other reports that Peter abandoned Torah Law, although Acts 10 can be understood as Peter’s rejection of the dietary laws. *Live like Jews*, lit., “Judaize,” to follow Jewish practices, see 1.13n. Paul interprets Peter’s refusal to dine with Gentile believers as an effort to compel them to observe Torah Law. As the translator’s note *a* indicates, v 14 is addressed to Peter, and 3.1 is addressed to the Galatians. Somewhere in between (the break is usually put right after v 14) Paul slides imperceptibly from a report of what he told Peter to an exposition of his argument. ^2-14

### 2.15–21: Faith versus Law

*[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-15|Text]], p. 378* ^2-15

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *We*, Paul and Peter. *By birth*, or “by nature”; see [[Romans 02#^2-27|Rom 2.27]]; [[Romans 11#^11-21|11.21–24]]. *Gentile*s \[are\] *sinners* because they worship idols and do not follow the Torah. Since Paul believes that Gentiles can be justified before God even without circumcision or converting to Judaism (v 16), either he is speaking ironically when he calls Gentiles “sinners” or he is citing the view of his traditionalist opponents. ^2-15-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-16|16]]:** *Justified*, or “reckoned as righteous.” The LXX uses the Gk “dikaiosynê” to translate Heb “tzedaqah,” “righteousness,” especially in [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]], a significant verse for Paul (3.6; [[Romans 04#^4-3|Rom 4.3]]). *Works of the law*, lit., “works of law,” legal observances (3.2; [[Romans 03#^3-28|Rom 3.28]]). With the definite article (*works of the law*; see 4.21n.) the phrase would be equivalent to “maʾasei ha-Torah*,*” lit., “works of the Torah,” which appears at Qumran (4QMMT). Greek-speaking Jews used “nomos” to translate “Torah,” even though the connotations of the two terms differ: “nomos,” usually translated “law,” connotes proper practice or the accustomed way of doing things, while “Torah” connotes instruction or teaching. *Faith* (Gk “pistis”), like Heb “emunah*,*” connotes “trust, confidence”; it never refers to indemonstra-ble belief. *Faith in Jesus Christ*, means confidence in Christ. The alternative translation, “faith of Jesus Christ,” means that Jesus had confidence in God. The phrase is usually understood in the former sense (see 3.26, which is unambiguous), but the latter is possible. *No one will be justified*, [[LXX Psalms 142#^142-2|Ps 142.2]], “for no one living is righteous before you.” Paul adds *by the works of the law*, see [[Romans 03#^3-20|Rom 3.20]]. ^2-16

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-17|17]]:** *We ourselves have been found to be sinners*, according to you, Peter; because you separated from Gentiles, implying that our table fellowship with them had been sinful. *Certainly not!* Because faith in Christ allows us to associate with Gentiles. ^2-17

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-18|18]]:** *The very things*, the very aspects of Torah piety, including separation from Gentiles ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-24|Lev 20.24]],[[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-26|26]]). ^2-18

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-19|19]]:** *Through the law I died to the law*, a paradoxical and difficult expression. *Through,* because of *the law*, which separates Jew from Gentile, *I died to the law*, I am no longer bound by the constrictions of the Torah. In [[Romans 06#^6-2|Rom 6.2]],[[Romans 06#^6-10|10]],[[Romans 06#^6-11|11]], Paul speaks of “dying to sin,” that is, sin no into the following paragraph b Or *reckoned as righteous;* and so elsewhere c Or *the faith of Jesus Christ* d Or *the faith of Christ* longer has a claim upon him. C*rucified with Christ*, a Pauline tenet: 5.24; 6.14; [[Romans 06#^6-5|Rom 6.5–11]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-7|2 Cor 4.7–12]]. ^2-19

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-20|20]]:** *Christ who lives in me*, another central tenet: [[Romans 08#^8-9|Rom 8.9–11]]; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-5|2 Cor 13.5]]; see [[Colossians 01#^1-27|Col 1.27]]. *I live by faith,* see 2.16n. ^2-20

**[[NRSVue Galatians 02#^2-21|21]]:** By rejecting works of law, Paul might be thought to be *nullify*\[ing\] *the grace of God*, who gave Israel the Torah. Paul denies he is doing this. He discusses why God gave the Torah in 3.19–25. ^2-21
