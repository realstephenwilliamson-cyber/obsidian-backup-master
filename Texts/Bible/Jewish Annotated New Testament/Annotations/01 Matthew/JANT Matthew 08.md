### 8.1–17: Jesus’ authority as healer

*[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-1|Text]], p. 25* ^8-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-1|1–4]]:** Healing of the man with leprosy ([[Mark 01#^1-40|Mk 1.40–45]]; [[Luke 05#^5-12|Lk 5.12–16]]). ^8-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-1|1]]:** *Down from the mountain*, Jesus, like Moses, descends ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-29|Ex 34.29]]; see also 5.1–2n.). Moses received divine commandments; Jesus interprets them. ^8-1-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-2|2]]:** *A leper*, biblical leprosy, a scaly skin disease (not Hansen’s disease, what we now call leprosy), created serious ritual impurity ([[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^5-2|Lev 5.2–6]]; 13–14; Num 12; *b. Sanh.* 47a*; b. Hor*. 10a; *b. Ned*. 64b; the rabbis considered a person suffering leprosy to be in a state of death). Curing leprosy indicates a prophetic role ([[II Kings 05#^5-8|2 Kings 5.8]]), missionary work (10.8), and an eschatological sign (11.5). ^8-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-3|3]]:** *Touched him,* rabbinic sources mention healing by touch (e.g., *b. Ber*. 5b). No law forbids touching people with leprosy. ^8-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-4|4]]:** *Say nothing*, the messianic secret; see Introduction to Mark’s gospel, p. 67. *Show yourself to the priest*, to be pronounced clean. *Gift that Moses commanded*, [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^14-2|Lev 14.2–9]]. *Testimony to them*, the priests. *and easy* b Gk *he* c The terms *leper* and *leprosy* can refer to several diseases ^8-4

### 8.5–13: Healing the centurion’s servant

*[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-5|Text]], p. 26* · ([[Luke 07#^7-1|Lk 7.1–10]]; cf. [[John 04#^4-46|Jn 4.46–54]]). ^8-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-5|5]]:** *Capernaum*, 4.13n. *Centurion,* Roman (Gentile) army officer. Since Rome in the 20s–30s stationed no troops in Galilee, the centurion may be a decommissioned officer. ^8-5-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-7|7]]:** *I will come and cure him*, the sentence could be read as a question indicating Jesus’ hesitance to perform a healing for Rome’s representative. ^8-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-8|8]]:** *I am not worthy*, the centurion occupied a higher social position than Jesus, yet he recognizes Jesus’ superiority. ^8-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-10|10]]:** *Faith*, anticipates the Gentile mission (see also 9.2,20–22,27; 11.6,25; 12.46–50; 15.28; 18.6; 21.21; 24.13–14; 28.19). ^8-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-11|11]]:** [[Luke 13#^13-28|Lk 13.28–30]]. *From east and west*, evoking the eschatological hope of the restoration of those in exile ([[Psalms 107#^107-3|Ps 107.3]]). *Kingdom of heaven*, see 3.2n. ^8-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-12|12]]:** *Outer darkness*, eschatological punishment. *Gnashing of teeth*, indicates anger or frustration (13.42; 22.13; 24.51; 25.30; cf. [[Psalms 112#^112-10|Ps 112.10]]; [[Acts 07#^7-54|Acts 7.54]]). ^8-12

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-13|13]]:** *According to your faith*, see 15.28. ^8-13

### 8.14–17: Healing the sick and possessed

*[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-14|Text]], p. 26* · ([[Mark 01#^1-29|Mk 1.29–34]]; [[Luke 04#^4-38|Lk 4.38–41]]). ^8-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-14|14]]:** See [[John 04#^4-52|Jn 4.52]]; [[Acts 28#^28-8|Acts 28.8]]. ^8-14-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-15|15]]:** *Touched her hand*, typical healing gesture. Jesus violates no ritual law or social rule. *To serve*, Gk “diakonein,” whence the English “deacon,” can connote ministerial service. ^8-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-16|16]]:** 8.28–34. *Demons . . . spirits*, see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-8|Lev 16.8]],[[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-10|10]],[[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-26|26]]; [[Isaiah 13#^13-21|Isa 13.21]]; [[Isaiah 34#^34-14|34.14]]; [[LXX Tobit 06#^6-7|Tob 6.7–8]],[[LXX Tobit 06#^6-16|16–17]]; [[LXX Tobit 08#^8-2|8.2–3]]; 1QM 13.11–12; *b. Git*. 70a; *b. Ber*. 6a; Josephus, *Ant*. 8.45–49; *Jub*. 10.10–13. ^8-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-17|17]]:** [[Isaiah 53#^53-4|Isa 53.4]]. The “suffering servant” motif is linked elsewhere to the redemption from sin (see 5.4n.; [[1 Peter 02#^2-18|1 Pet 2.18–25]]); in *b. Ber*. 5a the rabbis seem to indicate that Israel is the suffering servant whom God will redeem, possibly in the messianic era; see also *b. Sanh*. 98a. ^8-17

### 8.18–34: Stilling the storm and casting out demons

*[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-18|Text]], p. 26* ^8-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-19|19]]:** *Scribe*, see 7.28–29n. ^8-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-20|20]]:** *Son of Man*, see 16.27; see also [[Psalms 062#^62-13|Ps 62.13]]; [[Proverbs 24#^24-12|Prov 24.12]]; [[Romans 02#^2-6|Rom 2.6]]; [[1 John 02#^2-28|1 Jn 2.28]]; [[Revelation 02#^2-23|Rev 2.23]]; [[Revelation 22#^22-12|22.12]]. Rabbinic sources, following [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13–14]], use the phrase in an eschatological sense linked to the Messiah (*b. Sanh*. 98a, “And behold, the son of man came with the clouds of heaven”; cf. 10.23n.). See “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682. ^8-20

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-21|21–22]]:** [[Luke 09#^9-57|Lk 9.57–62]]. *Bury my father*, cf. [[I Kings 19#^19-19|1 Kings 19.19–21]]. Jews observed a seven-day period of mourning (Heb “shiva,” “seven”; [[LXX Judith 16#^16-24|Jdt 16.24]]; [[LXX Sirach 22#^22-12|Sir 22.12]]; *Apoc. Mos*. 43.3; *b. Sanh*. 47b). *Let the dead bury their own dead*, Jewish sources note the prime importance of accompanying a corpse to burial (*b. Ber*. 18a, cit- *even* ing [[Proverbs 17#^17-5|Prov 17.5]]); [[Torah 05 - Chayei Sara#^25-9|Gen 25.9]] states that Abraham’s sons bury him; [[LXX Tobit 06#^6-13|Tob 6.13–15]] includes a story about a son who worried that if he died there would be no one to bury his parents. ^8-21

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-23|23–27]]:** [[Mark 04#^4-35|Mk 4.35–41]]; [[Luke 08#^8-22|Lk 8.22–25]]. ^8-23

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-24|24]]:** *Windstorm*, Gk “seismos,” usually translated “earthquake,” an apocalyptic image (see 24.7; 27.51; 28.2). *He was asleep*, relaxing in a crisis indicates faith ([[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-6|Lev 26.6]]; [[Psalms 003#^3-6|Ps 3.5–6]]; [[Psalms 004#^4-9|4.8]]; [[Proverbs 03#^3-24|Prov 3.24–26]]; [[Job 11#^11-18|Job 11.18–19]]; [[Acts 12#^12-6|Acts 12.6]]). ^8-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-26|26]]:** *Rebuked the winds*, Matthew portrays Jesus, like God, as lord over nature, thus surpassing Jonah. ^8-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-28|28–34]]:** [[Mark 05#^5-1|Mk 5.1–20]]; [[Luke 08#^8-26|Lk 8.26–39]]. ^8-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-28|28]]:** *Gadarenes*, the people of Gadara, a town six miles southeast of the Sea of Galilee (see [[Mark 05#^5-1|Mk 5.1]], citing Gerasa; see Josephus, *Life* 9.42). Matthew likely changed the location to fit the story’s details. ^8-28-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-29|29]]:** *Before the time*, judgment day (*1 En.* 16.1; *Jub.* 10.7–10). ^8-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-31|31]]:** *Swine*, pigs are not kosher ([[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-7|Lev 11.7]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-8|Deut 14.8]]; [[Isaiah 65#^65-4|Isa 65.4]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-3|66.3]],[[Isaiah 66#^66-17|17]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-47|1 Macc 1.47]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-18|2 Macc 6.18–23]]); Gadara is Gentile territory. ^8-31

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-32|32]]:** *Go*, rabbinic tradition cites an instance where R. Shimon bar Yohai performed an exorcism with this command (*b. Meʾil.* 17b). *Whole herd . . . perished*, the story illustrates Jesus’ authority over evil spirits; pigs can swim. ^8-32

**[[NRSVue Matthew 08#^8-34|34]]:** *Begged him to leave*, for the Gadarenes, economic interests trump miraculous healing. ^8-34
