### Thessalonica: historical context
- Founded c. 316/315 BCE by Cassander, named for his wife Thessalonike, Alexander's half-sister. Capital of the province of Macedonia (146 BCE), a port on the Thermaic Gulf, on the Via Egnatia (Adriatic to Byzantium).
- **Free city** from 42 BCE (sided with Antony and Octavian at Philippi). Council, assembly, and **politarchs** (Acts 17:6, 8). The famous Vardar Gate stone (British Museum) is 2nd c. CE. The earlier attestations do the real work, including a 2nd-c. BCE civic dedication to Dionysus naming two politarchs.
- **Roman presence:** inscription of Roman *negotiatores* dated 27 BCE–14 CE.
- Most of the ancient city lies under modern Thessaloniki. The visible forum and Galerian monuments are 2nd–4th c.
- **Religion:**
    - *Isiac sanctuary* (the so-called Sarapeion): found after the 1917 fire; 3rd c. BCE onward; 70+ inscriptions, mostly to Isis and Osiris and mostly from the imperial period. Includes a letter of Philip V (187/6 BCE) protecting Sarapis's property, an Isis Lochia dedication (23/22 BCE), an Osiris temple with a crypt, the Opus dream inscription, and associations including initiates of Zeus Dionysos Gongylos.
    - *Dionysus:* a civic cult on Hellenistic coins and in a Dionysia festival; no imperial-era coins show him.
    - *Kabeiros* (singular at Thessalonica): the evidence is mostly later (coins from the Flavian era onward, abundant in the 2nd–3rd c.). Jewett's thesis (*The Thessalonian Correspondence*, 1986, p. 176: a murdered benefactor expected to return for laborers, co-opted by the imperial cult) rests on Witt and on Samothracian extrapolation. Critique: C. de Vos, *Church and Community Conflicts* (1999). The murder myth comes from Clement of Alexandria (*Protrepticus*), and the Thessalonian link from Lactantius and Firmicus Maternus (4th c.).
    - *Imperial cult:* D. C. Burnett, JBL 139 (2020). Honors were voluntary and went to select Julio-Claudians. No *kyrios* title. *Theos* was used only for Julius Caesar and Augustus after apotheosis, and for Livia in her lifetime. The cults were embedded in traditional ones. Christ-confessors were harassed for exclusivity, which threatened religion and politics together. See also Donfried NTS 31 (1985); Hendrix (Th.D. 1984); Judge, "Decrees of Caesar" (1971). On "peace and security," Weima NTS 2012 reads it as Roman propaganda; contested.
- **Jews:** Acts 17 is the only first-century witness. The earliest material evidence is a Samaritan Greek/Hebrew inscription (Num 6:22–27), 4th–6th c. Philo, *Legatio* 281, lists Macedonia among regions with Jewish settlement. Paul's own wording in 1 Thess 2:14 (*symphyletai*, fellow citizens) points to gentile persecutors. On 2:14–16 as an interpolation, see Pearson, HTR 1971; the case is contested.
- **The church:** founded c. 49/50 CE. Members were former idolaters (1:9) and working people (2:9; 4:11). Phil 4:16 implies a stay longer than "three Sabbaths." Leaders are described by function only (5:12). Named members: Jason, Aristarchus, Secundus. The community suffered affliction (1:6; 3:3–4) and deaths (4:13). Secondary: Barclay, CBQ 1993; Ascough, JBL 2000 (professional association); Malherbe 1987 and AB 2000 (philosophical paraenesis, "gentle as a nurse"); Hock 1980.

### Critique of a circulating "historical context" summary
- Acts 17:7 is *basilea heteron* ("another king"), not *kyrios*.
- Tax exemption is unverified: free status ≠ *immunitas*.
- The specific trades (tentmakers, leatherworkers, stonecutters) are invented. "Guilds" is anachronistic, membership was voluntary, and Ascough reads the church *as* an association.
- The idleness explanation conflates Jewett (eschatological idleness) and Winter (patronage dependence). 4:11–12 mentions neither.
- "The Cabiri" (plural) imports Samothrace. "Dominant religion" and "ritualized sexual union" have no evidence for Thessalonica.
- "Married men were legally expected…" is overstated. Roman law *permitted* sex with slaves and prostitutes (the lex Julia de adulteriis of 18 BCE did not count it as adultery) but required nothing. "Wives for heirs" comes from Ps.-Demosthenes 59.122 (4th c. BCE Athens). Musonius Rufus argued against the double standard.
- **Method:** 4:1–12 presents itself as a reminder of founding catechesis ("you know what instructions we gave," 4:2; "as we told you beforehand," 4:6; 4:9; "as we instructed you," 4:11), not as a response to a local crisis.

### 1 Thess 4:3–8 and the arayot
- **Holiness Code / LXX echoes:**
    - *porneia* as the umbrella term for the Lev 18 categories (1 Cor 5:1); "abstain from porneia," cf. Acts 15:29.
    - 4:5 "gentiles who do not know God": Jer 10:25 and Ps 79:6; cf. Lev 18:3, 24.
    - 4:7 impurity vs. holiness: Lev 20:25–26 ("separated you from the peoples").
    - 4:6 *ekdikos* followed by 4:9 *philadelphia*: LXX Lev 19:18 has "καὶ οὐκ ἐκδικᾶταί σου ἡ χείρ … ἀγαπήσεις τὸν πλησίον σου."
    - 4:8 "gives his Holy Spirit into you": LXX Ezek 37:14, "δώσω τὸ πνεῦμά μου εἰς ὑμᾶς"; Ezek 36:25–29 (cleansing from *akatharsia*).
    - Lev 18:24–28 (the nations expelled for these acts) explains why the arayot bind gentiles, as in the Noahide laws.
- **skeuos (4:4), three readings:** wife (rabbinic *keli*; 1 Pet 3:7), body (2 Cor 4:7), sexual organ (1 Sam 21:5 LXX; 4Q416 2 ii 21 per Elgvin, NTS 43 (1997), and J. Smith, CBQ 63 (2001) and BBR 11 (2001)). None is a Temple vessel.
- Klawans, *Impurity and Sin* (2000): moral impurity. Lev 10:10 keeps holy/common and clean/unclean distinct; *kil'ayim* (Lev 19:19) is a third category.
- **On the Gemini "mystical layers" analysis:** the tractate is Kiddushin (betrothal, c. 200 CE), not "Kedushah." *Akatharsia* ≠ "mixture." Tzimtzum is Lurianic. Body-as-*keli* is Kabbalistic vessel language. Mapping the limbs to the letters of the Name is Kabbalistic; the rabbinic body-mapping is b. Makkot 23b (248/365). "Body replaced the Temple" brings in replacement logic; compare Qumran's community-as-sanctuary (1QS 8) alongside the Temple. The real case for Paul as a mystic rests on 2 Cor 12:2–4 and 2 Cor 3:18–4:6 (Segal; Rowland); Schäfer cautions against retrojecting the Hekhalot literature.
- **Strata:** Paul c. 50 · Mishnah c. 200 · Talmud c. 500s · Zohar late 13th c. · Luria 16th c. Later readings are allowed but must be labeled.

### Hagigah and boundaries (my reading)
- m. Hagigah 2:1 is graded (arayot not before 3, creation not before 2, the chariot not even before 1), which makes it a ranking, not an equation. The Bavli's reason for the arayot restriction (11b) is practical: inattentive students may come to permit a forbidden relation. "Secrets of the arayot" comes from later commentators (Tosafot Yom Tov, Tiferet Yisrael). Halperin, *The Merkabah in Rabbinic Literature*: an exegetical restriction (with m. Megillah 4:10), not about ecstatic practice.
- **Firmament as hinge:** the *raqia* appears in both Gen 1:6–8 and Ezek 1:22–26.
    - Ben Zoma gazing between the upper and lower waters (t. Hagigah 2:6; b. Hagigah 15a).
    - "Do not say 'water, water'" (b. Hagigah 14b).
    - Genesis Rabbah 4:6: R. Hanina, *mahloket* was created on the second day; Rav Tavyomi's a fortiori.
    - The Flood undoes day two (Gen 7:11). Rabbinic tradition ties the Flood to *zenut* (Genesis Rabbah 26; b. Sanhedrin 108a). Ham "saw his father's nakedness" (Gen 9:22), the arayot idiom. The Noahide covenant includes the arayot, and its sign, the rainbow, is the likeness of the glory in Ezek 1:28. b. Hagigah 16a: R. Abba reads "no concern for his Maker's honor" as gazing at a rainbow; Rav Yosef reads it as sinning in secret.
- **Thesis (constructive):** the arayot belong with creation and the chariot because sexual boundaries are among the separations that keep the second day's work from collapsing.

### Lev 18:22 (property/status reading), tangent
- **Support:** the kinship laws use ownership language ("it is your father's nakedness"); status protocol (penetration marks hierarchy); MAL A §20 punishes only the active partner against a "comrade" (Wenham reads it as rape); Walsh, JBL 120 (2001), honor/shame; Olyan, JHS 5 (1994); Dershowitz, HeBAI 2017 (male kin once listed in the incest laws).
- **Objections:** Lev 20:13 executes both and says "the two of them did" (caveat: 20:15–16 kills the animal too). 18:22 lacks the ownership formula and sits between Molech and bestiality as *to'evah*. Philo shares the status rationale but ignores consent. b. Sanhedrin 54a holds both liable.
- **The gap:** a status *rationale* does not by itself limit the *scope* to non-consensual acts. That needs a premise the texts don't supply. Rabbinic shape: R. Shimon's *darish ta'ama di-kra* (b. Bava Metzia 115a, the widow's pledge).
- **Gymnasium thesis:** fails as the *origin* of the language on chronology (Lev predates Greek rule; Jerusalem gymnasium 175 BCE). As *reception*, it holds:
    - *to'evah* is cross-cultural boundary language from the start (Gen 43:32; 46:34; Exod 8:22; Lev 18:3, 26–30).
    - The 1–2 Maccabees objection is nudity and circumcision.
    - Jub 3:31 condemns nudity (VanderKam: the Greeks after 175).
    - Aristeas 152; Sib. Or. 3:764; Philo, *Spec.* 3.37–42 (pederasty); Loader, *Religions* 2018.
    - Rabbinic: Sifra on Lev 18:3 ("a man would marry a man…"), Genesis Rabbah 26:5, b. Hullin 92b; plausibly Roman background (Nero: Suetonius, *Nero* 28–29). These target consensual marriage, which cuts against the exploitation-only reading.
    - Satlow, JHS 5 (1994) and Boyarin, JHS 5 (1995) on gender blurring support the rationale, not a consent limit.
- **Warrior pederasty:** Greek, institutional in places (Crete, Strabo 10.4.21; the Theban Sacred Band, Plut. *Pelopidas* 18–19; Plato, *Symp.* 178e–179b; contested by Xenophon). Rome was the reverse (Polybius 6.37.9; Marius and Trebonius, Plut. *Marius* 14): the line ran by status, and the *willing* free partner was the offender.
- **Pairs:**
    - *David and Jonathan:* 1 Sam 18:1–4; 20:30 (*ervat immekha*); 20:41; 2 Sam 1:26. *'ahav* as covenant love (Moran, CBQ 1963; Thompson, VT 1974). Ackerman, *When Heroes Love* (2005): the erotic language feminizes Jonathan to legitimize David; she compares Gilgamesh and Enkidu. Saul's love (16:21) is patronage; David becomes *nose' kelim*.
    - *Moses and Joshua:* *na'ar* is a rank (Joshua commands in Exod 17:9–13 before 33:11). No erotic markers.
    - *The beloved disciple:* John 13:23–25; *kolpos* echoes 1:18; witness (19:35; 21:24); the Gospel's recognizer (13:25–26; 20:8; 21:7). A found reading for *The Gospel of Recognition*. Erotic reading: Jennings, *The Man Jesus Loved* (2003); Secret Mark disputed (Carlson 2005 vs. Brown). Reception: Aelred, *Spiritual Friendship*; James I (1617).

### Na'ar → Metatron → 4:16–17
- 3 Enoch 3–4 (Metatron called "Youth"); 12:5, the "lesser" bearer of the Name (Exod 23:21); b. Sanhedrin 38b; b. Yevamot 16b (Ps 37:25; Tosafot: Metatron); Kabbalistic pun on Prov 22:6 (*Ḥanokh la-na'ar*). Justin, *Dialogue* 75: the angel of Exod 23:20–21 is Jesus (Hoshea renamed Joshua = *Iēsous*). Schäfer, *The Jewish Jesus* (2012). b. Hagigah 15a: Acher, Metatron, "two powers," in the same chapter as Ein Dorshin.
- **4:16–17:**
    - Sinai: Exod 19:16–20, with LXX 19:17 "εἰς συνάντησιν τοῦ θεοῦ" (Dupont, via Plevnik, *Biblica* 80, 1999). Ezek 1 is the Shavuot haftarah (b. Megillah 31a); Halperin, *Faces of the Chariot*.
    - *harpagēsometha* = 2 Cor 12:2–4, "caught up … into paradise" = *pardes* (Morray-Jones, HTR 86, 1993).
    - The archangel and the sleepers: Dan 12:1–2.
    - *apantēsis:* Peterson's welcoming party (Acts 28:15; Matt 25:6) vs. Dupont/Plevnik (Sinai lacks the escort). Either way the Lord descends first.

### The readings together
- **Amos 5:18–24:** probably the earliest "day of the Lord," and Amos reverses it. The lion/bear/snake image is dark comedy. "Take away the noise of your songs" comes right before a sung psalm and the sequence hymn. *Naḥal 'êtān* = a stream that never dries up (vs. seasonal wadis). King quoted it in "I Have a Dream," and it is on the Civil Rights Memorial in Montgomery.
- **Psalm 70** ≈ Ps 40:13–17; Elohistic Psalter (42–83). The heading *lehazkir* = LXX *eis anamnēsin*, as in "Do this for the remembrance of me" (Ps 38 is the only other psalm with it). The opening line begins the daily round of prayer (BCP Noonday and Evening Prayer). The last line, LXX "κύριε μὴ χρονίσῃς," uses the verb of Matt 25:5, *chronizontos*.
- **Matthew 25:1–13:** the lamps were probably torches. All ten sleep. *eis apantēsin* (v. 6). "I do not know you." Chrysostom, *Hom. Matt.* 78: oil = "humanity, almsgiving, succor to them that are in need." The bulletin cover is Blake's *Wise and Foolish Virgins* (c. 1799–1800, Met), with a trumpeting angel overhead taken from 1 Thess 4:16.
- **Through-line:** all four look toward "the day" and disagree about how eager to be for it. Wisdom-track alternative (not used at Ascension): Wis 6:13, 16 (*hypantaō*: Wisdom "meets them in every thought").
