### 11.1–40: Heroes of faith

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 481* · The list of biblical heroes functions to encourage hope in salvation, even though present circumstances appear to justify despair. Similar lists appear in an array of postbiblical literature (see “Heroes of the Faith,” p. 481). ^11-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-1|1]]:** *Faith* includes connotations of endurance, trust, hope, and knowledge of divine realities (see also 6.1n.). The concept of faith in Hebrews overlaps with Paul’s, especially in regard to trusting in God’s promises even when things look hopeless (for Hebrews, in addition to ch 11 see 4.2–3; 10.36–39; 12.1–2; for Paul see esp. [[Romans 03#^3-22|Rom 3.22–5.1]]). The idea of faith as spiritual knowledge, which is particularly vivid in the opening verses of ch 11, finds little resonance with Paul’s usage. Both Hebrews and Paul make use of [[Habakkuk 2#^2-4|Hab 2.4]] but with different emphasis**:** Paul offers elaborate theological reflection on the connection between faith and righteousness (Rom 4; [[Galatians 03#^3-6|Gal 3.6–22]]), while Hebrews develops the connection between faith and endurance—to “live by faith” is to endure hardship with confidence and boldness, never doubting the divine promise. “Faith” in the Tanakh (Heb “emunah”) generally implies fidelity, loyalty, and following God’s commandments faithfully, and this sense carries over into rabbinic thinking (see, e.g., *Mek. R. Ishmael*, “Beshallah” 7). *Assurance*, Gk “hy-postasis,” “substance, steadiness, firmness”; in 1.3 the same word is translated “very being.” *Conviction*, Gk “elengchos,” “proof, test.” ^11-1-2

> [!note] Heroes of the Faith *(p. 481)*
> The list of heroes resembles lists of biblical heroes in Jewish literature ([[LXX Sirach 44#^44-1|Sir 44.1–49.16]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 10#^10-1|Wis 10.1–21]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-51|1 Macc 1.51–60]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 18#^18-11|4 Macc 18.11–19]]; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.105–111), but it reflects some unusual choices. Except for a passing mention of David at the end ([[Hebrews 11#^11-32|Heb 11.32]]), the author includes no priests or kings. Certain expected highlights do not appear, and unexpected ones do. For example, no mention is made of God’s circumcision covenant with Abraham from Gen 17, nor is Sinai mentioned despite the attention given to Moses. Instead of focusing on the heroes’ leadership roles, Hebrews emphasizes the characters as largely set apart from the people. While rabbinic literature also uses lists, it neither lists biblical heroes nor presents “gospels” in the sense of biographies of such heroes. Rather, it lists, e.g., “the ten tests of Abraham” or “three who did not suffer death . . . ” Generally speaking, the heroes in Hebrews have three characteristics: (1) Near-death experience: Noah would have per- ished in the flood; Moses would have died as an infant; (2) Ability to see the future and act faithfully in light of that knowledge: Noah receives an oracle about the flood and builds the ark; Abraham receives a promise about Isaac, and very late in his life that promise is realized; (3) Alienation: The heroes are portrayed as alienated from the people of their generation. Abraham lived in the land “as in a foreign land,” and Moses was not really one of his people, because he was raised by Pharaoh’s daughter. The heroes are not distinguished *by* their comrades, as those depicted in Sir 44–50; they are distinguished *from* them. The list functions as a kind of genealogy for the early Christ-believing community; it creates a lineage independent from the people of Israel because the heroes are portrayed as outsiders and includes non-Israelites. Hebrews 11 set the stage for the way in which Christians would eventually come to understand the Old Testament as a precursor to the New Testament.

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Worlds*, see 1.1–2n. Creation by *the word of God* is a common theme; see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-3|Gen 1.3]]; [[Psalms 033#^33-6|Ps 33.6]]; [[John 01#^1-1|Jn 1.1–3]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-1|Wis 9.1]]; *2 Bar.* 14.17; Philo, *Sacr.* 65. The idea of creation by divine speech both supports the claim that the visible is made from what is *not visible* and connects to the idea of divine promise of future reward, a major theme in what follows. ^11-3

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-4|4]]:** [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-1|Gen 4.1–16]]. *Abel*, viewed as the first martyr and judge of souls; see [[LXX 4 Maccabees 18#^18-11|4 Macc 18.11]]; [[Matthew 23#^23-35|Mt 23.35]]; *1 En.* 22.7; *T. Abr.* 13. *Still speaks*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-10|Gen 4.10]]; [[Hebrews 12#^12-24|Heb 12.24]]. ^11-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-5|5]]:** [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-24|Gen 5.24]]. *Enoch* became a figure of great legend in extra-canonical literature because the words “God had taken him” were interpreted as a heavenly exaltation; see *1* and *2 En.* ; *Jub.* 19.24–27; Philo, *Names* 38; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.3.4; *Gen. Rab.* 25.1. Because Enoch *pleased God* ([[LXX Genesis 05#^5-22|Gen 5.22]]\[LXX\]; Hebrews describes Enoch as having “walked” with God), he was seen as exemplary, with a focus on his repentance (see [[LXX Sirach 44#^44-16|Sir 44.16]]; Philo, *Migr.* 17–26). Rabbinic sources contain contradictory evaluations of him (cf. *Pesikta de-Rabbi Eliezer* 7.40 and *Gen. Rab.* 25.1). In *Tg. Onq.* [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-24|Gen 5.24]], Enoch is identified with the mysterious angel Metatron. ^11-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-7|7]]:** *Noah*, [[Torah 02 - Noach#^6-9|Gen 6.9–9.28]]. *Condemned the world*, legend has it that *Noah* preached repentance to his generation; *Mek.* “Shirah” 5; *Sifre Num.* 43; [[Sanhedrin 108#108a|*b. Sanh.* 108a]]-b; *Pirqe R. El.* 22. *Righteousness*, Noah is the first person Scripture calls righteous, Heb “tzaddiq,” Gk “dikaios” ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^6-9|Gen 6.9]]). ^11-7

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-8|8–19]]:** *Abraham* is the premier example of faith in Jewish tradition; see [[LXX Sirach 44#^44-19|Sir 44.19–21]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-52|1 Macc 2.52]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.7; *Gen. Rab.* 43; *Mek.* “Mishpatim” 18. ^11-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-8|8]]:** *Abraham obeyed*, [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-1|Gen 12.1–9]]. Jewish interpreters highlight Abraham’s immediate response as a sign of great faith; see Philo, *Migr.* 66. One midrash proclaims proudly “thus our father Abraham inherited this world and the world-to-come as a reward only for the faith he had” (*Mek R. Ishmael*, “Beshallah” 7). Jesus’ exemplary status is also predicated on obedience; see 5.7. *Place*, Canaan, the land of Israel. *Not knowing where*, God’s command to Abraham is to go to a “land that I will show you” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-1|Gen 12.1]]). ^11-8-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-9|9–10]]:** *Land he had been promised, as in a foreign land*, Hebrews emphasizes Abraham’s faith as future-oriented, since Abraham does not live to see his descendants inherit the land (see v 13). Ancient interpreters frequently highlight Abraham’s patient faith in wandering (*Jub.* 19.8–9), but here the focus is on Abraham’s displacement. ^11-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-10|10]]:** *City*, the heavenly Jerusalem. ^11-10

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-11|11]]:** *Power of procreation*, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-9|Gen 18.9–15]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-1|21.1–8]]. ^11-11

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-12|12]]:** *As good as dead*, namely very old; see [[Romans 04#^4-19|Rom 4.19]]. *Stars . . . sand*, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-17|Gen 22.17]]. ^11-12

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-13|13]]:** *Died in faith*, therefore their reward still awaits them (see 11.39–40). *From a distance*, Hebrews continues to connect the ancient worthies with faith displayed outside the land of Israel. *Strangers and foreigners*, [[Torah 05 - Chayei Sara#^23-4|Gen 23.4]]. *On the earth*, see 10.1n. ^11-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-14|14–16]]:** *Homeland . . . better country*, a heavenly dwelling; see 12.22–28. *Prepared a city*, heavenly Jerusalem, a notion found in later rabbinic literature (e.g., [[Chagigah 12#12b|*b. Hag.* 12b]]). ^11-14

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-17|17–19]]:** *Put to the test*, rabbinic lore sees Abraham undergoing ten tests of faith, culminating with the “Akedah” (“binding” of Isaac, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-1|Gen 22.1–19]]), which explicitly uses the word “test” or “try” ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-1|Gen 22.1]]); see *m. Avot* 4.3; *Avot de R. Natan* A 33; *Pirqe R. El.* 26. The earliest extant source for Abraham’s trials is *Jub.* 17.17. ^11-17

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-18|18]]:** see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-12|Gen 21.12]]. ^11-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-19|19]]:** *Raise someone from the dead*, *Pirqe R. El.* 31 draws a connection between resurrection and the “Akedah” (the “binding of Isaac,” Gen 22), and some Jewish sources suggest that Abraham sacrificed Isaac, who was then resurrected by God (see e.g., *Mek.* “Piskha” 7). *Figuratively speaking*, Gk “en parabolē,” the same word used in 9.9 (there translated “symbol”); the event is symbolic of resurrection. ^11-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-20|20]]:** *Isaac invoked blessings*, [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^27-1|Gen 27.1–40]]. *Jacob and Esau*, Isaac’s sons. ^11-20

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-21|21]]:** *Jacob, when dying* . . . *sons of Joseph*, Ephraim and Manasseh; [[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^48-8|Gen 48.8–22]]. *Bowing in worship*, [[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^47-31|Gen 47.31]] *Top of his staff* (LXX), “head of his bed”**:** Heb “matteh,” “staff” and “mittah,” “bed, couch” have the same consonants; LXX reads the former (“staff”), MT reads the latter (“bed”). ^11-21

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-22|22]]:** [[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^50-24|Gen 50.24–25]]. The reburial of Joseph’s bones in the promised land featured prominently in retellings of Joseph’s story; see [[LXX Sirach 49#^49-15|Sir 49.15]]; *Jub.* 46.5; *T. Sim.* 8.3–4; *Mek.* “Beshallah” 1. ^11-22

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-23|23]]:** [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-1|Ex 2.1–10]]. In the MT, only Moses’ mother hides him; in the LXX both parents do; see also Philo, *Life of Moses* 1.8–11. ^11-23

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-24|24–25]]:** Like the patriarchs (vv 13–16), Moses is portrayed as alienated from his native land. For more, see “Heroes of the Faith,” p. 481. Elaborations of Moses’ switch of allegiance to the Israelites appear in Philo, *Life of Moses* 1.32–39; Josephus, *Ant.* 2.238–53. ^11-24

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-25|25]]:** *Ill-treatment*, slavery in Egypt. *Pleasures of sin*, characterizations of Egypt as a locus of sin are commonplace; see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-3|Lev 18.3]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 12#^12-23|Wis 12.23–27]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 17#^17-1|17.1–21]]; Philo, *Decalogue* 16.76–81. ^11-25

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-26|26]]:** *Abuse suffered for the Christ*, it is unlikely the author means that Moses envisioned Christ; rather he projects the current situation onto the past, namely, Moses willingly suffers humiliation, foreseeing his divine reward. ^11-26

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-27|27]]:** [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-11|Ex 2.11–15]]. *He saw him who is invisible*, [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-11|Ex 33.11]]; [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-8|Num 12.8]]. ^11-27

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-28|28]]:** [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-1|Ex 12.1–28]]; [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-43|12.43–13.10]]. *Destroyer*, parts of the Exodus narrative attribute the killing of the firstborn to the “destroyer” ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-23|Ex 12.23]]), a demon under God’s control, and other parts to God (12.12,29). ^11-28

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-29|29]]:** See [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-30|Ex 14.30]]; Josh 5. A midrash on Ex 15 (*b*. *Meg.* 10b) reports that when the Israelites sang the song at the sea, the heavenly host wanted to join in, but God rebuked them saying, “My creatures are drowning in the sea and you want to sing songs?” The midrash highlights the common humanity of both Israelites and Egyptians. Hebrews highlights the drastically different fates of each group; the former are favored, the latter condemned. ^11-29

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-30|30–31]]:** [[Joshua 02#^2-1|Josh 2.1–6.17]]. Jewish and Christian traditions remember *Rahab*, despite the fact that she was a prostitute, as a heroine and the ancestor of other great biblical figures; see [[Matthew 01#^1-5|Mt 1.5]]; [[James 02#^2-25|Jas 2.25]]; *power to conceive, even when she was too old, because she considered him faithful who had promised. Sifre Num.* 78; *Num. Rab.* 8; [[Megillah 14#14b|*b. Meg.* 14b]]. ^11-30

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-32|32]]:** *Gideon,* Judg 6–8; *Barak*, Judg 4–5; *Samson*, Judg 13–16; *Jephthah,* Judg 11–12. This incomplete list of judges omits Ehud and Deborah, among others (see [[I Samuel 12#^12-11|1 Sam 12.11]]). *David* and *Samuel*, 1, 2 Samuel passim. ^11-32

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-33|33]]:** *Shut the mouths of lions*, stories of heroes defeating lions occur in [[Judges 14#^14-6|Judg 14.6]]; [[Daniel 06#^6-20|Dan 6.19–23]]. ^11-33

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-34|34]]:** *Quenched raging fire*, see Dan 3; Song of Thr 26–27. *Escaped the edge of the sword* . . . *strength out of weakness . . . foreign armies to flight*, these descriptions could refer to several different figures, including Elijah. ^11-34

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-35|35]]:** *Women received their dead by resurrection*, i.e., the dead persons were restored to life and presented to the *women* (mothers); [[I Kings 17#^17-17|1 Kings 17.17–24]]; [[II Kings 04#^4-18|2 Kings 4.18–37]]. *Others were tortured* likely refers to [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-18|2 Macc 6.18–7.42]]. In contrast to resuscitation, *a better resurrection* refers to eternal life. ^11-35

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-36|36–38]]:** Sufferings of various prophets**:** *Stoned to death* refers either to Zechariah ([[II Chronicles 24#^24-21|2 Chr 24.21]]) or to legends about Jeremiah (Tertullian, *Scorp.* 8); [[II Kings 21#^21-16|2 Kings 21.16]], concerning the murderous King Manasseh, who was often accused of killing prophets; see *Seder Olam Rab.* 20. *Sawn in two*, the fate of Isaiah according to Jewish, Christian, and Islamic legend; see *Mart. Isa.* 5.11–14; Justin, *Dial.* 120; *y. Sanh.* 10; [[Yevamot 49#49b|*b. Yebam.* 49b]]. *Skins of sheep and goats* evokes the garb of Elijah and Elisha ([[LXX 3 Kingdoms 19#^19-13|1 Kings 19.13]],[[LXX 3 Kingdoms 19#^19-19|19]]\[LXX\]). *Wandered . . . holes in the ground* evokes the trials of Elijah and Elisha ([[I Kings 17#^17-1|1 Kings 17.1–7]]; [[I Kings 19#^19-3|19.3–9]]; [[II Kings 02#^2-25|2 Kings 2.25]]). ^11-36

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-39|39]]:** *Did not receive what was promised*, see 11.13. ^11-39

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 11#^11-40|40]]:** *Made perfect*, see 2.10; 5.8; 7.28; 10.14; 12.2. *he was grown up, killed the Egyptian, because he observed the humiliation of his people* (Gk *brothers*) c Or *the Messiah* d Or *because* e Gk *would not touch them* f Or *unbelieving* g Other ancient authorities add *they were tempted* ^11-40
