> Can you find records of the Archbishop of Canterbury visiting the diocese of Western North Carolina in the 90's

Yes. The clearest record is from **1992**, and it centers on Kanuga rather than a formal visitation hosted by the bishop.

**The 1992 visit**

An AP story carried by the Buffalo News reports that George Carey, on his first visit to the United States as Archbishop of Canterbury, was in North Carolina to preside over an international meeting of church officials. The paywall scrambles the rest of the snippet with a simple character-shift cipher. Decoded, it says:

- He flew into Asheville on a Friday evening.
- He was to preach that Sunday at St. James Episcopal Church in Hendersonville.
- The meeting would be at Kanuga the following week, with representatives from 13 countries, including Presiding Bishop Browning.
- The source was Jim Rosenthal of the Anglican Communion Office.

The year comes from the Anglican Consultative Council's 1993 resolutions, which note the action taken by the Joint Standing Committees at Kanuga, 1992. Separately, the Washington Post described Carey's first official U.S. visit in September 1992, which began in Maryland. So the Kanuga trip was probably earlier in 1992. I couldn't see the AP dateline, though, so the month is unconfirmed.

A caveat on "the diocese": St. James Hendersonville is a Western North Carolina parish. Kanuga is inside the diocese's territory, but it is owned by a nonprofit ceded in 1972 from five Carolina dioceses, including Western North Carolina. I found nothing showing an official visit hosted by the Bishop of Western North Carolina.

**Other trips I checked**

- On his October 1993 U.S. trip he first stopped at the ELCA offices in Chicago, then went to Colorado Springs, St. Louis, Virginia Theological Seminary, and New York. He didn't go to North Carolina.
- The Primates' Meeting he chaired at Kanuga was held from 2 to 9 March 2001. That's just past the decade, but it's easy to conflate with the 1992 visit.

**Where to confirm details**

- **Lambeth Palace Library.** The Archbishop's papers are closed for at least thirty years after their creation. By that rule, the 1992 files should now be open, and they would include itineraries.
- **Local newspapers.** The Hendersonville Times-News and Asheville Citizen-Times from 1992 should have covered the St. James sermon. Newspapers.com is the easiest way to search them.
- **Parish and diocesan records.** St. James Hendersonville's records and the diocese's archives from that period.
- **The ACC-9 report** (Cape Town, 1993), which should summarize the Kanuga meeting.
