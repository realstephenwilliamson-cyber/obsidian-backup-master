### 18.1–17: Events in Corinth

*[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-1|Text]], p. 260* · Paul was instrumental in establishing a Christian community in Corinth and wrote at least two letters to the church there. ^18-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-1|1]]:** *Corinth*, Roman administrative center in the province of Achaia (present-day Greece); home of a sizeable Jewish community (Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 281). ^18-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-2|2]]:** *Aquila . . . Priscilla*, see 18.18. A couple known to the Corinthian Christians ([[1 Corinthians 16#^16-19|1 Cor 16.19]]) and once present in Rome ([[Romans 16#^16-3|Rom 16.3–4]]). *Pontus*, province in northern Asia Minor (present-day Turkey). *Claudius had ordered all Jews to leave Rome*, the Roman historian Suetonius also reports this act and adds that Jews *one blood* b Other ancient authorities read *the Lord* c Gk *He* **Chs 18–21:** Third missionary journey of Paul. were causing disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus (*Claud.* 25), which may be a corruption of “Christ,” and thus suggest that tension flared because of the proclamation by Jesus’ followers. The extent of the expulsion is uncertain and apparently short-lived, as a sizeable Jewish community lived in imperial Rome. ^18-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-3|3]]:** *Tentmakers*, Paul engaged in manual labor; see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|1 Cor 4.12]]. The rabbis placed great value in manual labor (*b. Qidd.* [[Kiddushin 29#29a|29a]]; *b. Ned.* [[Nedarim 49#49b|49b]]; *Avot de R. Nat.* 11.1). ^18-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-4|4]]:** Paul follows the typical pattern of teaching on the Sabbath (see 17.2) in a synagogue (see 17.1) to Jews and Gentiles (see 13.16). ^18-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-5|5]]:** *Silas and Timothy*, see 17.10–15. *The Messiah was Jesus*, the claim provokes a harsh response from Jews (9.22; 17.3–5; 18.28). ^18-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-6|6]]:** *Shook the dust*, symbolizing separation; see 13.51; [[Luke 09#^9-5|Lk 9.5]]. *Your blood*, see [[II Samuel 01#^1-16|2 Sam 1.16]]; [[Matthew 27#^27-25|Mt 27.25]]. Jews are seen as responsible for failing to acknowledge Jesus as the Messiah. *Innocent*, Paul is not responsible for their failure to believe. *Go to the Gentiles*, see 13.46; 28.28. ^18-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-7|7]]:** *Titius Justus*, Acts highlights Gentile adherents to Judaism, e.g., the Ethiopian official (8.26–28), Cornelius (10.1), Lydia (16.14); see also the Gospel’s Roman centurion ([[Luke 07#^7-1|Lk 7.1–5]]). ^18-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-8|8]]:** *Crispus*, see [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-14|1 Cor 1.14]]. *Official of the synagogue*, see 13.15. *Household*, see 11.14. ^18-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-9|9]]:** *Vision*, see 9.10. ^18-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-10|10]]:** *I am with you*, see [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-12|Ex 3.12]]; [[Isaiah 43#^43-5|Isa 43.5]]. *My people*, including Jews and Gentiles. ^18-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-12|12]]:** *Gallio*, L. Junius Gallio Annaeanus was proconsul (chief Roman magistrate) in Achaia around 51–52. See 13.7n. *Jews*, singled out as Paul’s opponents (13.45; 17.5,13; 20.3,19). ^18-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-13|13]]:** *The law*, either Jewish (as Gallio understands the accusation, v 15; cf. 21.21) or Roman (16.21; 17.7). ^18-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-15|15]]:** *See to it yourselves*, Rome granted Jewish communities permission to practice their own law (Josephus, *Ant.* 16.163; 19.290) and to decide matters concerning their own customs (*Ant.* 14.195; 235,260). ^18-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-17|17]]:** *Sosthenes*, a man by this name was known to the Corinthian church ([[1 Corinthians 01#^1-1|1 Cor 1.1]]). *Official of the synagogue*, see 13.15. ^18-17

### 18.18–23: Paul concludes the journey

*[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-18|Text]], p. 262* ^18-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-18|18]]:** *Priscilla and Aquila*, see v 2. *Cenchreae*, Eastern port of Corinth. *Hair cut*, the act completes the Nazirite vow ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-1|Num 6.1–21]]). Acts does not indicate why Paul took a vow but uses the ritual action to portray him as observing Jewish Law. ^18-18-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-19|19]]:** *Ephesus*, administrative seat of the province of Asia (present-day Turkey). *Synagogue*, see 13.5. ^18-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-21|21]]:** *I will return*, Paul does so on his next journey (19.1–41). *If God wills,* a common expression used by Paul ([[1 Corinthians 04#^4-19|1 Cor 4.19]]), as well as Jews (Josephus, *Ant.* 2.333; 7.373) and Greeks (Plutarch, *Alc.* 135d; Epictetus, *Diatr.* 1.1.17). ^18-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-22|22]]:** *Caesarea*, see 8.40. ^18-22

### 18.23–20.38: Paul’s return journey to Asia Minor and Greece

*[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-23|Text]], p. 262* · Paul embarks on a third journey. ^18-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-23|18.23]]:** *Galatia and Phrygia*, see 16.6. *Strengthening all the disciples*, see 14.22. ^18-23-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-24|24]]:** *Apollos*, important figure in the Corinthian church (19.1; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-12|1 Cor 1.12]]; [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-4|3.4–6]],[[1 Corinthians 03#^3-22|22]]; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-6|4.6]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-12|16.12]]). Alexandria was a noted center of learning with a large Jewish community. ^18-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-25|25]]:** *Way*, see 9.2n. *Baptism of John*, see 13.24. Possibly a ritual performed by followers of John the Baptist, here presented as a rival messianic sect. ^18-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-26|26]]:** *Speak boldly*, see 4.13n. *Baptism of John*, see 13.24–25. *Priscilla and Aquila*, see v 2n. ^18-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 18#^18-28|28]]:** *Refuted the Jews*, Jews appear as the primary opponents of Jesus and those who preaching him. *The Messiah is Jesus*, see 9.22. ^18-28
