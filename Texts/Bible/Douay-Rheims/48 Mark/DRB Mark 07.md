# Mark 7 (Douay-Rheims)

[[DRB Mark 06|← Mark 6]] · [[Douay-Rheims|index]] · [[DRB Mark 08|Mark 8 →]]

Compare: [[Mark 07|BSB and SBLGNT]]

**7:1** And there assembled together unto him the Pharisees and some of the scribes, coming from Jerusalem. ^7-1

**7:2** And when they had seen some of his disciples eat bread with common, that is, with unwashed hands, they found fault. ^7-2

**7:3** For the Pharisees, and all the Jews eat not without often washing their hands, holding the tradition of the ancients: ^7-3

**7:4** And when they come from the market, unless they be washed, they eat not: and many other things there are that have been delivered to them to observe, the washings of cups and of pots, and of brazen vessels, and of beds. ^7-4

**7:5** And the Pharisees and scribes asked him: Why do not thy disciples walk according to the tradition of the ancients, but they eat bread with common hands? ^7-5

**7:6** But he answering, said to them: Well did Isaias prophesy of you hypocrites, as it is written: This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. ^7-6

**7:7** And in vain to they worship me, teaching doctrines and precepts of men. ^7-7

**7:8** For leaving the commandment of God, you hold the tradition of men, the washing of pots and of cups: and many other things you do like to these. ^7-8

**7:9** And he said to them: Well do you make void the commandment of God, that you may keep your own tradition. ^7-9

**7:10** For Moses said: Honour thy father and thy mother; and He that shall curse father or mother, dying let him die. ^7-10

**7:11** But you say: If a man shall say to his father or mother, Corban, (which is a gift,) whatsoever is from me, shall profit thee. ^7-11

**7:12** And further you suffer him not to do any thing for his father or mother, ^7-12

**7:13** Making void the word of God by your own tradition, which you have given forth. And many other such like things you do. ^7-13

**7:14** And calling again the multitude unto him, he said to them: Hear ye me all, and understand. ^7-14

**7:15** There is nothing from without a man that entering into him, can defile him. But the things which come from a man, those are they that defile a man. ^7-15

**7:16** If any man have ears to hear, let him hear. ^7-16

**7:17** And when he was come into the house from the multitude, his disciples asked him the parable. ^7-17

**7:18** And he saith to them: So are you also without knowledge? understand you not that every thing from without, entering into a man cannot defile him: ^7-18

**7:19** Because it entereth not into his heart, but goeth into the belly, and goeth out into the privy, purging all meats? ^7-19

**7:20** But he said that the things which come out from a man, they defile a man. ^7-20

**7:21** For from within out of the heart of men proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders, ^7-21

**7:22** Thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness. ^7-22

**7:23** All these evil things come from within, and defile a man. ^7-23

**7:24** And rising from thence he went into the coasts of Tyre and Sidon: and entering into a house, he would that no man should know it, and he could not be hid. ^7-24

**7:25** For a woman as soon as she heard of him, whose daughter had an unclean spirit, came in and fell down at his feet. ^7-25

**7:26** For the woman was a Gentile, a Syrophenician born. And she besought him that he would cast forth the devil out of her daughter. ^7-26

**7:27** Who said to her: Suffer first the children to be filled: for it is not good to take the bread of the children, and cast it to the dogs. ^7-27

**7:28** But she answered and said to him: Yea, Lord; for the whelps also eat under the table of the crumbs of the children. ^7-28

**7:29** And he said to her: For this saying go thy way, the devil is gone out of thy daughter. ^7-29

**7:30** And when she was come into her house, she found the girl lying upon the bed, and that the devil was gone out. ^7-30

**7:31** And again going out of the coasts of Tyre, he came by Sidon to the sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis. ^7-31

**7:32** And they bring to him one deaf and dumb; and they besought him that he would lay his hand upon him. ^7-32

**7:33** And taking him from the multitude apart, he put his fingers into his ears, and spitting, he touched his tongue: ^7-33

**7:34** And looking up to heaven, he groaned, and said to him: Ephpheta, which is, Be thou opened. ^7-34

**7:35** And immediately his ears were opened, and the string of his tongue was loosed, and he spoke right. ^7-35

**7:36** And he charged them that they should tell no man. But the more he charged them, so much the more a great deal did they publish it. ^7-36

**7:37** And so much the more did they wonder, saying: He hath done all things well; he hath made both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak. ^7-37
