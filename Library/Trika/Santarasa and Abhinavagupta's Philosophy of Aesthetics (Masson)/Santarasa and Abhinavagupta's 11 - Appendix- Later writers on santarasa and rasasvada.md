“ Dayàvira (in which the sthāyibhāva utsāha is based on or is concerned with mercy or benevolence ), etc., are not identical with this ( śānta ), as ( ŝānta ) is without even the slightest trace of egoism ( while dayāvira, etc., are marked by egoism ) ”.

In dayāvira, etc., such as for example in the case of Jimūtavàhana, etc., we do not find an extinction of egoism, in as much as we observe in the middle of the play, Jimûtavāhana’s love for Malayavati, and in the end his attainment of the status of sovereignity over the Vidyàdharas. Śànta, however, cannot be included under dayāvira, etc., because its exclusive nature is the extinction of egoism in every way. Hence the view that in the Nāàgānanda, ŝānta is the dominant sentiment, is refuted.

The answer is that origination ( tpatti } and cessation ( vināŝa) really belong to the aesthetic experience ( carran ) of the vibhāvas, anubhāvas and vyabhicārihhāvas which are the suggestors of the rasa.
