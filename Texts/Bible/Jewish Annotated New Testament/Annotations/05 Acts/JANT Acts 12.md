### 12.1–19: Persecution of apostles

*[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 247* · A central literary theme in Acts. The frequent depiction of believers in Jesus as under persistent threat, particularly by Jews and Jewish leaders, manifests Luke’s literary and theological interests, rather than a reliable historical record. ^12-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-1|1]]:** *King Herod*, Agrippa I, grandson of Herod I, who ruled Judea (under Roman authority) 41–44 CE. Only Acts refers to Agrippa as Herod, possibly to build continuity with Herod Antipas, who was involved in the deaths of John the Baptist ([[Luke 03#^3-18|Lk 3.18–20]]; [[Luke 09#^9-7|9.7–9]]) and Jesus ([[Luke 13#^13-31|Lk 13.31]]; [[Luke 23#^23-6|23.6–12]]; [[Acts 04#^4-27|Acts 4.27]]), and with his grandfather, Herod the Great (37–4 BCE). See Mt 2; [[Luke 01#^1-5|Lk 1.5]]; [[Luke 03#^3-1|3.1]]. Jewish tradition remembers Agrippa as a fair, honorable, and religiously observant (Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 266–75; Josephus, *Ant.* 19.330–31; *m. Bik.* 3.4; *b. Ketub. 17a*; *b. Sot.* 41a; *Lev. Rab.* 3.5). ^12-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-2|2]]:** *James, the brother of John*, James (Greek “Iakobos,” Jacob) the son of Zebedee. James and John were early disciples of Jesus ([[Luke 05#^5-10|Lk 5.10]]; [[Luke 06#^6-14|6.14]]). ^12-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-3|3]]:** *Pleased the Jews*, Jews as a whole, not just individuals such as the high priest or groups such as Sadducees, frequently appear in Acts as persecutors of Christians (13.45; 14.2,19; 17.5; 18.12; 20.3; 21.27; 22.30; 23.12; 24.9; 25.2,7; 26.2,21). *Unleavened Bread*, Passover, the festival during which Jesus was arrested and executed ([[Luke 22#^22-1|Lk 22.1]],[[Luke 22#^22-7|7]]). ^12-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-4|4]]:** *Squads*, Gk “stratiō-tos,” a group consisting of about ten soldiers; four squads (40 or so soldiers), excessive for guarding one person, emphasizes the hostility against Peter and the subsequent miraculous escape. ^12-4

### 12.6–11: Miraculous release from prison

*[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-6|Text]], p. 247* · See 5.17–21; 16.23–29. ^12-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-7|7]]:** *Angel*, on angelic rescues, see 5.19; 27.23; [[Daniel 03#^3-19|Dan 3.19–28]]; [[Daniel 06#^6-23|6.22]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 11#^11-6|2 Macc 11.6]]. ^12-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-12|12]]:** *House of Mary,* Mary is a Jewish woman of financial means who uses her resources to support the Christian community. Acts frequently depicts women as possessing wealth and occupying important positions in the community (e.g., 9.36; 16.14; 18.26; 21.8). *John* . . . *Mark*, he accompanies Paul and Barnabas in their work (12.25) but, after a disagreement, separates from Paul and follows Barnabas (15.37–39). He is sometimes identified as Peter’s interpreter (Papius) and the author of the Gospel of Mark. ^12-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-13|13]]:** *Outer gate*, suggesting a large house. ^12-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-14|14]]:** *Saved*, see 2.40n. ^12-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-17|17]]:** *James*, the brother of Jesus ([[Mark 06#^6-3|Mk 6.3]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-19|Gal 1.19]]), leader of Jerusalem church (15.13–21; 21.18). ^12-17

### 12.20–25: Death of Herod

*[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-20|Text]], p. 248* · According to Josephus (*Ant.* 19.343–52), Agrippa, while presiding over public spectacles in Caesarea, was acclaimed as a god. He accepted the flattery but almost immediately fell ill and died shortly thereafter (44 CE). ^12-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-22|22]]:** God punishes persons who think themselves equal to a god ([[Ezekiel 28#^28-1|Ezek 28.1–10]]). ^12-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-23|23]]:** *Worms*, appropriate punishment for those who persecute God’s people (e.g., [[LXX Judith 16#^16-17|Jdt 16.17]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 09#^9-9|2 Macc 9.9]]). ^12-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-24|24]]:** Threats against the believers regularly fail (4.4; 5.42; 14.6–7,19–20; 16.23–34). ^12-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 12#^12-25|25]]:** See 11.29–30. *John . . . Mark*, see v 12; 9.36n. ^12-25
