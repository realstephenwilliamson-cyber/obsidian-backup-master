## About

The first encyclical of Pope Leo XIV, on the dignity of the human person in a time of artificial intelligence. It applies Catholic social teaching to AI, robotics and digital technology.

- **Title:** the Vatican's English text is headed *Magnifica Humanitas*, Encyclical Letter of His Holiness Pope Leo XIV on Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence. The bishops of England and Wales gave the theme as preserving the human person in the age of artificial intelligence.
- **Dates:** signed 15 May 2026, the 135th anniversary of Leo XIII's *Rerum novarum* (1891), as paragraph 3 notes; published Monday 25 May 2026.
- **Presentation:** the Pope presented it in the Vatican's Synod Hall with Cardinals Víctor Manuel Fernández and Michael Czerny, the theologians Anna Rowlands and Léocadie Lushombo, and Christopher Olah of Anthropic; Cardinal Pietro Parolin gave closing remarks. Wikipedia notes that popes have usually left such presentations to cardinals.
- **Shape:** 245 numbered sections: an introduction (1–16), five chapters and a conclusion (229–245). The chapters run from the Church's method and the principles of its social doctrine to technology and dominance; truth, work and freedom; and *the culture of power and the civilization of love*. Wikipedia reports a New York Times count of 42,300 words.
- **Argument:** human dignity, grounded in the image of God, is the measure of technical progress. In Wikipedia's summary, AI lacks consciousness and moral conscience; the letter opposes an AI arms race and AI in war, criticizes transhumanism, deepfakes and threats to labour, and apologizes for the Church's historical role in slavery.
- **Languages:** first issued in eight modern languages; Wikipedia found no official Latin text as of July 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says it sets out the Church's position on AI and continues the social teaching begun by *Rerum novarum*. Governments including Canada, Spain and the European Commission responded, and the UK House of Lords debated it on 5 June 2026.
