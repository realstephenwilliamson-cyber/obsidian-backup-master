# Rashi 50 - Ki Tavo

← [[Rashi 49 - Ki Teitzei]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 51 - Nitzavim]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-1|Deuteronomy 26:1–29:8]]* · Text: [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo]]

## Chapter 26

### 26:1

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-1|Deuteronomy 26:1]]

**When it happens that you come . . . and you inherit it and settle it.** This teaches that they were not obligated to bring the first fruits until they conquered the land and apportioned it.

### 26:2

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-2|Deuteronomy 26:2]]

**Of the first.** But not all the first, as not all fruits are subject to the first fruit obligation, but only the seven species. The phrase here is “the land,” and the phrase there is “A ‘land’ of wheat, barley, etc.” Just as the reference there is to the seven species by which Eretz Yisroel is extolled, so, too, is the reference here to the extollment of Eretz Yisroel, meaning the seven species.

**The oil olive.** The *aguri* olive, with its oil gathered inside.

**Honey.** This refers to date honey.

**Of the first.** When a man enters his field and notices the first fig to mature, he wraps a papyrus strip around it as a marking symbol, and declares, “This is *bikurim.*”

### 26:3

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-3|Deuteronomy 26:3]]

**Who shall be [of service] during those days.** You have no one except the *kohein* who serves during your days, as he may be.

**And you will say to him.** That you are not ungrateful.

**I ascertain today.** Once each year, not twice.

### 26:4

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-4|Deuteronomy 26:4]]

**The kohein shall then take the basket from your hand.** In order to wave it — the *kohein* places his hand below the owner’s hand and waves.

### 26:5

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-5|Deuteronomy 26:5]]

**You shall then proclaim.** This denotes a loud proclamation.

**The Aramite destroyed my forefather.** He cites the Omnipresent’s benevolence. The Aramite destroyed my forefather — Lavan sought to uproot everything when he pursued Yaakov, because he intended to act, the Omnipresent considered it as if he had actually acted; for with the nations, the Omnipresent equates the [evil] intent with the deed.

**Then he descended to Egypt.** Yet others fell upon us to annihilate us, for after this Yaakov descended to Egypt.

**With a tiny community.** With seventy souls.

### 26:9

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-9|Deuteronomy 26:9]]

**To this place.** This refers to the Beis Hamikdosh.

**And He gave us this land.** As indicated by the plain connotation.

### 26:10

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-10|Deuteronomy 26:10]]

**You shall then place it.** This teaches that he takes it after the kohein’s waving, holds it in his hand while reciting, and then waves it again.

### 26:11

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-11|Deuteronomy 26:11]]

**You shall rejoice with all the good.** From here they derive that one does not recite the first-fruit reading except during the time of rejoicing — from the *Atzeres*-Shavuos holiday until the Succos celebration, when a man gathers his grain, fruit, wine, and oil. However, from Succos and onward, he brings without reciting.

**You, the Levi.** The Levi, too, is subject to the first obligation — if they planted in their cities.

**And the proselyte in your midst —** who brings without reciting, as he is unable to say, “to our forefathers.”

### 26:12

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-12|Deuteronomy 26:12]]

**When you finish tithing all the tithes of your produce in the third year.** When you finish setting aside the tithes of the third year, designate the time for the divesture and the recitation on the eve of Pesach of the fourth year, as it is said, “At the end of three years you shall remove, etc.” Here, “at the end of” is said, and below, “at the end of seven years” is said, concerning *Hakhel.* Just as, there, [it refers to] a festival, so here, [it refers to] a festival. Perhaps, just as there [it refers to] the Succos festival, so here, [it refers to] the Succos festival? The Torah therefore teaches, ‘When you complete tithing the tithes of the third year’ — the festival during which the tithes are completed, which is Pesach, since there are many trees which are harvested after Succos. Thus, the tithes of the third [year] are completed at Pesach of the fourth [year], and if anyone delayed his tithes, Scripture requires that he divest his property of them.

**The year of the tithe.** The year when the tithing obligation is for only one of the two tithes which were applicable during the two preceding years; during the first and second years of the *Shemittah* cycle, the obligation is for the first tithe — as it is said, “. . .when you take, from the Israelites, the tithe...” — and the second tithe, as it is said, “You shall eat, before Adonoy your God, the tithe of your grain, wine, and oil.” Hence, there are two tithes. Here the teaching is that, during the third year, there is an obligation only for one of those two tithes — which one? The first tithe, and in place of the second tithe, he must give the tithe for the indigent. For it is said here, “You have given to the Levi,” his due — thus, the first tithe. “To the proselyte, orphan, and widow” — this is the tithe for the indigent.

**So that they may eat within your portals to their satisfaction.** Supply them with enough to satisfy them. We derive from here that one may not reduce the gift to the poor person at the threshing area to less than half a *kav* of wheat.

### 26:13

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-13|Deuteronomy 26:13]]

**You shall declare before Adonoy, your God.** Acknowledge that you gave your tithes.

**I have divested my estate of sacred material.** This refers to the second tithe and the fourth-year fruit, and teaches you that if one tarried with his two year tithes, and did not bring them up to Jerusalem, he must bring them up now.

**And have also presented it to the Levi.** This refers to the first tithe. **Also.** This includes the *terumah*-gift, and the first fruits.

**The proselyte, the orphan, and the widow.** This refers to the indigent tithe.

**Totally according to Your command.** I gave them in their proper order — I did not give precedence to the *terumah*-gift over the first fruits, or to the tithe over the *terumah,* or to the second [tithe] over the first; for *terumah* is entitled “the first,” as it is first after becoming grain. And it is written, “Your fully ripened produce and your *terumah* gift, do not delay” — do not change the order.

**I did not transgress Your commandments.** I did not set aside the tithe of one species for another, nor of the new crop for the old (or of detached fruits for those still growing, or of growing fruits for those detached).

**Nor did I forget.** To bless you for the setting aside of tithes.

### 26:14

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-14|Deuteronomy 26:14]]

**I did not eat of it when grieving.** From here we derive that it is forbidden during grief.

**Nor did I devour it when ritually defiled.** Whether I was defiled and it was clean, or I was clean and it was defiled. Where is the admonition concerning this? “You may not eat in your cities” — this refers to eating during defilement, as it is said concerning disqualified sacred offerings, “You may eat it in your cities, the defiled and the clean, etc.” But this, you may not eat after the manner of the eating in your cities mentioned elsewhere.

**Nor did I make use of it for the dead.** To make a casket or shrouds.

**I have heeded the voice of Adonoy my God.** I have brought it to the Temple.

**I have fulfilled everything that You commanded me.** I rejoiced, and brought joy with it.

### 26:15

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-15|Deuteronomy 26:15]]

**View, from Your sacred residence.** We have fulfilled what You decreed for us — now You fulfill what is incumbent upon You to do, as You said, “If you follow My decrees, etc., I shall give you rain at the proper time.”

**Which You have given us, as You swore to our forefathers.** To give to us, and You fulfilled — a land flowing with milk and honey.

### 26:16

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-16|Deuteronomy 26:16]]

**This very day, Adonoy, your God, commands you.** Each day let them appear novel to you, as if, on that very day, you had been commanded to perform them.

**You shall observe and perform them.** A heavenly echo blesses him; ‘You have brought the first fruits on this day — may you reiterate next year.’

### 26:17

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-17|Deuteronomy 26:17]]

**You have distinguished . . . He has distinguished you.** There is no compelling evidence concerning these words in Scripture; but it appears to me that the word denotes setting aside, distinguishing — you have distinguished Him, from the alien gods, to be your God, and He set you aside, for Himself, from the peoples of the earth, to be His treasured people. (Another interpretation, to which I have found evidence; it denotes glory, as in “All evildoers glorify themselves.”)

### 26:18

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-18|Deuteronomy 26:18]]

**As He said to you.** “You shall be My treasure.”

### 26:19

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-19|Deuteronomy 26:19]]

**And, so that you may be a people sanctified as He said.** “You shall be sanctified to me.”

## Chapter 27

### 27:1

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-1|Deuteronomy 27:1]]

**Keep all of the commandment.** This denotes constancy. *Gardant* in Old French.

### 27:2

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-2|Deuteronomy 27:2]]

**Erect.** In the Yardein. Afterward, remove others from there, and construct an altar with them on Mount Eival. Consequently, we may state that there were three kinds of stones; twelve in the Yardein, a similar number at Gilgal, the same at Mount Eival, as stated in Tractate Sotah.

### 27:8

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-8|Deuteronomy 27:8]]

**Explained properly.** In seventy languages.

### 27:9

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-9|Deuteronomy 27:9]]

**Take heed.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders.

**This day you have become a people.** Each day let it appear to you as if, on that very day, you had entered the covenant with Him.

### 27:12

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-12|Deuteronomy 27:12]]

**To bless the people.** As stated in tractate Sotah. Six tribes ascended the summit of Mount Gerizim, and six the summit of Mount Eival, with the *kohanim* and *Levi’im* and the ark below, at the center. The *Levi’im* directed their faces toward Mount Gerizim, and began the blessing: ‘Blessed is the man who does not make a sculpture or molten idol, etc.’ These, and the others as well, answered, *Amein.* They turned, directed their faces toward Mount Eival, and began the curse, saying: ‘Cursed is the man who makes a sculpture, etc.’ [These, and the others as well, answered *Amein.* They turned, directed their faces toward Mount Gerizim, and said: ‘Blessed is the one who does not revile his father or mother,’ etc. Then they turned, directed their faces toward Mount Eival, saying: ‘Cursed is the one who reviles his father or mother.’] Similarly with all of them, until ‘Cursed is whoever does not uphold.’

### 27:16

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-16|Deuteronomy 27:16]]

**Whoever reviles his father.** To denigrate. This means the same as “Your brother shall be denigrated.”[25:3]

### 27:17

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-17|Deuteronomy 27:17]]

**Whoever casts aside the boundary.** Who casts it back, and steals the property. This means the same as “Has been cast back.”

### 27:18

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-18|Deuteronomy 27:18]]

**Whoever misleads the blind.** Blind concerning a particular matter, and he offers ill advice.

### 27:24

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-24|Deuteronomy 27:24]]

**Whoever strikes his fellow in secret.** This refers to slander. I have seen [this] in R’ Moshe Hadarshan’s commentary: There are eleven curses here, corresponding to eleven tribes. However, corresponding to Shimon, no curse is recorded. Because he (Moshe) did not intend to bless him before his death, as he blessed the other tribes, he, therefore, did not want to curse him.

### 27:26

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-26|Deuteronomy 27:26]]

**Whoever does not uphold.** Here, he encompasses all of the Torah in its entirety, which they accepted by an imprecation and an oath.

## Chapter 28

### 28:4

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-4|Deuteronomy 28:4]]

**The issue of your cattle.** The offspring of your cattle, which the animal sends forth from her stomach.

**And the flocks of your sheep.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders. The Rabbis comment: Why are they called עַשְׁתָּרוֹת ? Because they enrich their owners and strengthen them, like the palisades, which are powerful cliffs.

### 28:5

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-5|Deuteronomy 28:5]]

**Blessed will be your basket.** Your fruit. Another interpretation: Your basket — liquids which you filter through baskets.

**And your solid foods.** Solids, which remain in the utensil without dripping.

### 28:6

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-6|Deuteronomy 28:6]]

**You will be blessed at your arrival, and blessed at your departure.** For your departure from the world will be without sin, like your arrival into the world.

### 28:7

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-7|Deuteronomy 28:7]]

**But by seven roads they will flee from you.** This is the way the panic-stricken flee — by scattering in every direction.

### 28:20

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-20|Deuteronomy 28:20]]

**Ruin.** Disintegration, like “ruinous leprosy.”

**Panic.** Dismay, the roar of confusion.

### 28:22

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-22|Deuteronomy 28:22]]

**With blistering.** Whereby the flesh swells and blisters.

**Searing temperature.** Meaning the same as “For a flame sears my nostrils,” which refers to the fever of the diseased. *Malweid* in Old French, which is extremely hot.

**Raging fever.** Hotter than קַדַּחַת — searing temperature. These are various diseases.

**With charchur.** A disease whereby the fever penetrates throughout the body, causing constant thirst. In Old French, *Esardement. This means the same as “My bones have become parched with heat,” “The bellows are shrivelled by fire.”*

**With the sword.** He will incite armed forces against you.

**With wind-blight and yellowing.** Injury to the crops in the field. **Wind-blight.** The east wind, *Hasle* in Old French.

**Yellowing.** Withering, whereby the produce pales and turns yellow. *Cro* in Old French.

**Until your annihilation.** Targum: עַד דְּתֵיבָד, meaning until the annihilation of you, until you are annihilated of yourself.

### 28:23

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-23|Deuteronomy 28:23]]

**The heavens above your head will be like copper.** These curses Moshe articulated himself, while those at Mount Sinai, by the word of God he (Moshe) pronounced them, as indicated by the text: there, it is said, “If you do not heed ‘Me’.” “If you relate to ‘Me’ in a fickle way.” Here, however, it says, “to the voice of ‘Adonoy, your God’,” “‘Adonoy’ shall attach to you” “‘Adonoy’ shall strike you.” Moshe moderated his curses by expressing them in the singular form. He also moderated this curse, for in the first ones, it states, “your heavens like iron and your land like copper.” The heavens will not secrete moisture, just as iron does not secrete moisture, so that the world will subsequently suffer from drought. But the land will secrete moisture, just as copper secretes moisture, so that its produce will rot. Here, however, it says, ‘Your heavens copper and your land iron,’ the heavens will secrete moisture. Although they will not pour rain, still, there won’t be a ruinous drought in the world. And the land will not secrete moisture, just as iron does not secrete moisture, so that the produce will not rot. Still, this is a curse. Whether it is like copper or like iron, it will not produce fruit. Similarly, the heavens will not pour rain.

### 28:24

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-24|Deuteronomy 28:24]]

**The rain of your land into dust and soil.** When the wind follows a rainfall. The rain falls, but insufficiently, not enough to settle the dust. The wind follows and lifts the dust, so that it blankets the shoots of the seeds which were moistened by the water. It becomes attached to them and turns to mud, and they wither and rot.

### 28:25

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-25|Deuteronomy 28:25]]

**A cause of shuddering.** Terror and shuddering. Anyone learning of your injuries will shudder because of you, and exclaim, “Woe to us! Let this not befall us the way it befell them”!

### 28:27

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-27|Deuteronomy 28:27]]

**With Egyptian boils.** Extremely pernicious, moist outside and dry inside, as stated in [tractate] Bechoros.

**With running boils.** Wet boils.

**Dry boils.** Boils that are dry, like ceramics.

### 28:28

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-28|Deuteronomy 28:28]]

**And with utter confusion.** Stupefaction. *Estordison* in Old French.

### 28:29

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-29|Deuteronomy 28:29]]

**The victim of wrangling.** Whatever you do will be subject to altercation.

### 28:30

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-30|Deuteronomy 28:30]]

**Will lie with her.** The meaning is related to “mistress,” “concubine,” but Scripture prefers a more refined term, “Will lie with her.” This is the kind of refinement made by scribes.

**Redeem it.** During the fourth year, in order to eat its fruit.

### 28:32

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-32|Deuteronomy 28:32]]

**And pine for them.** Yearning for them to return, but they shall never return. Any unfulfilled longing is described as “the pining of eyes.”

### 28:37

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-37|Deuteronomy 28:37]]

**Stupefaction.** Similar to utter confusion, *Estordison.* Anyone who sees you will be stupefied by you.

**Be cited as an example.** If a terrible blow befalls a person, they will say, ‘This is like that person’s blow.’

**And become the subject of gossip.** This means the same as “You shall discuss them.” They will talk about you. Targum’s translation is the same — “ וּלְשׁוֹעֵי,” meaning to relate, “And he related.”

### 28:38

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-38|Deuteronomy 28:38]]

**Will demolish it.** Will destroy it. For this reason it is called חָסִיל, [lit.] the demolisher, as it destroys everything.

### 28:40

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-40|Deuteronomy 28:40]]

**Will be discarded.** Will discard its fruit, meaning the same as “And the iron discards.”

### 28:42

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-42|Deuteronomy 28:42]]

**The cicada will impoverish.** The cicada will render it impoverished of fruit.

**Will impoverish.** Will make destitute.

**The cicada.** A species of locusts. But it is not feasible to interpret יְיָרֵשׁ as inheritance, for, if so, it should have written יִירַשׁ . Nor, as expulsion and ejection, for, if so, it should have written יוֹרִישׁ .

### 28:47

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-47|Deuteronomy 28:47]]

**In total affluence.** While you had every benefit.

### 28:49

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-49|Deuteronomy 28:49]]

**As the eagle soars.** Suddenly, travelling successfully, with fleet steeds.

**Whose language you will not hear.** You will not recognize their language. Similarly, “You listen to a dream so as to interpret it.” Similarly, “That Yoseif was listening.” *Entendre* in Old French.

### 28:52

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-52|Deuteronomy 28:52]]

**Until mastering your ramparts.** The meaning is mastering, conquering.

### 28:53

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-53|Deuteronomy 28:53]]

**You will eat . . ., the flesh of your sons, etc., in the siege.** Because they will lay siege to the city, resulting in torment there — the agony of starvation.

### 28:54

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-54|Deuteronomy 28:54]]

**The delicate among you, and the fastidious.** The delicate is the same as the fastidious, meaning pampered. “Out of fastidiousness and softness” indicates conclusively that both are the same. Although pampered, and revolted by anything disgusting, he will savor, in his starvation, the flesh of his sons and daughters, so that he will begrudge his remaining children, and refuse to give any of them the flesh of his children — their siblings — which he has eaten. Another interpretation. The compassionate among you. The merciful and tender hearted, being extremely famished, will become brutal, and refuse to give the flesh of their slaughtered children to their remaining children.

### 28:56

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-56|Deuteronomy 28:56]]

**Her eye will begrudge the husband of her bosom, and her son and daughter.** The grown ones.

### 28:57

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-57|Deuteronomy 28:57]]

**And her infant.** Young children. She will begrudge all of them, when eating one, and refuse to give those remaining with her any of the flesh.

### 28:59

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-59|Deuteronomy 28:59]]

**Adonoy will strike you with prodigious blows.** Prodigious, classified separately from other blows.

**And dependable.** To torment you, thus fulfilling their mission.

### 28:60

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-60|Deuteronomy 28:60]]

**Which you dreaded.** The plagues. For, when the Israelites saw the extraordinary plagues visited on Egypt, they were fearful of them, lest they be visited on them as well. The proof is that the following is written: “If you obey, etc., all of the diseases which I visited upon Egypt, I will not visit upon you.” One does not threaten a man except with something he dreads.

### 28:61

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-61|Deuteronomy 28:61]]

**Will raise up.** The connotation is rising up.

### 28:62

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-62|Deuteronomy 28:62]]

**You will remain numerically few, instead, etc.** Few instead of many.

### 28:63

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-63|Deuteronomy 28:63]]

**So will Adonoy bring joy.** To your enemies, concerning you, to annihilate, etc. [However, the Holy One, may He be blessed, will not rejoice Himself. From here [we derive that] the Holy One, may He be blessed, does not rejoice at the downfall of the wicked. For, it is not said, “will rejoice,” but, rather, “will bring joy.” He brings joy to others, but does not rejoice. However, with the benefit of the righteous, He rejoices Himself, as it is said, “just as Adonoy rejoiced over you.”]

**You will be uprooted.** The word denotes uprooting. Similarly, “The house of the haughty, Adonoy shall uproot.”

### 28:64

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]

**You shall serve there [those who serve] other gods.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders. Not literally idolatry, but, rather, paying income or poll taxes to the priests of idolatry.

### 28:65

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-65|Deuteronomy 28:65]]

**You will not be tranquil.** You will not rest, as in, “This is the tranquility.”

**A fearful heart.** A trembling heart, as Targum [Onkelos] renders, “fearful.” Like, “The nether world trembles towards you,” “Peoples heard and trembled,” “The foundations of the firmament tremble.”

**Pining eyes.** Yearning for salvation, but it shall not come.

### 28:66

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-66|Deuteronomy 28:66]]

**Your life will dangle before you.** In suspense. All suspense is considered dangling — ‘Perhaps I shall die today, by the sword visited upon us.’ The Rabbis expound: This refers to one who purchases produce at the marketplace.

**You will have no faith in your living.** This refers to one who depends on the bakeshop.

### 28:67

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-67|Deuteronomy 28:67]]

**In the morning you will say, ‘Would that it were evening.’** That it were last evening.

**And in the evening, you will say, ‘Would that it were morning.’** This morning, for the tribulations constantly increase, with every minute’s curses exceeding the previous one’s.

### 28:68

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-68|Deuteronomy 28:68]]

**On ships.** By ship, as captives.

**There you will attempt to sell yourselves to your enemies.** You will attempt to be sold to them as slaves and maidservants.

**But no one will buy.** For they will sentence you with death and destruction.

**You will attempt to be sold.** In Old French, *Eporwendrez wos.* But it is unfeasible to interpret וְהִתְמַכַּרְתֶּם as ”you will be sold,” through other sellers, because of the statement following, “but no one will buy.”

### 28:69

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-69|Deuteronomy 28:69]]

**To ratify with Bnei Yisroel.** Whereby they were to accept the Torah by imprecation and oath.

**Beside the covenant.** The imprecations in *Toras Kohanim* pronounced at Sinai.

## Chapter 29

### 29:3

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^29-3|Deuteronomy 29:3]]

**Yet Adonoy did not grant you a knowledgeable mind.** To recognize God’s benevolence, and adhere to him.

**Until this day.** I have heard: because on that day, when Moshe presented the Torah scroll to the sons of Levi — as it is written, “He presented it to the *kohanim,* the sons of Levi” — all Yisroel appeared before Moshe, and said to him: “Our master Moshe, we, too, stood at Sinai. We accepted the Torah, and it was given to us. How is it that you are empowering your fellow tribesmen over it, so that they may tell us, some day in the future, ‘It was never given to you, it was given to us”’? Moshe rejoiced over this matter, and it was concerning this that he said to them, “This day, you have become a nation, etc.” This day I understand that you are attached to, and crave, the Omnipresent.

### 29:6

[[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^29-6|Deuteronomy 29:6]]

**Then when you came to this place.** You now view yourselves in prominence and glory; do not rebel against the Omnipresent, do not become arrogant, observe the tenets of this covenant. Another interpretation of “God did not grant you a knowledgeable mind”; A man cannot fathom the full extent of his master’s thought, and the wisdom of his teaching, until forty years. Therefore, the Omnipresent was not exacting with you until this day. However, from this day forward, He will be exacting. Therefore: **Observe the tenets of this covenant, etc.** (Avodah Zorah 5.)
