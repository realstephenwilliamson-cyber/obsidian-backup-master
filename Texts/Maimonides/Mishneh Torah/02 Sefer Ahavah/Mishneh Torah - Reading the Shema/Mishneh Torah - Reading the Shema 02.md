**1.** One who recites the first verse of *Kri'at Shema*[^1] - i.e., *Shema Yisrael*... - without intention,[^2] does not fulfill his obligation.[^3] [One who recites] the rest without intention fulfills his obligation.[^4]  
Even a person studying Torah in his usual way[^5] or proofreading these portions[^6] at the time of *Kri'at Shema* fulfills his obligation provided he concentrates his intention for the first verse.[^7] ^2-1

**2.** A person may recite [the *Shema*] as he is,[^8] whether standing, walking, lying down[^9] or riding on the back of an animal.[^10] It is forbidden to recite the *Shema* while lying face down on the ground or flat on one's back with his face pointing upwards.[^11] However, one may recite it lying on his side.[^12]  
A particularly obese person who cannot turn over onto his side or a sick person should lean slightly to the side and [then] recite it.[^13] ^2-2

**3.** A person who is walking on foot must stop for the first verse.[^14] He may recite the rest while walking.[^15] If one is sleeping, we should disturb him by awakening him until he reads the first verse.[^16] From that point on, if he is overcome by sleep, we are not obligated to disturb him.[^17] ^2-3

**4.** A person who is involved in work must stop while he recites the whole first section.[^18] Artisans[^19] must also interrupt their work for the first section, in order that their recitation should not be haphazard.[^20] They may recite the rest while working[^21] in their normal manner. Even one standing in a tree or on top of a wall may read [the *Shema*] where he is,[^22] reciting the blessings before and after it.[^23] ^2-4

**5.** A person who is studying Torah when the time to recite the *Shema* arrives should stop to recite the *Shema*[^24] with the blessings before and after it.[^25] One who is involved in community matters should not stop,[^26] but rather finishes his work[^27] and reads the *Shema* if there is still time left.[^28] ^2-5

**6.** A person who is eating, is in the bathhouse, is having a haircut, is working with skins[^29] or is involved in court,[^30] should complete [his task] and recite the *Shema* afterwards. If he fears that the time for its recitation[^31] will pass, and, [therefore,] stops to recite it, he has acted in a praiseworthy fashion.[^32] ^2-6

**7.** A person who immerses in a ritual bath and is able to come up and dress before sunrise, should do so, and [then] recite [the *Shema*].[^33] If he is afraid that perhaps the sun will rise[^34] before he can recite the *Shema*,[^35] he should cover himself with the water in which he stands and recite the *Shema*.[^36]  
He should not cover himself with putrid water that has an unpleasant odor[^37] or with water that has been used for soaking flax[^38] or with water so clean that his nakedness is visible.[^39] However, he may cover himself with murky water that has no unpleasant odor and recite [the *Shema] where he is.[^40]* ^2-7

**8.** While reciting the Shema, one should not gesture with his eyes or lips, or point with his fingers,[^41] in order that his reading not be haphazard.[^42] If one were to do this, although he does fulfill his obligation, he has acted improperly.[^43]  
One should recite the *Shema* so that his words are audible to himself.[^44] [However, even] if he does not do this, he fulfills his obligation.[^45] One must enunciate the letters clearly.[^46] [However, even] if he does not do this, he fulfills his obligation.[^47] ^2-8

**9.** How must one enunciate? He must be careful not to pronounce [a letter with] a strong *dagesh* as if there were no *dagesh*,[^48] or [a letter with] no *dagesh* as if there were one.[^49] Nor should one pronounce the silent *sheva*[^50] or silence the pronounced *sheva*.[^51]  
Therefore, one must pause between two words in which the first word ends with the same letter with which the second word begins.[^52] For example, when reading בכל לבבך (*bechol levavcha*) ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deuteronomy 6:5]]), one should pause slightly between בכל (*bechol*) and לבבך (*levavcha*). [One should act] similarly in the cases of ואבדתם מהרה (*va'avad'tem meheirah*) ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-17|Deuteronomy 11:17]]) and הכנף[^53] פתיל (*hacanaf p'til*) ([[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-38|Numbers 15:38]]).  
One must also pronounce distinctly the *zayin* of תזכרו (*tizkeru*) (Numbers[^54] 15:40).  
One should sufficiently elongate the *dalet* in אחד (*echad*) ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-4|Deuteronomy 6:4]]) in order to proclaim God's sovereignty over the Heaven and the Earth, and all four directions. The *chet* in אחד (*echad*, *ibid.*) should not be[^55] shortened so that the word sounds like איחד (*ee-chad*).[^56] ^2-9

**10.** A person may recite the *Shema* in any language he understands.[^57] One who recites in a foreign language must be as scrupulous in his enunciation as if he were reciting it in the Holy Tongue.[^58] ^2-10

**11.** One who reads [the *Shema*] out of order does not fulfill his obligation. This refers to the order of the verses.[^59] However, were one to reverse the order of the sections,[^60] even though it is not permitted,[^61] I hold[^62] that he does fulfill his obligation, since these sections are not sequential in the Torah.[^63]  
To recite a verse and then repeat it again is improper. One who reads a word and then repeats it, such as one who recites *Shema, Shema*, should be silenced.[^64] ^2-11

**12.** If one reads intermittently,[^65] he fulfills his obligation, even if he pauses between each reading an amount of time sufficient to complete the entire *Kri'at Shema*.[^66]  
This refers to the one who recites it in order.[^67] If one recites it while drowsy[^68] - i.e., not fully awake, but not fast asleep - he fulfills his obligation, as long as he was fully awake while reciting the first verse.[^69] ^2-12

**13.** One who is unsure whether or not he recited the *Shema*,[^70] should recite it with the blessings before and after it.[^71] However, if he is sure that he recited the *Shema*, but is in doubt regarding whether he recited the blessings before and after it, he need not recite the blessings again.[^72]  
A person who made a mistake while reciting [the *Shema*][^73] should return to the point of his mistake.[^74] If one becomes confused and forgets which section he has just completed,[^75] he should return to the first section[^76] - i.e., "And you shall love God, your Lord" ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deuteronomy 6:5]]). ^2-13

**14.** A person who errs in the middle of a section and is unaware of where he paused,[^77] should return to the beginning of that section.[^78]  
One who recited וכתבתם (*uch'tavtam*)[^79] but does not know whether or not he recited *uch'tavtam* of [the section of] "*Shema*"[^80] or of [the section of] והיה אם שמוע (And if you will listen),[^81] should return to *uch'tavtam* of "*Shema*."[^82] However, if his doubt arises only after having recited למען ירבו ימיכם (In order that your days be multiplied) ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-21|Deuteronomy 11:21]]), he need not return, because [we assume] he has recited in accordance with the natural pattern of his speech.[^83] ^2-14

**15.** [The following rules apply when] one encounters other people or is approached by them while reciting the *Shema*.[^84] If he is between sections,[^85] he should stop and greet those he is obligated to honor[^86] - e.g., his father,[^87] his teacher[^88] or anyone greater than he in learning.[^89] He may return the greetings of any person who initiates the friendly exchange. ^2-15

**16.** If one is in the middle [of a section], he may stop and initiate an exchange of greetings only with someone of whom he is afraid[^90] - e.g., a king or tyrant. However, he may return the greetings of those he is obligated to honor[^91] - e.g., his father or his teacher. ^2-16

**17.** These are the intervals between the sections: between the first blessing and the second; between the second [blessing] and *Shema*;[^92] between the first and second sections of *Kri'at Shema*; between the second and third sections of *Kri'at Shema*.  
Between these sections, one initiates an exchange with one whom it is his duty to honor and responds to the greetings of anyone.[^93] However, the interval between the end of the third section of *Kri'at Shema* and [the paragraph beginning with] *Emet v'yatziv* is considered the middle of a section,[^94] and one may interrupt only to greet one of whom one is afraid, or to respond to the greetings of someone one is obligated to honor.[^95] ^2-17

This is dependent on the discussion (See commentary, Halachah 2) regarding the extent of the obligation to read *Shema* required by the Torah. Rabbi Meir understands intention to be absolutely necessary only for the first verse and this opinion is accepted by the *Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim* 60:5.
[^2]: The definition of the term "intention" is the subject of much discussion by the Rabbis.  
    There is a dispute among the *Rishonim* regarding whether the performance of commandments requires intention, i.e., must a person have in mind that he is performing the required act in fulfillment of God's command or not? The Rambam (See *Hilchot Shofar* 2:4 and note *Hilchot Chametz U'Matzah* 6:4, and the commentary of the Moznaim editions of those Halachot) maintains that it is necessary to have such an intention. This opinion is also supported by Rabbenu Yitchak Alfasi and Rabbenu Asher and is accepted as Halachah by the *Shulchan Aruch* (*Orach Chayim* 60:4, 598:8). [The dissenting opinions are held by the Rashba and others.]  
    However, concerning the first verse of *Kri'at Shema*, an extra level of concentration is required. In addition to the intent to carry out God's command, the *Shema* must be read in fear and awe, trembling and trepidation. One accepts upon oneself the kingship of Heaven and proclaims the unity of God. It is unthinkable that at such a time, one would not focus his attention on the words he is uttering, but would rather think of mundane matters. (See *Shulchan Aruch HaRav* 60:5.)  
    Each day one should imagine that he is reciting *Shema Yisrael* for the first time, and not as if he had heard it many times before (*Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim* 61:1,2).  
    *Kri'at Shema* contains 248 words (245 of the three sections, plus the three extra words of *Ado-nai Elo-heichem Emet* repeated upon completion of the third section). This corresponds to the 248 positive commandments in the Torah and the 248 limbs of the human body (*Midrash Ne'elam*; quoted by the *Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim* 61:3.) Thus, the recitation of the *Shema*, with its affirmation of God's kingship and Unity, imbues the totality of one's being with these fundamental concepts.

    There is an apparent difficulty with the Rambam's statements. Halachah 5 states that one must interrupt Torah study in order to recite the *Shema*, and yet, in this halachah, such mundane matters as eating and haircuts are deemed sufficient reasons to postpone *Kri'at Shema*.  
    The *Kessef Mishneh* explains that the study of Torah is interrupted, since, indeed, the recitation of the *Shema* need not be understood as an interruption at all.
