## Chapter Seventeen

Arjuna then asked:

\[1\] “Those who cast the scriptures aside
but still sacrifice, filled with faith,
what would be their standing, Krishna?
*Sattvic*, *rajasic*, or *tamasic*?”

The Blessed Lord said:

\[2\] “The faith of embodied beings
is of three kinds, innate in them:
either *sattvic* or *rajasic*
or *tamasic*. Now hear of this.

\[3\] Faith aligns with the true nature
of every being, Arjuna.
A person is made of his faith.
Whichever faith he has, he is.

\[4\] *Sattvic* people worship the gods;
*rajasic*, spirits and demons;
the others, *tamasic*, worship
ghosts and hordes of nature spirits.

\[5\] Those who suffer austerities
not prescribed in the scriptures,
hypocritical, egoistic,
with desire, force, and passion,

\[6\] Mindlessly torturing the body’s
multitudinous elements,
and therefore Me in the body—
see their intent as demonic.

[p. 92] \[7\] Peoples’ preferences for food
are also triple, as are gifts,
sacrifices, and austerities.
Hear now how all these differ.

\[8\] Foods that promote longevity,
strength, health, virtue, satisfaction,
and are tasty, smooth, digestible—
these foods are prized by the *sattvic*.

\[9\] Foods that are bitter, sour, salty,
hot and spicy, dry and burning,
are desired by the *rajasic*.
They cause misery and sickness.

\[10\] Putrid, rotten, leftover food,
tasteless, foul, rejected garbage,
not suitable for sacrifice,
is preferred by the *tamasic*.

\[11\] Sacrifice observing scripture
without desire for its fruits,
the mind fixed only on the thought
‘this must be offered’—that is *sattvic*.

\[12\] But sacrifice that is offered
for the hypocritical purpose
of its fruit, such a sacrifice
understand to be *rajasic*.

\[13\] A sacrifice without scripture,
lacking mantras, no food offered,
the fee unpaid, devoid of faith,
is regarded as *tamasic*.

\[14\] Worshipping gods, the twice-born, sages,
teachers too; purity, virtue,
[p. 93] celibacy, and nonviolence
are called bodily austerities.

\[15\] Language that is not distressing
but truthful, pleasing, beneficial,
and practice reciting sacred texts,
these are vocal austerities.

\[16\] Mental calmness, benevolence,
keeping silence, self-restraint,
purity of being also,
these are mental austerities.

\[17\] These three austerities, practiced
with utmost faith by men who are
steadfast and not desiring fruits,
are considered to be *sattvic*.

\[18\] But if practiced insincerely
for honor, reverence, or respect,
austerity is called *rajasic*,
unsteady and impermanent.

\[19\] Austerity that is performed
with delusions of self-torture
or to destroy another
is declared to be *tamasic*.

\[20\] A gift with just the thought ‘giving,’
not repaying, to someone worthy
and at the proper time and place,
such a gift is seen as *sattvic*.

\[21\] A gift, however, that is given
as recompense, and grudgingly,
or with the aim of some profit,
is regarded as *rajasic*.

[p. 94] \[22\] A gift at the wrong place and time,
given to an unworthy person,
without respect or with contempt,
is declared to be *tamasic*.

\[23\] *Om tat sat*. This three-word mantra
signifies and summons *Brahman*.
This mantra of old created
*brahmins*, *Vedas*, and sacrifices.

\[24\] So when beginning sacrifice,
acts of giving, austerities,
expounders of *Brahman* utter ‘*Om*’
as prescribed in Vedic scriptures.

\[25\] Saying ‘*tat*’ without thought of fruit,
various acts of sacrifice,
of giving and austerity,
are done by those who seek release.

\[26\] ‘*Sat*’ is used in its meanings
of goodness and reality.
The word ‘*sat*’ is also used
for praiseworthy acts, Arjuna.

\[27\] Steadfastness in austerity,
in sacrifice and in giving
and in any related action,
is likewise referred to as ‘*sat*.’

\[28\] Oblations and austerities
that are performed with lack of faith
are called ‘*asat*,’ Son of Pritha,
and are void here and hereafter.”
