### 25.1–12: Paul and Festus

*[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-1|Text]], p. 273* ^25-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-1|1]]:** *Caesarea*, see 8.40n. ^25-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-3|3]]:** *Ambush*, Jewish leaders renew a previous plot against Paul; see 23.12–35. Unlike Roman authorities in Acts, Jewish leaders are not interested in justice, but in killing Paul. ^25-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-7|7]]:** *Which they could not prove*, confirmation that Paul violated no Jewish law. ^25-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-8|8]]:** Paul consistently denies that he has ever violated Torah, the sanctity of the Temple, or public order (22.1–21; 23.1,6; 24.10–21). ^25-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-9|9]]:** *Favor*, see 24.27. The “favor” is presumably aimed at keeping the populace quiet. ^25-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-10|10]]:** *Emperor’s tribunal*, Paul demands to be tried under Roman jurisdiction. ^25-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-11|11]]:** *Appeal to the emperor,* a judicial procedure permitted to Roman citizens (see 22.25; 23.27). ^25-11

### 25.13–26.32: Paul and Agrippa

*[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-13|Text]], p. 274* ^25-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-13|25.13]]:** *King Agrippa*, Marcus Julius Agrippa (Agrippa II), son of Agrippa I (see 12.1), who was appointed ruler of Syrian Chalcis, and later the Decapolis, Galilee, and Perea (53– ca. 93 CE). *Bernice*, Julia Berenice, daughter of Agrippa I, sister of Agrippa II and Drusilla (24.24). On several occasions she attempted to ameliorate the conditions that ultimately led to the First Revolt against Rome (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.310; 2.333; 2.402–7). She was rumored to have carried on romantic liaisons with Titus, the Roman general responsible for the destruction of the Temple and later emperor (Tacitus, *Hist.* 2.2). ^25-13-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-14|14–21]]:** Festus summarizes the events previously narrated in 24.27–25.12, but claims that the dispute centered on religious matters and Jesus (v 19; cf. 25.7–8) and thus omits mention of violations of Jewish or Roman law (24.5; 25.8–9). ^25-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-16|16]]:** *Custom of the Romans,* Roman legal procedures called for accusers to participate in the proceedings (see 23.35; Justinian I, *Digest* 48.17.1). Festus’s description shows himself and Romans in general as responsible officials. ^25-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-19|19]]:** *About their own religion*, presumably resurrection and related topics, see 23.6. *Died . . . alive,* according to Festus, Paul’s Jewish accusers had dropped all charges that Paul violated Jewish Law or Temple practices, and focused their attack on Paul’s belief in the resurrection of Jesus, thus confirming Paul’s previous understanding of the accusations against him (23.6; 24.21). ^25-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-21|21]]:** *Imperial Majesty*, Gk “sebastos,” title for the Roman emperor, equivalent to Latin “Augustus.” ^25-21

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-24|24]]:** *Whole*, Gk “plēthos” indicating a large crowd (2.6; 4.32; 5.14; 14.1; 17.4). ^25-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 25#^25-25|25]]:** *Nothing deserving death*, see 23.29. ^25-25
