### 10.1–42: Missionary instructions

*[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 29* ^10-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-1|1–4]]:** Twelve Disciples ([[Mark 06#^6-7|Mk 6.7]]; [[Mark 03#^3-13|3.13–19]]; [[Luke 06#^6-12|Lk 6.12–16]]; [[Luke 09#^9-1|9.1]]). ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Twelve*, number of the tribes of Israel (19.28; [[Torah 08 - Vayishlach#^35-22|Gen 35.22–26]]; Num 1). ^10-1-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Apostles*, from Gk “apostolos,” “one who is sent” (see Heb “shaliaḥ,” “messenger”). *Peter*, see 16.18. ^10-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-3|3]]:** *Matthew*, see 9.9. ^10-3

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-4|4]]:** *Cananaean*, likely from an Aramaic term (“Canai”; see *Avot de R. Natan* 6B) indicating Zealot affiliation. *Judas*, 26.14–16,20–5,47–56; 27.3–10. *Iscariot*, perhaps from Heb “ ʾish,” (“man of”) “Kerioth,” a town in southern Judea ([[Joshua 15#^15-25|Josh 15.25]]). ^10-4

> [!note] The Extent of the Mission (10.v5b–6; 15.24; 18.19; 28.19) *(p. 29)*
> In two passages in Matthew’s Gospel, Jesus restricts his teachings, and those of his disciples, to Jews: in the mission discourse, he states: “Go nowhere among the Gentiles . . . go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel” (10.5b–6) and regarding the Canaanite woman he states, “I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel” (15.24). Yet in 28.19, the resurrected Jesus extends his mission to “all nations” or “all the Gentiles” (Gk *panta ta ethne*). Some early Christian sources interpreted this final commission to refer to pagans only (*1 Clem.* 16.6, “We all, like sheep, went astray. . .”), which is a possible reading. However, the mission to Israel is never abrogated.
>
> Jesus himself appears to have restricted his mission to his fellow Jews; the few Gentiles who encounter him (the centurion of 8.5–13; the Canaanite woman of 15.24–28) are exceptions, and they come to him. Thus Matthew presents a Gospel in which Gentiles are already included in the genealogy, and their entry into the church, by their faith, is foreshadowed by the genealogy, the faithful soldier, and the faithful mother. Yet Matthew also shows fidelity to the Jewish tradition, and to Jesus’ own activities, by noting the first stage of the mission is to the people Israel. The same concern for the two-stage mission is expressed in Paul’s refrain, “To the Jew first and also to the Greek” ([[Romans 01#^1-16|Rom 1.16]]; [[Romans 02#^2-9|2.9]],[[Romans 02#^2-10|10]]; [[Romans 03#^3-12|3.12]]).

### 10.5–15: Missionary instructions

*[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-5|Text]], p. 30* ^10-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-5|5]]:** *Nowhere among the Gentiles*, Jesus restricts the mission to Jews; 28.19 expands it to Gentiles. *Samaritans*, Jews and Samaritans had mutual animosity (see [[Luke 09#^9-51|Lk 9.51–56]]; [[Luke 10#^10-29|10.29–37]]; [[John 04#^4-9|Jn 4.9]]; Josephus, *J.W*. 1.63; *Ant.* 11; 13.255; *T. Levi* 5–7). ^10-5-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-6|6]]:** *Lost sheep of the house of Israel*, see 15.24; either all Israel depicted as lost or the people as opposed to the leadership. ^10-6

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-7|7]]:** *Kingdom of heaven has come near*, see 3.2n. ^10-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-8|8]]:** *Raise the dead*, see *Lev. Rab.* 10.4, where Yehuda ha-Nasi (Rabbi Judah the Prince) is also said to be able to raise the dead. ^10-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-9|9]]:** *Take no gold, or silver, or copper*, God provides for those who study and/or teach the Torah (in *b. Bek*. [[Bekhorot 29#29a|29a]] the rabbis note that one should teach the Torah without expectation of payment; see also *Tanh. Ex.* 29). ^10-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-10|10]]:** *Laborers deserve their food*, missionaries should receive hospitality ([[1 Corinthians 09#^9-14|1 Cor 9.14]]; see *b. Shabb.* [[Shabbat 127#127a|127a]] on the importance of hospitality). ^10-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-15|15]]:** *Sodom and Gomorrah,* destroyed for lack of charity and hospitality according to [[Ezekiel 16#^16-49|Ezek 16.49]]; on Sodom and Gomorrah’s wickedness see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^13-13|Gen 13.13]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-20|18.20]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-24|19.24]],[[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-28|28]]. ^10-15

### 10.16–25: The disciples’ fate

*[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-16|Text]], p. 30* · ([[Mark 13#^13-9|Mk 13.9–13]],[[Mark 13#^13-22|22]]; [[Luke 12#^12-11|Lk 12.11–12]]; [[Luke 21#^21-12|21.12–19]]; [[John 16#^16-2|Jn 16.2]]; [[John 15#^15-18|15.18–20]]). ^10-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-16|16]]:** *Wise as serpents*, see *Song Rab.* 2.30 (God instructs Israel to be “cunning as serpents” when dealing with other nations). ^10-16-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-17|17]]:** *Flog*, see *m. Makk.* 3.12 (floggings were allowed, according to the rabbis, and carried out by a government official). ^10-17

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-18|18]]:** *Them*, the persecutors are Jews, given “their synagogues” ([[2 Corinthians 11#^11-24|2 Cor 11.24]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-6|1 Thess 1.6]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-1|2.1–2]],[[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|14–16]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-4|3.4]]). ^10-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-20|20]]:** *Spirit . . . through you*, the Spirit of God was seen as making it possible for those without prophetic gifts to speak out, as in [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^11-25|Num 11.25]]. ^10-20

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-21|21]]:** *Brother will betray brother*, see *m. Sot.* 9.15 on familial depravity and violence as a precursor to the messianic age; see 10.35; [[Micah 7#^7-6|Mic 7.6]]. ^10-21

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-23|23]]:** *Son of Man comes*, final judgment (see 8.20n.; 10.23; 19.28; 25.30–31; see also [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]). ^10-23

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-25|25]]:** *Beelzebul*, from Heb, “lord of lofty abode,” a Canaanite god, called pejoratively Beelzebub, “Lord of the flies,” in [[II Kings 01#^1-2|2 Kings 1.2–3]],[[II Kings 01#^1-6|6]],[[II Kings 01#^1-16|16]], and associated with the demonic in early Jewish and Christian tradition (*T. Sol.* 3.6; 6.1–2; Origen, *Cels*. 8.25). *Lebbaeus called Thaddaeus* b Or *is at hand* c The terms *leper* and *leprosy* can refer to several diseases ^10-25

### 10.26–42: Final instructions

*[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-26|Text]], p. 31* · ([[Mark 08#^8-34|Mk 8.34–35]]; [[Luke 12#^12-1|Lk 12.1–12]],[[Luke 12#^12-49|49–53]]). ^10-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-26|26]]:** *Have no fear of them*, likely reflecting the dedication of Jeremiah (1.8). ^10-26-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-28|28]]:** *Fear him*, see [[Hebrews 10#^10-31|Heb 10.31]]. *Hell,* lit, “Gehenna”; see 5.22n. ^10-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-29|29–31]]:** *Sparrows*, if God cares for seemingly insignificant animals, how much more so will God care for human beings (a “qal vahomer” argument; see 6.26n.; 10.30–32); *b. B. Metz.* [[Bava Metzia 85#85a|85a]] states that if a man is compassionate to animals, how much more so should he receive compassion (see. *y. Seb.* 9.1; *Gen. Rab*. 33.18; see also [[Luke 12#^12-6|Lk 12.6]]). ^10-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-33|33]]:** *Whoever denies me*, a warning to be faithful despite persecution. ^10-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-34|34–36]]:** [[Micah 7#^7-6|Mic 7.6]]; cf. [[Ezekiel 38#^38-21|Ezek 38.21]]. ^10-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-37|37–39]]:** 16.24–25. ^10-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-38|38]]:** *Take up the cross*, risk suffering and death. ^10-38

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-41|41]]:** *Righteous*, see 1.19n. ^10-41

**[[NRSVue Matthew 10#^10-42|42]]:** *Little ones*, possibly missionaries (25.45). ^10-42
