# Revelation 18 (JANT notes)

### 18.1–19.5: Lament over Babylon

*[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-1|Text]], p. 567* · This multivoiced dirge over the whore Babylon/Rome is inspired by biblical taunt-songs, wherein a kingdom’s past glories are rehearsed to sharpen the description of its downfall (see esp. Isa 14; 47; and Jer 30–31 on Babylon; see also Isa 23; Ezek 26–27 on Tyre). ^18-1

### 18.1–8: Hymn against Babylon

*[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-1|Text]], p. 567* ^18-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-2|2]]:** *Demons*, believed to inhabit desolate cities, [[Isaiah 13#^13-21|Isa 13.21–22]]; [[Isaiah 34#^34-14|34.14]]; [[Jeremiah 09#^9-10|Jer 9.11]]; 10.22; 51.37; [[Lamentations 5#^5-18|Lam 5.18]]. ^18-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-3|3]]:** *Wine . . . fornication*, see 14.8. ^18-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-4|4]]:** Echoing [[Jeremiah 51#^51-45|Jer 51.45]], heavenly figures encourage the people of God who dwell in Babylon/Rome (*my people*, presumably the Jews of Rome) to leave for their own safety. ^18-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-6|6]]:** *Render to her . . . repay her double*, see [[Isaiah 40#^40-2|Isa 40.2]]. ^18-6

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-7|7]]:** *Rule as a queen . . . I will never see grief*, this type of language attributed to Babylon/Rome comes from a biblical literary form in which a great political power trumpets its infallibility; thus its downfall seems the more ironic (see [[Isaiah 14#^14-12|Isa 14.12–14]]; [[Ezekiel 28#^28-2|Ezek 28.2]]). *every foul beast* and attach the words *and hateful* to the previous line so as to read *a haunt of every foul and hateful bird* b Other ancient authorities read *She has made all nations drink* c Or *resources* ^18-7

### 18.9–19: Mourning Babylon

*[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-9|Text]], p. 568* · Digressions to the spectacle of the whore’s burning body and the misery of those who loved her punctuate the dirge (also vv 11,15,17b–18a). ^18-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-10|10]]:** *Alas, alas*, see [[Zechariah 02#^2-10|Zech 2.10]]; see also vv 16,19 below. ^18-10

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-11|11–19]]:** The precious trade-goods and the attention to maritime merchants echoes Ezekiel’s dirge over Tyre (ch 27). ^18-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-16|16]]:** *Purple and scarlet*, see 17.3–4n. ^18-16

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-17|17]]:** *Shipmasters and seafarers*, see 17.15. ^18-17

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-19|19]]:** *Dust on their heads*, indication of mourning ([[Ezekiel 27#^27-30|Ezek 27.30]]; [[Job 02#^2-12|Job 2.12]]). ^18-19

### 18.20–24: Response to Babylon’s destruction

*[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-20|Text]], p. 568* ^18-20

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-21|21]]:** *Millstone* . . . , the angel’s action echoes the symbolic ways that biblical prophets demonstrated God’s plans, see Jer 27–28; 51.63–64. ^18-21

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-22|22]]:** [[Jeremiah 25#^25-10|Jer 25.10]]; [[Ezekiel 26#^26-13|Ezek 26.13]]; [[Lamentations 5#^5-14|Lam 5.14–15]]. ^18-22

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-23|23]]:** *Sorcery* (Gk “pharmakeia”), is not meant metaphorically; protective rituals were part of state ceremony in many ancient cultures (see [[Isaiah 47#^47-9|Isa 47.9]], where God overrides Babylon’s sorceries), while unauthorized sorcery was a crime in the Roman Empire (e.g., Julius Paulus, *Responsa* 5.23.14–19, third century ce); for John, sorcery constituted the sorts of impure practices that demonic forces introduced to the world through fornication (with which sorcery is often paired): *1 En*. 8–9; see [[II Kings 09#^9-22|2 Kings 9.22]]; [[Revelation 21#^21-8|Rev 21.8]]; [[Revelation 22#^22-15|22.15]]. Early rabbis forbade sorcery as antithetical to Torah but had difficulty defining it (e.g., *m. Sanh.* 7.11). ^18-23

**[[NRSVue Revelation 18#^18-24|24]]:** *Blood of prophets and of saints*, allusion to [[Jeremiah 51#^51-49|Jer 51.49]] but a contrast to [[Matthew 23#^23-34|Mt 23.34–36]] and [[Luke 11#^11-49|Lk 11.49–51]], which blame the murder of prophets on Israel (see 16.6n.). ^18-24
