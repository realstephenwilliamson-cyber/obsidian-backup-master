**1.** Moses, our teacher, ordained that the Jews should read the Torah publicly on the Sabbath[^1] and on Monday and Thursday[^2] mornings,[^3] so the [people] would never have three days pass without hearing the Torah.[^4]  
Ezra ordained that [the Torah] should be read during the *Minchah* service on the Sabbath, because of the shopkeepers.[^5] He also ordained that on Mondays and Thursdays, three[^6] people should read[^7] [from the Torah][^8], and that they should read no fewer than ten verses.[^9] ^12-1

**2.** These are the days when the Torah is read publicly: Sabbaths, festivals,[^10] *Rashei Chadashim*,[^11] fast days,[^12] Chanukah, Purim,[^13] and Mondays and Thursdays each week.[^14]  
The *haftarah*[^15] is read only[^16] on Sabbaths, festivals,[^17] and Tish'ah B'Av.[^18] ^12-2

**3.** The Torah is never read in public in the presence of fewer than ten adult free men.[^19] No fewer than ten verses are read.[^20] *Vayedaber*[^21] is counted as one of them.[^22] No fewer than three men should read.[^23]  
[When] beginning a passage[^24] from the Torah, [one should read] at least three verses,[^25] and one should not conclude less than three verses from the conclusion of a passage.[^26] Each reader should not read fewer than three verses.[^27] ^12-3

**4.** [When] three people read ten verses: Two read three [verses each] and one, four [verses]. It is praiseworthy regardless of whether the one who read four [verses] is first,[^28] last,[^29] or in the middle.[^30] ^12-4

**5.** Each one of the readers opens the Torah scroll and looks at the place from which he is to read.[^31] Afterwards, he declares,[^32] *Barchu et Ado-nai hamevorach*, and all the people[^33] answer: *Baruch Ado-nai hamevorach le'olam va'ed*.[^34] He then recites the blessing:  
Blessed[^35] are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has chosen us from among all the nations and given us His Torah. Blessed are You, God, the Giver of the Torah.[^36]  
All the people respond: "Amen."[^37] Afterwards, he reads until he completes the reading, rolls the scroll [closed][^38] and recites the blessing:  
Blessed are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who has given us His Torah, the Torah of truth,[^39] and implanted eternal life[^40] in our midst.[^41] Blessed are You, God, the Giver of the Torah. ^12-5

**6.** The person reading the Torah is not allowed to begin reading until the congregation ceases responding "Amen."[^42] If one erred while reading, even regarding the careful pronunciation of one letter,[^43] [the reader] is forced to repeat [the reading] until he reads it correctly.[^44]  
Two people should not read at the same time. Rather, one should read alone.[^45] If one was reading and lost the ability to speak, another should replace him. He should begin from the place where the one who lost the ability to speak began,[^46] and recite the blessing after concluding.[^47] ^12-6

**7.** The reader is not permitted to [begin] reading until the person of greatest stature within the community[^48] tells him to [begin] reading.[^49] Even the *chazan* or the *gabbai*[^50] should not begin reading on their own initiative until the community [as a whole] or the person of greatest stature begins to read.[^51]  
[When the *chazan* reads from the Torah,][^52] another person should stand with him while he reads, just as the *chazan* stands together with the other readers.[^53] ^12-7

**8.** The reader may skip from place[^54] to place[^55] in one subject[^56] - for example,[^57] from *Acharei mot...*[^58] to *Ach be'asor*, in the portion *Emor el Hacohanim*[^59] - provided he does not read by heart. It is forbidden [for a reader] to say even one word [without looking at the text].[^60] [When] skipping [in this fashion, the reader] should not wait longer than it takes for the translator[^61] to translate one verse.[^62] ^12-8

**9.** Once the reader begins reading the Torah, it is forbidden [for the congregants] to talk,[^63] even regarding matters of Torah law.[^64] Rather, everyone should listen, remain silent, and pay attention to what is being read, as [Nechemiah 8:3] states: "The ears of all the people were [attentive] to the Torah scroll."  
It[^65] is forbidden to leave the synagogue while the reader is reading from the Torah.[^66] However, one is permitted to leave between *aliyot*.[^67] A person who is constantly involved in Torah study, and Torah is his occupation,[^68] is permitted to involve himself in Torah study while the Torah is being read.[^69] ^12-9

**10.** From the time of Ezra,[^70] it was customary that a translator would translate to the people the [passages] read by the reader from the Torah, so that they would understand the subject matter.[^71]  
The reader should read one verse alone and remain silent while the translator translates it.[^72] Afterwards, he should read a second verse. The reader is not permitted to read to the translator more than one verse [at a time].[^73] ^12-10

**11.** The reader is not permitted to raise his voice above that of the translator, nor should the translator raise his voice above that of the reader. The translator is not permitted to [begin] translating until the reader completes reading the verse, nor may the reader [begin] reading another verse until the translator has completed the translation.  
The translator should not lean on a beam or on a pillar. Rather, he should stand with awe and fear. He should not translate from a written text, but rather should recite the translation by heart.  
The reader is not permitted to assist the translator, lest people say: "The translation is written in the Torah scroll." A person of lesser stature may serve as a translator for a person of greater stature. However, it is not befitting the honor of a person of greater stature to serve as a translator for a person of lesser stature. Two people should not serve as translators simultaneously; rather, one person should read and one should translate. ^12-11

**12.** Not all passages from the Torah are translated in public. All [of the following passages] should be read, but not translated: the incident involving Reuven, the priestly benediction, [the passage describing the sin] of the golden calf from "And Moses told Aharon" ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-21|Exodus 32:21]] until "And Moses saw the people" ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-25|Exodus 32:25]] and one other verse, "And God set a plague upon the people" ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^32-35|Exodus 32:35]] .  
In the [description of] the incident concerning Amnon (II Samuel, Chapter 13), the verse which states, "Amnon, the son of David" (13:1) should be neither read nor translated. ^12-12

**13.** The person who reads the *haftarah*[^74] must read from the Torah first.[^75] Even three verses [are sufficient].[^76] He should read again the passage that had been read previously.[^77]  
He should not [begin] reading the *haftarah* until the Torah scroll has been rolled closed. He should not read fewer than twenty-one verses [as the *haftarah*].[^78] However, if a concept is completed in fewer [verses] than that, he need not add more.[^79] If he read only ten verses, but the *haftarah* is translated, it is sufficient even if the concept is not completed.[^80]  
[When reading] from the prophets, one reads and even two may translate.[^81] One may skip from one concept to another.[^82] However, one should not skip from one prophet to another,[^83] except among the twelve prophets.[^84] Furthermore, [even within a book from a single prophet,] one should not skip from the conclusion of the book until its beginning.[^85] Whenever one skips, one should not wait longer than it takes the translator to complete his translation.[^86] ^12-13

**14.** A person reading from the prophets may read three verses to the translator at one time,[^87] and the translator translates them one after another. If the three verses are three separate passages,[^88] [the reader] should read them to the translator only one at a time.[^89] ^12-14

**15.** The person who reads the *haftarah* recites one blessing before [beginning his reading]: Blessed are You, God, our Lord, King of the universe, who chose prophets....[^90]  
After [completing the reading], he recites four blessings.[^91] He concludes the first blessing:[^92] "the God who is faithful in all of His words."[^93] He concludes the second blessing: "who builds Jerusalem."[^94] He concludes the third blessing: "the Shield of David."[^95] He concludes the fourth blessing[^96] with the mention of the sacred aspect of the day, as he does in the[^97] *Shemoneh Esreh*.[^98] Similarly, if Rosh Chodesh falls on the Sabbath, the one who reads the *haftarah* mentions Rosh Chodesh in[^99] this blessing, as he does in the[^100] *Shemoneh Esreh*.[^101] ^12-15

**16.** How many readers [are called to the Torah]? On Sabbath morning, seven; on Yom Kippur, six; on festivals, five.[^102] This number may not be reduced.[^103] However, it may be increased.[^104]  
On *Rashei Chadashim* and on *Chol Hamo'ed*, four people [are called to read [from the Torah].[^105] On the Sabbath and Yom Kippur during the *Minchah* service, on Mondays and Thursdays throughout the entire year, on Chanukah and Purim in the morning service, and on fast days in the morning and *Minchah* services[^106] three people [are called to] read [from the Torah]. This number[^107] may not be reduced,[^108] nor may it may be increased.[^109] ^12-16

**17.** A woman should not read the Torah publicly, as a token of respect for the community.[^110] A minor[^111] who knows how to read[^112] and is aware of the One who is being blessed[^113] may be counted as one of the required number [of people[^114] called to the Torah].  
Similarly, the one who recites the *haftarah* is counted as one of the required number [of people called to the Torah], because he also reads from the Torah. [However,] if the leader of the congregation interrupted [by reciting] *Kaddish* between the conclusion of the Torah reading and the reading of the person who recites the *haftarah*,[^115] [the latter] is not included as one of the required number [of people called to the Torah][^116].  
If there is only one person in the community who knows how to read [from the Torah], he should be called to the Torah,[^117] read, descend[^118] [from the platform],[^119] return and read again a second and a third time until he completes the number of *aliyot* designated for that day.[^120] ^12-17

**18.** In all of these [Torah] readings, a priest reads first;[^121] after him, a Levite; and after him, an Israelite.[^122] It is common custom at present[^123] that even a priest who is a common person is given precedence and allowed to read before a wise man of great stature in Israel.[^124]  
Whoever is greater than his colleague in wisdom is given precedence regarding the reading [of the Torah].[^125] The last person who rolls the Torah scroll closed receives a reward equivalent to that of all the others.[^126] Therefore, even the person of the greatest stature in the community can receive the concluding *aliyah*.[^127] ^12-18

**19.** When there are no priests present,[^128] an Israelite is called to the Torah[^129] and a Levite should not be called after him at all.[^130]  
When there are no Levites present, the priest who received the first *aliyah* returns and reads [from the Torah] a second time in place of the Levite. Another priest should not read [from the Torah] after him, lest others say that there is a blemish in the first's lineage,[^131] and, therefore, another priest was given the *aliyah*.[^132]  
Similarly, one Levite should not read [from the Torah] after another Levite, lest others say that there is a blemish in the lineage of one of them. ^12-19

**20.** What is the order [of the service] when the Torah is read after prayer?[^133] On a day when there is a *Musaf* service,[^134] after the leader of the congregation completes the morning service, he recites *Kaddish*[^135] and takes out the Torah scroll.[^136] He calls the members of the community, one by one, and they ascend[^137] and read from the Torah. When they have completed the reading, he returns the Torah scroll to its place,[^138] recites *Kaddish*,[^139] and then the [congregation] recites the *Musaf* service.[^140]  
On days when the *haftarah* is read and there is a *Musaf* service,[^141] it is customary to recite *Kaddish* before the person who reads the *haftarah* ascends [for his *aliyah*].[^142] There are places where it is customary to recite *Kaddish* after the person who reads the *haftarah* [completes his Torah reading].[^143] ^12-20

**21.** During the *Minchah* service on the Sabbath and on Yom Kippur, after the leader of the congregation completes *Tehillah l'David* and the order of *Kedushah*,[^144] he recites the *Kaddish*,[^145] and takes out a Torah scroll.[^146] [Those called to the Torah] ascend and read and then, [the Torah scroll] is returned [to its place.[^147] The leader of the congregation] recites *Kaddish*,[^148] and the [congregation] recites the *Minchah* service.[^149]  
Similarly, on a fast day,[^150] the Torah is read [before] the *Minchah* service.[^151] Afterwards, *Kaddish* is recited,[^152] and the *Minchah* service is recited. On festivals, it is not customary to read [the Torah] in the *Minchah* service.[^153] ^12-21

**22.** [When the Torah is read on] a day when *Musaf* is not recited,[^154] after the morning *Shemoneh Esreh* is completed,[^155] [the leader of the congregation] recites the *Kaddish*[^156] and takes out a Torah scroll. [After the portion is] read from it, [the Torah scroll] is returned [to its place.[^157] The leader of the congregation] recites *Tehillah l'David* and the order of *Kedushah*,[^158] as is the practice every day.[^159] [Afterwards,] he recites the *Kaddish*[^160] and the people depart. ^12-22

**23.** It is not proper to read from *chumashim* in synagogues, as a token of respect for the community.[^161]  
A Torah scroll should not be rolled [from one portion to another portion] in the presence of the community, because of the difficulty it would cause the people, forcing them to remain standing while the Torah scroll is being rolled.[^162] Therefore, if it is necessary to read two separate concepts, two Torah scrolls are taken out.[^163] [However,] one person should not read one concept from two Torah scrolls,[^164] lest people say that the first scroll was invalid and, therefore, they read from the second.[^165] ^12-23

**24.** When a person rolls a Torah scroll [closed],[^166] he should roll it from the outside.[^167] When he ties it, he should tie it from the inside.[^168] He should leave the stitching[^169] [in the center], so that it will not rip.[^170]  
In a place where a Torah scroll is taken [from the synagogue] to another room where it is kept, the congregation[^171] is not allowed to leave until the Torah scroll is taken.[^172] They should accompany it, following it to the place where it is kept.[^173] ^12-24
