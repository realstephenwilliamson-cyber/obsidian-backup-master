# Job 40 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Job 39|← Job 39]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Job 41|Job 41 →]]

**40:1** And the LORD said to Job: ^40-1

**40:2** “Shall a faultfinder contend with the Almighty?^[Traditional rendering of Heb *Shaddai*] Anyone who argues with God must respond.” ^40-2

## Job’s response to God

**40:3** Then Job answered the LORD: ^40-3

**40:4** “See, I am of small account; what shall I answer you? I lay my hand on my mouth. ^40-4

**40:5** I have spoken once, and I will not answer, twice but will proceed no further.” ^40-5

## God’s challenge to Job

**40:6** Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind: ^40-6

**40:7** “Gird up your loins like a man; I will question you, and you declare to me. ^40-7

**40:8** Will you even put me in the wrong? Will you condemn me that you may be justified? ^40-8

**40:9** Have you an arm like God, and can you thunder with a voice like his? ^40-9

**40:10** “Deck yourself with majesty and dignity; clothe yourself with glory and splendor. ^40-10

**40:11** Pour out the overflowings of your anger, and look on all who are proud and humble them. ^40-11

**40:12** Look on all who are proud and bring them low; tread down the wicked where they stand. ^40-12

**40:13** Hide them all in the dust together; bind their faces in the world below.^[Heb *the hidden place*] ^40-13

**40:14** Then I will also acknowledge to you that your own right hand can give you victory. ^40-14

**40:15** “Look at Behemoth, which I made just as I made you; it eats grass like an ox. ^40-15

**40:16** Its strength is in its loins and its power in the muscles of its belly. ^40-16

**40:17** It makes its tail stiff like a cedar; the sinews of its thighs are knit together. ^40-17

**40:18** Its bones are tubes of bronze, its limbs like bars of iron. ^40-18

**40:19** “It is the first of the great acts of God; only its Maker can approach it with the sword. ^40-19

**40:20** For the mountains yield food for it where all the wild animals play. ^40-20

**40:21** Under the lotus plants it lies, in the covert of the reeds and in the marsh. ^40-21

**40:22** The lotus trees cover it for shade; the willows of the wadi surround it. ^40-22

**40:23** Even if the river is turbulent, it is not frightened; it is confident though Jordan rushes against its mouth. ^40-23

**40:24** Can one take it with hooks^[Cn: Heb *in his eyes*] or pierce its nose with a snare? ^40-24
