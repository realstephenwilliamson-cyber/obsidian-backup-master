## Mūla

अत्रापि वेद्यता नाम तादात्म्यं वेदकैः सह ।
ततः सकलवेद्योऽसौ घटः सकल एव हि ॥ ^mula

atrāpi vedyatā nāma tādātmyaṃ vedakaiḥ saha |
tataḥ sakalavedyo'sau ghaṭaḥ sakala eva hi || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 12 - Chapter Ten, part 3#^pg414|p. 395]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=414>))
>
> Here (in this case) also, perceptibility is one with (the corresponding) perceivers. Thus, the jar that is perceptible to a Conditioned (perceiver) (sakalavedya) is itself conditioned (sakala). To the degree in which that (object) is perceptible to Śiva alone, it is just Śiva who manifests. To the same degree (as in all these cases), consciousness manifests as possessing one body (which is all things). (with 10.165–166)
