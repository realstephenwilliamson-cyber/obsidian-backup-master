## Chapter 23

**23:1** The “Tyre” Pronouncement.  
Howl, you ships of Tarshish![^1]  
For havoc has been wrought, not a house is left;  
As they came from the land of Kittim,  
This was revealed to them. ^23-1

**23:2** Moan, you coastland dwellers,  
You traders of Sidon,  
Once thronged by seafarers, ^23-2

**23:3** Over many waters  
Your[^2] revenue came:  
From the trade of nations,  
From the grain of Shihor,  
The harvest of the Nile. ^23-3

**23:4** Be ashamed, O Sidon!  
For the sea—this stronghold of the sea—declares,  
“I am as one who has[^3] never labored,  
Never given birth,  
Never raised youths  
Or reared maidens!” ^23-4

**23:5** When the Egyptians heard it, they quailed  
As when they heard about Tyre. ^23-5

**23:6** Pass on to Tarshish—  
Howl, you coastland dwellers! ^23-6

**23:7** Was such your merry city  
In former times, of yore?  
Did her feet carry her off  
To sojourn far away? ^23-7

**23:8** Who was it that planned this  
For crown-wearing Tyre,  
Whose merchants were nobles,  
Whose traders the world honored? ^23-8

**23:9** The LORD of Hosts planned it—  
To defile all glorious beauty,  
To shame all the honored of the world. ^23-9

**23:10** Traverse your land like the Nile,  
Fair Tarshish;[^4]  
This is a harbor[^5] no more. ^23-10

**23:11** The LORD poised His arm o’er the sea  
And made kingdoms quake;  
It was He decreed destruction  
For Phoenicia’s[^6] strongholds, ^23-11

**23:12** And said,  
“You shall be gay no more,  
O plundered one, Fair Maiden Sidon.  
Up, cross over to Kittim—  
Even there you shall have no rest.” ^23-12

**23:13** [^7]Behold the land of Chaldea—  
This is the people that has ceased to be.  
Assyria, which founded it for ships,  
Which raised its watchtowers,  
Erected its ramparts,  
Has turned it into a ruin. ^23-13

**23:14** Howl, O ships of Tarshish,  
For your stronghold is destroyed! ^23-14

**23:15** In that day, Tyre shall remain forgotten for seventy years, equaling the lifetime of one king. After a lapse of seventy years, it shall go with Tyre as with the harlot in the ditty: ^23-15

**23:16** Take a lyre, go about the town,  
Harlot long forgotten;  
Sweetly play, make much music,  
To bring you back to mind. ^23-16

**23:17** For after a lapse of seventy years, the LORD will take note of Tyre, and she shall resume her “fee-taking” and “play the harlot”[^8] with all the kingdoms of the world, on the face of the earth. ^23-17

**23:18** But her profits and “hire” shall be consecrated to the LORD. They shall not be treasured or stored; rather shall her profits go to those who abide before the LORD, that they may eat their fill and clothe themselves elegantly. ^23-18

[^1]: See note at 2.16.
[^2]: Heb. “her.”
[^3]: Lit. “I have.”
[^4]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain. Emendation yields “Pass on to the land of Kittim, / You ships of Tarshish.”
[^5]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; taking mezaḥ as a by-form of maḥoz: cf. Ps. 107.30.
[^6]: Heb. “Canaan’s.”
[^7]: Meaning of verse uncertain. Emendation yields “The land of Kittim itself—/ Which the Sidonian people founded, / Whose watchtowers they raised, / Whose citadels they erected—/ Exists no more; / Assyria has turned it into a ruin.”
[^8]: I.e., “trading…trade.”
