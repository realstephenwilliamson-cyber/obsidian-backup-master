### 2.1–10: Household rules

*[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 452* · Household codes (“Haustafeln”) appear also in [[Ephesians 05#^5-21|Eph 5.21–6.9]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-18|Col 3.18–4.1]]; and [[1 Peter 02#^2-18|1 Pet 2.18–3.7]]; in Titus the codes are organized differently, lacking the submission of children and the reciprocal duties of the male householder. The Greek form (Aristotle, *Pol*. 1.2.1–2; *Eth. nic.* 8.10.4–6) was adopted by Jewish writers (Philo, *Hypoth*. 7.14; Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.23–29). ^2-1

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Sound* (lit., “healthy”) *doctrine*, a particular concern of the Pastorals (1.9; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-10|1 Tim 1.10]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-3|2 Tim 4.3]]). ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-2|2]]:** Male leaders are enjoined similar virtues in [[1 Timothy 03#^3-2|1 Tim 3.2]],[[1 Timothy 03#^3-8|8]]. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-3|3]]:** Women are enjoined similar virtues in [[1 Timothy 03#^3-11|1 Tim 3.11]]. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-4|4–5]]:** Although wives had more legal rights than slaves, both submit to the male house-holder in a properly ordered home. Silence is coupled with submission in [[1 Timothy 02#^2-11|1 Tim 2.11–15]]. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-5|5–6]]:** *Self-controlled*, one of four cardinal virtues advocated by both Jewish and Gentile writers (Aristotle, *Pol*. 7.1.2; Plato, *Leg*. 1.630–31; 12.963–65; Philo, *Leg. all*. 1.63–65, lists wisdom \[Gk “phronesis”\], self-control \[Gk “sophrosyne”\], courage \[Gk “andreia,” lit., “manliness”\], and justice/righteousness \[Gk “dikaiosyne”\], which he associates with the four rivers in the Garden of Eden \[[[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-10|Gen 2.10–14]]\]). ^2-5

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Good works*, those consistent with the author’s values (see 3.1; [[1 Timothy 02#^2-10|1 Tim 2.10]]). ^2-7

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-9|9]]:** See [[1 Timothy 06#^6-1|1 Tim 6.1–2]]. *Slaves*, integral to Hellenistic households, perhaps constituting a majority in some urban areas. Jewish or Gentile writers rarely question the institution, although Phi- lo mentions a few Jewish communities that rejected slavery, including the Essenes (*Good Person* 79). Christian masters forcibly baptized some slaves as part of the household; others joined voluntarily. ^2-9

### 2.11–3.8a: Salvation and exhortations

*[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-11|Text]], p. 453* ^2-11

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Grace* (Gk “charis”), lit., “favor, beneficence.” *Grace of God* never appears in LXX, though people frequently “find grace” before God (see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-8|Gen 6.8]]), meaning that God regards them with favor. *Salvation*, see 1.14n. and 3.5n. ^2-11-2

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-12|12]]:** Exhortations reflecting Hellenistic moral philosophy, specifically Stoic concerns with self-control; see 2.5–6n. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Great God and Savior*, language related to Roman emperor cult here describes Jesus as God (see [[John 01#^1-14|Jn 1.14]]; [[John 20#^20-28|20.28]]; [[Hebrews 01#^1-8|Heb 1.8]]). ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Titus 02#^2-14|14]]:** Perhaps quoting an early hymn to Jesus. *Redeem*, from sin, as slaves are manumitted (see [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^6-6|Ex 6.6]]; [[Psalms 130#^130-8|Ps 130.8]] \[129.8 LXX\]). *People of his own*, lit., “chosen people” (Gk “laon periousion”), only here in the NT but five times in the LXX ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-5|Ex 19.5]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-22|23.22]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-6|Deut 7.6]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-2|14.2]]; [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-18|26.18]]; see also [[Ezekiel 37#^37-23|Ezek 37.23]]) to refer to Israel’s election. The (predominantly Gentile) church sees itself in continuity with Israel. ^2-14
