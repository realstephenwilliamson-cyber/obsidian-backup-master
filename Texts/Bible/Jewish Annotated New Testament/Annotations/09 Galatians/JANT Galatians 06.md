### 6.1–10: Concluding exhortations

*[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 385* ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Anyone*, lit., “a person,” here a fellow believer in Jesus. ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-2|2]]:** Paul cites Jesus’ teachings ([[1 Corinthians 07#^7-10|1 Cor 7.10]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-14|9.14]]; [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|11.23]]; perhaps [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-15|1 Thess 4.15]]**–**17), which may constitute *the law of Christ*. Perhaps Paul means the life animated by Christ’s spirit, see [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-21|1 Cor 9.21]]; [[Romans 08#^8-2|Rom 8.2]]. ^6-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-6|6]]:** *Word,* presumably the word of God, either scripture or the lessons derived from scripture. A *teacher* ([[1 Corinthians 12#^12-28|1 Cor 12.28]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-11|Eph 4.11]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-17|1 Tim 5.17]]) is entitled to compensation from students ([[1 Corinthians 09#^9-4|1 Cor 9.4]]**–**14; [[Philippians 04#^4-14|Phil 4.14]]**–**19; see also [[Matthew 10#^10-9|Mt 10.9]]**–**11). According to the Mishnah, a teacher is to receive no compensation for teaching a fellow Jew rabbinic law and lore, but may receive compensation for teaching scripture (*m. Ned.* 4.3). ^6-6

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-7|7]]:** *You reap whatever you sow*, proverbial expression found in classical literature (e.g., Cicero, *De Or.* 2.65) as well as [[Hosea 08#^8-7|Hos 8.7]]; [[Hosea 10#^10-12|10.12]]; [[Proverbs 22#^22-8|Prov 22.8]]; Job 4.8. See also [[2 Corinthians 09#^9-6|2 Cor 9.6]]. ^6-7

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-8|8]]:** *Eternal life*, from [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]. See [[Romans 02#^2-7|Rom 2.7]]; [[Romans 05#^5-21|5.21]]; [[Romans 06#^6-22|6.22–23]]; [[Romans 08#^8-5|8.5–8]]. ^6-8

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-10|10]]:** *Those of the family* (lit., “household”), Paul expresses the unity of believers through various images: temple of God ([[1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|1 Cor 3.16–17]]; [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-16|2 Cor 6.16]]); house (or building) of God ([[1 Corinthians 03#^3-9|1 Cor 3.9]]); body of Christ ([[1 Corinthians 12#^12-12|1 Cor 12.12–27]]). Here Paul conceives of the community as a “household of faith”*;* see [[Ephesians 02#^2-19|Eph 2.19]]; [[1 Timothy 03#^3-15|1 Tim 3.15]]; [[1 Peter 04#^4-17|1 Pet 4.17]]. Hence all followers of Jesus are siblings, see 1.2n. ^6-10

### 6.11–18: Postscript

*[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-11|Text]], p. 386* ^6-11

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-11|11]]:** Paul dictated this letter to a secretary, but adds the postscript in his *own hand*, see [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-21|1 Cor 16.21]]; [[Colossians 04#^4-18|Col 4.18]]; [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-17|2 Thess 3.17]]; Philem 19. ^6-11-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-12|12]]:** *Persecuted* by fellow Jews *for the cross*, Jews who do not believe in Christ are persecuting (what exactly this means is unclear) Jews who do believe in Christ, presumably because the former suspect the latter of rejecting the Torah and effacing the boundary between Jews and Gentiles. Hence to avoid persecution, Jewish followers of Jesus demonstrate their loyalty to the Torah by demanding circumcision of the Galatian Gentiles who believe in Christ. See 5.11n. It has also been suggested that the Jews are wary of the attempts by Jewish believers in Christ to attract Gentiles to their movement, thereby inducing them to abandon the pagan gods. This, the Jews feared, would lead the local Gentiles to suspect the Jewish community of disloyalty. (See “Paul and Judaism,” p. 633). ^6-12

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Even the circumcised*, lit., “even those who are being circumcised,” Galatians who follow Paul’s opponents. The variant, “even those who have been circumcised,” may refer to Jewish followers of Jesus. *Do not themselves obey the law*, if referring to circumcised Galatians, the verse implies that they did not, and apparently were not expected to, observe the entire Torah (see 5.3). If to Jewish believers in Christ, perhaps Paul is alluding to Peter’s alleged failure to observe the Law (2.14) or something similar. Paul re-prises his argument that those subordinate to the Law must observe the entire Law, which is impossible (3.10–14; 5.3). This argument will not convince rabbinic Jews who, knowing that “there is no one who does not sin” ([[I Kings 08#^8-46|1 Kings 8.46]]), magnify the importance of repentance: “great is repentance for it brings about redemption . . . great is repentance, for on account of an individual who repents, the sins of all the world are forgiven” (*b. Yoma* [[Yoma 86#86a|86a]]**-**b). Paul ignores the power of repentance. ^6-13

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-15|15]]:** See 5.6; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-19|1 Cor 7.19]]. *New creation*, [[Isaiah 65#^65-17|Isa 65.17]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-22|66.22]]; [[2 Corinthians 05#^5-17|2 Cor 5.17]]. ^6-15

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-16|16]]:** *This rule*, the rule or principle of 6.15, that circumcision does not matter; only new creation in Christ matters. *Peace be upon them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God*, Paul adapts the traditional salutation “peace be upon Israel” ([[Psalms 125#^125-5|Ps 125.5]]; [[Psalms 128#^128-6|128.6]]). In this translation Paul invokes a double blessing (see 1.3n.) upon two groups, those who live according to the rule of 6.15, and the Israel of God. The meaning of *Israel of God* is disputed. This is the first time Galatians uses *Israel,* and the only time anywhere that Paul qualifies Israel with *of God*, a locution never found in the Tanakh*.* Some commentators treat “Israel” and “Israel of God” as synonymous expressions for “Jews.” It is more likely, however, that “Israel of God” should be understood in the light of what Paul says elsewhere, that “not all Israelites truly belong to Israel” ([[Romans 09#^9-6|Rom 9.6]]) and that “Israel according to the flesh” ([[1 Corinthians 10#^10-18|1 Cor 10.18]]) is apparently not the same as Israel according to the spirit. The *Israel of God* is the new Israel which consists of Jewish and Gentile believers in Christ, what medieval Christians would call “the true Israel.” Presumably Paul’s opponents argued that if the Galatians wish to be part of God’s chosen, the people Israel, they need to be circumcised and observe the Torah. Paul argues that the old distinction between circumcision and foreskin, between ethnic Israel and ethnic Gentile, no longer matters (3.28), because the true Israel, the Israel of God, consists of all those who are a new creation in Christ (see [[Romans 02#^2-29|Rom 2.29]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-3|Phil 3.3]]). ^6-16

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-17|17]]:** *Marks of Jesus branded on my body*, lit., “For I carry the scars (Gk “stigmata”) of Jesus on my body” (“branded” is not in the Greek). “Stigmata” can indicate branding (as of a slave) or tattooing (as of a religious devotee). Paul’s scars are the result of his beatings ([[2 Corinthians 06#^6-4|2 Cor 6.4–6]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|11.23–27]]) which he understands to mirror Jesus’ sufferings ([[2 Corinthians 04#^4-7|2 Cor 4.7–10]]). Alternatively, the scars may be metaphorical (see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-32|1 Cor 15.32]], whose reference to “fighting beasts” might be literal or metaphorical). ^6-17

**[[NRSVue Galatians 06#^6-18|18]]:** This is the only Pauline letter in whose closing benediction Paul refers to his addressees as *brothers*; Paul wishes to end on an irenic note. ^6-18
