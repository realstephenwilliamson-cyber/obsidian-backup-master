## Mūla

क्वचित्संदर्शितं ब्रह्महत्याविधिनिषेधवत् ।
भक्ष्यादिविधयोऽप्येनं न्यायमाश्रित्य चर्चिताः ॥ ^mula

kvacitsaṃdarśitaṃ brahmahatyāvidhiniṣedhavat |
bhakṣyādividhayo'pyenaṃ nyāyamāśritya carcitāḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 10 - Chapter Four, part 9#^pg356|p. 343]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=356>))
>
> Thus (Vedic) injunctions are seen in certain circumstances to be contradicted even by their own statements (in the sense that generally applicable norms are subject in certain cases to exceptions). Such is the case with the prohibitions and prescriptions regarding the killing of a Brahmin. (with 4.246cd–247ab)
>
> The prescriptions regarding what may be eaten etc. have also been considered in the light of this same principle. (with 4.247cd)
