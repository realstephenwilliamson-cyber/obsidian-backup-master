**1.** It is a positive commandment of the Torah to eat matzah on the night of the fifteenth [of Nisan], as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-18|Exodus 12:18]]] states: "In the evening, you shall eat matzot." This applies in every place and at every time. Eating [matzah] is not dependent on the Paschal sacrifice. Rather, it is a mitzvah in its own right. The mitzvah may be fulfilled throughout the entire night.  
Throughout the other days of the festival, eating matzah is left to one's choice: If one desires, one may eat matzah. If one desires, one may eat rice, millet, roasted seeds, or fruit. Nevertheless, on the night of the fifteenth alone, [eating matzah] is an obligation. Once one eats the size of an olive, he has fulfilled his obligation. ^6-1

**2.** A person who swallows matzah[^1] [without chewing it] fulfills his obligation.[^2] A person who swallows maror[^3] [without chewing it] does not fulfill his obligation.[^4] A person who swallows matzah and maror together fulfills the obligation of matzah,[^5] but not that of maror, for the maror is secondary to the matzah.[^6] If he wrapped them in fibers or the like and swallowed them,[^7] he does not even fulfill the obligation of matzah.[^8] ^6-2

**3.** A person who eats matzah without the intention [to fulfill the mitzvah][^9] - e.g., gentiles or thieves force him to eat[^10] - fulfills his obligation.[^11]  
A person who ate a כזית matzah in delirium, while possessed by an epileptic fit, and afterwards recovered,[^12] is obligated to eat another [כזית].[^13] The consumption of [the first כזית] took place while he was free from the obligation to perform any mitzvot.[^14] ^6-3

**4.** A person does not fulfill the obligation of eating matzah unless he partakes [of matzah made] from one of the five species[^15] [of grain],[^16] as [Deuteronomy[^17] 16:3] states: "Do not eat chametz upon it... eat matzot for seven days."  
[From the verse's association of chametz and matzah, we may derive:] substances which can become leavened[^18] may be eaten as matzah to fulfill one's obligation.[^19] In contrast, other substances[^20] - e.g., rice, millet, and *kitniyot* - cannot be used to fulfill the obligation of matzah, for they can never become leavened. ^6-4

**5.** A person who makes dough from wheat and rice:[^21] if it has the taste of grain,[^22] one may fulfill his obligation with it.[^23] Dough made as food for dogs:[^24] if the shepherds also eat from it,[^25] one may fulfill his obligation with it. If the shepherds do not eat from it,[^26] one cannot fulfill his obligation[^27] by eating it, for it is not watched for the sake of eating matzah.[^28]  
Matzah that was kneaded with fruit juice,[^29] one may fulfill one's obligation with it on Pesach.[^30] However, [the dough] should not be kneaded with wine, oil, honey, or milk, because of the requirement for poor man's bread, as explained above.[^31] A person who kneaded [dough with one of these liquids] does not fulfill his obligation.[^32]  
One cannot fulfill his obligation with matzah made from thin bran or[^33] coarse[^34] bran. However, one may knead flour together with its bran and make it into a loaf and fulfill one's obligation with it.[^35] Similarly, a loaf made with very fine flour[^36] is permitted, and a person may fulfill his obligation with it. We do not say: this is not poor man's bread. ^6-5

**6.** [One may fulfill his obligation] with matzah baked in either an oven[^37] or a roasting pot.[^38] This applies whether the dough was stuck to the roasting pot and then the [pot] was heated,[^39] or whether the [pot] was heated and then the [dough] stuck to it.[^40] Even if the dough was baked in the ground, one may fulfill his obligation with it.[^41]  
Similarly, even if the matzah was not thoroughly baked,[^42] one may fulfill his obligation with it, provided strands of dough will not extend from it when broken.[^43] A person[^44] may fulfill his obligation with a cake [of matzah][^45] soaked[^46] [in other substances],[^47] so long as it has not dissolved.[^48] However, a person cannot fulfill his obligation with matzah that has been cooked,[^49] for it does not have the taste[^50] of bread. ^6-6

**7.** A person cannot fulfill his obligation by eating matzah which is forbidden to him; for example, a person who ate [matzah made from] *tevel*,[^51] [matzah made from] the first tithe from which *terumat [ma'aser]* had not been separated,[^52] or [matzah] that was stolen.[^53]  
This is the governing principle: All [matzah] upon which the grace after meals is recited[^54] may be used to fulfill one's obligation.[^55] If the grace after meals may not be recited upon it, it may not be used to fulfill one's obligation.[^56] ^6-7

**8.** The priests[^57] may fulfill their obligation with [matzot made from] Challah[^58] or *terumah*[^59] even though it is matzah which is not suited to be eaten by all people.[^60] Similarly, a person may fulfill his obligation with matzah [made] from *ma'aser sheni[^61]* [if he is] in Jerusalem.[^62] However, one[^63] may not fulfill his obligation with matzah made from *bikkurim*[^64] even [if he is] in Jerusalem.[^65]  
[The difference is] because there is no permissible way of eating *bikkurim* in all [Jewish] settlements.[^66] [In contrast,] *ma'aser sheni* can be redeemed and eaten in all [Jewish] settlements.[^67] [Our Sages[^68] interpreted [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-20|Exodus 12:20]],] which states: "Eat matzot in all of your settlements," [to imply that] only matzah that is fit to be eaten in all settlements may be used to fulfill one's obligation.[^69] ^6-8

**9.** Loaves from the thanksgiving offering[^70] and cakes from the Nazirite offering,[^71] which an individual made for his personal use,[^72] may not be used to fulfill one's obligation,[^73] [as can be implied] from the statement \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-17|Exodus 12:17]]]: "And you shall watch the matzot."[^74] Matzah which is watched with the sole intention [that it be used to fulfill the mitzvah] of matzah may be used to fulfill one's obligation. However, this matzah[^75] is also watched with the intention of [being used for a] sacrifice.[^76] Those[^77] that were made to be sold in the marketplace,[^78] [the baker] has the intention that if they are not sold, he will eat them. Therefore, while he was making them[^79] he [also] watched for the sake of matzah.[^80] ^6-9

**10.** All are obligated to [fulfill the mitzvah to] eat matzah,[^81] even women and slaves.[^82] A minor [old enough to eat bread] should be trained in [the fulfillment of] mitzvot[^83] and be given an olive's size of matzah[^84] to eat. [For] a sick or elderly person who cannot eat,[^85] we can soak a cake [of matzah] in water and feed it to him, provided it does not dissolve.[^86] ^6-10

**11.** It is a Rabbinic ordinance that nothing at all is eaten after the matzah,[^87] not even roasted seeds, nuts, or the like.[^88] Rather, even though one ate matzah[^89] and afterwards, ate other foods, fruit, and the like,[^90] one returns and eats a second olive's size of matzah at the end [of the meal][^91] and ceases [eating].[^92] ^6-11

**12.** The Sages forbade a person from eating matzah[^93] on Pesach eve,[^94] in order for there to be a distinction between [partaking of it as food] and eating it on the evening [of the fifteen as a mitzvah.][^95] Whoever eats matzah on Pesach eve is given "stripes for rebellion"[^96] until his soul expires.[^97]  
Similarly, it is forbidden to eat[^98] on Pesach evening from slightly before the time of *Minchah*,[^99] in order that one will approach eating matzah with appetite. However, one may eat some fruit or vegetables,[^100] but should not fill up on them.[^101]  
The Sages of the former generations would starve themselves on Pesach eve[^102] so that they would eat matzah with appetite, and thus hold the mitzvot as dear. In contrast, on the eve of Sabbaths or other festivals,[^103] one may continue eating until darkness.[^104] ^6-12
