### 11.1–18: Peter’s report to the “circumcised believers.”

*[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 245* · Peter reviews the events that led to Cornelius’s baptism, and the apostles acknowledge that divine gifts are extended to Gentiles. Luke uses the repetition of stories, such as Jesus’ ascent into heaven ([[Luke 24#^24-51|Lk 24.51]]; [[Acts 01#^1-9|Acts 1.9]]) or Paul’s reports of his vision of Jesus (9.1–9; 32.1–21; 26.2–33), to indicate an important narrative transition, in this case a shift in focus from Jews to Gentiles as constituting the community of believers. ^11-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-3|3]]:** The objection to Peter eating with Gentiles, rather than converting Jews, reinforces an image that Jews are xenophobic (see 10.28n.). ^11-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-5|5–9]]:** Peter repeats the account of his vision with minor variations (see 10.9–15). ^11-5

> [!note] Christians *(p. 245)*
> Acts employs several terms to designate the community of believers in Jesus as the prophesied Messiah who has been raised from the dead, including “disciples” (11.29; 13.52; 14.20,22,28; 15.10; 16.1; 18.23; 19.1,9,30; 20.1,30; 21.4,16), “Way” (9.2; 19.9,23; 22.4; 24.14,22), “sect” (Gk *hairesis*) used in Acts as a derogatory term (24.5,14; 28.22), and “Nazarenes” (24.5). Acts also contains one of the earliest uses of the term “Christian” (11.26; 26.28). The origin of the term is obscure. Acts says that the term was first applied to the disciples in Antioch, although the term itself (*Christianius*) was originally Latin and therefore unlikely to have originated in a Greek-speaking city. The designation was already in use by the 60s, as seen in an inscription from Pompey (*CIL* 4.679), and it became common by the time Acts was likely written (Pliny, *Letters* 10.96; Tacitus, *Ann.* 15.44; Suetonius, *Nero* 16.2). The term is similar to contemporary expressions, such as “Pompeianus” or “Augustianus,” for followers or partisans of noted leaders. It is probable that others initially coined the term in order to disparage those who venerated a man executed as a criminal. Christians themselves adopted it as the primary term of identification probably sometime in the late first or early second century ([[1 Peter 04#^4-16|1 Pet 4.16]]; Ignatius, *Eph.* 11.2; *Magn.* 4.1).

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-14|14]]:** *Household*, the conversion of the head of the household brings about the conversion of the entire household (16.15,34; 18.8). *Will be saved*, see 4.12. ^11-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-15|15]]:** *Holy Spirit*, see 10.44n. ^11-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-16|16]]:** See 1.5n. ^11-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-18|18]]:** *Repentance*, including belief in Jesus (see 2.38n.). ^11-18

### 11.19–30: The church in Antioch

*[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-19|Text]], p. 246* ^11-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-19|19]]:** *Scattered*, see 8.1. *Phoenicia*, on the Mediterranean coast, north of Mt. Carmel. *Antioch*, seat of Roman administration in Syria, and site of an early, largely Gentile church ([[Galatians 02#^2-11|Gal 2.11–14]]). ^11-19-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-20|20]]:** *Cyrene*, important city in North Africa. *Hellenists*, here Greek-speaking Gentiles (6.1; 9.29). ^11-20

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-22|22]]:** Sending representatives from the Jerusalem church (see 8.14) sanctions the expansion. ^11-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-25|25]]:** *Tarsus*, see 9.30n. ^11-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-26|26]]:** *Christians*, the community of believers has previously been referred to as the Way (see 9.2n.). The origin of the name is uncertain. A new name, especially when connected to Gentile converts, indicates that the church is no longer fully identifiable with Jews. ^11-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-27|27]]:** *Prophets*, recognized role among Jews (Josephus, *J.W.* 1.78–80; 2.112–13; 2.258–63; 6.283–87; 6.300–309; 7.437–50; *Ant*. 14.22–24; 14.172–76; 15.373–79; 17.345–48; 20.97–99; 20.167–72; 20.188) and early Christians ([[1 Corinthians 12#^12-28|1 Cor 12.28–29]]; [[1 Corinthians 14#^14-29|14.29–37]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-11|Eph 4.11]]). ^11-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-28|28]]:** *Agabus*, see 21.10. *Famine*, contemporaneous local famines, including in Judea, are known (Josephus, *Ant.* 20.51–53,101), but the hyperbolic “all the world” implicates Rome as the failed ruler. *Claudius*, Roman emperor 41–54 CE. ^11-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 11#^11-30|30]]:** *Elders*, Gk “presbyteroi”; church leaders (14.23; 15.2,4,22,23; 16.4; 20.17; 21.18; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-14|1 Tim 4.14]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-17|5.17]],[[1 Timothy 05#^5-19|19]]; [[Titus 01#^1-5|Titus 1.5]]; [[James 05#^5-14|Jas 5.14]]; [[Revelation 04#^4-4|Rev 4.4]]). The title was used in many Jewish communities, including Qumran (1QS 6.8, Heb “zekenim”); although etymologically related to old age, it refers to leaders, since this role was typically held by older men. ^11-30
