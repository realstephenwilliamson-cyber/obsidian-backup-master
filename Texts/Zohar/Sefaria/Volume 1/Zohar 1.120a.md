# Zohar 1.120a

← [[Zohar 1.119b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 1.120b]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 2.03 - Parashat Va-Yera#^z1-120a|Parashat Va-Yera, at 1:120a]]

*Volume 1, folio 120a* · Vayera

*Passage Vayera 33:494 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.119b#^vayera-33-494|Vayera 33:494]].*

**Vayera 34:495** ON THE THIRD DAY ABRAHAM LIFTED UP HIS EYES, AND SAW THE PLACE AFAR OFF. As we have already been told that Abraham went to the place, all this seems superfluous. But the truth is that “the third day” means the third generation, i.e. Jacob, and the words “he saw the place from afar” are parallel to the expression “from afar the Lord appeared unto me” (Jer. 31, 3). ^vayera-34-495

**Vayera 34:496** Or again, “the place” alludes to Jacob, of whom it is written, “and he took one of the stones of the place” (Gen. 28, 11). For Abraham scrutinised the “third day”, which is the third grade, and he beheld Jacob, who was destined to descend from him. “Afar off’, to wit, at some distant time, and not soon. ^vayera-34-496

**Vayera 34:497** R. Eleazar said to R. Judah: ‘What credit is herein ascribed to Abraham, if whilst about to bind Isaac he saw that Jacob was destined to descend from him?’ ^vayera-34-497

**Vayera 34:498** R. Judah replied: ‘Indeed Abraham did see Jacob, since even before that Abraham was endowed with the higher Wisdom; and now he scrutinised the third day, which is the third grade, in order to make sure. And indeed he did see him, but now only “from afar”, for the reason that he was going to bind Isaac, and he did not wish to question the ways of the Holy One. ^vayera-34-498

**Vayera 34:499** “Afar off’, that is, he saw him through a “dim glass” only, and therefore only partially; for if the “clear glass” had been resting upon the “dim glass”, Abraham would have seen him properly. ^vayera-34-499

**Vayera 34:500** The “clear glass” did not function on this occasion, because this is the grade of Jacob, who, not yet being born, had not reached that grade; and also in order that Abraham’s reward might be all the greater. ^vayera-34-500

**Vayera 34:501** AND THEY CAME TO THE PLACE WHICH GOD HAD TOLD HIM OF, ETC. Here it is intimated that although Abraham had some vision of Jacob, yet he said to himself, “Assuredly the Holy One knows another way which will serve.’ Forthwith, therefore, ABRAHAM BUILT THE ALTAR THERE. ^vayera-34-501

**Vayera 34:502** Before this it is written: AND ISAAC SPOKE UNTO ABRAHAM HIS FATHER, AND SAID, MY FATHER. As explained elsewhere, the reason why Abraham did not respond to him immediately was because the normal compassion of a father towards a son left him, and hence he simply said: ‘Here I am, my son”, implying that the quality of mercy in him had been transmuted into rigour. ^vayera-34-502

**Vayera 34:503** AND ABRAHAM SAID. It is not written: “and his father said”, which shows again that he was regarding him not as his father but as his adversary. GOD WILL PROVIDE FOR HIMSELF THE LAMB FOR A BURNT OFFERING, MY SON. He should have said: “provide for us”, but what he meant was, “God will provide for Himself when necessary, but for the present it is going to be my son and nothing else.” Forthwith, AND THEY WENT BOTH OF THEM TOGETHER. ^vayera-34-503

**Vayera 34:504** R. Simeon discoursed here on the verse: *Behold, angels cry abroad, the angels of peace weep bitterly* (Is. 33, 7). ‘These angels’, he said, ‘are superior angels who “cried abroad” because they no longer knew what to make of God’s promise to Abraham at the time when “He brought him forth abroad” (Gen. 15, 5). ^vayera-34-504

**Vayera 34:505** The “angels of peace” are those other angels who were destined to go forth to meet Jacob, for whose sake the Holy One promised them peace, as it is written, “And Jacob went on his way, and the angels of God met him” (*Ibid.* 32, 2), and these are called “angels of peace”. All these wept when they saw Abraham binding Isaac, the upper and the lower beings trembled and shook, and all on account of Isaac. ^vayera-34-505

**Vayera 35:506** AND THE ANGEL OF THE LORD CALLED UNTO HIM… ABRAHAM, ABRAHAM. There is in the text a disjunctive mark between the two Abrahams, to show that the latter was not like the former; the latter was the perfected Abraham, while the former was still incomplete. Similarly, in the passage where the name Samuel is repeated with a disjunctive line between (I Sam. 3, 10), the second is the perfected Samuel, whilst the first was not yet so. The second Samuel was a prophet, but not the first. But when we come to “Moses, Moses” (Ex. 3, 4), we do not find any pausal sign between, for the reason that from the day Moses was born the Shekinah never departed from him. R. Hiya said that the angel repeated Abraham’s name in order to animate him with a new spirit, and spur him to a new activity with a new heart. ^vayera-35-506
