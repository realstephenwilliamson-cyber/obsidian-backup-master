# John 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT John 02|John 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue John 01|NRSVue]] · [[John 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT John - Introduction]]

### 1.1–18: Prologue

*[[NRSVue John 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 174* · In contrast to Matthew and Luke, John lacks a description of Jesus’ conception and birth; neither Joseph nor Mary figures in the account of Jesus’ human origins. Rather, Jesus’ arrival in the world is described in cosmological terms, and his role as God’s son is emphasized. The poetic structure of the Prologue and the presence of some terms that are otherwise not found in the Gospel suggest that 1.1–18 may have been built around an earlier hymn. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-1|1–3]]:** *In the beginning*, echoing [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-1|Gen 1.1]]. *The Word*, God’s power of creation and redemption ([[Psalms 033#^33-6|Ps 33.6]]); identified with Jesus ([[John 01#^1-9|Jn 1.9]],[[John 01#^1-14|14]],[[John 01#^1-17|17]]). It suggests Wisdom ([[Proverbs 08#^8-7|Prov 8.7–30]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-1|Wis 9.1]],[[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-9|9]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 18#^18-15|18.15]]; [[LXX Sirach 24#^24-9|Sir 24.9]]; [[LXX Sirach 43#^43-26|43.26]]). For the Alexandrian Jewish philosopher Philo, God’s Logos was the first fruit of creation (*Leg. all.* 3.175). The Wisdom of Ben Sira (Sirach) identifies Wisdom with the divine commandment, that is, the Torah ([[LXX Sirach 24#^24-22|Sir 24.22–23]]). This identification persists throughout the rabbinic period (cf. *Gen. Rab*. 1.10, no earlier than the fifth century, which declares that God consulted the Torah as a blueprint before creating the world). See also the use of memra (“word”) in the Aramaic Targums to Genesis (see “*Logos*,” p. 688). *With God*, as in [[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22–31]], “I \[Wisdom\] was there . . . I was beside him \[the Lord\].” ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Life*, whose source is God ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-20|Gen 1.20–25]]); *light*, the first created thing ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-3|Gen 1.3]]); a frequent image for God or God’s presence or favor ([[Psalms 027#^27-1|Ps 27.1]]; [[Psalms 036#^36-10|36.9]]; [[Isaiah 02#^2-5|Isa 2.5]]; see also [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-26|Wis 7.26]]). *All people*, perhaps foreshadowing the idea, stated in 10.16; 11.52; 12.32, that Jesus has come to save “the world” rather than a particular ethnic group. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Light shines*, evoking [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-3|Gen 1.3]]. Light/darkness contrast, prominent in the Gospel of John, appears also in the Dead Sea Scrolls, e.g. 1QS 3.13–4.26, but direct influence of the Scrolls on the Gospel is unlikely. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-6|6–8]]:** These verses preview 1.19–34. *John*, the Baptizer, is mentioned by Josephus (*Ant*. 18.116–119). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-10|10–12]]:** These verses summarize the Gospel’s historical and cosmological plots: Jesus’ own people, the Jews, failed to accept him and plotted to kill him; those who did accept him become God’s children and receive eternal life. *World* (Gk “kosmos”), both creation and humankind, or, frequently, the people who reject Jesus (12.31; 16.11). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-11|11]]:** *His own people*, the Jews (4.22; see also [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-5|Ex 19.5]]). ^1-11

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-12|12–13]]:** Contrast between an ancestral covenant, such as that of the Jews, and a faith-based covenant (see 8.33–40). *Believed in his name*, gave him due honor. This expression appears also in *1 En.* 67.8–10 with reference to God. *He gave power to become*, perhaps referring to the “right” or “au- *came into being that has come into being. 4In him was life* b Or *He was the true light that enlightens everyone coming into the world* c Or *to his own home* thority,” but more likely another way of saying “became” as a consequence of divine action. *Children of God*, in contrast to “children according to the flesh,” that is, the children of Abraham (see 8.33–40). ^1-12

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Blood*, biological birth. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Word became flesh*, a paradoxical formulation since “flesh” are perishable and “logos” is an eternal divine quality; cf. [[Isaiah 40#^40-6|Isa 40.6–8]], “All people \[lit., ‘flesh’\] is grass \[that\] withers . . . but the word of our God will stand forever.” This point marks the “Incarnation,” the Word becoming flesh. The idea that a divine being can simultaneously be human is often seen as a major dividing line between Judaism and Christianity. Jews in the biblical and Second Temple periods, however, believed supernatural beings, such as angels, could take human form (e.g., the angel in Judges 13; Raphael in the book of Tobit). The boundaries between human and divine were then understood as more porous and less absolute. *Glory*, Gk “doxa,” usual LXX equivalent of Heb “kavod,” the visible manifestation of God’s presence (e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-10|Ex 16.10]]). *Only son*, unique in his filial relationship to God. In the background may lie Aristotelian ideas of procreation according to which the father is the one whose likeness, in ideal circumstances, is imprinted on his son (see Aristotle, *On the Generation of Animals*, 722 passim). *Lived among us*, the Gk means “tabernacled,” an allusion to the wilderness Tabernacle, the precursor of the Jerusalem Temple (e.g., [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-9|Ex 25.9]]). There may also be a connection to the Hebrew word “shekhinah,” which in some texts, such as *Tg. Onq*. at [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^12-5|Deut 12.5]], functions as a technical term for God’s presence among God’s people, the equivalent of the Tanakh’s “kavod.” ^1-14

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-15|15]]:** *John testified . . .* , refers forward to 1.19, the Baptist’s testimony to the Jerusalem priests and Levites. ^1-15

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Fullness*, Gk “plērōma” ([[Colossians 01#^1-19|Col 1.19]]) appears only here in John. The precise meaning is unclear; it may simply emphasize the abundance of spiritual nourishment (symbolized by water \[[[John 04#^4-14|Jn 4.14–15]]\] and bread \[6.1-14\] that believers receive). ^1-16

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-17|17–18]]:** Contrast between Jesus and *Moses*, and the superiority of the Gospel to the *Law* (Torah; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-18|Ex 34.18]]). ^1-17

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Grace and truth*, God’s loving presence (Heb “ḥesed,” “steadfast love,” e.g., [[Psalms 085#^85-11|Ps 85.10]]) and faithfulness (Heb “ ʾemet,” “truth” that does not yield). ^1-17-2

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-18|18]]:** *No one has ever seen God*, extending the contrast between Jesus and Moses, this phrase emphasizes that unlike Moses who is not permitted to see God face to face (Ex33.18), Jesus is the one who makes God known to the world. ^1-18

### 1.19–34: Testimony of John the Baptist

*[[NRSVue John 01#^1-19|Text]], p. 175* ^1-19

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Jews*, here specifically the Jerusalem authorities. *Priests and Levites from Jerusalem*, Jewish groups most closely associated with Temple ritual including ritual purification. This association suggests that they are interested in John’s baptizing activities. According to v 25, they view his baptizing activities as evidence of messianic claims, a point that John denies. [[John 03#^3-22|Jn 3.22]] and 4.1 present somewhat contradictory evidence as to whether Jesus, for whom the Gospel affirms these messianic claims, baptized or not. ^1-19-2

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Confessed and did not deny it, but confessed*, a better translation would be “insisted”; compare 9.22, where the same verb is used in the sense of confessing faith in Jesus as the Christ. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Elijah* ([[II Kings 02#^2-11|2 Kings 2.11]]), his ascent to heaven in a fiery chariot ([[I Kings 02#^2-11|1 Kings 2.11]]) led to speculation that he remained alive and would return to herald the messianic age (see [[Malachi 3#^3-23|Mal 4.5]] \[3.23 MT\]). *God,* or *It is the only Son* c Gk *bosom* d Or *the Christ* There is little concrete evidence to suggest this was a widespread belief among Jews in the Second Temple period. Elijah’s role as messianic forerunner is mentioned in Justin’s *Dial.* 8.4; 49.1 as well as in a baraita (a pre-Talmudic but non-mishnaic tradition found in the Talmud) in *b. Eruv.* 43a-b, which refers to this point in the context of a discussion of whether a Nazirite can drink wine on Sabbath and holidays, but indicates that the Messiah’s coming may or may not be preceded by Elijah’s return. *The prophet*, perhaps referring to the expected “prophet like Moses” ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15–18]]; see [[LXX 1 Maccabees 04#^4-46|1 Macc 4.46]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 14#^14-41|14.41]]; 1QS 9.11). The interrogation may reflect a polemic against those who viewed John the Baptist as the Messiah. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-23|23]]:** The quotation from [[Isaiah 40#^40-3|Isa 40.3]] reflects the Septuagint (LXX), in which “in the wilderness” modifies “voice” rather than the Hebrew (MT), in which the phrase modifies “way of the Lord.” Compare [[Matthew 03#^3-1|Mt 3.1–3]]; [[Mark 01#^1-2|Mk 1.2–5]]; [[Luke 01#^1-16|Lk 1.16–17]]. ^1-23

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-24|24]]:** *Pharisees*, a group with communal authority, identified by Josephus as one of the four main Jewish schools of thought in the period prior to the Temple’s destruction in 70 (*Ant*. 18). See “Jewish Movements of the NT Period,” p. 614. ^1-24

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-25|25]]:** *Baptizing*, water was used for ritual purification (e.g., [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-10|Ex 19.10]]; [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^14-8|Lev 14.8]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-25|Ezek 36.25]]). Although the language of purity and impurity is occasionally used in regard to moral issues, no Jewish sources unambiguously point to immersion as “cleansing” from sin. ^1-25

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-28|28]]:** *Bethany*, town in the Transjordan, of which no trace remains, and not the Judean town mentioned in [[John 11#^11-18|Jn 11.18]]. ^1-28

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-29|29]]:** *Lamb*, possible allusion to the Passover offering (Ex 12); the apocalyptic Lamb (*T. Jos.* 19.8) or the Suffering Servant (Isa 53) may also be in view; on any interpretation, the designation has a redemptive and eschatological meaning associated with the Passover. *Takes away the sin of the world*, the singular (sin) rather than plural (sins) suggests a redemptive function to the “Lamb of God” in that he removes the world’s sinful condition (as opposed to removing the consequences of each individual’s misdeeds). The fact that biblical law does not identify the Passover as a “sin” offering did not prevent the Gospel from associating lamb imagery with the redemption from sin in this passage. ^1-29

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-30|30]]:** *Before me*, an allusion to Jesus’ preexistence, the theme emphasized in the Prologue. ^1-30

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-31|31]]:** *Israel*, the people Israel, a positive designation in John meaning the “children of God” who know God through his son (1.18). ^1-31

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-32|32]]:** In contrast to the Synoptic Gospels ([[Matthew 03#^3-13|Mt 3.13–17]]; [[Mark 01#^1-9|Mk 1.9–11]]; [[Luke 03#^3-21|Lk 3.21–22]]), John does not describe Jesus’ baptism but only the Baptist’s recollection of the dove’s descent. *Dove*, NT baptism stories associate the dove with the Holy Spirit. Archaeological remains suggest that the origins of this association may go back to ancient Near Eastern beliefs about the dove representing goddesses. None of the Tanakh references to doves (see e.g., Gen 8; [[Isaiah 06#^6-8|Isa 6.8]]; [[Psalms 068#^68-14|Ps 68.13]]; [[Song of Songs 2#^2-14|Song 2.14]]) associate them with the divine spirit, in contrast to some early Christian references, such as the *Prot. James* 8.2, which states that the young Mary was fed like a dove. In *b. Ber.* 3a, the divine voice is compared to a dove. ^1-32

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-33|33]]:** *Baptizes with the Holy Spirit*, with some differences in wording, this phrase is also present in the Synoptic baptism accounts; it may be intended to emphasize the superiority of Jesus to John the Baptist. ^1-33

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-34|34]]:** *Son of God*, the most frequent christological designation in the Gospel of John, which portrays Jesus as God’s unique son, the one who does his will (5.30) and serves as his agent (e.g., 6.38). In [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^4-22|Ex 4.22–23]], God refers to Israel collectively as “my son” (see also [[Hosea 11#^11-1|Hos 11.1]]). In [[Job 01#^1-6|Job 1.6]] and 38.7 “sons of God” refers to angels. ^1-34

### 1.35–51: Call of the disciples

*[[NRSVue John 01#^1-35|Text]], p. 177* ^1-35

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-35|35]]:** *Two of his disciples*, one is Andrew (v 40); the other is sometimes identified as the Beloved Disciple. ^1-35-2

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-36|36]]:** *Lamb of God*, see 1.29n. ^1-36

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-38|38]]:** *What are you looking for?*, a Johannine double entendre, on the surface a natural question to ask when one discovers that one is being followed, but on another level, a reference to a spiritual search, the fulfillment of which is present but not yet recognized. See the similar wording in 20.15. *Rabbi*, Heb “my master.” ^1-38

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-41|41]]:** *Messiah*, Greek transliteration of the Aram “mešīḥâḥ,” “anointed one”; the Gk translation is “Christos” (Christ). The transliterated form “Messiah” recurs in 4.25 but nowhere else in the NT. ^1-41

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-42|42]]:** *Cephas*, “rock” in Aram; *Peter*, Gk “Petros,” also “rock”; see [[Matthew 16#^16-18|Mt 16.18]]. ^1-42

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-44|44]]:** *Bethsaida,* close to the Sea of Galilee. Although the Gospel implies that Bethsaida is in Galilee, it was in Gaulanitis, territory controlled by the tetrarch Philip. John’s reference may reflect popular usage, also reflected in Ptolemy’s *Geography* (v 16.4). ^1-44

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-45|45]]:** *Moses . . .the prophets*, Philip implies that he, and others, were searching for a Messiah who would fulfill the Torah and scriptural prophecies. The reference may be to the “prophet-like-Moses” described in [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15–18]], the son of Man ([[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]; [[Daniel 08#^8-18|8.18]]), and/or Elijah (see 1.21n.). *Son of Joseph*, see 6.42; [[Matthew 01#^1-23|Mt 1.23]]. There is no allusion to a virginal conception. ^1-45

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-46|46]]:** *Nazareth*, a small village, approximately 26 km (15 mi) west of the Sea of Galilee. ^1-46

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-47|47]]:** *Israelite*, the Israelite recognizes Jesus as his king (v 49). *No deceit*, allusion to Jacob, also known as Israel ([[Torah 08 - Vayishlach#^32-29|Gen 32.28–29]]) who deceitfully supplanted his twin Esau in receiving his father Isaac’s blessing ([[Torah 06 - Toldot#^27-35|Gen 27.35]]). ^1-47

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-48|48]]:** *Fig tree*, associated with both abundance and the eschaton; “gathering figs” in later sources means “studying” (apparently because the Tree of Knowledge \[Gen 3\] was believed to be a fig tree \[*b. Ber*. 40a\]). ^1-48

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-49|49]]:** *Son of God . . . King of Israel*, treated as synonymous; [[Psalms 002#^2-6|Ps 2.6–7]] uses adoption language in relating the king to God. ^1-49

**[[NRSVue John 01#^1-51|51]]:** *Very truly*, lit., “amen, amen” (Heb “amen,” “it is so,” “it is true”), formula used for emphasis (3.3,5,11; 5.19,24–25; 6.26,32,47,53; 8.34, etc.). Biblical and post-biblical instances of the double “amen” occur in [[Torah 35 - Nasso#^5-22|Num 5.22]]; [[Psalms 041#^41-14|Ps 41.13]]; 72.19; [[Psalms 089#^89-53|89.52]]; [[Nehemiah 08#^8-6|Neh 8.6]]; [[LXX Tobit 08#^8-8|Tob 8.8]]; [[LXX Judith 13#^13-20|Jdt 13.20]], and in the Dead Sea Scrolls (e.g., 1QS 1.20; 4Q286 f7 i:7), where it may have had a liturgical function. *Son of Man*, see [[Ezekiel 02#^2-1|Ezek 2.1]] (Heb “ben adam”); [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]] (biblical Aram: “Bar Enosh”; other Aram forms are “bar nash” and “bar nasha”); a cosmic messianic figure associated strongly with apocalyptic eschatology. *Angels . . . descending*, allusion to Jacob’s dream ([[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^28-12|Gen 28.12]]), implying that Jesus is the ladder connecting heaven and earth. Greek (*petra*), respectively d Both instances of the Greek word for *you* in this verse are plural ^1-51
