## Mūla

आराधिताः स्वोचितं तच्छीघ्रं विदधते फलम् ।
स्वकृत्यांशस्थितानां च धाम्नि येऽन्तं व्रजन्ति ते ॥ ^mula

ārādhitāḥ svocitaṃ tacchīghraṃ vidadhate phalam |
svakṛtyāṃśasthitānāṃ ca dhāmni ye'ntaṃ vrajanti te || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. X
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 10 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v10 05 - Chapter Twenty-Eight, part 3#^pg123|p. 106]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 10.pdf#page=123>))
>
> The one that, as a partial incarnation (of a deity) (arma),), is carrying out its own task, does do not move from its place. Such as, for example, Gaurī, who is engaged in practicing austerities in the caves of (the lands of) Kashmir, and so too there (in that same Kashmir) at Dhyānoqddāra, where Viṣnu (Vibhu), who in his incarnation as a lion (Narahari) rose up proudly, frightening (away) the demons who were leading (away) the (river) Vitastā. (Again, another example is) like Viṣṇu (who resides) in Sāligrāma, or iva (who rose up against the demons) who were eating his own (food) (svopabhoginah), or Nara and Nārāyaṇa engaged in prācticing austerities at Badarī. These and other gods, who have in this way assumed partial incarnations (aṁśa)) (to carry out) their own task, when propitiated bestow in a short time various fruits, in keeping with their own nature. (with 28.241cd–245ab)
>
> Those who die in the abodes (dhāman) of those deities who have assumed partial incarnations (ara)) (there to carry out) their own tasks, after they have lived out (their own) experiences there in that way, descend into (other) mortal (bodies). Those who have descended (thus into the world do so as) partial incarnations (amma)) of this or that (deity). And then again, one in nature with that, by (receiving) that initiation, knowledge and (their) observance (caryā) etc., they gradually attain Śiva’s nature. (The entire range of) fettered souls, beginning with plants and ending with animals, that have died in the (domains of these) two kinds (of divine beings), (reincarnate) in various ways in the world (they govern), according to (how they are) penetrated by the latent traces of their own Karma. (with 28.245cd–248ab)
