## About

Hannah Arendt's report on the 1961 Jerusalem trial of Adolf Eichmann, the SS officer who managed the deportation of Jews to ghettos and extermination camps. Its subtitle is *A Report on the Banality of Evil*.

- **Author:** Arendt (1906–1975), born in Hanover, was briefly held by the Gestapo in 1933, fled to Paris and reached the United States in 1941 (Penguin; the SEP agrees).
- **The trial:** Mossad abducted Eichmann in Argentina in May 1960; he was tried before the Jerusalem District Court and hanged on 1 June 1962. Arendt reported on it for The New Yorker; the SEP says she attended in part, David Cesarani (cited by Wikipedia) at most four days of testimony.
- **1963–64:** the report ran in The New Yorker in five parts, February–March 1963; Viking Press published the book in 1963, and a revised and enlarged edition followed in 1964. Penguin's current edition includes her postscript answering the controversy.
- **Argument:** Eichmann was not a monster or a fanatic but an ambitious, cliché-bound functionary whose crimes came from "thoughtlessness" (SEP). The SEP says the phrase describes the doer's character and motives, not the deeds, and was not a general theory of evil. She also criticized the trial: the abduction, Ben-Gurion's staging, and charges for acts not then criminal under German law.
- **Controversy:** her claim that the Jewish councils (Judenräte) cooperated with the Nazis and raised the death toll drew charges of blaming the victims. Gershom Scholem accused her of lacking love for the Jewish people (Encounter, January 1964). Jacob Robinson answered her in a book (1965); later critics, among them Cesarani (2006), Bettina Stangneth and Deborah Lipstadt, argue that Eichmann was a committed antisemite.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says "banality of evil" became one of the most memorable phrases of 20th-century intellectual life, used in debates on morality, justice and truth commissions. The SEP says the trial turned Arendt to the questions of thinking and judging pursued in *The Life of the Mind*.
