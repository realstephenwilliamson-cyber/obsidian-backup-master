# Ecclesiastes 06

## Chapter 6

**6:1** There is an evil I have observed under the sun, and a grave one it is for man: ^6-1

**6:2** that God sometimes grants a man riches, property, and wealth, so that he does not want for anything his appetite may crave, but God does not permit him to enjoy it; instead, a stranger will enjoy it. That is futility and a grievous ill. ^6-2

**6:3** Even if a man should beget a hundred children and live many years—no matter how many the days of his years may come to, if his gullet is not sated through his wealth, I say: The stillbirth, though it was not even accorded a burial,[^1] is more fortunate than he. ^6-3

**6:4** Though it comes into futility and departs into darkness, and its very name is covered with darkness, ^6-4

**6:5** though it has never seen or experienced the sun, it is better off than he— ^6-5

**6:6** yes, even if the other lived a thousand years twice over but never had his fill of enjoyment! For are not both of them bound for the same place? ^6-6

**6:7** [^2]All of man’s earning is for the sake of his mouth, [^3]yet his gullet is not sated. ^6-7

**6:8** What advantage then has the wise man over the fool, what advantage has the pauper who knows how to get on in life? ^6-8

**6:9** [^4]Is the feasting of the eyes more important than the pursuit of desire? That, too, is futility and pursuit of wind. ^6-9

**6:10** Whatever happens, it was designated long ago and it was known that it would happen; as for man, he cannot contend with what is stronger than he. ^6-10

**6:11** Often, much talk means much futility. How does it benefit a man? ^6-11

**6:12** Who can possibly know what is best for a man to do in life—the few days of his fleeting life? For[^5] who can tell him what the future holds for him under the sun? ^6-12

[^1]: Stillbirths were cast into pits or hidden in the ground in no recognizable graves; cf. v. 4 end.
[^2]: Cf. Prov. 16.26.
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; emendation yields “And if the gullet is not sated, what advantage has the wise man over the fool, he who knows how to get on in life over the pauper?”
[^4]: Meaning of first half of verse uncertain.
[^5]: Lit. “according to the shadow that”; cf. Qumran Aramaic betel and Syriac met·t·ol; and see 8.13.
