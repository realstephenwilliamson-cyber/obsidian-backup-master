# James 3 (JANT notes)

[[JANT James 02|← James 2]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT James 04|James 4 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue James 03|NRSVue]] · [[James 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT James - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-1|3.1–12]]:** **Importance of guarding speech.** ^3-1

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Teachers*, your students will compound your slight error and in turn will teach nonsense that will lead their students to seek foreign values (*Avot de R. Natan* A.11). See [[Acts 13#^13-1|Acts 13.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-28|1 Cor 12.28–29]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-11|Eph 4.11]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-7|1 Tim 1.7]]. ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Perfect*, those who control their thoughts and actions that they do not misspeak, let alone misbehave. [[Psalms 039#^39-2|Ps 39.1]] speaks of bridling the tongue; [[Proverbs 10#^10-31|Prov 10.31]] speaks of the mouth of the just; [[LXX Sirach 05#^5-11|Sir 5.11–6.1]] (see 28.12–26) warns against being “double-tongued.” See also [[Proverbs 17#^17-28|Prov 17.28]] (“Even fools who keep silent are considered wise”) and [[Ecclesiastes 05#^5-1|Eccl 5.2]] which counsels silence. Shimon ben Gamaliel (*m. Avot* 1.17) concluded the best medicine for the body’s welfare is silence. Likewise *b. Pesah.* 99a counsels silence. *B. Meg.* 18a contains a witty proverb: “A word is worth one sela; silence is worth two.” ^3-2

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-5|5]]:** *Tongue . . . exploits*, speaking ill of others is worse than the worst of sins (*Midr. Tanh. Lev.,* “Metzora” 4–5). ^3-5

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-6|6]]:** *Fire*, see [[Psalms 120#^120-3|Ps 120.3–4]] (HB vv 4–5): “Deceitful tongue . . . arrows . . . glowing coals,” see [[LXX Sirach 28#^28-11|Sir 28.11–12]]. ^3-6

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-9|9]]:** *Lord and Father*, “Lord” suggests a master-slave association while “father” suggests a nurturing relationship. The Talmud in various places (e.g., *B. Bat.* 10a) notes that Israel is sometimes compared to a son (e.g., [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1]]) and sometimes a slave (e.g., [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-55|Lev 25.55]]). The Jewish New Year liturgy notes the dual roles: “If \[we be\] as children, be merciful with us as the mercy of a father for children. If as slaves, our eyes \[look toward and\] depend upon You, until You be gracious to us.” ^3-9

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-10|10]]:** *Blessing and cursing*, that blessing and curse depend on the tongue is highlighted in *Mid. Tannaim* to [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^11-26|Deut 11.26–28]] in an exposition of [[Proverbs 18#^18-21|Prov 18.21]], “Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruits.” ^3-10

### 3.13–18: Dangers of envy

*[[NRSVue James 03#^3-13|Text]], p. 496* ^3-13

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-15|15]]:** *Wisdom . . . from above . . . earthly*, *unspiritual*, *Avot de R. Natan* B. 43 contrasts divine, gentle wisdom and earthly, wicked wisdom. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue James 03#^3-16|16–18]]:** *Envy . . . peace*, James’s words here concerning heavenly and earthly wisdom is echoed by rabbis (*b. Ber.* 17a) for whom the divine realms are devoid of envy, hatred, and ambition. Everyone requires peace (*Deut. Rab*. 5.12 on Sabbath observance). Many passages (e.g., *Num. Rab*. 11.7; *Sifre Num*. 42) extol peace, the reward for righteousness, using the imagery of [[Isaiah 32#^32-17|Isa 32.17]]. *M. Avot* 4.21 sums up this common rabbinic sentiment: “Jealousy, lust and seeking honor drive a person from the world.” ^3-16
