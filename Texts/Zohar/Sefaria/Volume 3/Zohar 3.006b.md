# Zohar 3.006b

← [[Zohar 3.006a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 3.007a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 7.03 - Parashat Va-Yiqra#^z3-6b|Parashat Va-Yiqra, at 3:6b]]

*Volume 3, folio 6b* · Vayikra

*Passage Vayikra 10:80 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 3.006a#^vayikra-10-80|Vayikra 10:80]].*

**Vayikra 10:81** So the Community of Israel, on all previous occasions in which she was in exile, when the appointed time came, used to return of herself to the King; but in this exile the Holy One, blessed be He, will himself take her by the hand and raise her and comfort her and restore her to his palace. So it is written: ‘In that day I will raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen’ (Amos 9, 11), the ‘tabernacle of David’ being identical with the ‘Virgin of Israel’. ^vayikra-10-81

**Vayikra 10:82** Said R. Judah: ‘Truly thou hast comforted and satisfied me, and this is the truth of the matter. And it reminds me of something similar which I had forgotten, of a saying of R. Jose, that the Holy One, blessed be He, will one day make proclamation concerning the Community of Israel, saying “Shake thyself from the dust, arise, sit thee down, Jerusalem” (Isa. 52, 2), like a man taking his neighbour by the hand and saying, Pull yourself together, rise.’ ^vayikra-10-82

**Vayikra 10:83** Said R. Aha to him: ‘All the prophets use similar language. Thus it is written, “Arise, shine forth, for thy light is come”, meaning that the King is here to be reconciled with her. And again, “Behold thy king cometh unto thee” (Zech. 9, 9): He shall come to thee to comfort thee, to raise thee, to repay thee all, to take thee into His palace and to espouse thee for evermore, as it is written: “And I shall betroth thee for ever” (Hos. 2, 19).’ ^vayikra-10-83

**Vayikra 11:84** As they went along they saw R. Abba approaching. They said: ‘Here comes a master of wisdom; let us greet the Shekinah.’ When, however, they came up, he himself slipped off the saddle and came down to the ground by them. ^vayikra-11-84

**Vayikra 11:85** He then began to discourse on the verse: “And there was the voice of a trumpet (*shofar*) exceedingly loud” (Ex. 19, 16). ‘The ancient scribes’, he said, ‘differed as to the correct intonation of this verse. Some punctuated it so as to read, “And there was a voice, the trumpet exceeding strong”, making the voice and the trumpet two things, the trumpet being the signal for slaves to go forth to everlasting freedom. ^vayikra-11-85

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Vayikra 11:86–11:87.*

**Vayikra 11:88** Some, again, made the voice and the trumpet one, this being the great voice whence issued the Torah, which is called “strong”, because there is no word in the Torah so apparently weak or feeble which when properly studied will not be found as strong as a hammer breaking the rock. ^vayikra-11-88
