## Metrics and Grammar

The text of the complete ṢSatsāhasra Samhitā is composed in ihe slaka-metre with only a few exceptions. It appears that the Soka is a very important implement for the prospective writer ofa Tantra. F serves as the skeleton on which the text is hung. For this reason the writer takes every pain to produce regular octo-syllabic pādas, the fundamental characteristic of a sloka. Within a pāda the writer has every possible freedom with regard to the length of the syllables as long as the fifth and the sixth syllables are shori and long, respectively. To achieve this latter condition, a short fifth and a long sixth syllable, even grammar is made

submissive to metrics, as we will see below.

The outward appearance of the sokas is very regular ihroughout the text of the first five chapters. There are only two instances found, where the division between two pãdas is ignored. In both cases it concerns an enumeration (3,46ab & 93ab). The rule that the pēda of a śloka should count eight syllables is scrupulously followed with only a few exceptions. When the pāda of a śłoka constitutes an irregularity with regard to its number of syllables, t may contain either too few or too many syllables. in most of the instances the irregularity concerns the difference of one syllable only. Thus an irregular pāda may contain either seven or nine syllables. One should make a distinc-

tion between these two irregularities, however.

In the case of a põda with only seven or even less syllables, it is very likely that the copy of the text itself is corrupt, and that the original author should not be blamed for this defect of the text. It requires only little knowledge of the Sanskrit language to add a mono-syllabic word with little meaning (e.g. ca, hi, tu etc.) 10 the text in order to obtain a regular octo-syllabic pãda. in case a pāda lacks two or more syllables, the chances are very great that in the process of copying a word has been dropped which was originally includeć in the text.
