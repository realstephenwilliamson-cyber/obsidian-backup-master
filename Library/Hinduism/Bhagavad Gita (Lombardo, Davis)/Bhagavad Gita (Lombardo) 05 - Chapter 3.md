## Chapter Three

Arjuna then said:

\[1\] “If You think that understanding,
O Krishna, surpasses action,
then why do You put upon me
this awful action, Handsome One?

\[2\] Your words have a double meaning
and confuse my understanding.
Tell me this, and tell me clearly,
how to attain the highest good.”

And the Blessed Lord said:

\[3\] “In this world two kinds of practice
have anciently been taught by Me:
*Jnana Yoga* of the *Samkhya*s,
and the *yogins*’ *Karma Yoga*.

\[4\] Neither by refraining from action
does one transcend activity,
nor is it by renunciation
that one can approach perfection.

\[5\] No one even for an instant
ever exists without acting.
Even unwilling, all must act,
forced by matter and the *gunas*.

\[6\] One who sits restraining action
but with a mind that dwells upon
sensory objects, self-deluded,
is called a false practitioner.

[p. 21] \[7\] But one who engages the mind
to curb the senses, Arjuna,
and to act in Karma *Yoga*
while unattached, is distinguished.

\[8\] Do your duty, for action is
indeed better than nonaction!
Mere maintenance of your body
could not be done without acting.

\[9\] Sacrificial action aside,
this world is constrained by action.
Act in order to sacrifice
without attachment, son of Kunti!

\[10\] Prajapati, when he created
sacrifice along with humans,
said of old: ‘Bring forth with this.
This will be your Cow of Plenty.’

\[11\] May you sustain the gods with this,
and may the gods sustain you in turn.
By sustaining each other so
you will attain the highest bliss.

\[12\] Sustained by sacrifice, the gods
will grant you all your desires.
If you enjoy these gifts without
giving in return, you are a thief.

\[13\] The good, who eat the remains
of the sacrifice, are freed from ills.
The wicked prepare their own food
and eat their own foul suffering.

\[14\] Beings exist because of food,
the origin of food is rain,
[p. 22] rain comes because of sacrifice,
the source of sacrifice is action.

\[15\] Know that action comes from *Brahman*,
and *Brahman* from the Imperishable.
And so the omnipresent *Brahman*
becomes eternal in sacrifice.

\[16\] Whoever fails to turn the wheel here
that has thus been set in motion
lives in vain, O Son of Pritha,
full of sensual pleasure and malice.

\[17\] Whoever is pleased by *Atman*,
satisfied only by the Self,
content in *Atman*, such a person
has no need to engage in action,

\[18\] Has no goal at all in action,
nor any goal in nonaction,
has no need of any being,
has no purpose whatsoever.

\[19\] So being always unattached,
do, Arjuna, what must be done!
One who acts with nonattachment
surely will attain transcendence.

\[20\] Janaka attained perfection,
and then others, by action only.
Just to hold the world together,
observe that you’re obliged to act!

\[21\] Whatever the greatest man does
all the others will imitate.
Wherever he sets the standard
the whole world will try to meet it.

[p. 23] \[22\] As for myself, there is nothing
in the three worlds for me to do,
nothing at all to be attained,
and yet I act, O Arjuna.

\[23\] If I were not at all engaged
in my weariless activity,
all the human race would follow
my example, Son of Pritha.

\[24\] These many worlds soon would perish
if I were to keep from acting.
I would cause a cataclysm
and destruction of these creatures.

\[25\] The unwise act from attachment
to their actions, O Bharata.
The wise should act without clinging,
intent on maintaining the world.

\[26\] But do not shatter the unwise minds
of those still attached to action.
Cause them to enjoy all actions,
by your discipline and wisdom.

\[27\] Every action is enacted
by the *gunas* born of matter.
Those deluded by the ego
think that they perform the actions.

\[28\] But one who knows the dual roles
of the *gunas* and of action
stays unattached with just this thought:
‘*Gunas* are at work with *gunas*.’

[p. 24] \[29\] Those confused about the *gunas*
are attached to *gunas*’ actions.
Those who know the whole should not
disturb dullards who know the part.

\[30\] Entrust to Me every action,
meditate on the highest Spirit;
free from desire and selfishness,
fever broken, fight in battle!

\[31\] Whichever human constantly
practices this teaching of Mine,
believing it, not disdainful,
is released from action’s bondage.

\[32\] But those who sneer at my teaching
and fail to put it into practice,
making a muddle of all wisdom—
regard them as lost and mindless.

\[33\] Even the wise act in accordance
with their own material nature.
Beings follow material nature.
What then can restraint accomplish?

\[34\] Passion and hatred are both seated
in the senses sensing objects.
Do not come under their power.
They are indeed your enemies.

\[35\] Better your own duty poorly done
than doing another’s duty well.
Better to die in your duty.
Another’s duty is perilous.”

[p. 25] Then Arjuna asked:

\[36\] “What then can impel a person
to commit this harmful evil
even against his will, O Krishna,
as if he were compelled by force?”

And the Blessed Lord answered:

\[37\] “Desire and anger are the force,
stemming from the *rajas guna*,
devouring and ruinous.
Know this as the great enemy.

\[38\] As Agni is obscured by smoke,
or as a mirror is by dust,
an embryo by its membrane,
so the mind is dimmed by passion.

\[39\] Even sages’ knowledge is dimmed
by this eternal enemy,
manifested as desire,
a fire that is insatiable.

\[40\] The senses, mind, and intellect
are said to be where it resides.
Along with these it confuses
the embodied soul, dims its knowledge.

\[41\] Therefore, first control the senses,
O Bull of the Bharatas.
Kill this demon that destroys
knowledge and discrimination.

[p. 26] \[42\] The senses are superior,
so they say, but mind is higher.
The intellect is higher still,
and the Self indeed much higher.

\[43\] Having learned what is much higher,
sustaining the self by the Self,
kill the enemy that takes the form
of intractable desire!”
