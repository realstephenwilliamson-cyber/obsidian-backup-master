### Urban Architecture and the Social Order

Adorno delivered this lecture – the first he gave on returning to Germany from emigration – on 9 December 1949 to a colloquium on urban architecture and planning at the Technische Hochschule in Darmstadt. It was published for the first time in Adorno, *Ein Bildmonographie*, ed. Theodor W. Adorno Archiv, Frankfurt am Main: Suhrkamp, 2003, pp. 214–28.

Sources for the lecture include the preparatory notes (Vt 21) entitled ‘Themes on Urban Architecture and Society’, materials that Adorno used as a basis for an improvised talk. The basis for the text published here is a transcription designated ‘hk’ that has been preserved as typescript (Vt 22) and as carbon copy (V23). The transcription is entitled: ‘Lecture by Prof. Adorno at the Colloquium on Urban Architecture and the Social Order at the Tech. College in Darmstadt on 9 December 1949’. Unfortunately, the beginning of the lecture is missing in the transcription.

### The Contemporary Relevance of Sociology

This lecture was delivered by Adorno on 23 February 1951 in the Auditorium Maximum of the University of Marburg. The context in which the lecture was given – a student-organized conference on ‘the sociology of the political’ – is illuminated by contemporary press reports surrounding the event. Three relevant newspaper articles have been preserved among Adorno’s literary remains: from the *Oberhessische Zeitung* (26 February), the *Marburger* 

*[p. 190]* ^pg197

*Presse* (27 February), and the *Neue Zeitung* (27 February). The article from the *Marburger Presse* commented:

The students from the Sociology Seminar, along with students in the field of the social sciences from Frankfurt and Darmstadt, organized a weekend conference on the theme of the ‘Sociology of the Political’. The conference got underway with a lecture from Prof. Th. W. Adorno (from the Institute for Social Sciences \[*sic*\] in Frankfurt) on the question of ‘The Contemporary Relevance of Sociology’. Prof. Graf Solms (Marburg) introduced the event and strongly welcomed this initiative on the part of the students. He challenged the widespread prejudices to which sociology is exposed and argued that there is nothing really sensational about the central concerns of sociology – as the conference would surely show. (Za 149)

A nine-page ‘schema for the Marburg lecture on “The Contemporary Relevance of Sociology”’ has been preserved among Adorno’s literary remains in the form of three typescripts that have been modified by hand (Vt 25–7), the last of which was revised by Adorno himself and served as a basis for his improvised lecture. The text as it is presented here has drawn on a transcription of the lecture that has been preserved as a typescript among Adorno’s literary remains (Vt 28). It was not subsequently revised in any way by Adorno.

### On the Relationship between Individual and Society Today

Adorno spoke on this subject on the final day of the seventeenth lecture series that was held in Bad Nauheim in connection with the sphere of higher education in the Hesse region between 3 and 13 February 1957.

*[p. 197]* ^pg204

Two weeks before presenting his lecture, Adorno wrote to Klaus Ziegler on 30 January 1957:

On 13 February I am to give a couple of lectures in Nauheim before officials of the Minister President of Hesse by way of conclusion to the Higher Education Conference – lectures on the question concerning ‘Individual and Society Today’. I do not have sufficient time to write them out in full beforehand, but the lectures will be recorded on tape. If I can manage to get hold of the transcriptions from the tapes quickly enough, then, with some intensive editing, I could certainly produce a text that you could accept for publication \[in a *Festschrift* for Helmuth Plessner\], and I would try and do so in such a way that as much as possible it avoids repeating the material in some earlier publications of mine, especially the essay on organization and the individual from the Darmstadt discussions \[see GS 8, pp. 440–56\]. (Br. 1708/15) The plan to incorporate the present lecture into the *Festschrift* for Plessner was subsequently abandoned.

A day after the event in Bad Nauheim was concluded, Adorno wrote to Max Horkheimer on 14 February:

The whole thing in Nauheim was also very gratifying, with prolonged applause and quite an impact. Since Gehlen had spoken there the week before, I got Habermas to put together a series of particularly striking quotations from Gehlen’s work for me, which I proceeded to criticize in my lecture. As a contrast, I adduced a few passages from the Economic and Philosophical Manuscripts and from the German Ideology which show just how much the concept of man can only be grasped as a socially mediated one. Both of the lectures – they were separated by a half-hour break and lasted about two hours in total – were focused specifically on this thesis, and I think I really managed to ‘get it across’, as they say so well in these parts. Any direct cases of overlap with your own lecture have been avoided, so that, if we wish, we both have these texts at our disposal for other possible occasions. ### The Purpose of Education in Relation to Students and Their Expectations

In the summer semester of 1957 the Chicago Committee at the Johann Wolfgang Goethe University in Frankfurt – in the context of an academic exchange programme, established in 1948, in which German and American scholars and teachers from the universities of Frankfurt and Chicago worked together on various projects – organized a seminar on the theme of ‘The University and Modern Society’. This furnished the occasion for Adorno’s lecture on ‘The Purpose of Education in Relation to Students and Their Expectations’, which was delivered on 12 July 1957. The lecture relates to the survey on ‘University and Society’ that was conducted by the Institute of Social Research between 1952 and 1954 (see GS 20.2, pp. 685–8) and to which Adorno refers in the course of his lecture.

The copy text for the text presented here is an incomplete sixteen-page tape transcription that is preserved in the Adorno Archive as a typescript with hand-written corrections (Vt 112). It was kept in a folder under the title ‘TWA / Lecture before the Chicago Committee of the University in “The Ends of Education”, 12.7.57 (tape transcription)’.

Unfortunately, a large part of Adorno’s text – possibly as much as half of it – has not survived. The opening passage is missing, but it is above all the middle section, where Adorno discusses the results of the survey on ‘The University and Society’, that is seriously incomplete. Although this part of the text cannot be supplied, the reader can be referred to an analysis of the results of the survey that was published by Christoph Oehler. ### Human Society Today

Adorno presented these reflections on ‘Human Society Today’ in the context of the twentieth session of the Hesse lecture series held in Bad Wildungen on 16 October 1957. This was the final day of the lecture series that had begun on 6 October under the general rubric of ‘Society and the State’.

Adorno’s text was printed under the title ‘The Contradictory Society’ in the proceedings of the Hesse lecture series, in *Vorträge, gehalten anläßlich der Hessischen Hochschulwochen*, Vol. 20, 1958, pp. 251–72. Once again Adorno prefaces the printed version with the reservations about the publication of freely delivered lectures that he had expressed in connection with the lecture on ‘Culture and *Kultur*’ (see the relevant editorial comments before the notes for that lecture in *Lectures 1949–1968*, Vol. 1, pp. 204–6).

Three pages of typescript have been preserved (Vt 114) containing the preparatory notes which Adorno used as a basis for his extemporized lecture. Adorno made some further additions to the typescript in pencil and in ink.

### Is Superstition Harmless?

‘Superstition’ is not just a theme in Adorno’s published writings but one that comes up again and again in his lectures delivered between 1958 and 1962. If he had already formulated the ‘Theses against Occultism’ in *Minima Moralia*, written between 1944 and 1947 (GS 4, pp. 273–80) and investigated the social-psychological dimensions of the astrology columns of the *Los Angeles Times* between 1952 and 1953 (GS 9.2, pp. 7–120), he had also been involved in live discussions of this subject. On 19 April 1958 Adorno delivered a lecture entitled ‘Superstition at Second Hand’ at the Congress of the German Society for Psychotherapy and Depth Psychology in Wiesbaden (a later version is available in GS 8, pp. 146–76) and also took part in a round table discussion on ‘Security and Magic’. He repeated the Wiesbaden lecture in a slightly modified form at the invitation of the Abendakadamie and Volkshochschule in Mannheim and delivered it in the Kunsthalle in Mannheim on 30 January 1959. On that occasion he used the title ‘Superstition at Second Hand: On the Social Psychology of the Fashion for Astrology’. On 25 April 1959 he participated in the Hesse lecture series in Bad Wildungen again and spoke on ‘The Sociology of Superstition’. This lecture appeared in 1960 in vol. 27 of the proceedings of the Hesse lecture series (and was also repeated five years later as a radio talk for Sender Freies Berlin). On 23 May 1962 Adorno delivered his lecture ‘On the Sociology of Superstition’ in Berlin.

Adorno addressed the question ‘Is Superstition Harmless?’ on 30 November 1962 in the context of the Hesse lecture series in Bad Nauheim. The lecture remained unpublished and, unlike a number of his other contributions, was not printed in the proceedings of the Hesse lecture series. Instead, at Adorno’s request, ‘The Sociology of Superstition’ (from vol. 27) was reprinted in vol. 41 in 1964.
