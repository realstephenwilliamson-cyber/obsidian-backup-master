## About

A very short dialogue in the Platonic corpus whose authorship is disputed. Clitophon answers a report that he has criticised Socrates and praised Thrasymachus: Socrates, he says, can awaken a desire for virtue but cannot teach how to attain it. Socrates says almost nothing.

- **People:** Clitophon and Socrates, with Thrasymachus mentioned. Wikipedia describes the historical Clitophon as an Athenian politician active in 411–405 BC, linked with Theramenes and Euripides, and a companion of Thrasymachus. In *Republic* I he speaks up for Thrasymachus' view of justice (340a–b).
- **Setting and dramatic date:** Wikipedia gives neither.
- **Composition and authenticity:** Wikipedia says only that it was written in the 4th century BC. Diogenes Laërtius and other ancient writers cited it without suspicion, and Plutarch knew it well. Ficino's translation of 1491 and the Aldine edition of 1513 denied it to Plato, and many 19th-century scholars, Schleiermacher among them, rejected it. S. R. Slings judged it probably spurious in 1981 but accepted it in his Cambridge edition of 1999. The IEP lists it among the *dubia*.
- **Length:** the shortest dialogue in the traditional corpus (Stephanus 406a–410e).
- **Subject:** the limits of protreptic, speech that urges people toward virtue. Slings reads the dialogue as a criticism of philosophical protreptic rather than of Socrates, favouring the elenchus; Brünnecke, Kester and Souilhe read it as an attack on Antisthenes.
- **Place among the dialogues:** Thrasyllus grouped it with the *Republic*, *Timaeus* and *Critias*. Scholars have read it as a criticism of *Republic* I (Grube), a work to which the rest of the *Republic* replies (Annas), or a fragment or preface of the *Republic* (Bury, Shorey, Grote); Slings does not endorse the preface view. The IEP says it might be early, early transitional or middle.
