### 4.1–11: Vision of heavenly throne

*[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 547* ^4-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-1|1–2]]:** John is again in or still assumed into an ecstatic state (*in the spirit*—the capitalization of “Spirit” in some translations derives from Christian theology). His ascent to the heavenly throne room is immediate and unmediated, unlike other apocalyptic ascent narratives like Daniel, *1 Enoch*, and the *Testament of Levi*, suggesting a recognition that prophetic ecstasy itself can bring one into the heavenly world (see Isa 6). ^4-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-3|3]]:** The vague reference to *one seated there* and the miner-alogical similes are inspired by [[Ezekiel 01#^1-16|Ezek 1.16]],[[Ezekiel 01#^1-26|26–27]]; [[Daniel 10#^10-6|Dan 10.6]]. ^4-3

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-4|4]]:** *Elders*, are John’s obscure innovation, unparalleled in any other apocalyptic vision. It is unclear whether they represent an angelic rank, ascended patriarchs, Jewish followers of Jesus, or some synthesis of these alternatives, but they subsequently serve as guides to and commentators on what is happening (11.16; 19.4). *Twenty-four thrones . . . elders*, a multiple of twelve, signifying perfection; see “The Numerology of Revelation,” p. 550. *White robes . . . crowns*, see 1.14n.; 2.10n. ^4-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-5|5]]:** [[Ezekiel 01#^1-4|Ezek 1.4–14]] may inspire this vision, but such imagery also occurs frequently around visions of God in Jewish apocalyptic literature (*4 Ezra* 13.1–11; *Apoc. Abr*. 17–18; *1 En*. 14); it draws on ancient Canaanite and Israelite traditions of storm theophanies (Ex 19; [[Isaiah 06#^6-1|Isa 6.1–4]]; Ps 29; [[Daniel 07#^7-9|Dan 7.9]]). *Seven spirits*, 1.4n. ^4-5

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Sea of glass*, the heavenly sea became a standard motif in Jewish ascent narratives (*1 En*. 14.10) and may be related to the dome (Heb “raqia”) in [[Ezekiel 01#^1-22|Ezek 1.22]] (see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-6|Gen 1.6]]). That it is *of glass* indicates that this sea is a boundary, not a place of fish and sea monsters. *Living creatures,* (Heb “chaiʾim”; Gk “zoa”) are inspired by [[Ezekiel 01#^1-5|Ezek 1.5–11]]. ^4-6

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-8|8]]:** By imagining the *creatures* with *six wings* and singing a version of the “Kedushah” ([[Isaiah 06#^6-1|Isa 6.1–3]]), John incorporates Isaiah’s vision; multiple *eyes* evoke the wheels on the “merkavah” (heavenly chariot-throne) of [[Ezekiel 01#^1-18|Ezek 1.18]]. ^4-8

**[[NRSVue Revelation 04#^4-9|9–11]]:** Heavenly *glory* and perfection are revealed in liturgical drama and responsive praise-songs, reminiscent of the “Sabbath Songs” of Qumran (4QShirShabba) that imagine the various ranks of angels of the Temple sanctuary calling upon one another in rhythmic verses to praise God. See [[Daniel 07#^7-9|Dan 7.9–10]]. *Our Lord and God*, is celebrated as the one God of creation. Acclaiming God for creation is a common theme in hymns of praise (e.g., [[Psalms 008#^8-4|Ps 8.3]]; 33.6–9; [[Psalms 102#^102-26|102.25]]). ^4-9
