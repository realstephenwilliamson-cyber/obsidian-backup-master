**1.** Women, slaves, and minors are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah of] sukkah.[^1] A *tumtum* and an *androgynous* are obligated because of the doubt [concerning their status].[^2] Similarly, a person who is half slave and half free is obligated.[^3]  
A minor who does not require his mother's [presence][^4] - i.e., a child of five or six[^5] - is obligated [to fulfill the mitzvah] of sukkah according to Rabbinic decree, to train him in [the performance of] mitzvot.[^6] ^6-1

**2.** The sick and their attendants[^7] are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah. This applies not only to a person who is dangerously ill,[^8] but also to one with a headache[^9] or a sore eye.[^10]  
A person who is uncomfortable [when dwelling in the sukkah] is freed from the obligation [to fulfill the mitzvah] of sukkah.[^11] This applies to the person himself, but not to his attendants.[^12]  
Who is "a person who is uncomfortable [when dwelling in the sukkah]"? A person who cannot sleep in the sukkah because of the wind or because of the flies, mites, or the like, or because of the smell.[^13] ^6-2

**3.** A mourner[^14] is obligated [to fulfill the mitzvah] of sukkah.[^15]  
A groom,[^16] his attendants,[^17] and all the members of the wedding party[^18] are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah throughout the seven days of [the wedding] festivities.[^19] ^6-3

**4.** Emissaries charged with a mission involving a mitzvah[^20] are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah[^21] both during the day and at night.[^22] People who journey during the day are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah during the day[^23] and are obligated at night.[^24] People who journey during the night are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah at night[^25] and are obligated during the day.[^26]  
A city's day watchmen are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah during the day[^27] and are obligated at night.[^28] Its night watchmen are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] of sukkah at night and are obligated during the day. The watchmen of gardens and orchards[^29] are freed from [fulfilling the mitzvah] during the day and the night, because if the watchman constructs a sukkah, a thief will realize that the watchman has a fixed place and will go to steal from another place.[^30] ^6-4

**5.** How must the mitzvah of dwelling in the sukkah be fulfilled? A person must eat, drink, and live[^31] in the sukkah throughout all seven days [of the festival],[^32] both during the day and at night,[^33] in the same manner as he dwells in his home throughout the year.[^34]  
During these seven days, he must consider his house as a temporary dwelling and the sukkah as his permanent home,[^35] as \[[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-42|Leviticus 23:42]]] states: "You shall dwell in sukkot for seven days."  
What does this imply? His attractive utensils and attractive bedding [should be brought] to the sukkah. His drinking utensils - i.e., his cups and crystal pitchers - [should be brought] to the sukkah.[^36] However, utensils used for food - i.e., pans and plates - [may be left] outside the sukkah.[^37] A candelabra[^38] [should be brought] to the sukkah.[^39] However, if the sukkah is small, it should be left outside the sukkah.[^40] ^6-5

**6.** We should eat, drink, and sleep in the sukkah through the entire seven days [of the festival], both during the day and at night. It is forbidden to eat a meal[^41] outside the sukkah for the entire seven [day period].[^42] However, [there is no prohibition] if one eats a snack[^43] the measure of a *k'beitzah* or less,[^44] or even slightly more.[^45]  
One may not sleep outside the sukkah at all, even a brief nap.[^46] It is permissible to drink water[^47] and eat fruit outside the sukkah.[^48] However, a person who follows the stringency of not drinking even water outside the sukkah is worthy of praise.[^49] ^6-6

**7.** Eating in the sukkah on the first night of the festival is an obligation.[^50] If a person eats merely a *k'zayit* of bread,[^51] he fulfills his obligation. Afterwards, [the matter is left to one's] volition.[^52] If one desires to eat a meal,[^53] one must eat it in the sukkah. If one desires, throughout the seven [days of the festival], one may eat only fruit or roasted grain[^54] outside of the sukkah.[^55] The same laws apply as those regarding the eating of matzah on Pesach.[^56] ^6-7

**8.** It is forbidden for a person to sit and eat with his head and the majority of his body inside a sukkah while his table is in his home or outside the sukkah.[^57] It is considered as if he did not eat inside the sukkah.[^58] [Rather,] the table must also be inside the sukkah. This was decreed lest one be drawn after one's table. This law applies even in a large sukkah. ^6-8

**9.** Throughout the seven days [of the festival], a person should read in the sukkah.[^59] However, when he attempts to comprehend what he reads in depth and appreciate its details, he should do so outside the sukkah,[^60] so that his mind will be settled.[^61]  
When a person prays,[^62] he may pray inside the sukkah or outside the sukkah, as he desires.[^63] ^6-9

**10.** If rain descends, a person may enter his home.[^64] When is one permitted to leave [the sukkah]?[^65] When enough raindrops descend into the sukkah so that they would spoil a cooked dish[^66] - even a dish of beans[^67] - were they to fall into it.[^68]  
If a person was eating in the sukkah and rain descended, and hence, he entered his home, if the rains stop we do not obligate him to return to his sukkah[^69] {that entire night}[^70] until he is finished eating.[^71]  
If he was sleeping and rain descended,[^72] and hence, he entered his home, we do not obligate him to return to his sukkah that entire night should the rains cease.[^73] Rather, he may remain sleeping in his house that entire night[^74] until dawn.[^75] ^6-10

**11.** A person should not take apart his sukkah after he finishes eating on the seventh day [of Sukkot].[^76] However, from the afternoon on, he may take down his utensils and remove them.[^77]  
If he has no place to put the utensils,[^78] he should reduce its space[^79] by at least four handbreadths by four handbreadths.[^80]  
If he has to eat later that day,[^81] he must eat in the sukkah, because the mitzvah extends throughout the seven days. ^6-11

**12.** Whenever a person enters a sukkah with the intention of sitting down throughout the seven [days of Sukkot], he should recite the following blessing[^82] before sitting:  
[Blessed[^83] are You...] who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to dwell in the sukkah.[^84]  
On the first night of the festival, one should first recite the blessing on the sukkah, and then the blessing for the occasion.[^85]  
One should recite all the blessings over a cup of wine.[^86] Thus, one should recite *kiddush*[^87] while standing,[^88] recite the blessing *leishev basukkah*,[^89] sit, and then recite the blessing *shehecheyanu*.[^90]  
This was the custom of my teachers and the Rabbis[^91] of Spain:[^92] to recite *kiddush* while standing on the first night of the Sukkot festival, as explained. ^6-12

**13.** At present,[^93] when we celebrate holidays for two days,[^94] we dwell in the sukkah for eight days.[^95] On the eighth day, which is the first day of the holiday of Shemini Atzeret,[^96] we dwell in the sukkah, but do not recite the blessing *leishev basukkah*.[^97]  
Similarly, a *tumtum* and an *androgynous*[^98] never recite the blessing *leishev basukkah*, because their obligation [to perform the mitzvah is based] on doubt,[^99] and a blessing is never recited when one is doubtful [of one's obligation].[^100] ^6-13

**14.** After a person finishes eating on the eighth day [of Sukkot], he should take down his utensils and remove them.[^101]  
When a person has no place to put the utensils,[^102] [the following rules[^103] apply]: If it is a small [sukkah], he should bring his candelabra into it;[^104] if it is a large sukkah, he should bring in his pots, plates, and the like,[^105] to bring to mind that it[^106] is no longer acceptable and that its mitzvah is completed. Since the day is a holiday, one may not reduce its space and nullify it.[^107] ^6-14

**15.** A person who did not construct a sukkah [before the holiday][^108] - whether intentionally or unintentionally - should construct a sukkah on Chol Hamo'ed.[^109] One should even construct a sukkah on the final moments of the seventh day, because its mitzvah lasts throughout the seventh day.[^110]  
The wood with which the sukkah was constructed is forbidden [to be used for other purposes][^111] on all eight days of the festival.[^112] This applies to both the wood used for the walls and the wood used for the *s'chach*.[^113] Throughout [these] eight days,[^114] no benefit may be derived from it for other purposes.[^115]  
[They are prohibited on the eighth day] because the sukkah is *muktzeh*[^116] the entire seventh day, including the period *beyn hash'mashot*.[^117] Since it was *muktzeh* during the period *beyn hash'mashot*, it is *muktzeh* on the entire day [that follows].[^118] ^6-15

**16.** Similarly, it is forbidden to take from the food and beverages that were hung in the sukkah as decorations[^119] for all eight days [of the festival].[^120] However, if at the time one hung [the decorations],[^121] he made the condition: "I will not refrain from using them during the entire period of *beyn hash'mashot*,"[^122] he is entitled to use them whenever he desires,[^123] because he did not set them aside, nor did the sanctity of the sukkah encompass them, nor are they considered part of it. ^6-16
