## About

A Sanskrit Upapurana of the Shakta tradition, devoted to the goddess and above all to Kamakhya and the sacred land of Kamarupa, in modern Assam. It is attributed to the sage Markandeya and is also called the Kali Purana or Kalika Tantra.

- **Place:** most probably composed in or near medieval Kamarupa. Hazra argued for an older text from Bengal; Biswanarayan Shastri rejected this, pointing to local geography, the Naraka myth tied to Kamarupa's dynasties, the Brahmaputra myth and the claim that Kamarupa is holier than Varanasi.
- **Date:** Rocher reports estimates for different sections from the 7th to the 12th century, some placing it in the 11th–12th because it names King Dharmapala of Kamarupa. Wikipedia, citing Shastri (1994), places it after the 10th century, but the Bagchee description of Shastri's translation says it was composed in Kamarupa in the late ninth century. Rosati (2017) says it was probably compiled no later than the tenth-eleventh century.
- **Contents:** 90 to 93 chapters, depending on the version. It opens with the goddess drawing Shiva back from asceticism into household life, treats Sati, Parvati, Kali and others as forms of one feminine power, details Kamakhya's worship and describes Kamarupa's rivers and mountains. Chapters 67–78, the Rudhiradhyaya, set out animal sacrifice and narrow conditions for human sacrifice. A 2022 notice of van Kooij's later book quotes its preface: the Tantric teaching was "polished and abridged in order to make the contents suitable to a lay audience, in this case, no doubt, a royal patron of Kāmarūpa".
- **Editions:** first printed by the Venkateshvara Press, Bombay, in Saka 1813 (1891 CE); a later edition from the same press is dated Saka 1829 (1907 CE), and the Vangavasi Press, Calcutta, issued one with a Bengali translation in 1316 Bengali Samvat (1909 CE). Shastri edited the text for the Chowkhamba Sanskrit Series, Varanasi (1972, per the Kerala University catalogue), and published a verse-by-verse English translation with Nag Publishers.
- **Van Kooij:** K. R. van Kooij's *Worship of the Goddess According to the Kalikapurana*, Part I (Leiden: Brill, 1972; Orientalia Rheno-Traiectina 14; also an Utrecht thesis) translates chapters 54–69 with introduction and notes. His *Tantric Teachings of the Kalika Purana* followed from Brill in 2021.
- **Why it matters:** Rosati calls it the earliest text devoted to the worship of Kamakhya. Wikipedia notes it is one of the rare Hindu texts that uses the word "Hindu".
