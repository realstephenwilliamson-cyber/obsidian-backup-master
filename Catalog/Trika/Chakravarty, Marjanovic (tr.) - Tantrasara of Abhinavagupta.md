## About

A Sanskrit work by Abhinavagupta, the best-known historical exponent of the Trika or Kashmir Shaiva tradition, that condenses his much longer *Tantraloka*. The title means "Essence of Tantra".

- **Author:** Abhinavagupta. Wikipedia gives his life as c. 950–1016 CE; Britannica says only that he flourished in 1014, in Kashmir. Britannica calls him an outstanding representative of the Pratyabhijna (recognition) school of Kashmiri Shaiva monism and a prolific writer on philosophy and aesthetics.
- **Relation to the Tantraloka:** Wikipedia's article on Abhinavagupta calls the *Tantrasara* a prose summary of the *Tantraloka*. The *Tantraloka*, written in the 10th century in 37 chapters on ritual and philosophy, is his most important work, described as a synthesis of the whole Trika system and an encyclopedia of nondual Hindu Tantra.
- **Further abridgements:** the summary was itself condensed again in the *Tantroccaya* and, finally, in a very short form called the *Tantravatadhanika*.
- **Note on sources:** the Wikipedia article on the *Tantrasara* is a stub. It rests the condensation claim on Paul Muller-Ortega's *The Triadic Heart of Siva* (SUNY Press; Wikipedia dates it 1989, the publisher 1988), and links an online English translation.
- **Gaps:** none of the pages opened gives the *Tantrasara*'s date of composition, its number of chapters, its printed editions, or the publication details of the Chakravarty–Marjanovic translation named in the vault's catalog note.
- **Why it matters:** it gives the content of the *Tantraloka*, the central synthesis of the Trika system, in a much shorter form, and stands in a chain of Abhinavagupta's own summaries of that work.
