# Luke 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Luke 02|Luke 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Luke 01|NRSVue]] · [[Luke 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Luke - Introduction]]

### 1.1–4: Prologue

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 109* · Comprising one Greek sentence, the opening follows Hellenistic conventions (see Josephus, *J.W*. 1.17; *Ag. Ap*. 1.1–18). ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Orderly account*, perhaps suggesting Luke’s dissatisfaction with other versions of the gospel story. *Fulfilled*, suggests prophetic fulfillment. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Most excellent Theophilus*, the address (also [[Acts 01#^1-1|Acts 1.1]]) suggests a patron. Whether Theophilus (a common name meaning “friend of God”) is an actual or ideal reader cannot be determined. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Instructed*, Gk “katēchēthēs,” whence “catechism” (see Josephus, *Life* 336; Philo, *Gaius* 295–97), the ideal reader likely has some knowledge of Jesus, and Luke seeks to insure it is correct and complete. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-5|1.5–10]]:** **Zechariah and Elizabeth.** ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Days of King Herod*, ruled ca. 37–4 BCE; [[Matthew 02#^2-1|Mt 2.1]],[[Matthew 02#^2-15|15]] places Jesus’ birth during Herod’s latter years. *Priestly order of Abijah*, the eighth of twenty-four divisions of Levites ([[I Chronicles 24#^24-10|1 Chr 24.10]]); each division served in the Jerusalem Temple twice annually. *Aaron*, Moses’ brother and progenitor of the priestly line. ^1-5-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Righteous . . . living blamelessly . . . commandments and regulations*, Luke emphasizes their halakhic fidelity. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Elizabeth was barren . . . getting on in years*, the elderly, faithful, childless couple anticipates an angelic annunciation and the conception of a special son ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^11-30|Gen 11.30]]; see also 16.1; 25.21; 29.31; [[Judges 13#^13-2|Judg 13.2–3]]; [[I Samuel 01#^1-2|1 Sam 1.2]]; cf. [[II Kings 04#^4-14|2 Kings 4.14]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Chosen by lot*, see [[Acts 01#^1-26|Acts 1.26]]; *m. Tamid* 5.2–6.3. *Offer incense*, there being so many priests, this was likely Zechariah’s only time and also a special privilege. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Time of the incense offering*, see [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^30-7|Ex 30.7–8]] (see also [[Psalms 141#^141-2|Ps 141.2]]); Josephus (*Ant*. 13.10.3) states that the Maccabean king, John Hyrcanus, received a divine revelation during the incense offering. The combination of incense offering, public worship, and daily service may suggest the afternoon “Tamid” service (*m. Tamid* 6.3–7.3). *Whole assembly* (lit., “All the number of the people”), Luke here portrays the Jews in Jerusalem as faithful and pious. ^1-10

### 1.11–23: Annunciation of John’s conception

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-11|Text]], p. 109* ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Angel*, heavenly messengers (see 1.7n.) gained distinct personalities in postbiblical Jewish texts. ^1-11-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Afraid*, common response to an appearance of an angel or divinity. *Prayer has been heard*, suggesting that Zechariah had prayed for a child. *You will name him*, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^16-11|Gen 16.11]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-19|17.19]]; see also [[Luke 01#^1-31|Lk 1.31]]. *John* (Heb “Yoḥanan”), “YHWH is gracious,” a name attested in [[Jeremiah 40#^40-8|Jer 40.8]] and increasingly in later Jewish texts. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Wine or strong drink*, indicting priestly requirements ([[Torah 26 - Shmini#^10-9|Lev 10.9]]) and connecting John to Samson, Samuel, and to Nazirite status ([[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-1|Num 6.1–4]]; [[I Samuel 01#^1-11|1 Sam 1.11]],[[I Samuel 01#^1-22|22]]; see also [[Judges 13#^13-4|Judg 13.4–6]]). *Holy Spirit*, a major Lukan theme, indicating divine presence and affirmation (e.g., [[Psalms 051#^51-13|Ps 51.11]] \[51.13 LXX\]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-17|Wis 9.17]]); the concept developed later in Jewish thought primarily in the “Shekhinah” (derived from Heb “dwell” \[e.g., [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^29-45|Ex 29.45]]\], a cognate of the Heb “mishkan,” or “tabernacle”), the feminine presence of God that dwells with Israel (e.g., *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 39#39a|39a]],103b; *b. Yoma* [[Yoma 56#56b|56b]]). The connection to Nazirites, and especially to [[Judges 13#^13-25|Judg 13.25]] (the Spirit inspires Samson), suggests that John, like Samson, will begin the process of saving Israel. ^1-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-16|16]]:** *People of Israel*, Jews; the eschatological promises come first to Israel. ^1-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Elijah*, the prophet (1 Kings 17–19;21), expected to announce the messianic age ([[Malachi 3#^3-23|Mal 3.23]] \[4.5\]; [[Matthew 11#^11-14|Mt 11.14]]); his traditional appearance at the Passover seder anticipates that final liberation. *Turn the hearts of parents*, Elijah’s role in [[Malachi 3#^3-24|Mal 3.24]] \[4.6\]; [[LXX Sirach 48#^48-10|Sir 48.10]]. *Lord* (Gk “kyrios”), can mean “sir” (Heb “ ʾadon”), but it is also the standard LXX translation of Heb “YHWH”; Christians accord this title to Jesus. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-18|18]]:** *How will I know*, asking angels for a sign connects Zechariah with Gideon ([[Judges 06#^6-13|Judg 6.13]]). *My wife is getting on*, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-12|Gen 18.12]]. ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Gabriel*, with Michael, one of two angels named in the Tanakh ([[Daniel 08#^8-16|Dan 8.16]]; see also *1 En*. 9,20,40; *Jub*. 2.18; 54.6; 1QH 6.13; 1QM 9.14–16; 15.14). In [[Daniel 09#^9-20|Dan 9.20–25]], Gabriel announces the messianic age. *Presence of God*, the heavenly throne-room. *Good news*, the noun form of this term, “euangellion,” “gospel” was in Rome, used for secular matters, e.g., tax relief or the emperor’s birthday; for the LXX ([[Isaiah 40#^40-9|Isa 40.9]]; [[Psalms 040#^40-10|Ps. 40.9]] \[HB v 10\]; 68.11 \[HB v 12\]; 96.2, and elsewhere), the verbal form \[Heb “basar,” noun “besorah”\] can refer to divine salvation. ^1-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Become mute*, see [[Ezekiel 03#^3-26|Ezek 3.26]]; [[Ezekiel 24#^24-27|24.27]]; [[Ezekiel 33#^33-22|33.22]]. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Vision in the sanctuary*, see [[Acts 22#^22-17|Acts 22.17–21]]. ^1-22

### 1.24–25: John’s conception

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-24|Text]], p. 110* ^1-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-25|25]]:** *Disgrace*, see [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^30-23|Gen 30.23]] in reference to Rachel’s giving birth to Joseph. God opens and closes wombs ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^16-2|Gen 16.2]]; [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^25-21|25.21]]; [[I Samuel 01#^1-1|1 Sam 1.1–18]]). These texts do not ascribe infertility to sin (contrary to some contemporary interpretations). ^1-25

### 1.26–38: The annunciation

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-26|Text]], p. 110* ^1-26

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-26|26]]:** *Sixth month*, after John’s conception. In contrast to dating according to political leaders (1.5; 3.1), Luke now prioritizes the dates of pregnancy. *Gabriel*, see 1.19n. *Galilee*, northern Israel. *Nazareth*, a small village ([[John 01#^1-46|Jn 1.46]]) in southern Galilee, unattested in first-century literature outside the NT. Although Matthew and Luke locate Jesus’ birth in Bethlehem ([[Matthew 02#^2-6|Mt 2.6]] specifies Judea), Jesus was known as being from Nazareth (see, e.g., [[John 01#^1-46|Jn 1.46]]). ^1-26-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-27|27]]:** *Virgin* (Gk “parthenos”), Luke emphasizes the term (also v 34), but does not cite [[LXX Isaiah 07#^7-14|Isa 7.14]] \[LXX\] (as does [[Matthew 01#^1-23|Mt 1.23]]). *Engaged*, the wedding contract (“ketubah”) had been signed. *M. Avot* 5.21 sets the ideal marital age for a man at eighteen; Josephus, following Roman norms, married at about thirty. Women usually married in their late teens. *House of David*, one dominant Jewish view was that the Messiah would be David’s descendant ([[II Samuel 07#^7-12|2 Sam 7.12]]). See 20.41–44n. *Mary* (Gk “Mariam”), from Heb “Miryam”; the name, recollecting Moses’ sister Miriam and Herod’s Hasmonean wife Mariamme, was common among first-century Jewish women. ^1-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-28|28]]:** *Greetings*, traditional Greek salutation, also translated “Hail” as in “Hail Mary” (Lat “Ave *among women* Maria”). *Favored one*, Lat “gratia plena,” “full of grace.” *The Lord is with you*, conventional greeting and affirmation ([[Judges 06#^6-12|Judg 6.12]]; [[II Samuel 07#^7-3|2 Sam 7.3]]; [[II Chronicles 15#^15-2|2 Chr 15.2]]). ^1-28

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-29|29]]:** *Perplexed* (Gk “diatarassō”); NRSV translates the related term (“tarassō”) in 1.12, referring to Zechariah, as “terrified.” ^1-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-31|31]]:** *You will name him*, see 1.13n. Jesus, Gk “Iesous,” from Heb “Yehoshua,” “the Lord saves” ([[Matthew 01#^1-21|Mt 1.21]]), common name in the Tanakh (variants include “Joshua” and “Hosea”) and subsequently. ^1-31

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-32|32]]:** *Son of the Most High*, indicating royal authority ([[II Samuel 07#^7-13|2 Sam 7.13–16]]) and rectitude ([[LXX Sirach 04#^4-10|Sir 4.10]]; see also [[Daniel 07#^7-25|Dan 7.25]]); “Most High” translates the Heb “Elyon” (e.g., [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^14-18|Gen 14.18–22]]; [[Psalms 018#^18-14|Ps 18.14]] \[HB\]; 78.35). *Throne of his ancestor David*, see v 27n.; 20.41–44nn.; on the promises to David, see also [[Jeremiah 23#^23-5|Jer 23.5–8]]; Ps 89; *Pss. Sol*. 17.4,6,21; etc. ^1-32

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-33|33]]:** *House of Jacob*, synonym for Israel ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-3|Ex 19.3]]; [[Isaiah 46#^46-3|Isa 46.3]]; [[Isaiah 48#^48-1|48.1]]; [[Jeremiah 02#^2-4|Jer 2.4]]; [[Psalms 114#^114-1|Ps 114.1]], and elsewhere). *Kingdom there will be no end*, see [[II Samuel 07#^7-13|2 Sam 7.13]],[[II Samuel 07#^7-16|16]] (Heb “ ʿad ʿolam,” “forever,”) concerning David’s dynasty. ^1-33

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-35|35]]:** *Holy Spirit . . . overshadow*, see 1.15n.; [[Torah 23 - Pekudei#^40-35|Ex 40.35]]. *Son of God*, in 3.38, Adam is a “son of God”; see [[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]. The term can also refer to divinities, e.g., [[Psalms 029#^29-1|Ps 29.1]]. The virginal conception is explicit only in [[Matthew 01#^1-23|Mt 1.23]]. No Jewish texts regard [[Isaiah 07#^7-14|Isa 7.14]] as predicting the Messiah’s birth or take *Son of God* as indicating anything other than divine adoption ([[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]; 4QFlor 10–13). ^1-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-36|36]]:** *Your relative Elizabeth*, indicating Mary’s priestly ancestry. ^1-36

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-37|37]]:** *Nothing will be impossible*, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-14|Gen 18.14]] concerning Isaac’s birth; see also [[Jeremiah 32#^32-17|Jer 32.17]]; [[Zechariah 08#^8-6|Zech 8.6]]. ^1-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-38|38]]:** *Servant*, Gk “doulē,” “slave.” ^1-38

### 1.39–45: The visitation

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-39|Text]], p. 111* · Mary’s meeting with Elizabeth confirms the relative roles of John the Baptist and Jesus. ^1-39

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-39|39]]:** *Judean*, in southern Israel, where Jerusalem is located. ^1-39-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-41|41]]:** *Filled with the Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n. ^1-41

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-43|43]]:** *Lord* (Gk “Kyrios”), here a divine title. ^1-43

### 1.46–56: Mary’s Magnificat

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-46|Text]], p. 111* · The first of four canticles in Lk 1–2. The song is modeled on Hannah’s prayer ([[I Samuel 02#^2-1|1 Sam 2.1–10]]; for similar songs with political implications by women see [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^15-19|Ex 15.19–20]] \[Miriam\]; [[Judges 05#^5-1|Judg 5.1–31]] \[Deborah\]; [[LXX Judith 16#^16-1|Jdt 16.1–17]] \[Judith\]). ^1-46

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-46|46]]:** *Mary said*, Several Latin manuscripts attribute the Magnificat to Elizabeth. *Magnifies*, Lat “Magnificat.” ^1-46-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-48|48]]:** See [[LXX 1 Kingdoms 01#^1-11|1 Sam 1.11]]\[LXX\]; Mary’s *lowliness* is the same Gk word (“tapeinōsis,” “low estate”) as Hannah’s “misery” (Heb “oni”). ^1-48

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-49|49]]:** *Done great things*, see [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-21|Deut 10.21]]. *Holy is his name*, see 11.1n. ^1-49

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-50|50]]:** *Fear him*, see 12.5n.; [[Proverbs 01#^1-7|Prov 1.7]]. *Generation to generation* (Heb “dor \[le\]ʾdor”), see [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^17-16|Ex 17.16]]; [[Isaiah 34#^34-10|Isa 34.10]]; [[Psalms 079#^79-13|Ps 79.13]]; *T. Levi* 18.8, and elsewhere. ^1-50

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-54|54]]:** *Servant Israel*, see [[Isaiah 44#^44-1|Isa 44.1]]; [[Psalms 136#^136-22|Ps 136.22]]; [[I Chronicles 16#^16-13|1 Chr 16.13]]. ^1-54

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-55|55]]:** *Promise he made*, see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-3|Gen 12.3]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|15.5]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-7|17.7]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-18|18.18]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-17|22.17]]; [[Micah 7#^7-20|Mic 7.20]], and elsewhere. Luke alludes to the covenants both with David and with *Abraham*, as well as emphasizes the promises to Israel first, and then, via Israel, to the nations. ^1-55

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-57|1.57–66]]:** **John’s birth and naming.** ^1-57

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-59|59]]:** *Circumcise*, sign of the covenant (Heb “brit”) between God and Israel, in accord with [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-11|Gen 17.11–12]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^21-4|21.4]]; [[Torah 27 - Tazria#^12-3|Lev 12.3]]; see also [[Luke 02#^2-21|Lk 2.21]]; *m. Shabb*. 18.3. *Zechariah after his father* ([[LXX Tobit 01#^1-1|Tob 1.1]]; Josephus, *J.W*. 5.13.2; *Ant*. 14.1.3), a tradition maintained in Sephardic (Mediterranean) but not Ashkenazic (Eastern European) Jewish culture; in the latter, children are named after deceased relatives. Naming sons at circumcision is not attested in Jewish sources until the eighth century. ^1-59

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-60|60]]:** *John*, see 1.13n. ^1-60

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-66|66]]:** *Hand of the Lord*, see [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^9-3|Ex 9.3]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-3|16.3]]; [[Joshua 04#^4-24|Josh 4.24]]; [[Joshua 22#^22-31|22.31]]; [[Isaiah 41#^41-20|Isa 41.20]]; [[Isaiah 66#^66-14|66.14]]; [[Ezekiel 33#^33-22|Ezek 33.22]] (also with reference to muteness) and elsewhere, often in contexts of judgment. ^1-66

### 1.67–79: The “Benedictus”

*[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-67|Text]], p. 112* · (Lat “blessed”; see v 68). ^1-67

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-67|67]]:** *Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n.; [[Joel 3#^3-1|Joel 2.28–31]] ([[Acts 02#^2-17|Acts 2.17–18]]) on the connection between the *Spirit* and prophecy. ^1-67-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-68|68]]:** *Blessed be the Lord*, familiar blessing ([[I Samuel 25#^25-32|1 Sam 25.32]]; [[I Kings 01#^1-48|1 Kings 1.48]]; [[I Kings 08#^8-15|8.15]]; etc.). *Redeemed*, Zechariah speaks as if redemption has already occurred (2.38; 24.21n.). ^1-68

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-69|69]]:** *Mighty savior*, lit., “horn (Heb “qeren”) of salvation,” see [[I Samuel 02#^2-10|1 Sam 2.10]]; [[II Samuel 22#^22-3|2 Sam 22.3]]; [[Psalms 018#^18-3|Ps 18.2]]; see also [[Psalms 089#^89-18|Ps 89.17–24]]; 132.17; 148.14; [[LXX Sirach 51#^51-12|Sir 51.12]]. ^1-69

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-70|70]]:** *Holy prophets*, the Tanakh depicts prophets as God’s servants ([[Ezekiel 38#^38-1|Ezek 38.1]]) but usually not with the term holy (but see [[Jeremiah 01#^1-5|Jer 1.5]]); contrast [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 11#^11-1|Wis 11.1]]; [[Ephesians 03#^3-5|Eph 3.5]]; *2 Bar*. 85.1. ^1-70

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-72|72–73]]:** *Remembered his holy covenant*, see [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-24|Ex 2.24]]; [[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-42|Lev 26.42]]; [[Psalms 105#^105-8|Ps 105.8]], as well as the “ḥesed avot” (“steadfast love of the fathers”) of [[Micah 7#^7-20|Mic 7.20]]; [[Psalms 106#^106-45|Ps 106.45]]; etc. *Holy covenant*, see [[Daniel 11#^11-30|Dan 11.30]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-15|1 Macc 1.15]],[[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-63|63]]. *Oath*, see [[Torah 06 - Toldot#^26-3|Gen 26.3]]. ^1-72

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-76|76]]:** *Most High*, see 1.32n. *Prepare his ways*, see 3.4–6n. ^1-76

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-77|77]]:** *Forgiveness of their sins*, see 3.3n. ^1-77

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-79|79]]:** *Sit in darkness*, see [[Isaiah 42#^42-7|Isa 42.7]] (and most of Second Isaiah \[chs 40–55\] in its prophecy of hope); [[Micah 7#^7-8|Mic 7.8]]; [[Psalms 107#^107-10|Ps 107.10]]; [[Psalms 143#^143-3|143.3]]; [[Lamentations 3#^3-6|Lam 3.6]]. *Shadow of death* ([[Matthew 04#^4-16|Mt 4.16]]), see [[Psalms 023#^23-4|Ps 23.4]]. *Peace*, major concern for the Tanakh and rabbinic tradition (e.g., *Pesiq. Rav Kah*. 18.6 states that in the messianic age, people will always seek peace). ^1-79

**[[NRSVue Luke 01#^1-80|80]]:** *Grew and became strong*, cf. 2.52. *Wilderness*, see 3.4; site of Israel’s encounter with God at Sinai; here, by the Jordan River. ^1-80
