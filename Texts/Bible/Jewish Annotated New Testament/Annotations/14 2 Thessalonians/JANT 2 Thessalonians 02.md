### 2.1–12: Day of the Lord

*[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 429* · The author seeks to dispel belief in an imminent end; cf. [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-1|1 Thess 5.1–3]]. ^2-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Coming*, the return of Jesus (2.8; [[Matthew 24#^24-3|Mt 24.3]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-23|1 Cor 15.23]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-13|1 Thess 3.13]]; see e.g., [[Joel 2#^2-1|Joel 2.1]], using similar eschatological terms). ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Spirit . . . word . . . letter*, the author fears misrepresentation of his views given by prophets or rival teachers, false interpretations of an earlier letter, or a new letter written in Paul’s name. *Day of the Lord*, the time of cataclysmic events, after which God’s justice prevails ([[Isaiah 13#^13-6|Isa 13.6]]; [[Joel 2#^2-1|Joel 2.1]]; Am 5.18). *Already here*, the author is concerned that congregants, acting as if the end is here, refuse to work or abandon moral strictures. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-3|3]]:** *That day will not come*, absent in the Greek manuscripts, is supplied by the translator. *Rebellion*, Gk “apostasia,” better “apostasy”; the author predicts religious transgression. *Lawless one*, see “The Man of Lawlessness and the Restrainer,” p. 429. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Temple of God*, see Introduction. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-6|6–7]]:** *And you know*, readers may know the restrainer’s identity. Confusingly, the text uses both neutral (*what*) and masculine (*who*) participles. ^2-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Mystery of lawlessness*, the end-time will be preceded by moral turpitude and anarchy, themes found in some Jewish eschatologies (Ezek 38–39; Joel 2; 1QM). *Only until . . . removed*, likely a reference not to something present but rather to the mystery or hiddenness of the “lawless one” that will end when he is revealed. Unlike in Paul’s undisputed epistles, “law” does not refer to the commands of the Torah but to apostasy and false teachings (cf. [[Romans 10#^10-4|Rom 10.4–5]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-16|Gal 2.16]]). ^2-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Destroy with the breath*, Jesus will establish justice with the breath that authorities read *the man of sin* c Gk *the son of destruction* “consumes,” perhaps recalling the messianic prophecy of [[Isaiah 11#^11-4|Isa 11.4]] (see translators note *b* — the same verb is translated “consume” in [[Luke 09#^9-54|Lk 9.54]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-15|Gal 5.15]]) and not by the sword (see [[Revelation 19#^19-15|Rev 19.15]],[[Revelation 19#^19-21|21]] cf. [[Isaiah 11#^11-4|Isa 11.4]]; [[Revelation 19#^19-11|Rev 19.11–21]]; *Pss. Sol.* 17.24; *1 En*. 62.2; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 13.10). ^2-8

> [!note] The Man of Lawlessness and the Restrainer ([[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-3|2 Thess 2.3–4]]) *(p. 429)*
> The description of the “lawless one” draws on Jewish apocalyptic literature (*Pss. Sol.* 17.11; 1QM 13.2–5; *3 Sib. Or.* 3.63–74) and is modeled on foreign oppressors such as the Seleucid emperor Antiochus IV (second century bce) and the Roman general Pompey (first century bce) ([[Daniel 11#^11-24|Dan 11.24-45]]; *Ps Sol.* 2.1–2; 17.11; Josephus, *Ant.* 14.72). Both violated the sanctity of the Jerusalem Temple by entering sacred areas closed to Gentiles. Antiochus, who thought himself divine (his nickname was Epiphanes, “\[God\] Manifest”\], also defiled the building by offering idolatrous worship there ([[Daniel 11#^11-31|Dan 11.31]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-46|1 Macc 1.46]]). In 40 CE, the emperor Caligula attempted to place his image in the Temple (Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 188; Josephus, *Ant*. 18.261). The offensive nature of the lawless one’s “tak\[ing\] his seat in the temple of God” evokes this history along with the deep Jewish attachment to Jerusalem and the Temple. The letter’s author may have an actual person in mind, perhaps a false teacher or a Roman emperor claiming divine status; the author also may have drawn upon a tradition of an unspecified opponent of God (and God’s people) who will ultimately be destroyed. If this letter were written after 70, the author may envision a third Jerusalem Temple, which many Jews and some Christians expected (*2 Bar.* 32.4; *Barnabas* 16.3–4; Justin, *Dial.* 80–81); this hope was later enshrined in Jewish liturgy (*Amidah* Blessing 14: “May you \[God\] rebuild it \[Jerusalem\] speedily in our days”). Despite their origins in different texts, the figures of the antichrist ([[1 John 02#^2-18|1 Jn 2.18]],[[1 John 02#^2-22|22]]; [[2 John 01#^1-7|2 Jn 1.7]]) and the lawless one were eventually linked in eschatological scenarios (e.g., J. Chrys., *Hom. 2 Thess.* 4, referring to emperor Nero).

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-9|9]]:** *Satan*, though subordinate to God, deceives believers ([[Job 01#^1-6|Job 1.6–2.7]]; [[I Chronicles 21#^21-1|1 Chr 21.1]]). Less cosmically powerful than in early Christian texts, Satan in rabbinic texts tempts the pious to sin (*b. Qidd.* [[Kiddushin 81#81a|81a]]) and even deceives God (e.g., into testing Job; *b. B. Bat.* [[Bava Batra 16#16a|16a]]). ^2-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Truth*, referring here to “traditions” they were taught. *Saved* recalls the rewards in 1.5,7,9 of deliverance from persecution and entering into the divine presence. ^2-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Powerful delusion*, perhaps recalling the false prophets God sent to deceive the evil king Ahab ([[I Kings 22#^22-22|1 Kings 22.22–23]]). ^2-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-13|2.13–17]]:** **Second thanksgiving.** As with the Salutation and Thanksgiving, the conventional conclusion parallels [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-13|1 Thess 2.13]]. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-13|13]]:** *First fruits*, if this reading is correct (see translation notes), believers are likened to a temple offering ([[Jeremiah 02#^2-3|Jer 2.3]]; see [[Romans 08#^8-23|Rom 8.23]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-20|1 Cor 15.20]]). *Sanctification*, “making holy” or being set apart by the Spirit as a worthy offering. ^2-13-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Obtain the glory*, believers will share in the glorified body of the resurrected Christ. ^2-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Thessalonians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Hold fast to the traditions*, see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-29|Deut 5.32–33]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-2|1 Thess 3.2]],[[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-11|11–13]]; Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.22; *m. Avot* 3.13. *Either by word of mouth or by our letter*, indicating reliance on both oral and written traditions. ^2-15
