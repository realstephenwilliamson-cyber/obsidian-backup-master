## The young woman’s beauty extolled

**4:1** How beautiful you are, my love, how very beautiful! Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats, moving down the slopes of Gilead. ^4-1

**4:2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes that have come up from the washing, all of which bear twins, and not one among them is bereaved. ^4-2

**4:3** Your lips are like a crimson thread, and your mouth is lovely. Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate behind your veil. ^4-3

**4:4** Your neck is like the tower of David, built in courses; on it hang a thousand bucklers, all of them shields of warriors. ^4-4

**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that feed among the lilies. ^4-5

**4:6** Until the day breathes and the shadows flee, I will hasten to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense. ^4-6

**4:7** You are altogether beautiful, my love; there is no flaw in you. ^4-7

**4:8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride; come with me from Lebanon. Depart^[Or *Look*] from the peak of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. ^4-8

**4:9** You have ravished my heart, my sister, my bride; you have ravished my heart with a glance of your eyes, with one jewel of your necklace. ^4-9

**4:10** How sweet is your love, my sister, my bride! How much better is your love than wine and the fragrance of your oils than any spice! ^4-10

**4:11** Your lips distill nectar, my bride; honey and milk are under your tongue; the scent of your garments is like the scent of Lebanon. ^4-11

**4:12** A garden locked is my sister, my bride, a garden^[Heb mss Gk Vg Syr: MT *heap of stones*] locked, a fountain sealed. ^4-12

**4:13** Your channel^[Meaning of Heb uncertain] is an orchard of pomegranates with all choicest fruits, henna with nard, ^4-13

**4:14** nard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all trees of frankincense, myrrh and aloes, with all chief spices— ^4-14

**4:15** a garden fountain, a well of living water, and flowing streams from Lebanon. ^4-15

**4:16** Awake, O north wind, and come, O south wind! Blow upon my garden that its fragrance may be wafted abroad. Let my beloved come to his garden that he may eat its choicest fruits. ^4-16
