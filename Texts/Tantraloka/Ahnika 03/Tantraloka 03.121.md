## Mūla

सूर्यं प्रमाणमित्याहुः सोमं मेयं प्रचक्षते ।
अन्योन्यमवियुक्तौ तौ स्वतन्त्रावप्युभौ स्थितौ ॥ ^mula

sūryaṃ pramāṇamityāhuḥ somaṃ meyaṃ pracakṣate |
anyonyamaviyuktau tau svatantrāvapyubhau sthitau || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. II
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 02 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v02 07 - Chapter Three, part 4#^pg242|p. 226]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 02.pdf#page=242>))
> Dyczkowski numbers 3.121cd as 3.122ab.
>
> The (wise) say that the Sun is the means of knowledge, and the Moon is said to be the object. (with 3.121cd)
>
> (Although) they are both independent, they are not separate from one another, and of the nature of the enjoyer, the object of enjoyment and both (together), they became intent on one another. Then the flaming conscious nature (which arises between them) is called Fire (citrabhānu, lit. ‘Many Rayed One’). This, the supreme principle of Fire, is indeed that of the subject. (with 3.121cd–123ab)
