## About

The Devī Khaḍgamālā (garland of the sword) is a Śrī Vidyā hymn and mantra that names, one after another, the goddesses placed in the Śrī Yantra (Śrī Cakra) or the Maha Meru. The list of names is the garland; the sword stands for the Goddess's power to strike down desire, hatred and delusion (Wikipedia, citing Douglas Renfrew Brooks, 1990).

- **Source:** the closing line of the text at sanskritdocuments.org places it in the *Vāmakeśvara Tantra*, in a dialogue between Umā and Maheśvara (Śiva). Hindupedia says the original source document is lost and only stray excerpts survive.
- **Ritual frame:** the opening names the seer Varuṇāditya, the gāyatrī metre and the goddess Lalitā (Hindupedia: Kāmeśvarī), with aiṃ, klīṃ and sauḥ as seed, power and pin (bīja, śakti, kīlaka). A meditation verse describes her as red, three-eyed, holding noose, goad, bow and arrows.
- **Structure:** after invoking three lines of gurus, the text moves through the nine enclosures (āvaraṇas) of the Śrī Cakra toward the central point (bindu). Each enclosure lists its goddesses and closes with the ruler of that circuit (cakra) and its yoginī. Hindupedia counts 98 yoginīs and describes the whole as one garland mantra of more than 1,000 letters.
- **Practice:** Hindupedia, reproducing a practitioners' FAQ, recommends daily recitation while visualising each goddess and says no initiation is required. The Devipuram temple site ties its deities to the Śrī Cakra pūjā (Navāvaraṇa pūjā).
- **Date and author:** none of the sources opened gives a date or a human author.
- **Why it matters:** in Wikipedia's description it names each goddess by her place in the Śrī Yantra; in Hindupedia's FAQ the text takes the reciter mentally through the Śrī Cakra, whose nine enclosures surround a central point.
