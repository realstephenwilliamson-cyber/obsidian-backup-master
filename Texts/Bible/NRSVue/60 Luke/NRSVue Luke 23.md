# Luke 23 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Luke 22|← Luke 22]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Luke 24|Luke 24 →]]

Compare: [[Luke 23|BSB and SBLGNT]] · [[DRB Luke 23|Douay-Rheims]] · [[JANT Luke 23|JANT notes]]

## Jesus before Pilate

**23:1** Then the assembly rose as a body and brought Jesus^[Gk *him*] before Pilate. ^23-1

**23:2** They began to accuse him, saying, “We found this man inciting our nation, forbidding us to pay taxes to Caesar and saying that he himself is the Messiah, a king.”^[Or *is an anointed king*] ^23-2

**23:3** Then Pilate asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?” He answered, “You say so.” ^23-3

**23:4** Then Pilate said to the chief priests and the crowds, “I find no basis for an accusation against this man.” ^23-4

**23:5** But they were insistent and said, “He stirs up the people by teaching throughout all Judea, from Galilee where he began even to this place.” ^23-5

## Jesus before Herod

**23:6** When Pilate heard this, he asked whether the man was a Galilean. ^23-6

**23:7** And when he learned that he was under Herod’s jurisdiction, he sent him off to Herod, who was himself in Jerusalem at that time. ^23-7

**23:8** When Herod saw Jesus, he was very glad, for he had been wanting to see him for a long time because he had heard about him and was hoping to see him perform some sign. ^23-8

**23:9** He questioned him at some length, but Jesus^[Gk *he*] gave him no answer. ^23-9

**23:10** The chief priests and the scribes stood by vehemently accusing him. ^23-10

**23:11** Even Herod with his soldiers treated him with contempt and mocked him; then he put an elegant robe on him and sent him back to Pilate. ^23-11

**23:12** That same day Herod and Pilate became friends with each other; before this they had been enemies. ^23-12

## Jesus sentenced to death

**23:13** Pilate then called together the chief priests, the leaders, and the people ^23-13

**23:14** and said to them, “You brought me this man as one who was inciting the people, and here I have examined him in your presence and have not found this man guilty of any of your charges against him. ^23-14

**23:15** Neither has Herod, for he sent him back to us. Indeed, he has done nothing to deserve death. ^23-15

**23:16** I will therefore have him flogged and release him.”^[Here, or after 23.19, other ancient authorities add 23.17: *Now he was obliged to release someone for them at the festival*] ^23-16

**23:18** Then they all shouted out together, “Away with this fellow! Release Barabbas for us!” ^23-18

**23:19** (This was a man who had been put in prison for an insurrection that had taken place in the city and for murder.) ^23-19

**23:20** Pilate, wanting to release Jesus, addressed them again, ^23-20

**23:21** but they kept shouting, “Crucify, crucify him!” ^23-21

**23:22** A third time he said to them, “Why, what evil has he done? I have found in him no ground for the sentence of death; I will therefore have him flogged and then release him.” ^23-22

**23:23** But they kept urgently demanding with loud shouts that he should be crucified, and their voices prevailed. ^23-23

**23:24** So Pilate gave his verdict that their demand should be granted. ^23-24

**23:25** He released the man they asked for, the one who had been put in prison for insurrection and murder, and he handed Jesus over as they wished. ^23-25

## The crucifixion of Jesus

**23:26** As they led him away, they seized a man, Simon of Cyrene, who was coming from the country, and they laid the cross on him and made him carry it behind Jesus. ^23-26

**23:27** A great number of the people followed him, and among them were women who were beating their breasts and wailing for him. ^23-27

**23:28** But Jesus turned to them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me, but weep for yourselves and for your children. ^23-28

**23:29** For the days are surely coming when they will say, ‘Blessed are the barren, and the wombs that never bore, and the breasts that never nursed.’ ^23-29

**23:30** Then they will begin to say to the mountains, ‘Fall on us,’ and to the hills, ‘Cover us.’ ^23-30

**23:31** For if they do this when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?” ^23-31

**23:32** Two others also, who were criminals, were led away to be put to death with him. ^23-32

**23:33** When they came to the place that is called The Skull, they crucified Jesus^[Gk *him*] there with the criminals, one on his right and one on his left. \[\[ ^23-33

**23:34** Then Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.”\]\]^[Other ancient authorities lack the sentence *Then Jesus . . . what they are doing*] And they cast lots to divide his clothing. ^23-34

**23:35** And the people stood by watching, but the leaders scoffed at him, saying, “He saved others; let him save himself if he is the Messiah^[Or *the Christ*] of God, his chosen one!” ^23-35

**23:36** The soldiers also mocked him, coming up and offering him sour wine ^23-36

**23:37** and saying, “If you are the King of the Jews, save yourself!” ^23-37

**23:38** There was also an inscription over him,^[Other ancient authorities add *written in Greek and Latin and Hebrew* (that is, *Aramaic*)] “This is the King of the Jews.” ^23-38

**23:39** One of the criminals who were hanged there kept deriding^[Or *blaspheming*] him and saying, “Are you not the Messiah?^[Or *the Christ*] Save yourself and us!” ^23-39

**23:40** But the other rebuked him, saying, “Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence of condemnation? ^23-40

**23:41** And we indeed have been condemned justly, for we are getting what we deserve for our deeds, but this man has done nothing wrong.” ^23-41

**23:42** Then he said, “Jesus, remember me when you come in^[Other ancient authorities read *into*] your kingdom.” ^23-42

**23:43** He replied, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.” ^23-43

## The death of Jesus

**23:44** It was now about noon, and darkness came over the whole land^[Or *earth*] until three in the afternoon, ^23-44

**23:45** while the sun’s light failed,^[Or *the sun was eclipsed.* Other ancient authorities read *the sun was darkened*] and the curtain of the temple was torn in two. ^23-45

**23:46** Then Jesus, crying out with a loud voice, said, “Father, into your hands I commend my spirit.” Having said this, he breathed his last. ^23-46

**23:47** When the centurion saw what had taken place, he praised God and said, “Certainly this man was innocent.”^[Or *righteous*] ^23-47

**23:48** And when all the crowds who had gathered there for this spectacle saw what had taken place, they returned home, beating their breasts. ^23-48

**23:49** But all his acquaintances, including the women who had followed him from Galilee, stood at a distance watching these things. ^23-49

## The burial of Jesus

**23:50** Now there was a good and righteous man named Joseph who, though a member of the council, ^23-50

**23:51** had not agreed to their plan and action. He came from the Jewish town of Arimathea, and he was waiting expectantly for the kingdom of God. ^23-51

**23:52** This man went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. ^23-52

**23:53** Then he took it down, wrapped it in a linen cloth, and laid it in a rock-hewn tomb where no one had ever been laid. ^23-53

**23:54** It was the day of Preparation, and the Sabbath was beginning.^[Gk *was dawning*] ^23-54

**23:55** The women who had come with him from Galilee followed, and they saw the tomb and how his body was laid. ^23-55

**23:56** Then they returned and prepared spices and ointments. On the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment. ^23-56
