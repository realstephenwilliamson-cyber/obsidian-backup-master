### 2.1–3: False prophets

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 514* ^2-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-1|1]]:** *False prophets . . . false teachers*, these terms appear separately in LXX as well as later Jewish writings ([[Isaiah 09#^9-14|Isa 9.14]]; [[Jeremiah 05#^5-31|Jer 5.31]]; [[Habakkuk 2#^2-18|Hab 2.18]]; [[Matthew 07#^7-15|Mt 7.15]]; [[Matthew 24#^24-11|24.11]],[[Matthew 24#^24-24|24]]; [[Mark 13#^13-22|Mk 13.22]]; [[Luke 06#^6-26|Lk 6.26]]; 1QpHab 10.9; 12.11; *m. Sanh*. 1.5: “The false prophet *. . .* \[is judged\] only by a court of seventy-one judges”). *Opinions* (Gk “heiresis”), i.e., schools of thought. *Deny*, see [[LXX Psalms 09#^9-25|Ps 9.25]]\[LXX\] (“There is no God before him”) and *Midr. Ps.* 10.6, which interprets the Psalm as saying “There is no judgment and there is no Judge. The Only One, blessed be He, has gone away and sits in heaven.” *Master* (Gk “despotes”), see LXX [[LXX Genesis 15#^15-2|Gen 15.2]]; [[Isaiah 01#^1-24|Isa 1.24]]; [[Jeremiah 04#^4-10|Jer 4.10]], where the term refers to God. Jesus. *Bought them*, regarding Jesus as “master” of the household is in correlation with the believers being his slaves, and therefore his property. See also Jude 4 (a shorter version of [[1 Peter 02#^2-1|1 Pet 2.1–3a]]). The image may suggest Jesus’ purchasing or ransoming his followers from Satan, Death, or both ([[1 Corinthians 06#^6-20|1 Cor 6.20]]; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-23|7.23]]; [[1 Peter 01#^1-18|1 Pet 1.18–19]]). ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Licentious*, the author again focuses on improper sexual behavior. Whether the author is combatting a group who regard Jesus’ “buying” them from the power of the Law and so feel free to engage in any action the author deems immoral, or whether this is conventional invective cannot be determined. *Way of truth*, see 1QS 3.13–4.26 on the two paths. *Maligned*, Gk “blasphemein.” ^2-2

### 2.4–11: Precedents from Genesis

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-4|Text]], p. 514* · [[2 Peter 02#^2-3|2 Pet 2.3b–10a]] appears to be dependent on Jude 5–8. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Spare the angels*, the author equates contemporary false prophets with fallen angels, legends developed from [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-1|Gen 6.1–4]] as interpreted by Second Temple sources such as *1 En.* 6–19 and *Jub.* 5.1 (probably known in rabbinic circles, cf. *Pirqe R. El.* 22), and related to the Greek myth of the fall of the Titans in Hesiod’s *Theogony*. *Sinned*, 2 Peter does not specify the sin, though it is likely to be the sexual intercourse with *who were to be ungodly* humans, given Jude and the epistle’s concern with sexuality propriety. *Hell*, Gk “Tartaros,” a robust notion of a differentiated heaven and hell began to develop in Hellenistic Judaism (see LXX [[Job 40#^40-20|Job 40.20]]; [[LXX Job 41#^41-24|41.24]]; *Sib. Or.* 2.302; *1 En*. 20.2; Philo, *Leg. all.* 103; *4 Ezra* 4.8; 7.36). The author alludes to the punishment of the Watchers as well as to the fall of the Titans (see Hesiod, *Theogony* 617–735) and for the connection of the two stories, Josephus, *Ant*. 1.73, “These men did what resembled the acts of those whom the Grecians call giants.” *Judgment*, the final judgment at the return of Jesus. ^2-4-2

> [!note] Balaam in Jewish and Christian Tradition *(p. 514)*
> The story of Balaam, introduced in Num 22–24, is developed in later Jewish, rabbinic, and early Christian literature. Philo (*Mos*. 1.295) states that Balaam “endeavored to seduce \[Israel\] by means of debauchery and intemperance.” The rabbis contrast Balaam to Abraham (see esp. *m. Avot* 5.19: “The disciples of Abraham have a good eye, a meek spirit and a humble soul. The disciples of Balaam have an evil eye, a haughty spirit and a gross soul”), and Balaam himself serves as a negative example of a Gentile prophet who has no share in the world to come (*m. Sanh*. 10.2). The rabbis do recognize his pro- phetic abilities, but they emphasize his inferiority compared to the prophets of Israel (*Gen. Rab*. 52.5; *Lev. Rab.* 1.13). Christian attitudes toward his prophecy are usually positive, especially since his prophecy, “A star shall come out of Jacob” ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-17|Num 24.17]]) was interpreted Christologically, but he is sometimes viewed negatively (e.g., Jude 11; [[Revelation 02#^2-14|Rev 2.14]], which refers to a teacher the author condemns). Ascribing greed to Balaam contradicts the plain sense of [[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-18|Num 22.18]], but [[2 Peter 02#^2-15|2 Pet 2.15]] (*the wages of doing wrong*) agrees with a later rabbinic tradition that saw the verse as proof of his greediness (*Avot de R. Natan* B 45; see also Philo, *Mos*. 1.267–68; *Num. Rab*. 20.7,10).

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-5|5]]:** *Noah*, [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-5|Gen 6.5–9.28]]. Associated with salvation in relation to water, and so baptism (see [[1 Peter 03#^3-20|1 Pet 3.20]]). Jude 5 speaks of the people who came out of Egypt; 2 Pet extends the reference to one with both universal import and no opportunity of redemption. *Herald of righteousness*, as opposed to the false teachers, perhaps hinting to the tradition about Noah’s preaching to his evil generation (Josephus, *Ant*. 1.74; *Sib. Or.* 1.128–29; *Gen. Rab*. 30.7; [[Sanhedrin 108#108b|*b. Sanh*. 108b]]). *Seven others*, Noah’s wife, three sons, and their wives. ^2-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-6|6]]:** *Sodom and Gomorrah*, see Gen 19. Rabbinic sources accuse the people of Sodom of both sexual and social sins (*t. Sanh*. 13.8; *Gen. Rab*. 40). ^2-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Lot*, some Jewish and Christian sources depict Lot as righteous ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 10#^10-5|Wis 10.5]]; *1 Clem*. 11.1; [[Berakhot 54#54b|*b. Ber*. 54b]]). The correlation between licentiousness and Sodom is common in rabbinic sources (see *Gen*. *Rab*. 40.7). ^2-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-9|9]]:** *Rescue . . . from trial*, see [[Mark 13#^13-11|Mk 13.11]]; [[1 Peter 01#^1-6|1 Pet 1.6]]. *Under punishment*, the author envisions a post-mortem hell, prior to the final judgment. In rabbinic sources such punishment is usually located in Gehinnom (*m. Ed*. 2.10 \[attributed to R. Akiva, “ . . . the judgment of the generation of the Flood is twelve months . . . and the judgment of the wicked in Gehenna is twelve months”\]; *t. Sanh.* , 13.3–4). ^2-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-10|10]]:** [[2 Peter 02#^2-10|2 Pet 2.10b–16]] follows Jude 8–12. *Despise authority*, those who do not follow the author’s teaching. *Glorious ones*, lit., “glories,” that is, angels. ^2-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Slanderous judgment*, sinful angels do not dare slander the holy angels, whereas the false teachers do slander them. ^2-11

### 2.12–20: Castigation of opponents

*[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-12|Text]], p. 515* ^2-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Like irrational animals*, see [[Psalms 049#^49-13|Ps 49.13]]. The author may hint at Balaam (2.15–16), who was less knowledgeable than his donkey ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-21|Num 22.21–30]]; [[Avodah Zarah 4#4b|*b. Avod. Zar.* 4b]]). ^2-12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Feast with you*, Gk “suneuoxoumenoi” occurs elsewhere only in Jude 12. The false teachers join the meals only to make them the occasion of self-indulgence (cf. [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-20|1 Cor 11.20–22]]). ^2-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-15|15–16]]:** *Balaam*, see “Balaam in Jewish and Christian Tradition,” p. 514. Bosor instead of Beor (Balaam’s father) is probably a scribal error (though supported by most mss). ^2-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-16|16]]:** *Donkey*, even a mute animal rebuked Balaam ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-28|Num 22.28–30]]); rabbinic texts develop the rebuke (*Gen. Rab*. 93.10). ^2-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-17|17]]:** [[2 Peter 02#^2-17|2 Pet 2.17–18]] follow Jude others lack the phrase c Gk *in their destruction* d Other ancient authorities read *receiving* e Other ancient authorities read *love-feasts* f Other ancient authorities read *Beor* 12–13,16. *Deepest darkness*, cf. Jude 12. Tartaros (see 2.4n.) was depicted as utterly dark. ^2-17

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-18|18]]:** *Just escaped*, from the heathen and their false ways. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-19|19]]:** *Promise them freedom*, from law or moral restraints (see 2.7; 3.17; [[Galatians 05#^5-13|Gal 5.13]]). *Slaves*, see [[Romans 06#^6-6|Rom 6.6]],[[Romans 06#^6-16|16]]. ^2-19

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-20|20]]:** *After they have escaped*, opponents, who had been part of the author’s community, mistake licentiousness for *freedom*. *Knowledge*, see 1.2. *Lord and Savior*, cf. 1.11. ^2-20

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-21|21]]:** *Holy commandment*, probably not one commandment but referring to faith in general or to the Torah’s commandments as a whole. ^2-21

**[[NRSVue 2 Peter 02#^2-22|22]]:** *The dog turns back to its own vomit*, [[Proverbs 26#^26-11|Prov 26.11]]. The second proverb is unattested elsewhere, though the pig is elsewhere mentioned alongside the dog as a dis-gusting animal ([[Isaiah 66#^66-3|Isa 66.3]]; [[Matthew 07#^7-6|Mt 7.6]]). ^2-22
