### 1.1–3: Introduction

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 462* · A poetic presentation of the main theme**:** the superiority of Christ, called *Son* to emphasize his unique status. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-1|1–2]]:** *Long ago . . . last days*, contrasting periods of divine revelation anticipate the contrast between old and new covenants (8.6–13). *Our ancestors*, explicit indication that the author and implied audience understand themselves as rooted in the same lineage as any ancient or modern Jew would claim; in line with the biblical tradition of Israel as God’s children, the language of kinship to describe the relationship with God pervades Hebrews (e.g., 2.10–16). *Many . . . ways*, some church fathers took this phrase to indicate both Israel’s prophets and (esp. Plato’s) philosophy (Clement, *Strom.* 1.5,9; 6.7,8,11; 7.16; Origen, *On First Principles* 4.2.4,9). *Last days*, the end of the present age, awaiting the beginning of the new era. Although Hebrews does not dwell at length on world-ending scenarios, an eager eschatology is manifest at several points (9.26; 13.14). *Son*, Gk “huios,” preferred title for Jesus in Hebrews. More often than not in the NT, “Son” is part of a longer epithet like “Son of God” or “Son of Man,” but the freestanding “Son” sometimes occurs, notably in the Gospel of John (e.g., 3.35; 5.19) and occasionally in Paul (e.g., [[Romans 05#^5-10|Rom 5.10]]; [[Romans 08#^8-3|8.3]],[[Romans 08#^8-29|29]],[[Romans 08#^8-32|32]]). In ancient Jewish texts, the freestanding “Son” is not common, but the infrequent Tanakh designation of David (and by extension his descendants) as God’s Son, who is anointed into a unique, everlasting relationship with God, enabled the term “Son” to serve as virtual equivalent to Messiah (see [[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-21|89.20–37]]). In addition, while Israel is often referred to as “bnai Yisrael” (Heb, lit., “sons of/children of Israel”) in the Tanakh*,* Israel is sometimes designated God’s “Son/child” in the singular (e.g., [[Jeremiah 31#^31-20|Jer 31.20]]). Because Jesus is “Son” and his followers are also God’s “sons/children” (Gk “huioi”) in Hebrews (2.10; 12.7), Jesus’ followers become his siblings and there- fore will share in the divine patrimony (2.11–12,17). *Heir*, in Hebrews, as in Paul, to be an heir means one is assured of eternal life and inherits the next world. Here the term specifically designates Jesus and is the functional equivalent of “Son.” *Created the worlds*, Gk “aionas,” whence “aeons.” Although the word is plural, it does not imply a multiplicity of worlds. See Wisdom’s presence at creation ([[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-9|Wis 9.9]]; [[LXX Sirach 01#^1-4|Sir 1.4]]). Philo says similar things about the “logos,” “divine reason” (*On the Creation* 5.20; 8.30–31). See “*Logos*,” p. 688. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Reflection of God’s glory and the exact imprint of God’s very being*, see [[Proverbs 08#^8-22|Prov 8.22–31]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-25|Wis 7.25–26]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-9|9.9]]; Philo, *QG* 1.57. Wisdom was seen as working alongside God at creation; *reflection* (as of an object that returns light exactly as it received it) and *imprint* (as of a coin that exactly reproduces the contours of its originating mold) are ways of expressing the Son’s transmission of God’s nature without any flaw. *Glory* (Heb “kavod,” Gk “doxa”) was a quality frequently attributed to God (e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-10|Ex 16.10]]; [[Isaiah 06#^6-3|Isa 6.3]]; [[Psalms 019#^19-2|Ps 19.1]]; and many other places). *Sustains*, lit., “bears.” *Purification*, see 9.14n. and “The Law,” p. 655. *Sat down at the right hand*, an implicit evocation of [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]; the author appeals to this Psalm several times (1.13; 5.6; 7.17; 8.1; 12.2). *Majesty*, Gk “megalosynēs,” not a title so much as a way to express exuberance over God’s splendor. Several Hebrew words, such as “hod,” “hadar,” and “geʾut,” are translated into English as “majesty” in reference to God’s glory and magnificence. Such terms are typically found in poetry and in contexts where God is effusively praised either as creator or king, so “majesty” suits the high literary style of Hebrews well. See e.g., [[Job 37#^37-22|Job 37.22]]; [[Job 40#^40-10|40.10]]; [[Psalms 021#^21-6|Ps 21.6]]\[LXX 20.6\], 96.6\[LXX 95.6\], 104.1\[LXX 103.1\]. ^1-3

### 1.4–14: Superior to angels

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-4|Text]], p. 463* · The audience possibly understood Jesus as an angel. See “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Name*, perhaps meaning “Lord” (see [[Philippians 02#^2-9|Phil 2.9–11]]). In Jewish tradition, God’s name contains the divine essence and is therefore treated with great reverence. Heb “Ha-shem,” “the Name,” functions as a circumlocution for God, since the divine name is ineffable. ^1-4-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-5|5]]:** See [[II Samuel 07#^7-14|2 Sam 7.14]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]], in reference to the Davidic king. These texts appear together in two Qumran collections of biblical quotations (4QTest; 4QFlor 1.10–19). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Firstborn*, Jesus. This designation is not meant literally, but rather “specially chosen.” It is used of David in [[Psalms 089#^89-28|Ps 89.28]]. Followers of Jesus are also “firstborn” (12.23). *Let all God’s angels* . . . ,” alluding to [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-43|Deut 32.43]]. “Worship him, all you gods” is not in the Hebrew text (MT), but it is preserved in the LXX and 4QDeutq. While modern readers may think of angels primarily as mediators or messengers, in Psalms and Hebrew poetry they are more commonly invoked as part of the heavenly host, offering praise to God (see “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-7|7]]:** [[Psalms 104#^104-4|Ps 104.4]]\[LXX\]. In the Psalm, the praise is to God; here God speaks of angels; to the author of Hebrews, this reflects the lower status of angels. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-8|8–9]]:** [[Psalms 045#^45-7|Ps 45.6–7]], a royal wedding hymn that calls the Davidic king “God”; here, it is applied to Jesus. *O God*, twice applied to Jesus by God who is cast as the speaker of the Psalm. Although *God* (Gk “theos”) was a common designation for all sorts of divine and semi-divine beings in Hellenistic and Roman culture, such usage is out of keeping with Jewish linguistic habits, and the use of “theos” for Jesus is by no means typical of first-century Christian texts. The reasons for this deployment of god-language in Hebrews are not transparent, but the answer may be that Hebrews has cast the Son in very intimate role with the Father God in 1.1–4, both in theological and affective terms. *Righteousness*, Heb “tzedek,” Gk “dikaiosynē.” The theme of God’s righteousness is pervasive in the Bible and essential to Jewish theology, but in Hebrews it is not developed. ^1-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-10|10–12]]:** [[Psalms 102#^102-26|Ps 102.25–27]]. The Psalmist praises God’s handiwork in creation while also proclaiming that it is passing away. The tension between the magnificence of creation and its ephemeral nature suits Hebrews’s eschatological expectations of a new era, “a kingdom that cannot be shaken” (12.26–28). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-13|13]]:** [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]], frequently cited as a prophecy about Jesus (e.g., [[Matthew 22#^22-43|Mt 22.43–45]]; [[Acts 02#^2-34|Acts 2.34–36]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-24|1 Cor 15.24–25]]); Ps 110 is the most quoted Psalm in the NT. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Divine service*, Gk “leitourgia” (also in v 7), whence “liturgy,” a term used for priestly worship in the Temple. That the angels are described as in service to “those who are to inherit salvation” recalls the tradition that the angels were made to worship Adam; see *Life of Adam and Eve* (I) 14–15; see also *Jub.* 2.2, where God creates many classes of angels who minister before God (Gk “leitourgika pneumata,” lit., “ministering spirits”; see Philo, *Virtues* 74; *Mek.* “Beshallah” 3) on the second day of creation. *Inherit salvation*, eternal life. see 1.1–2n. ^1-14
