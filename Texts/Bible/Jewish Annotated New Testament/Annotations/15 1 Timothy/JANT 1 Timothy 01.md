# 1 Timothy 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 1 Timothy 02|1 Timothy 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01|NRSVue]] · [[1 Timothy 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 Timothy - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-1|1.1–2]]:** **Opening greeting**. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Apostle*, from the Greek for “one sent out.” It comes to take the technical meaning as one specifically commissioned by Jesus to proclaim the gospel. *God our Savior*, see also 2.3; 4.10; the title appears in the NT only in the Pastorals numerous times, and in [[2 Peter 01#^1-1|2 Pet 1.1]] and Jude 25, but nowhere else in the NT. “Savior” or “deliverer” (Gk “sotēr,” Heb “yishaʿ,” see [[Psalms 024#^24-5|Ps 24.5]] \[LXX 23.5, “you are God my savior”\]) is typically a divine epithet. For 1 Timothy, God establishes this salvation through the *Christ* \[Heb “mashiaḥ”\]. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Loyal child*, a term of endearment (as also [[2 Timothy 01#^1-2|2 Tim 1.2]]), not a designation of kinship. *Grace*, Gk “charis,” “favor, thanks,” comparable to Heb “ḥen” (see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-8|Gen 6.8]]). *Mercy*, Gk “eleos,” in LXX for Heb “ḥesed,” “lovingkindness” (see [[Psalms 085#^85-11|Ps 85.10]] \[HB 85.11; LXX 84.11\]). *Peace*, Gk “eirēnē,” in LXX for Heb “shalom.” The salutation combines Greek and Hebrew (Jewish) greetings. *God the Father*, occasionally used in the Tanakh (see [[Jeremiah 03#^3-4|Jer 3.4]]; [[Psalms 068#^68-6|Ps 68.5]]). In Pauline usage increasingly it meant the believers’ union with Jesus as God’s (adoptive) children, see e.g., [[Romans 08#^8-15|Rom 8.15]]. ^1-2

### 1.3–11: Countering false teaching

*[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-3|Text]], p. 433* ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Macedonia*, [[Acts 16#^16-20|Acts 16.20]] describes Paul’s journeys there; for Paul’s references, see [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-5|1 Cor 16.5]]; 2 Cor, passim. *Ephesus*, see Acts 18–29; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-32|1 Cor 15.32]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-8|16.8]]; the traditional address of the epistle to the Ephesians, whose Pauline authorship is disputed. *Teach any different doctrine* (Gk “heterodidaskaleo”), appears in the NT only here and in 6.3. ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Myths*, author dismisses all opponents as “mythmakers” rather than true teachers. There is no consensus on the content of such myths, mentioned in the NT only here and in other relatively late texts such as 4.7; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-4|2 Tim 4.4]]; [[Titus 01#^1-14|Titus 1.14]] (in reference to “Jewish myths”—perhaps postbiblical interpretations or midrashim); and [[2 Peter 01#^1-16|2 Pet 1.16]]. *Genealogies*, only elsewhere in [[Titus 03#^3-9|Titus 3.9]]; perhaps a concern with proto-Gnostic speculation. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Pure heart*, see [[Psalms 024#^24-4|Ps 24.4]]; [[Proverbs 22#^22-11|Prov 22.11]]. *Conscience* (Gk “syneidēsis”), the self-awareness that comes with moral maturity (see [[Romans 02#^2-15|Rom 2.15]]); see 1.19; 3.9; 4.2; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-3|2 Tim 1.3]]. *Faith*, suggesting correct beliefs or teachings rather than trust. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-7|7–8]]:** *Law*, in Pauline usage the referent is usually “Torah,” not civil law, although there is nothing specific in the ensuing verses to Torah as opposed to civil law. *Law is good*, see [[Romans 07#^7-12|Rom 7.12]],[[Romans 07#^7-16|16]]. The comment does not appear to be a response to antinomianism. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Law is laid down not for the innocent*, since the Torah is for everyone; the law (Gk “nomos”) thus could refer to any legal code. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-9|9b–10]]:** The list of sins is conventional (see [[Mark 07#^7-21|Mk 7.21–23]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|1 Cor 6.9–10]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-19|Gal 5.19–20]]) and vice lists are common (see [[Colossians 03#^3-5|Col 3.5–9]]; see also, e.g., Tob 4.12; *Jub.* 7.20), especially in later NT writings. ^1-9-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Sodomites,* (Gk “arsenokoites,” lit., “men who lie down/men who bed”), see [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|1 Cor 6.9]]; there Paul’s usage is the term’s first appearance. If this is a reference to male homosexuality, the condemnation is consistent with some contemporary Jewish sources (see Philo, *Spec*. *Laws* 3.37–42*; QG* 2.49; *Virtues* 20–21; *Heir*. 274; and elsewhere; Josephus, *Ag. Ap*. 2.199; Pseudo-Phocylides, *Sent.* 3; *T. Sol*. 4.5). *Sound teaching*, is the key both to the glorious gospel and to proper action**.** ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Gospel*, the “good news” (Gk “euangelion”) of God’s action through Jesus. ^1-11

### 1.12–20: Summary of “Paul’s” role

*[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-12|Text]], p. 434* ^1-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-13|13–14]]:** *Formerly a . . . persecutor*, Paul’s early persecution of Jesus’ followers ([[1 Corinthians 15#^15-9|1 Cor 15.9]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]; cf. [[Acts 08#^8-1|Acts 8.1]]; [[Acts 09#^9-1|9.1–2]]). *Ignorantly*, the epistle is consistent with [[Acts 03#^3-17|Acts 3.17]], where Peter tells the Jews in Jerusalem, “I know that you acted in ignorance, as did also your rulers” in crucifying Jesus. *Mercy* (Gk “eleos,” “compassion”; Heb “hesed”). . . *grace*, (Gk “charis,” “grace,” “favor”; Heb “ḥein”), the loving actions of God to which the believer responds *with the faith and love* . . . *in Christ Jesus*. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Saying is sure*, a frequent phrase in the Pastoral Epistles but absent from the undisputed letters. *Save sinners*, for rabbinic ideas of God as the one who pardons sin, see especially *b. Rosh Ha-Shanah* [[Rosh Hashanah 17#17b|17b]], the thirteen attributes of divine mercy. ^1-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-16|16]]:** *An example*, an appeal to mimesis (imitation): to imitate one’s teachers as they in turn imitate Christ ([[1 Corinthians 11#^11-1|1 Cor 11.1]]). *Eternal life*, see *m. Avot* 2.7, “\[If\] he has gotten teachings of Torah, he has gotten himself life eternal \[Heb “ḥayei ha-ʿolam habaʾ ”\].” ^1-16

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-17|17]]:** *King of the ages* (Gk “basilei tōn aiōnōn”), perhaps a rendering of Heb “melekh ha-ʿolam,” “king of the universe/king of eternity/king forever,” a phrase in the standard rabbinic benediction formulary, based on [[Jeremiah 10#^10-10|Jer 10.10]] and [[Psalms 010#^10-16|Ps 10.16]]. *Immortal, invisible . . . only God*, attributes reflecting Greek philosophy and consistent with the Tanakh. *Amen*, Heb “so be it.” ^1-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-18|18]]:** *Prophecies*, see 4.14; not predictions but words presumed to be from God, in this case concerning Timothy’s ministry. *Fight the good fight*, a military expression, consistent with Pauline language ([[2 Corinthians 10#^10-3|2 Cor 10.3–4]]; Philem 2, and see [[2 Timothy 02#^2-4|2 Tim 2.4]]). ^1-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Conscience*, see 1.4–5nn. *Shipwreck*, common metaphor (e.g., Josephus, *Ant*. 5.183; 12.355; Philo, *Names* 215). ^1-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Timothy 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Hymenaeus*, [[2 Timothy 02#^2-17|2 Tim 2.17]] lists a person by this name who denies resurrection from the dead)*. Alexander*, see [[2 Timothy 04#^4-14|2 Tim 4.14]], probably a reference to the same person. *Turned over to Satan*, for correction ([[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|1 Cor 5.5]]) or for punishment (3.6–7; [[2 Timothy 02#^2-26|2 Tim 2.26]]). *Satan*, (Heb “adversary”; see [[Job 01#^1-6|Job 1.6]]) becomes personified in later Jewish texts (*Jub* 10.11) as the chief evil spirit opposing God’s will. In Christian texts, he is often interchanged with the devil who is seen as leading believers astray (5.15). ^1-20
