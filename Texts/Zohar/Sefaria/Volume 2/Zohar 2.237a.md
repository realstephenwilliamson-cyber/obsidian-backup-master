# Zohar 2.237a

← [[Zohar 2.236b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 2.237b]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 6.06 - Parashat Pequdei#^z2-237a|Parashat Pequdei, at 2:237a]]

*Volume 2, folio 237a* · Pekudei

*Passage Pekudei 36:315 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 2.236b#^pekudei-36-315|Pekudei 36:315]].*

**Pekudei 36:316** In this way the unclean spirit prevailed and regained his former rule over the world. For Israel, through their sin, drew to themselves the evil impulse as formerly. And in order to purge themselves they needed to offer up a goat, inasmuch as the goat is the portion of the evil impulse, that is, the unclean spirit, as already said. ^pekudei-36-316

**Pekudei 36:317** It is written: “And they exchanged their glory for the likeness of an ox that eateth grass” (Ps. 106, 20). This is an allusion to the calf that came from the “left side”. “Their glory” signifies the Shekinah that led the way before them, but which they exchanged for something unclean. Thus will the slimy venom not pass out of the world until the time when the Holy One, blessed be He, will remove it, as it says: “and I will cause the unclean spirit to pass out of the earth” (Zech. 13, 7). ^pekudei-36-317

**Pekudei 36:318** Now some sorcerers succeed in their art and others do not succeed, although they use the same practices, since the success of sorcery depends on the man himself. ^pekudei-36-318

**Pekudei 36:319** We have an example in Balaam, who was the very man for such arts, he being, as tradition tells us, blind in one eye,[^1] and looking askance with the other. But of those who have to serve with the holy spirit it is written: “For whatsoever he be that hath a blemish, he shall not approach: a blind man or a lame” (Lev. 21, 18). ^pekudei-36-319

**Pekudei 36:320** So here the way was paved for the unclean spirit to enter and obtain dominion. It found a desert utterly uninhabitable, as it is written, “wherein were serpents, fiery serpents, and scorpions”, etc. (Deut. 8, 15), a spot that belongs to his dominion, and that contained gold enough for his use. It also found in Aaron a medium through which to insinuate itself into the “right” side. Thus the place being in all ways suitable it emerged into the open, and the deed was done. ^pekudei-36-320

**Pekudei 36:321** So when Aaron wished to become purged, he offered up a calf so as to execute judgement, as it were, on the “evil side”. Whereas before he brought forth the Calf to make it ruler, he afterwards offered up a calf in order to subdue the evil power, for once punishment is executed on the “left side” all the subordinate rulers of that side are subdued. ^pekudei-36-321

**Pekudei 36:322** In regard to this, Scripture says: “Eat not of it raw… but roast with fire, its head on its legs” (Ex. 12, 8), so as to break the evil power and subdue it, and so that all its subordinates should no more exercise rule. ^pekudei-36-322

**Pekudei 37:323** Similarly we read: “a red heifer, faultless”, etc. (Num. 19, 2). This had the same object, namely, to subdue all those sides of the unclean spirit.’ ^pekudei-37-323

**Pekudei 37:324** Said R. Abba to R. Eleazar: ‘But is not the heifer sacred and purifying? and how can this be?’ R. Eleazar replied: ‘Indeed, it is so. It has been expounded that it was an epitome of the four Kingdoms[^2] Thus, the “heifer” is Israel, of whom it is written, “For Israel is stubborn like a stubborn heifer” (Hos. 4, 16); “a red [heifer]” indicates the Kingdom of Babylonia, regarding which it says, “thou art the head of gold” (Dan. 2, 38); “faultless” points to the Kingdom of Media;[^3] and “wherein is no blemish” indicates the Kingdom of Greece (who were near the true faith). “Upon which never came yoke” alludes to the Kingdom of Edom,[^4] which was never under the yoke of any other power. ^pekudei-37-324

**Pekudei 37:325** It is written: “Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? Not one” (Job. 14, 4). The heifer is such a thing; for first it was an unclean thing,[^5] but after judgement had been executed on her, after she had passed through the fire and was burned to ashes, she was transformed into a purifying agency. ^pekudei-37-325

[^1]: v. Rashi ad Num. 24, 15.
[^2]: v. Daniel VIII.
[^3]: An allusion to Cyrus, the liberator of the Babylonian Jews.
[^4]: i.e. Rome.
[^5]: i.e. a symbol of the evil power that was to be destroyed.
