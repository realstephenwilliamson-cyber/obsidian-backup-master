### 24.1–25.12: Paul appears before Roman governors

### 24.1–27: Paul and Felix

*[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-1|Text]], p. 272* ^24-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-1|1]]:** *Ananias*, see 23.2. *Tertullus*, the otherwise unknown person bears a Latin name. *Attorney,* the use of lawyers in judicial proceedings was an established practice. The presence of a trained professional strengthens the forces allied against Paul and thus makes his defense more impressive. ^24-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-2|2–4]]:** The speech begins conventionally by praising the governor for accomplishments and virtues. ^24-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-5|5]]:** *Agitator*, a serious charge of fomenting rebellion (16.20; 17.6; 19.40). *Sect*, Gk “hairesis” meaning “party” or “school”; Josephus uses it to describe Pharisees, Sadducees, and Essenes (see 26.5). *Nazarenes*, Acts frequently uses “Nazarene” for Jesus (2.22; 3.6; 4.10; 6.14; 22.8; 26.9), but only here, in the plural, for Christians. Tertullus’s use of this term, rather than a self-identification such as the Way or Christians, suggests that he has a poor understanding of the believers. ^24-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-6|6]]:** *Profane the temple*, see 21.28–29. Such an act could have caused an uprising, which would have been a concern of the Roman government. ^24-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-9|9]]:** *The Jews,* indicating that the persecution of Paul is not restricted to the Jewish leadership. ^24-9

### 24.10–21: Paul refutes each charge

*[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-10|Text]], p. 272* ^24-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-10|10]]:** *Cheerfully*, lit., “with a good spirit,” willingly. ^24-10-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-14|14–16]]:** Paul is presented as a faithful and observant Jew; see 16.3; 18.18; 20.16; 22.3; 25.4–5. ^24-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-14|14]]:** *Way*, see 9.2. This term, rather than “sect,” seems to reflect Paul’s understanding of Christianity as the one, true way of God, rather than a particular or exclusive school or community (see v 5n.). *Law . . . prophets*, the phrase had come to connote the fullness of Jewish teachings, and possibly a written collection of authoritative texts ([[Matthew 05#^5-17|Mt 5.17]]; [[Matthew 22#^22-40|22.40]]; [[Luke 16#^16-16|Lk 16.16]]; Sir Prologue; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 15#^15-9|2 Macc 15.9]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 18#^18-10|4 Macc 18.10]]). ^24-14-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-15|15]]:** *Resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous*, see [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]. ^24-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-17|17]]:** *Bring alms*, possibly a continuation of Paul’s self-presentation as a pious Jew, or a reference to a collection of funds from Paul’s congregations to the Jerusalem church (not explicitly mentioned in Acts but described in Paul’s letters: [[Romans 15#^15-25|Rom 15.25–29]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-1|1 Cor 16.1–4]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-10|Gal 2.10]]). ^24-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-19|19]]:** *Some Jews from Asia*, see 21.27. ^24-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-21|21]]:** *Resurrection*, see 23.6. ^24-21

### 24.22–27: Felix’s response

*[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-22|Text]], p. 273* ^24-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-22|22]]:** *Lysias*, see 23.26. ^24-22-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-24|24]]:** *Drusilla*, Julia Drusilla, daughter of Agrippa I (see 12.1n.); known for her beauty, she had been previously married to Azizus, king of Syrian Emesa, who had himself circumcised (Josephus, *Ant* 20.139). ^24-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-26|26]]:** *Money would be given him*, Felix had a reputation for corruption and cruelty (Joesphus, *Ant* 20.160–65; Tacitus, *Ann.* 12.54; *Hist.* 5.9). ^24-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 24#^24-27|27]]:** *Two years*, a possible indication of the time Paul was imprisoned, or an indication that Felix’s term of service (i.e., a two-year term) had expired. If the former, the extensive time in prison without a formal sentence or punishment speaks to Paul’s innocence. *Porcius Festus*, procurator of Judea, assumed office in the late 50s until 62. Josephus reports that Festus took forceful action against nationalist and messianic groups (*J.W.* 2.271; *Ant.* 20.185–88). *Grant the Jews a favor*, Felix’s successor, Festus, acts from the same motivation; see 25.9. ^24-27
