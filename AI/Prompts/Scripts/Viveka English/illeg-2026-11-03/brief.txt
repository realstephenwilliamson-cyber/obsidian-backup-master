READING BOXED WORDS ON PRINTED PAGES (Dyczkowski's translation of the Tantraloka, 3 Nov 2026). Reading only: write nothing but your
own output files.

The images are pages of Mark Dyczkowski's English translation of Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka with Jayaratha's commentary. You are
checking what the print actually shows. Report the print exactly, even where the print itself looks wrong. Never fill in what a word
"should" be.

FILES (all under /home/claude/ill/)
- sheets2/<sheet>.png: each sheet stacks up to 12 strips. Each strip shows one printed line (sometimes two) under a grey bar with its
  ID, and a red box around a word or a few words.
- Your task file lists, sheet by sheet, each ID with the text just BEFORE and just AFTER the boxed text, from a transcription that
  may contain errors. Use it only to be sure which words are meant. The boxed text itself is deliberately not given.

FOR EACH ID
1. Find the boxed text. The box may be a little too wide or too narrow: transcribe the whole word(s) between the end of the "before"
   text and the start of the "after" text.
2. Transcribe it exactly as printed, letter by letter, with every diacritic: ā ī ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ṅ ñ ṭ ḍ ṇ ś ṣ ḥ, capitals Ś Ā Ī Ū Ṛ Ṭ Ḍ, and
   the anusvāra as ṁ (this book prints m with a dot above). Look closely at the first and the last letter of each word.
3. Include punctuation, quotation marks, brackets and verse bars printed against the word, exactly as printed: ( ) [ ] ‘ ’ “ ” , . ; :
   | || (write the bars with the ordinary vertical bar).
4. A small raised footnote number printed against the word is not part of it: write it as ^N after the word (e.g. them),^22).
5. If you cannot read a letter with confidence, write ? for it and set conf to "low". A dot or macron you cannot actually see is
   not there: mark it ? and low.
6. If the box is on the wrong words, or the text runs over a line end with a hyphen, say so in note.
