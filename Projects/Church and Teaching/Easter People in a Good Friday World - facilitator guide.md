**Movement I — Good Friday Is Real**

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 13%" />
<col style="width: 86%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>READ<br />
ALOUD</strong></td>
<td><em>The Episcopal tradition does not flinch from lament. [[Psalms 022#^22-1|Psalm 22]] — the psalm Jesus quoted from the cross — opens: “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” That is not a failure of faith. That is faith. Bringing raw grief to God is not the opposite of prayer — it is prayer. A Good Friday world means illness, injustice, grief that doesn’t resolve on any schedule. We do not rush past this.</em></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

**Movement II — Easter Is Also Real**

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 13%" />
<col style="width: 86%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>READ<br />
ALOUD</strong></td>
<td><em>Easter people are not optimists. They hold a specific theological claim: death does not have the final word. Paul writes in [[Romans 08#^8-1|Romans 8]] of hope that is ‘not yet seen’ — hope with teeth, not wishful thinking. And notice: the risen Christ still bears wounds. Easter does not erase the scars. It holds them differently.</em></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

**Movement III — The Tension Is the Spiritual Life**

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 13%" />
<col style="width: 86%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>READ<br />
ALOUD</strong></td>
<td><em>In [[John 11#^11-1|John 11]], Jesus weeps at Lazarus’s tomb — not because he doesn’t know what he’s about to do, but because he enters fully into the grief of those he loves before he acts. Being Easter people means we are allowed — called, even — to grieve fully while trusting ultimately. Holding both is not confusion. It is the spiritual life.</em></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

> **Note:** *Spend at least 7–8 minutes on Movement I. Don’t rush to resurrection.*
