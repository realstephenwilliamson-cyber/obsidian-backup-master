## Chapter 4

**4:1** The angel who talked with me came back and woke me as a man is wakened from sleep. ^4-1

**4:2** He said to me, “What do you see?” And I answered, “I see a lampstand all of gold, with a bowl above it. The lamps on it are seven in number, and the lamps above it have[^1] seven pipes; ^4-2

**4:3** and by it are two olive trees, one on the right of the bowl and one on its left.” ^4-3

**4:4** I, in turn, asked the angel who talked with me, “What do those things mean, my lord?” ^4-4

**4:5** “Do you not know what those things mean?” asked the angel who talked with me; and I said, “No, my lord.” ^4-5

**4:6** Then he explained to me as follows:[^2]  
“This is the word of the LORD to Zerubbabel:[^3] Not by might, nor by power, but by My spirit[^4]—said the LORD of Hosts. ^4-6

**4:7** Whoever you are, O great mountain in the path of Zerubbabel, turn into level ground! For he shall produce that excellent stone; it shall be greeted with shouts of ‘Beautiful! Beautiful!’” ^4-7

**4:8** And the word of the LORD came to me: ^4-8

**4:9** “Zerubbabel’s hands have founded this House and Zerubbabel’s hands shall complete it. Then you shall know that it was the LORD of Hosts who sent me to you. ^4-9

**4:10** Does anyone scorn a day of small beginnings? When they see the stone of distinction[^5] in the hand of Zerubbabel, they shall rejoice.  
“Those seven are the eyes of the LORD, ranging over the whole earth.” ^4-10

**4:11** “And what,” I asked him, “are those two olive trees, one on the right and one on the left of the lampstand?” ^4-11

**4:12** And I further asked him, “What are the two tops[^6] of the olive trees that feed their gold[^7] through those two golden tubes?”[^8] ^4-12

**4:13** He asked me, “Don’t you know what they are?” And I replied, “No, my lord.” ^4-13

**4:14** Then he explained, “They are the two anointed dignitaries[^9] who attend the Lord of all the earth.” ^4-14

[^1]: Emendation yields “bowl above it has.”
[^2]: The explanation is given in the last sentence of v. 10.
[^3]: A grandson of King Jehoiachin (1 Chron. 3.17–19) and the secular head of the repatriated community (Hag. 1.1; etc.).
[^4]: I.e., Zerubbabel will succeed by means of spiritual gifts conferred upon him by the Lord; cf. Isa. 11.2. ff.
[^5]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; others “plummet.”
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; literally “ears” (as of grain).
[^7]: Emendation yields “oil”; cf. v. 14.
[^8]: Or “funnels”; through them the oil runs from the olive trees into the bowl of vv. 2 and 3.
[^9]: I.e., the high priest and the king (cf. 3.8–9 with note); lit. “sons of oil.”
