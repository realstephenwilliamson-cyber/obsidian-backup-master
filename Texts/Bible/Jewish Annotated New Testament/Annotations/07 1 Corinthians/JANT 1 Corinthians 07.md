### 7.1–7: Sexuality in marriage

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-1|Text]], p. 332* · This chapter, further clarifying how the “ekklēsia” should maintain its purity, addresses concerns raised in a letter Paul received from Corinth. ^7-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-1|1]]:** *“It is well . . .*,*”* another Corinthian slogan (see 6.12n.); teachings associated with Jesus ([[Matthew 19#^19-12|Mt 19.12]]), Paul himself (7.7), or end-time preparation, perhaps influenced by apocalyptic and mystical celibates, like the Essenes (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.119; *1 En*. 83.2). ^7-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-2|2]]:** *His own wife . . .  her own husband*, marriage helps prevent immorality (see Tob 4.12; *T. Levi* 9.9–10). Monogamous marriage, in this view, is the only permissible relationship (see “Sexual Mores,” p. 222). ^7-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-3|3]]:** *Conjugal rights*, Jewish tradition mandates a certain frequency of sexual intercourse according to a man’s profession; it also requires limited periods of abstinence during times of ritual impurity ([[Torah 28 - Metzora#^15-19|Lev 15.19–30]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-19|18.19]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-18|20.18]]; *m. Ketub.* 5.6; *t. Ned.* 5.6; *b. Nidd.* 66a). ^7-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-5|5]]:** *Deprive . . . set time*, for example, abstaining from sexual intercourse before prayer or other contact with the Divine ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-15|Ex 19.15]]; Philo, *Mos.* 2.68–9; *T. Naph.* 8.8; *Sifre Num*. on [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-1|Num 12.1]] \[99\]; *Ex. Rab.* 19; 46.3; *Avot de R. Natan B* 9.39; *Tanh.* 111.46; *Sylloge Inscriptionum Graecarum* 3.982; *b. Pesah.* 87b; *b. Shabb.* 87a;). *Lack of self-control*, see [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-3|1 Thess 4.3–6]]; Paul, like other Jewish authors, regards sexual license as part of Gentile culture (*Jub*. 3.31; *m*. *Avod. Zar.* 2.1; *b. Yebam.* 98a). ^7-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-6|6]]:** *Concession*, celibacy is preferable to marriage. ^7-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-7|7]]:** *As I myself am*, celibate; there is no evidence that Paul married. *Gift*, see 3.5–9; 7.17–24; 10–13; 12–14. ^7-7

> [!note] Sexual Mores *(p. 333)*
> Sexual behavior was highly regulated in the Re-publican Roman period. The Emperor Augustus’s adultery law mandated that an unfaithful wife be divorced, but it allowed husbands to engage in sexual relations with unmarried women and slaves (*Lex Julia* 123). Roman law presumed the husband’s *authority* over the wife’s body, but not the wife’s *authority* over the husband’s body, the position advanced by Paul and others (Plutarch, *Mor.* 144b; Musonius Rufus, frag. 9.5,7). Rabbinic law accorded women some authority over her husband’s body, namely, the right to sexual relations (*m*. *Ketub.* 5.6). Furthermore, Roman law did not permit polygyny, and it only recognized marriage between citizens, not slaves (Gaius, *Inst.* 1.63–4; Ulpian, frag. 5.3). Jewish law of the time defined adultery in terms of the wife’s infidelity but not the husband’s (as long as his sexual partner was unmarried \[[[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.14]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-10|Lev 20.10]]; [[Proverbs 06#^6-32|Prov 6.32]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 3.274\]), since polygyny (marrying multiple wives) was permissible. Polygyny among Second Temple period Jews is attested (Josephus, *Ant.* 12.186–89; 17.14; *J.W.* 1.477; *m. Yebam.* 4.11), although the practice is condemned by the Essenes (CD 4.20–21; 11Q19 57.17-18); the tenth-century German Rabbi Gershom ben Judah Me*ʾ*or ha-Golah banned polygyny.
>
> Divorce was easily available and widely tolerated by Roman citizens. Jewish attitudes toward divorce were not homogeneous, as illustrated by the divergent rulings attributed to the two first-century sages Hillel and Shammai as well as to Jesus ([[Matthew 05#^5-31|Mt 5.31–32]]; [[Mark 10#^10-2|Mk 10.2–12]]; also see [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-1|Deut 24.1]]; [[Matthew 01#^1-9|Mt 1.9]]; [[Luke 16#^16-18|Lk 16.18]]). Whereas Hillel permitted divorce for even the most trivial reason, Shammai only allowed a man to divorce his wife if she was not faithful to him (*m. Git.* 9.10). King Herod’s sister Salome initiated her own divorce proceedings, which Josephus claims is contrary to Jewish law (*Ant.* 15.7.10 \[259\], 4.8.23 \[253\]; see also Philo, *Spec. Laws* 3.5 \[30–31\]). See “Gender,” p. 611.
>
> Celibacy was cultivated by some Essenes (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.8.2 \[119\]) and, according to Philo, by the Jewish enclave in Egypt, the Therapeutae (*Cont. Life* 68). Jesus praises those who “make themselves eunuchs” for the kingdom of heaven ([[Matthew 19#^19-12|Mt 19.12]]), and the book of Revelation identifies the first who are saved in the final judgment as male *virgins* ([[Revelation 14#^14-4|Rev 14.4]]). In light of his expectation of the Christ’s imminent return, Paul was more concerned about *changes* in marital status than marital status per se. Nonetheless, Paul’s teachings inspired a second-century movement of Christian celibacy (*Acts of Paul and Thecla* 3.5), and virginity was esteemed as a Christian virtue (Tertullian, *Exh. cast.* ). The practice did not survive among Jews, perhaps in part because it was so emphasized by the early church. Christians began mandating celibacy for priests in the fourth century (Council of Elvira, canon 33). While the tradition of clerical celibacy continued in the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Church permitted clergy to remain married if they were already married at the time of their ordination (Council of Trullo, canon 13). After the Protestant Reformation, nearly all Protestant churches abolished compulsory celibacy for clergy, though some sectarian movements (the Shakers) and non-Roman Catholic monastics (e.g., Anglican religious orders) reintroduced it.

### 7.8–16: On divorce

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-8|Text]], p. 334* ^7-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-9|9]]:** *Self-control* over sexual desire, a philosophical discipline cultivated by Neo-Pla-tonists, Aristotelians, Stoics, Cynics, Philo, and rabbis (Philo, *On the Creation.* 164; *Avot de R. Natan* 16). ^7-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-10|10]]:** *Lord*, Jesus (see [[Mark 10#^10-9|Mk 10.9]] and “Sexual Mores,” p. 222). ^7-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-11|11]]:** *Unmarried or else be reconciled*, as opposed to marrying another. Paul glosses Jesus’ command regarding divorce ([[Matthew 05#^5-31|Mt 5.31–32]]). ^7-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-12|12]]:** Fearing idolatrous influences, moral corruption, and/or defilement of Israel’s holy seed, some Jews banned exoga-my ([[Ezra 09#^9-1|Ezra 9.1–2]]; Tob 4.12 \[see also [[I Kings 11#^11-2|1 Kings 11.2]]\]; *Jub.* 30.7; 4QMMTd frags. 5+6, 6; *T. Levi* 9.10; *T. Job* 45.3; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 3.29; *L. A. B.* 9.5; 21.1) although there is ample evidence for intermarriage ([[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1–4]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 8.191–93; 16.225; 18.109,139–40; 20.139–47). *B. Qidd.* 68b and *b. Yebam.* 45a regard intermarriages as invalid. ^7-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-14|14]]:** *Holy*, elevated to a status of sanctity. Paul’s view that unbelievers do not defile believers aligns with rabbinic opinion that regards sexual contact with Gentiles as neutral (*Sifra Pereq Zavim* 1.1). In Paul’s time, Jews followed biblical norms regarding children of intermarriages, who were considered Jewish if their fathers were Jewish (see 7.12n.; biblical examples include the children of Joseph and Moses, and Absalom \[[[II Samuel 03#^3-3|2 Sam 3.3]]\]). Although rabbinic opinions diverged, by late antiquity, matrilineal descent became normative in Jewish law (*t. Qidd.* 4.16). ^7-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-16|16]]:** *Save*, at the final judgment (see 7.14). ^7-16

### 7.17–24: Eschatological urgency and social stability

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-17|Text]], p. 334* ^7-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-18|18]]:** *Remove the marks*, epispasm, or foreskin reconstruction, likely motivated by the embarrassment of nude athletic competitions and public bathing (see [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-15|1 Macc 1.15]]; Josephus *Ant.* 12.241; *T. Mos.* 8.3). *Circumcised*, some Jesus-followers advocated circumcision for Gentiles ([[Galatians 02#^2-3|Gal 2.3–12]]). *Not seek circumcision*, elsewhere Paul notes benefits of circumcision (i.e., as a member of the Jewish people; [[Romans 03#^3-1|Rom 3.1–2]]). ^7-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-19|19]]:** *Nothing*, since the eschatological “new covenant” (11.25) privileged moral *commandments* (e.g., prohibitions of idolatry, sexual immorality, theft, greed, murder \[[[Romans 13#^13-9|Rom 13.9]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-18|1 Cor 6.18]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-3|Eph 5.3–5]]\]) over circumcision*.* ^7-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-21|21]]:** *Even if . . . present condition* (words added by the NRSV and absent in the laconic Gk; the conjunction could also mean “but”, and the verb *make use of* lacks a direct object), as slaves. According to the NRSV translation, slaves should make the best of their current status, but Paul may be encouraging them to *make use of* the opportunity for emancipation *if* available to them. ^7-21

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-22|22]]:** *Slave . . .  freed person*, Paul plays on the terms’ Roman legal usage and their met-aphoric sense in relation to Christ. ^7-22

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-23|23]]:** *Bought*, see 6.20n. ^7-23

### 7.25–40: Unmarried believers

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-25|Text]], p. 335* ^7-25

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-25|25]]:** *Virgins*, never-married women. *Trustworthy*, Paul’s authority derives from *the Lord’s mercy* in choosing him to spread the gospel (see [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-3|2 Cor 3.3–6]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-1|4.1]]). ^7-25-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-26|26]]:** *Impending crisis*, imminent end-time. ^7-26

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-27|27]]:** *Seek to be free*, see 7.11. ^7-27

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-28|28]]:** *Distress*, demands of marriage and family (7.33–34). ^7-28

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-29|29]]:** *Be . . . had none*, practice abstinence. ^7-29

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-30|30–31]]:** Mourners, celebrants, and patrons behaving contrary to their expected roles in preparation for the end-time (cf. 2 Esd \[*4 Ezra*\] 3.24; 4.26; 16.42–45). ^7-30

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-32|32]]:** *Anxieties*, see 7.28. ^7-32

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-36|36]]:** *Fiancée*, lit., “his virgin”; Jewish and Roman marriage consisted of two stages, betrothal (binding pledge) and nuptials (consummation). ^7-36

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-37|37]]:** *Keep her*, without consummation. ^7-37

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-39|39]]:** *In the Lord*, within the church, extending the Jewish desideratum of endogamy to include Gentile believers (see 7.12n.). ^7-39

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 07#^7-40|40]]:** *I too have the Spirit of God*, to exercise his *judgment* and issue directives to the community. ^7-40
