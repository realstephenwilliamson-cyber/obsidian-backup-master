# Ecclesiastes 1 (NRSVue)

— · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Ecclesiastes 02|Ecclesiastes 2 →]]

## Reflections of a royal philosopher

**1:1** The words of the Teacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem. ^1-1

**1:2** Vanity of vanities, says the Teacher, vanity of vanities! All is vanity. ^1-2

**1:3** What do people gain from all the toil at which they toil under the sun? ^1-3

**1:4** A generation goes, and a generation comes, but the earth remains forever. ^1-4

**1:5** The sun rises, and the sun goes down and hurries to the place where it rises. ^1-5

**1:6** The wind blows to the south and goes around to the north; round and round goes the wind, and on its circuits the wind returns. ^1-6

**1:7** All streams run to the sea, but the sea is not full; to the place where the streams flow, there they continue to flow. ^1-7

**1:8** All things^[Or *words*] are wearisome, more than one can express; the eye is not satisfied with seeing or the ear filled with hearing. ^1-8

**1:9** What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done; there is nothing new under the sun. ^1-9

**1:10** Is there a thing of which it is said, “See, this is new”? It has already been in the ages before us. ^1-10

**1:11** The people of long ago are not remembered, nor will there be any remembrance of people yet to come by those who come after them. ^1-11

## The futility of seeking wisdom

**1:12** I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem. ^1-12

**1:13** I applied my mind to seek and to search out by wisdom all that is done under heaven; it is an unhappy business that God has given to humans to be busy with. ^1-13

**1:14** I saw all the deeds that are done under the sun, and see, all is vanity and a chasing after wind. ^1-14

**1:15** What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is lacking cannot be counted. ^1-15

**1:16** I said to myself, “I have acquired great wisdom, surpassing all who were over Jerusalem before me, and my mind has had great experience of wisdom and knowledge.” ^1-16

**1:17** And I applied my mind to know wisdom and to know madness and folly. I perceived that this also is but a chasing after wind. ^1-17

**1:18** For in much wisdom is much vexation, and those who increase knowledge increase sorrow. ^1-18
