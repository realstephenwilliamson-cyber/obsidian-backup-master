## Mūla

ज्योतीरूपमथ प्राणशक्त्याख्यं जीवमाहरेत् ।
जीवं समरसीकुर्याद्देवीचक्रेण भावनात् ॥ ^mula

jyotīrūpamatha prāṇaśaktyākhyaṃ jīvamāharet |

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 05 - Chapter Sixteen, part 1#^pg40|p. 20]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=40>))
>
> By virtue of this procedure, the teacher may extract from the animal all its other (vital) essences also, such as (its) humours (rasa), blood and the like, each by itself or all together. Or he may also extract just the soul (jīva) (of the animal) alone, the form of which is light (jyotis) and, called the power of the vital breath (prāṇaśakti), is present in the centre (of the heart), where Fire, Sun and Moon unite. (with 16.45cd–47ab)
>
> The (animal’s) soul should be made one (samarasa) with the wheel of the goddesses by imagining (it to be so) (bhāvanāt). That itself is the principal (and supreme) libation (tarpaṇa), which is a state of (oneness) of the enjoyer and the object of enjoyment. (with 16.47cd–48ab)
