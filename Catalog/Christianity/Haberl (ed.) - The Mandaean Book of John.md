## About

A Mandaean holy book in Mandaic Aramaic, called *Drāšā ḏ-Yaḥyā*. Mandaeans attribute it to John the Baptist, their prophet. It gathers accounts of John's life and miracles, polemical exchanges with Jesus, and tractates on creation, the soul and the planets.

- **Contents:** 76 chapters (tractates), arranged by subject rather than date. Chapters 18–33 concern John (Yahya or Yuhana); in the last, chapter 76, Anush (Enosh) works miracles in Jerusalem that resemble those of Jesus.
- **Date:** Wikipedia says it was compiled around the 7th century, shortly after the Muslim conquest of Persia, from texts many of which are centuries older; Häberl and McGrath hold that its present form is no earlier than the Islamic conquests. Wikipedia reports up to five stages of redaction. Chapter 11 refers to an "end of the Age of Mars" that falls in 678; chapter 43 holds Second Temple material that fits a first-century setting. Chapter 30 is disputed: Latin loanwords put it after the 4th century, one view calls it pre-Islamic, and a later analysis finds an Arabic loanword.
- **Manuscripts:** three in the Bibliothèque nationale de France (Paris A–C); two in the Bodleian (Huntington 71, and Drower Collection 30, copied at Shushtar in 1166 AH, c. 1753); fragments in the British Library; and later copies, among them three in the United States copied in 1910, 1922 and 1989.
- **Translations:** Mark Lidzbarski's German, *Das Johannesbuch der Mandäer* (1915); the first complete English translation, by Carlos Gelbert and Mark Lofts (Living Water Books, 2017); and Charles G. Häberl and James F. McGrath's critical edition, translation and commentary (De Gruyter). Wikipedia and the JAOS review date it 2020; De Gruyter lists both formats as released on 18 November 2019, and Butler University's repository cites it as 2019.
- **Why it matters:** Britannica lists it among the more important extant Mandaean writings. De Gruyter's description calls Mandaeism the only Gnostic religion to survive from antiquity to the present.
