### 8.1–39: The Spirit-enabled life

*[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-1|Text]], p. 301* · Continuing the argument from chs 5–7, Paul explains that although still in the body and thus subject to human limitations, God’s Spirit lives in those who set their minds on the things of the Spirit in faithfulness to Christ. Conflict between spiritual conventions and human limitations will continue, for Christ’s followers are still living in bodies (i.e., in the present age), but victory is assured for all of creation, which will be rescued when the awaited age arrives fully. ^8-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-1|1]]:** *No condemnation*, the ch continues the change of direction begun in 7.25, and the overall argument of ch 7. ^8-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-2|2]]:** *Spirit*, Gk “pneuma,” also meaning “breath,” see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-2|Gen 1.2]]; Ezek 37; [[Psalms 139#^139-7|Ps 139.7]]. *Spirit of life*, the presence of the divine righteousness and life, working through *Christ Jesus*, to *free* the followers of Jesus from *the law* (i.e., the principle) *of sin and of death*. *Law of the Spirit . . . of sin and of death,* Gk “nomos tou pneumatos . . . hamartias . . . thanatou,” “the law/principle of the spirit” as opposed to that “of error and death.” The rightness of the law/principle is confirmed, not dismissed, by those enabled to fulfill it by conducting their lives according to the spirit of life instead of, with Adam, the spirit of death. ^8-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-3|3]]:** *The law, weakened by the flesh*, the moral convention or principle that provoked the actions it was prohibiting because of humanity’s slavery to sin. *Flesh*, Heb “basar,” can mean the body ([[Job 19#^19-26|Job 19.26]]); all of humankind ([[Isaiah 66#^66-16|Isa 66.16]]); the weaker side of human nature (Ezek 35.26); weakness ([[Isaiah 31#^31-3|Isa 31.3]]); and food ([[Daniel 07#^7-5|Dan 7.5]]). *To deal with sin*, lit., “concerning/for sin” or (less likely), “and on behalf of (being) a sin-offering” (see translators’ note *c*). The “sin-offering” (better, “purification offering”) translation, evoking Lev 4,12,14, and 15, involved sprinkling of blood, cleansing parts of the Temple, so that God’s presence could continue to reside there (see “Sacrifice and the Temple,” p. 658). ^8-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-4|4–6]]:** Paul moves from declaring a state of change already accomplished, to a conditional state that one can accomplish in a new way, but one that requires “walking” or “norms for living” (i.e., “halakhah”) to accomplish it. *Just requirement of the law*, God’s principle/norm. *Spirit*, i.e., divine capability, in contrast to human limitations/desires (*flesh,* passions in the limbs). ^8-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-6|6]]:** *Peace*, cf. Heb “shalom,” well-being. ^8-6

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-8|8–9]]:** *In the flesh . . . not in the flesh,* the contrast is between those who focus on how they are constrained by human limitations and those who are enabled by God’s Spirit. Those having the spirit of Christ are not constrained by the flesh, although they are in mortal bodies. ^8-8

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-10|10]]:** *Dead*, not functioning as it was designed to be lived in; they are not simply spiritual beings, but people with bodies, and bodies involve flesh, both in terms of being known to look a certain way, and in terms of social reputation*. Spirit is life*, lit., “spirit is alive.” ^8-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-13|13]]:** *You will live,* appears to ancient authorities read *me* or *us* c Or *and as a sin offering* d Other ancient authorities read *the Christ* or *Christ Jesus* or *Jesus Christ* e Other ancient authorities read *on account of* f Gk *brothers* indicate living spiritually rather than sinfully. ^8-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-14|14]]:** *Children,* lit., “sons,” both Jews and Gentiles; members of the family who do right rather than wrong. ^8-14

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-15|15]]:** *For you*, Gentiles; *Spirit of adoption*, not naturally born, but adopted as sons/children. *When we*, whether Jew or Greek. *Abba*, Aramaic for “Father,” i.e., what Jews say; *Father*, Gk “Patēr,” what Gentiles say. Together they invoke the same father, albeit in different languages (since they are from different nations). ^8-15

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-16|16]]:** *We*, Jew and Greek. ^8-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-17|17]]:** *If . . . we suffer with him*, suffering is endured for identification with Christ. ^8-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-18|18–39]]:** Paul turns to the hope of Christ-believers and of creation. Assertions of delivery from the body of death are now qualified (in v 15 adoption is a present reality; in v 23 it is still awaited). Thus it is not clear how the reality claimed in Christ has changed for the Christ-following Gentiles: suffering continues, including weakness in the body that requires the Spirit’s intercession. Hope — anticipation of what has been promised — and thus living in a way that is both faithful to that reality and that is also reliant on God’s spirit (e.g., [[Isaiah 32#^32-15|Isa 32.15–17]]; 44; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-24|Ezek 36.24–38]]; 37; [[Psalms 139#^139-7|Ps 139.7]]), was already a central concern in Jewish tradition. Qumran literature speaks of dependence upon the Spirit (1QH 7.6; 12.11–12; 13.18–19; 16.11–12). ^8-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-19|19]]:** *Creation waits*, [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-17|Gen 3.17]], creation also became enslaved because of Adam’s sin. ^8-19

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-20|20]]:** *Subjected to futility*, perhaps as a consequence of the disobedience of Adam. *By the will of*, “the will” not in Gk; *the one who subjected it*, or because of the one submitting, i.e., Adam submitting to or giving in to sin. ^8-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-23|23]]:** *For adoption*, omitted in some of the best manuscripts. *Redemption of our bodies*, see [[Psalms 044#^44-26|Ps 44.25–26]]. ^8-23

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-26|26–27]]:** See Ps 7.11; 139.1. *Spirit intercedes*, articulates the needs of *the saints* better than they can themselves. *And God*, lit., “But the one”; *searches the heart*, probes the innermost thoughts; see, e.g., [[Jeremiah 17#^17-10|Jer 17.10]]; [[Psalms 139#^139-23|Ps 139.23]]; [[I Chronicles 28#^28-9|1 Chr 28.9]]. *The Spirit*, not in Gk; lit., “because it/he”; *according to the will of God*, lit., “according to God.” ^8-26

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-28|28]]:** *For good*, see [[LXX Sirach 39#^39-25|Sir 39.25–27]]. *Love God*, see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-6|Ex 20.6]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-10|Deut 5.10]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|6.5]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-9|7.9]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-12|10.12]]; [[Psalms 031#^31-24|Ps 31.24]]; 97.10. For similar statements, see *Pss. Sol.* 4.25; Plato, *Resp.* 10.12 (612e–613a). ^8-28

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-9|9]]:** Cf. 1QS 3.15–16; 11.10–11,17–20; 1QH 1.7–8; CD 2.8. ^8-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-29|29]]:** *Foreknew*, God’s sovereignty over the future, and not a comment on free will; see 9.10–13n. *Within a large family*, lit., “among many brothers (and sisters)” (see translators’ note *d*). Jesus Christ is the firstborn of those raised from the dead (for non-Pauline expression of this idea, see [[Colossians 01#^1-15|Col 1.15]],[[Colossians 01#^1-18|18]]). ^8-29

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-30|30]]:** Cf. 1QM 3.2; 4.10–11. *Justified*, see 2.13; *glorified*, presented in a redeemed/resurrected body. Paul speaks as though these things had already been accomplished. ^8-30

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-31|31]]:** See Ps 118, esp. vv 5–9. Diatribe style through v 39, often with an implied negative answer. ^8-31

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-32|32]]:** *Withhold his own Son*, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-16|Gen 22.16]], the *Akedah* (binding of Isaac). See [[Romans 03#^3-24|Rom 3.24–25]]; *Father!” 16The Spirit itself bears witness* c Or *by* d Other ancient authorities read *awaits* e Other ancient authorities add *for us* f Gk *the one* 5.6–11; 11.28–29; 15.8–9. ^8-32

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-33|33]]:** *Bring any charge*, see [[Isaiah 50#^50-7|Isa 50.7–9]]. *Elect*, chosen. ^8-33

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-34|34]]:** *Right hand*, application of [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]], referring to the Davidic king, to Jesus. This sentence may be a question. *Intercedes*, prays for, appeals on behalf of. Intercessory figures played a minimal role in the Tanakh ([[Job 33#^33-23|Job 33.23]]), and became more prominent in the late Hellenistic period. ^8-34

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-35|35]]:** It is not clear if this relates to a specific situation in Rome or is more general; see Deut 28; [[I Chronicles 21#^21-11|1 Chr 21.11–12]]; [[II Chronicles 20#^20-9|2 Chr 20.9]]; [[II Chronicles 32#^32-11|32.11]]. ^8-35

**[[NRSVue Romans 08#^8-36|36]]:** [[Psalms 044#^44-23|Ps 44.22]], in its context, concerning suffering in the psalmist’s earlier time period; Paul applies the psalm to his argument about present suffering, in the style of pesher: the point in Ps 44 is that, for God’s sake, faithful Israel suffers. ^8-36
