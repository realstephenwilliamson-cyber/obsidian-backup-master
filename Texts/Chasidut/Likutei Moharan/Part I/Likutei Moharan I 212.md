# Likutei Moharan I 212

← [[Likutei Moharan I 211]] · [[Likutei Moharan]] · [[Likutei Moharan I 213]] →

*Part I · Lesson 212* · [[Likutei Moharan]]

## 1

This is the aspect of clapping, by means of which we are able to peer at God’s portrait.

God’s portrait consists of the representations by which we depict Him: merciful, compassionate, and the other sobriquets and representations with which we address Him. All these representations were revealed by the *neviim* (prophets). *NeVIim* is the aspect of the spoken word of prayer, as in ([[Isaiah 57#^57-19|Isaiah 57:19]]), “a *NIV* (expression) of the lips.” Thus, when we express the portrayals and representations with our lips and we clap hands, we fulfill “by the hands of the prophets I am depicted” ([[Hosea 12#^12-11|Hosea 12:11]]). For the prophets are the words, and the hand-clapping is “the hands of the prophets.” Then the *DiMyon* (representation) is revealed, in the aspect of “by the hands of the prophets *aDaMeh* (I am depicted).” And with this, [the verse,] “he peers at God’s portrait,” is fulfilled ([[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-8|Numbers 12:8]]).

## 2

In addition, clapping is the aspect of “human hands under their wings” ([[Ezekiel 10#^10-8|Ezekiel 10:8]]). This is because wings are words, in the aspect of “a winged creature will speak the word” ([[Ecclesiastes 10#^10-20|Ecclesiastes 10:20]]).

## 3

In addition, [through clapping] prayer is encompassed within the Written Law and the Oral Law, which are two hands: the hand that writes and the hand that seals (Tikkuney Zohar, p.32b).

## 4

The power of speech comes from attachment to the tzaddikim, as is brought (Tikkuney Zohar #18, p.32b): It is called “mouth” by virtue of the tzaddik.
