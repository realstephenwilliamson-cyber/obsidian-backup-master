## Dividing syllables (p. 25)

Where does a single consonant between two vowels go when a word is split into syllables?::It counts as standing at the beginning of a syllable: भरति bharati is bha-ra-ti.

Where do two or more adjacent consonants go?::All but the last consonant are counted with the syllable before (in the name शकुन्तला Śakuntalā, with the second syllable: Śa-kun-ta-lā).

## Heavy and light syllables (pp. 25–26)

When does a syllable count as heavy?::When it contains a long or complex vowel (ā ī ū ṝ e ai o au), when its vowel is followed by more than one consonant (and thus it ends in a consonant itself), or when it contains a vowel followed by anusvāra or visarga.

When does a syllable count as light?::When it contains a short vowel (a i u ṛ ḷ) that is followed by only one consonant (which would belong to the following syllable).

कथा kathā ‘story’: syllables and weight?::ka-thā: the first is light; the second is heavy, as it contains a long vowel (थ is one sound, though written th).

स्वयंवरः svayaṃvaraḥ ‘self-choice’: which syllable does Ruppel mark as heavy, and why?::sva-yaṃ-va-raḥ: the third-last syllable yaṃ is heavy because it contains a vowel that is followed by an anusvāra.

## Which syllable is stressed (pp. 26–27)

Which syllable is stressed (the system usually employed in the West)?::If the second-last syllable is heavy, it is stressed; if it is light, the stress goes back to the third-last syllable. If a word has fewer than three syllables, the accent goes as far back as it can.

भरति bharati ‘he carries’: syllables, weight and stress?::bha-ra-ti, all light. The second-last, ra, is light, so the stress goes to the third-last: **bha**rati (light, but the accent cannot go further back).

भरामि bharāmi ‘I carry’: syllables, weight and stress?::bha-rā-mi: bha and mi light, rā heavy (long ā). The second-last, rā, is heavy, so it is stressed: bha**rā**mi.

शकुन्तला Śakuntalā: syllables, weight and stress?::Śa-kun-ta-lā: kun is heavy (it ends in a consonant), lā is heavy (long vowel). The second-last, ta, is light, so the accent goes back to kun, which is heavy and keeps it: Śa**kun**talā.

What is the alternative method of word stress?::Not one stress per word, but more weight to each heavy syllable, however many there are: in Mahābhārata the second and third syllables, in Śakuntalā the second and last.
