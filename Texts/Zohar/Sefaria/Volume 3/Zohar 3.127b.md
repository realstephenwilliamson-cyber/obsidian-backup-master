# Zohar 3.127b

← [[Zohar 3.127a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 3.145a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 8.08 - Parashat Naso#^z3-127b|Parashat Naso, at 3:127b]]

*Volume 3, folio 127b* · Nasso, Idra Rabba

*Passage Nasso 12:131 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 3.127a#^nasso-12-131|Nasso 12:131]].*

**Nasso 12:132** Now in regard to the Levites it says: “And thus shalt thou do unto them to *cleanse* them: sprinkle the water of purification upon them, and let them cause a razor to pass over all their flesh” (Num. 8, 7). After the hair has been removed and all the details performed, the Levite is designated “pure”, but not “holy”. But the Nazirite, having abstained from the side of rigour, is designated “holy” and not simply “pure”. So Scripture says: *All the days of his vow of Naziriteship…in which he consecrateth himself unto the Lord, he shall be holy, he shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow long*. ^nasso-12-132

**Nasso 12:133** This is explained by the passage, “and the hair of his head [was] like pure wool”[^1] (Dan. 7, 9), inasmuch as the Nazirite in this regard resembles the celestial pattern.’ R. Judah said: ‘It is indeed by his hair that the Nazirite is distinguished as holy. This is in allusion to “his locks are curled” (S.S. 5, 11).’ ^nasso-12-133

**Nasso 12:134** A teaching of R. Simeon says: ‘Did men but understand the inner significance of the Scriptural passages regarding the hair, they would acquire a knowledge of their Master by means of the Superior Wisdom. ‘[^2] ^nasso-12-134

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Idra Rabba 1:1–1:4.*

[^1]: i.e. white, the symbol of mercy.
[^2]: Here follows in the text the *Idra Rabba*; v. Appendix.
