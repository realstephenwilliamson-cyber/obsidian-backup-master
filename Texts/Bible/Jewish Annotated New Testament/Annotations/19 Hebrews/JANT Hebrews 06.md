# Hebrews 6 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Hebrews 05|← Hebrews 5]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Hebrews 07|Hebrews 7 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Hebrews 06|NRSVue]] · [[Hebrews 06|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Hebrews - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-1|6.1]]:** *Perfection*, Gk “teleiotes,” lit., “maturity” (see translators’ note *c*), appears only here and in [[Colossians 03#^3-14|Col 3.14]] among NT texts. The *basic teaching* has not been discussed. The items listed collectively in vv 1–2 show that nothing is as important as understanding the significance of Jesus. *Dead works,* activities that do not serve God. *Faith toward God*, Gk “pisteos epi theon,” a unique and unclear expression in the NT. Counted among the “basic teachings,” however, *faith* here must not carry the same theological weight that it does for Paul (see [[Romans 03#^3-21|Rom 3.21–30]]). Faith in Hebrews can mean obedience or fidelity (3.4; 10.39), but frequently its meaning is akin to “hope” (11.1; 12.2). ^6-1

> [!note] Melchizedek *(p. 471)*
> Although Melchizedek is mentioned twice and only briefly in the Tanakh ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^14-18|Gen 14.18–20]] and [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]]), he received substantial attention from later commentators who recognized his special status as a priest-king (Philo, *Migr.* 235; *Leg. all.* 3.79–82; Josephus, *Ant.* 1.177–82). Among the Dead Sea Scrolls, an entire work is devoted to him (11QMelch); there he becomes a superhuman figure who executes divine judgment. The text does not describe him as a priest, but he is described as having priestly functions (11QMelch 2.6). In rab- binic tradition, Melchizedek bestows the priesthood on Abraham, who then becomes a “priest forever” (*b. Ned.* 32b; *Lev. Rab.* 25.6). Some rabbinic texts identify Melchizedek as descended from Noah’s son Shem, which may be a polemic against Hebrews’s claim that Melchizedek lacks human genealogy (*b. Ned.* 32b; *Num. Rab.* 4.8; *Pirqe R. El.* 27.3). *2 En.* 71–72 describes Melchizedek’s miraculous birth to Nir, the wife of Noah’s brother. Melchizedek is there chosen to be saved from the flood so that he might continue the priestly lineage inaugurated by Seth (see 71.29,32).

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-2|2]]:** *Baptisms*, “washings,” the plural indicates this is most likely not the Christian practice of initiate baptism, but exactly what the author has in mind is not clear. See “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674. *Laying on of hands* occurs during healings and the conferral of special status; it can both convey the gift of the spirit ([[Acts 08#^8-17|Acts 8.17]]; [[Acts 19#^19-6|19.6]]) and initiate a commission ([[Acts 06#^6-6|Acts 6.6]]; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-14|1 Tim 4.14]]). *Resurrection of the dead*, a belief held by both Pharisees and Christians; see *m. Sot.* 9.15; *m. Sanh.* 10.1. *Eternal judgment*, an idea common in Jewish and Christians sources; see [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.33–44; [[Matthew 25#^25-31|Mt 25.31–46]]. The Mishnah speaks of the unrighteous going to Gehenna, but never describes the punishment as eternal (*m. Ed.* 2.10; *m. Avot* 1.5; 5.19,20). ^6-2

### 6.4–8: Warning against apostasy

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-4|Text]], p. 472* ^6-4

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-4|4–6]]:** A baptized member who has *fallen away* has no opportunity for repentance; apostasy results in expulsion. The claim resembles the “karet” (“cut off”) punishment, which some interpret to mean that those who commit certain violations of the Torah, such as idolatry and incest, are excluded from the community (e.g., [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-15|Ex 12.15]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-19|19]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-29|Lev 18.29]]). For the Qumran community, violations of the “Community Rule” sometimes resulted in expulsion (1QS 7.23–24; 8.21–24). Jesus’ followers held different views on how to treat sin after baptism (cf. [[Mark 03#^3-29|Mk 3.29]]; Herm., *Vis.* 5.7). While some later interpreters such as Tertullian (*Pud.* 20) and Novatian (see Eusebius, *Hist*. *Eccl.* 6.43.1), took *impossible to restore again* to indicate that apostates cannot return to the community (see also 10.26–31), others such as Augustine and Aquinas argued that apostates should be permitted to return and that Hebrews prohibits only a second baptism. *M. Avot* 5.18 states, “he who causes the community to sin—they never give him a sufficient chance to attain penitence,” although Jewish tradition generally grants that even the most wicked can atone and be forgiven. ^6-4-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-5|5]]:** *Powers of the age to come*, see 2.8n. ^6-5

### 6.9–20: Divine fidelity

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-9|Text]], p. 472* ^6-9

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-10|10]]:** *Love* for God is a significant idea in the Tanakh, especially in Deuteronomy (e.g., [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-5|Deut 6.5]]). *Saints,* lit., “holy ones,” referring to Jesus’ followers generally in [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-2|1 Cor 1.2]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|2 Cor 1.1]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]. To whom it refers here is unclear. ^6-10

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-12|12]]:** *Imitators of those who . . . inherit the promises*, see ch 11. ^6-12

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-13|13–20]]:** Having introduced the idea that God can alter seemingly unchangeable institutions, Hebrews’s appeal to God’s promise to Abraham and swearing an oath in [[Psalms 110#^110-4|Ps 110.4]] to establish this new priesthood reassures the audience lest they imagine God as unreliable and capricious. ^6-13

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-13|13–14]]:** *Promise to Abraham* included progeny, land, and an eternal covenant; see [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-2|Gen 12.2–3]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|15.5]]; [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^17-5|17.5]]. *Swore by himself*, one usually swears by a deity or authority who can hold the oath-maker accountable, but God cannot appeal to any higher power. ^6-13-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Patiently endured*, Abraham waited until he was one hundred years old for the fulfillment of the *promise* of his son Isaac. ^6-15

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-17|17]]:** *Heirs of the promise*, the descendants of Abraham, extended to mean Jesus’ followers. *Guaranteed it by an oath*, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-17|Gen 22.17]]; see Philo, *Migr.* 273, for a similar argument. ^6-17

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-18|18]]:** *Two unchangeable things*, God’s promise and oath. *Impossible . . . false*, see [[Torah 40 - Balak#^23-19|Num 23.19–20]]. ^6-18

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-19|19]]:** *Anchor of the soul*, in Greek and Roman literature, anchors are symbols of security (e.g., Virgil, *Aeneid* 6.3–5; Pindar, *Olympian Odes* 6.100). *Inner shrine*, the Holy of Holies, which only the high priest may enter on Yom Kippur; see 9.3; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-2|Lev 16.2]]. C*urtain*, see [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^26-31|Ex 26.31–35]]; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-12|Lev 16.12]],[[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-15|15]]; see also [[Mark 15#^15-38|Mk 15.38]], which claims the curtain tore at Jesus’ death. ^6-19

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 06#^6-20|20]]:** *Melchizedek*, see 5.6n. ^6-20
