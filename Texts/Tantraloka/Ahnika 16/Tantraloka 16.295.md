## Mūla

तत्राधिकारितालब्ध्यै दीक्षां गृह्णीत दैशिकात् ।
तेन मन्त्रज्ञानयोगबलाद्यद्यत्प्रसाधयेत् ॥ ^mula

tatrādhikāritālabdhyai dīkṣāṃ gṛhṇīta daiśikāt |
tena mantrajñānayogabalādyadyatprasādhayet || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 07 - Chapter Sixteen, part 3#^pg151|p. 131]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=151>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 16.294cd–295ab.
>
> It is also stated with these (words) that, in order to achieve oneness with (ultimate) reality (tattvaika), the yogi must dedicate himself (āśrayet) to the root Mantra alone, and to be intent on this (until he attains) the final culmination (of its practice, which is a state of reflective awareness) free of (dichotomizing) thought constructs. He who, wishing to attain (any specific result), be it worldly benefit (bhoga) or even liberation, by the practice of Mantra, must first take initiation from a teacher in order to obtain the authority there (to access and practice Mantra). (with 16.293cd–295ab)
>
> Thus, even though the disciple may have been applied (yukta) by the teacher to (some) other principle (tattva), he can (nevertheless) accomplish every single thing (tat tat) there may be (in any of the principles) by the might (bala) of Mantra, knowledge and Yoga. (with 16.295cd–296ab)
