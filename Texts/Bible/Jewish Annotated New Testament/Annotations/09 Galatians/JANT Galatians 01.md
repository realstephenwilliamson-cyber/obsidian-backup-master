# Galatians 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Galatians 02|Galatians 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Galatians 01|NRSVue]] · [[Galatians 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Galatians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-1|1.1–5]]:** **Salutation.** Paul’s salutations typically combine autobiography with theology. Paul perceives himself as sent by God (1.15; [[Acts 09#^9-3|Acts 9.3–6]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-8|1 Cor 15.8]]). *Sent . . . through Jesus Christ*, [[Acts 09#^9-1|Acts 9.1–2]] claims that Paul was sent by the high priest of Jerusalem to Damascus, but Paul himself makes no such claim. Rabbinic literature mentions emissaries (Heb “sheluḥim”) sent to the hinterlands of Judea or the Diaspora (*m. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 1.3) in order to announce the intercalation of the calendar. ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Jesus Christ and God the Father*, Paul regularly refers to God as “father,” “our father,” or “father of the Lord” (e.g., 1.3; [[Romans 15#^15-6|Rom 15.6]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-3|2 Cor 1.3]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-11|Phil 2.11]]). Jesus is “Lord” or “our Lord” (e.g., [[Romans 01#^1-4|Rom 1.4]]; [[Romans 04#^4-24|4.24]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-57|1 Cor 15.57]]). *Who raised him from the dead*, Jesus’ crucifixion and resurrection are for Paul the only important facts of Jesus’ biography. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Members of God’s family*, lit., “brothers,” a common early Christian address. In NT Gk “synagôgê,” usually translated “synagogue,” commonly designates a Jewish place of worship or the congregation meeting there, while “ekklēsia,” usually translated “church” but better translated “assembly,” commonly designates a local place of worship or congregation of worshippers of those who believe in Christ. (Gk “ekklēsia” is the usual LXX translation of Heb “qahal.”) Believers in Jesus usually called their assemblies “ekklēsiai” to distinguish them from “synagôgai” (but see [[James 02#^2-2|Jas 2.2]] \[Gk “synagoge”; NRSV “assemblies”\]). Usually the reference is to a local assembly of believers, but some passages (like 1.13) use the term to refer to the whole body of believers wherever they may be (as we say in English, “the Church”). ^1-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Grace*, a Gvk salutation, *and peace,* reflecting the Heb salutation “shalom” ([[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-3|1 Cor 1.3]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-2|2 Cor 1.2]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]). ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Who gave himself for our sins*, perhaps alluding to [[Isaiah 53#^53-5|Isa 53.5–6]]. *Set us free*, remove us *from the present evil age*, controlled by evil or demonic forces (4.3) but soon to be either destroyed or radically transformed. Christ’s death rescues humanity from these forces. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-5|5]]:** This is the only Pauline letter whose salutation ends with a doxology, the attribution of *glory* (Gk “doxa”) to God under the influence of such verses as [[Ezekiel 03#^3-12|Ezek 3.12]]; [[Psalms 029#^29-1|Ps 29.1–2]], and especially [[Psalms 104#^104-31|Ps 104.31]]. *Glory* (Heb “kavod”), is a common attribute or manifestation of God in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-10|Ex 16.10]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-16|24.16]]; [[Psalms 024#^24-8|Ps 24.8]],[[Psalms 024#^24-10|10]]). ^1-5

### 1.6–9: Paul rebukes the Galatians

*[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-6|Text]], p. 374* · Unlike other Pauline epistles, Galatians lacks a thanksgiving section (e.g., [[Romans 01#^1-8|Rom 1.8]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-4|1 Cor 1.4]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-2|1 Thess 1.2]]). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-6|6]]:** *The one who called you*, in Paul’s letters, the subject of “to call” (Gk “kalein”) is always God. See 1.15; 5.8. Some manuscripts omit *of Christ*, confirming that the one who has called is God. *Different gospel*, see [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-4|2 Cor 11.4]]. *Different* or “other,” meaning “wrong, illegitimate,” appears in rabbinic literature: a wrong view is “another way” (Heb “derekh aḥeret,” *t. Ber.* 6.6); idolaters are sometimes called “other ones, different ones” (Heb “aḥerim,” *Mek. Neziqin* 12); the arch-heretic Elisha ben Abuyah was known as “the other one” (“aḥer,” *b. Hag.* 14b). *Gospel* (Gk “euangelion”), lit., “good tidings,” translation of Heb “besorah” (e.g., [[II Samuel 18#^18-25|2 Sam 18.25]]). The Heb root “b-s-r” is used in Deutero-Isaiah’s prophecies of redemption (40.9; 52.7; 60.6; 61.1). ^1-6-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Angel* (Gk “angelos”), lit., “messenger” (see “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682). Since this verse says the messenger is *from heaven*, the translation “angel” is indicated. Perhaps Paul’s opponents claimed that their gospel was revealed to them through an angel; see 3.19. *Let that one be accursed*, lit., “let him be ‘anathema,’” the Gk translation of biblical Heb “ḥerem” (7.26; Josh 17). ^1-8

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-9|9]]:** *What you received*, via teaching and tradition; see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-1|1 Cor 15.1–3]]. The rabbinic equivalent is “qibbel,” “to receive” (*m. Avot* 1.1). 30° 35° 40° Places mentioned in Galatians 1–2. ^1-9

### 1.10–12: Paul’s independence

*[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-10|Text]], p. 375* ^1-10

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Servant of Christ*, a common Pauline self-designation ([[Romans 01#^1-1|Rom 1.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-22|1 Cor 7.22]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]). ^1-10-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-12|12]]:** *A revelation of Jesus Christ*, claiming his “gospel” derives directly from God (see [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|1 Cor 11.23]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-3|15.3]]), Paul stresses his independence and authority. ^1-12

### 1.13–15: Paul’s life in Judaism

*[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-13|Text]], p. 375* ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-13|13–14]]:** *Judaism,* appears in the New Testament only here, “Judaize” “(live . . . like a Jew”) and “Judaicly” (“compel . . .  to live like Jews”) only in 2.14. (The adjective “Judaic” (“Jewish”) appears in [[Titus 01#^1-14|Titus 1.14]].) The Greek term connotes the distinctive ways and manners, customs and 40° 35° 0 100 200 Kilometers beliefs of the Judean people, see [[Philippians 03#^3-5|Phil 3.5]] (see “Paul and Judaism,” p. 633, and “Judaism and Jewishness,” p. 592). Paul’s *earlier life* was *in Judaism*, among Jews, but his current life, after he received his revelation from God (1.15–16), is among Gentiles. *Violently persecuting*, see Acts 8–9; 22.4–5. *Church of God*, see 1.2n. ^1-13-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Zealous*, the language of “zeal” had currency in first-century Jewish circles; the revolutionaries whom Josephus calls “Zealots” (e.g., *J.W.* 7.268) probably depicted themselves as zealots for God and Torah, inspired by such verses as [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^25-11|Num 25.11]] and [[I Kings 19#^19-10|1 Kings 19.10]],[[I Kings 19#^19-14|14]]. Here the word means “a devotee of.” *Traditions of my ancestors* (lit., “of my fathers”), a quintessentially Pharisaic concept (see [[Philippians 03#^3-5|Phil 3.5]]). Josephus writes: “the Pharisees handed down to the people certain observances by succession from their fathers, which are not written in the laws of Moses” (*Ant.* 13.297). See [[Mark 07#^7-3|Mk 7.3–4]]. Rabbinic literature also refers to the sages of old as “fathers” (*m. Ed.* 1.4), but does not use the phrase “traditions of the fathers.” For an ancient tradition the Mishnah uses the term “devar soferim,” “a law (lit., “word”) of the scribes” (*m. Yebam.* 2.4; *m. Sanh.* 11.3). The force of *my* is not clear. ^1-14

### 1.15–17: Paul’s call

*[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-15|Text]], p. 376* ^1-15

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Before I was born*, lit., “from the womb of my mother,” see [[Isaiah 49#^49-1|Isa 49.1]]; [[Jeremiah 01#^1-5|Jer 1.5]]. Paul understands himself to be a prophet ([[1 Corinthians 14#^14-37|1 Cor 14.37]]). ^1-15-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Son*, Paul regularly refers to Jesus as “Son of God” (e.g., 4.4; [[Romans 08#^8-3|Rom 8.3]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-19|2 Cor 1.19]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-10|1 Thess 1.10]]), even if he was also “descended from David according to the flesh” ([[Romans 01#^1-3|Rom 1.3]]). *To me*, lit., “in me” parallel to “in the Gentiles.” *Gentiles* (Gk “ethnē”), lit., “nations” (Heb “goyyim”). Following Tanakh usage (e.g., [[Torah 34 - Bamidbar#^2-9|Num 2.9]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-10|Isa 52.10]]; Ps 79), Paul divides humanity between Jews (or “Israel”) and Gentiles/the “nations” (e.g., 2.2) or “Greeks” (e.g., 2.3). *Any human being*, lit., “flesh and blood,” a frequent rabbinic expression for humans (e.g., *b. Ber.* 33a). Paul denies that his teaching has a human source; apparently his opponents accused him of lacking independent knowledge of God or Christ. See 1.20n. ^1-16

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Go up to Jerusalem*, one ascends to Jerusalem (1.18; 2.1), then the church’s headquarters, see 2.1. *Apostles before me*, who knew Jesus. Paul argues that his God-given authority is equal to theirs. *Arabia*, the Transjordan and the Negev. *Returned to Damascus*, [[Acts 09#^9-8|Acts 9.8–25]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-32|2 Cor 11.32–33]]. ^1-17

### 1.18–24: Paul’s independence

*[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-18|Text]], p. 376* ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-18|18]]:** *Three years*, whether beginning with Paul’s revelation (1.15–16) or return to Damascus (1.17). *Visit Cephas*, not to study with him. *Cephas* (Gk transliteration of the Aram for “rock”), Paul’s usual name for Peter. Paul too had a Semitic and a Greek name ([[Acts 13#^13-9|Acts 13.9]]). *Fifteen days*, a brief period. [[Acts 09#^9-26|Acts 9.26–30]] has a different version of this visit. ^1-18-2

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-19|19]]:** *James* (Gk “Iakobos,” lit., “Jacob”), leader of the Jerusalem church (2.9; [[Acts 15#^15-13|Acts 15.13]]; [[Acts 21#^21-18|21.18]]). Josephus describes his death (*Ant.* 20.200). ^1-19

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-20|20]]:** Similar affirmations of veracity are in [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-23|2 Cor 1.23]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-31|11.31]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-5|1 Thess 2.5]]. Paul’s opponents apparently argued that he was subordinate to the Jerusalem apostles. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-21|21]]:** *Syria and Cilicia*, far from Jerusalem. Tarsus, Paul’s hometown ([[Acts 09#^9-11|Acts 9.11]]), is in Cilicia. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue Galatians 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Churches*, see 1.2n. ^1-22
