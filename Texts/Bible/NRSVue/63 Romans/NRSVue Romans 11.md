# Romans 11 (NRSVue)

Compare: [[Romans 11|BSB and SBLGNT]] · [[DRB Romans 11|Douay-Rheims]] · [[JANT Romans 11|JANT notes]]

## Israel’s rejection is not final

**11:1** I ask, then, has God rejected his people? By no means! I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, a member of the tribe of Benjamin. ^11-1

**11:2** God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew. Do you not know what the scripture says of Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel? ^11-2

**11:3** “Lord, they have killed your prophets, they have demolished your altars; I alone am left, and they are seeking my life.” ^11-3

**11:4** But what is the divine reply to him? “I have kept for myself seven thousand who have not bowed the knee to Baal.” ^11-4

**11:5** So, too, at the present time there is a remnant chosen by grace. ^11-5

**11:6** But if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works, otherwise grace would no longer be grace.^[Other ancient authorities add *But if it is by works, it is no longer on the basis of grace, otherwise work would no longer be work*] ^11-6

**11:7** What then? Israel has not achieved what it was pursuing. The elect have achieved it, but the rest were hardened, ^11-7

**11:8** as it is written, “God gave them a sluggish spirit, eyes that would not see and ears that would not hear, down to this very day.” ^11-8

**11:9** And David says, “Let their table become a snare and a trap, a stumbling block and a retribution for them; ^11-9

**11:10** let their eyes be darkened so that they cannot see, and keep their backs forever bent.” ^11-10

## The salvation of the gentiles

**11:11** So I ask, have they stumbled so as to fall? By no means! But through their stumbling^[Or *transgression*] salvation has come to the gentiles, so as to make Israel^[Gk *them*] jealous. ^11-11

**11:12** Now if their stumbling^[Or *transgression*] means riches for the world and if their loss means riches for gentiles, how much more will their full inclusion mean! ^11-12

**11:13** Now I am speaking to you gentiles. Inasmuch as I am an apostle to the gentiles, I celebrate my ministry ^11-13

**11:14** in order to make my own people^[Gk *my flesh*] jealous and thus save some of them. ^11-14

**11:15** For if their rejection is the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance be but life from the dead? ^11-15

**11:16** If the part of the dough offered as first fruits is holy, then the whole batch is holy; and if the root is holy, then the branches also are holy. ^11-16

**11:17** But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, a wild olive shoot, were grafted among the others to share the rich root^[Other ancient authorities read *the root and the richness*] of the olive tree, ^11-17

**11:18** do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember: you do not support the root, but the root supports you. ^11-18

**11:19** You will say, “Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in.” ^11-19

**11:20** That is true. They were broken off on account of unbelief,^[Or *faithlessness* or *lack of trust*] but you stand on account of belief.^[Or *faithfulness* or *trust*] So do not become arrogant, but be afraid. ^11-20

**11:21** For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.^[Other ancient authorities read *perhaps he will not spare you*] ^11-21

**11:22** Note then the kindness and the severity of God: severity toward those who have fallen but God’s kindness toward you, if you continue in his kindness; otherwise you also will be cut off. ^11-22

**11:23** And even those of Israel,^[Gk lacks *of Israel*] if they do not continue in unbelief,^[Or *faithlessness* or *lack of trust*] will be grafted in, for God has the power to graft them in again. ^11-23

**11:24** For if you have been cut from what is by nature a wild olive tree and grafted, contrary to nature, into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these natural branches be grafted back into their own olive tree. ^11-24

## All Israel will be saved

**11:25** I want you to understand this mystery, brothers and sisters, so that you may not claim to be wiser than you are: a hardening has come upon part of Israel until the full number of the gentiles has come in. ^11-25

**11:26** And in this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written, “Out of Zion will come the Deliverer; he will banish ungodliness from Jacob.” ^11-26

**11:27** “And this is my covenant with them, when I take away their sins.” ^11-27

**11:28** As regards the gospel they are enemies for your sake, but as regards election they are beloved for the sake of their ancestors, ^11-28

**11:29** for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable. ^11-29

**11:30** Just as you were once disobedient to God but have now received mercy because of their disobedience, ^11-30

**11:31** so also they have now been disobedient in order that, by the mercy shown to you, they also may now^[Other ancient authorities lack *now*] receive mercy. ^11-31

**11:32** For God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all. ^11-32

**11:33** O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways! ^11-33

**11:34** “For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor?” ^11-34

**11:35** “Or who has given a gift to him, to receive a gift in return?” ^11-35

**11:36** For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be the glory forever. Amen. ^11-36
