## About

Theodor W. Adorno's (1903–1969) book of 153 aphorisms and short essays, written in exile in America. It reads everyday matters, from toys and the family to conversation, semicolons and tact, as marks of capitalism, fascism and mass culture on the individual.

- **Title:** *Minima Moralia: Reflexionen aus dem beschädigten Leben* (*Reflections from Damaged Life*). It plays on the *Magna Moralia*, an ethics traditionally attributed to Aristotle.
- **Writing:** begun in 1944 and finished in 1949. Wikipedia says it was first written for the fiftieth birthday of Max Horkheimer, Adorno's co-author on *Dialectic of Enlightenment*. The dedication, punning on Nietzsche's *Gay Science*, calls its subject the sorrowful science, the teaching of the good life.
- **1951:** first published in German; the SEP gives Suhrkamp, Frankfurt am Main. It is volume 4 of Adorno's *Gesammelte Schriften*. The SEP dates Adorno's return to Frankfurt to 1949.
- **Structure:** three parts, of 50, 50 and 53 aphorisms (marxists.org). The epigraph is from Ferdinand Kürnberger.
- **English:** translated by E. F. N. Jephcott and published by New Left Books (NLB), London, in 1974, as the Penn Libraries catalogue and Wikipedia's Adorno bibliography record. Wikipedia's article cites a Verso edition of 1978, the SEP one of 2005.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says it shaped the development of critical theory and influenced work in aesthetics, moral philosophy, cultural studies and sociology. The SEP singles out its modernist, aphoristic style and its ethical negativism, quoting the line "there is no right living in the false."
