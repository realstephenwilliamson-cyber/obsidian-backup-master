### 13.1–14: Subordination to higher authorities enjoined

*[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 314* · The traditional view of Rom 13 is that it exhorts Christ-followers to obey the state. Although the “rulers” (Gk “archontes”) in 13.3 could be imperial authorities, more likely they are synagogue rulers ( [[Matthew 23#^23-2|Mt 23.2–3]]; [[Luke 08#^8-41|Lk 8.41]]; [[Luke 12#^12-11|12.11]]; [[Luke 14#^14-1|14.1]]; [[Luke 18#^18-18|18.18]]; [[Luke 23#^23-13|23.13]],[[Luke 23#^23-35|35]]; [[Luke 24#^24-20|24.20]]; [[Acts 07#^7-27|Acts 7.27]],[[Acts 07#^7-35|35]]; [[Acts 14#^14-5|14.5]]; *CIJ* 1.347). Paul’s principal concern is how non-Jews should behave among Jews who did not share, or who resisted, their convictions (as “enemies”; see 11.28n.). Gentile readers might well have expressed resistance to synagogue authorities and membership obligations (such as the Temple tax). Hence Paul’s continued challenge to resentment, most explicit in ch 11, now in the more practical matters of institutional behavior. Gentiles are to live respectfully toward Jews and so to accept their communal leaders’ authority. This includes paying the Temple tax for those claiming full membership in Jewish communities, as these Gentile Christ-followers do. See Josephus, *Ant.* 14.110; 16.160–73; Cicero, *Flac.* , 28.66–69; Tacitus, *Hist.* 5.5.1*.* See also 13.2n. ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Governing authorities*, or “protective/higher powers”; i.e., synagogue leaders; no article in Gk; *authority* (or “power”); . . . *authorities*. It is difficult to understand how Paul would sanction either the Roman empire as *instituted*, or “arranged by God”; the present age is “night,” “darkness” (v 12) and “evil” according to Paul, one from which people need to be freed (cf. [[Galatians 01#^1-4|Gal 1.4]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-3|1 Thess 5.3]]; [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-6|2 Thess 2.6–12]]). ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-2|2]]:** Jewish tradition includes critique of Israel’s leaders (kings, priests, and prophets), and all the more, of the rulers of other nations, who are often used to discipline the unrighteous but who are also guilty of harming the righteous; they will be punished, and are frequently resisted in acts of faithful righteousness (e.g., [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-14|Deut 17.14–20]]; 1 Sam 8; Isa 47; Jer 29; Dan 3; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-3|Wis 6.3–5]]; 2 Macc 7). ^13-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Rulers* (Gk “archontes”) could be synagogue rulers. *Approval*, lit., “praise for behaving righteously.” ^13-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-4|4]]:** *God’s servant*, it is unlikely Paul is saying that the Roman Empire was just. *Sword* (Gk “machaira”) represented judicial authority (“ius gladii”), descriptive of the synagogue leader’s rights, under the authority of the empire, e.g., the responsibility to collect taxes and keep order. Paul says he has been punished by synagogue authorities ([[2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|2 Cor 11.23–26]]; cf. [[Acts 14#^14-5|Acts 14.5]],[[Acts 14#^14-19|19]]; [[Acts 16#^16-22|16.22–23]]), and that he had, previously, persecuted Christ-believers apparently under some kind of Jewish communal police-like rights ( [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13–15]],[[Galatians 01#^1-23|23]]; cf. [[Acts 08#^8-1|Acts 8.1]]; [[Acts 09#^9-1|9.1–2]]; [[Acts 22#^22-4|22.4–5]]), a case which also brings into question any assertion that Paul would claim that justice is always carried out even by Jewish authorities. ^13-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-5|5]]:** *Conscience*, the sense of what is right, anticipating chs 14–15. ^13-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-6|6]]:** *Taxes*, see [[Mark 12#^12-17|Mk 12.17]]. The reference to *authorities* as *God’s servants* (Gk “leitourgoi”) could refer to priests (cf. 15.16; [[Isaiah 61#^61-6|Isa 61.6]]; [[Nehemiah 10#^10-40|Neh 10.39]]; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.24), as opposed to “diakonos” (“servant”) in v 4. “Leitourgos” often signifies a more cult-oriented activity, which would be a more likely view for Paul to hold toward collecting funds for the sacrificial cult activities in Jerusalem than toward the empire’s tax collectors. ^13-6

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-7|7]]:** *Due them*, not in Gk. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-8|8]]:** *For the one who loves another has fulfilled the law*, lit., “for loving the other fulfills Torah”; see [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; [[Matthew 05#^5-43|Mt 5.43]]; [[Matthew 19#^19-19|19.19]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-6|Gal 5.6]],[[Galatians 05#^5-13|13–14]]. ^13-8

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-9|9]]:** *The commandments*, drawn from the Decalogue in [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.13–17]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-17|Deut 5.17–21]]. The order of these short commandments is variable in different ancient sources. *Love your neighbor*, see [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]]; cf. [[Matthew 19#^19-19|Mt 19.19]]; [[Matthew 22#^22-39|22.39]] || [[Mark 12#^12-31|Mk 12.31]]; [[Luke 10#^10-27|Lk 10.27]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-14|Gal 5.14]]; [[James 02#^2-8|Jas 2.8]]. ^13-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-10|10]]:** *Love is the fulfilling of the law*, lit., “love is the fullness of Torah”; no article with “nomos” in Gk. Hillel, [[Shabbat 31#31a|*b. Shabb.* 31a]], similarly stated**:** “What is hateful to you, do not do to your neighbor. This is the whole Torah, the rest is commentary; now go and learn!” Paul plays off of the word translated “work” in his choice of verb: love does not “work” harm to a neighbor. *Fulfilling*, “fullness” (Gk “plêrôma”) can indicate completeness or full measure, which accentuates that Torah (“nomos”) is not a negative category, but one with ideal values, which can be subverted instead of carried out as ideally intended ( 2.12–13; 7.12). ^13-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-11|11–14]]:** The call to live in the full light of the impending day. ^13-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-11|11]]:** *Besides this* (Gk “kai” \[“and”\]) suggests continuity with v 10, i.e., that one can love in the way because one knows that the day of restoration is close. *Salvation*, or “restoration/rescue”; see 1.16; *believers*, or “faithful ones.” Cf. [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-7|2 Cor 6.7]]; [[2 Corinthians 10#^10-4|10.4]]; [[Ephesians 06#^6-11|Eph 6.11]]. ^13-11-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Day is near*, something is in the process of changing but not completed; see [[Joel 1#^1-15|Joel 1.15]] and other contexts that similarly suggest that the Day of the LORD is near. *Darkness . . . light*, see [[Proverbs 04#^4-16|Prov 4.16–19]]; [[Job 29#^29-14|Job 29.14]]; [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-7|2 Cor 6.7]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-22|Eph 4.22–24]]; [[Ephesians 06#^6-13|6.13–17]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-9|Col 3.9–14]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-8|1 Thess 5.8]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 17#^17-1|Wis 17.1–18.4]]; 1QS 2.7; 3.20–4.1; 1QM 15.9. *Works . . . armor* is not a parallel contrast; Paul’s point is to commit to certain behavior, eschewing other behavior. ^13-12

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Live honorably*, lit., “walk properly.” Walk (Gk “peripateo”) corresponds to Heb “halakh.” ^13-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 13#^13-14|14]]:** *The flesh, to gratify its desires*, or “for covetousnesses” \[i.e., intense desires\]; *to gratify* not in Gk. See [[Job 29#^29-14|Job 29.14]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-27|Gal 3.27]]. ^13-14
