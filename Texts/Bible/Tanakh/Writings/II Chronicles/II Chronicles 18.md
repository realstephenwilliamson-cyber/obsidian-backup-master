## Chapter 18

**18:1** [^1]So Jehoshaphat had wealth and honor in abundance, and he allied himself by marriage to Ahab. ^18-1

**18:2** After some years had passed, he came to visit Ahab at Samaria. Ahab slaughtered sheep and oxen in abundance for him and for the people with him, and persuaded him to march against Ramoth-gilead. ^18-2

**18:3** King Ahab of Israel said to King Jehoshaphat of Judah, “Will you accompany me to Ramoth-gilead?” He answered him, “I will do what you do; my troops shall be your troops and shall accompany you in battle.” ^18-3

**18:4** Jehoshaphat then said to the king of Israel, “But first inquire for the word of the LORD.” ^18-4

**18:5** So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, four hundred men, and asked them, “Shall I march upon Ramoth-gilead for battle, or shall I not?” “March,” they said, “and God will deliver it into the king’s hands.” ^18-5

**18:6** Then Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there not another prophet of the LORD here through whom we can inquire?” ^18-6

**18:7** And the king of Israel answered Jehoshaphat, “There is one more man through whom we can inquire of the LORD; but I hate him, because he never prophesies anything good for me but always misfortune. He is Micaiah son of Imlah.” Jehoshaphat replied, “Let the king not say such a thing.” ^18-7

**18:8** So the king of Israel summoned an officer and said, “Bring Micaiah son of Imlah at once.” ^18-8

**18:9** The king of Israel and King Jehoshaphat of Judah, wearing their robes, were seated on their thrones situated in the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria; and all the prophets were prophesying before them. ^18-9

**18:10** Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had provided himself with iron horns; and he said, “Thus said the LORD: With these you shall gore the Arameans till you make an end of them.” ^18-10

**18:11** All the other prophets were prophesying similarly, “March against Ramoth-gilead and be victorious! The LORD will deliver it into Your Majesty’s hands.” ^18-11

**18:12** The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him, “Look, the words of the prophets are unanimously favorable to the king. Let your word be like that of the rest of them; speak a favorable word.” ^18-12

**18:13** “By the life of the LORD,” Micaiah answered, “I will speak only what my God tells me.” ^18-13

**18:14** When he came before the king, the king said to him, “Micah,[^2] shall we march against Ramoth-gilead for battle or shall we not?” He answered him, “March and be victorious! They will be delivered into your hands.” ^18-14

**18:15** The king said to him, “How many times must I adjure you to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?” ^18-15

**18:16** Then he said, “I saw all Israel scattered over the hills like sheep without a shepherd; and the LORD said, ‘These have no master; let everyone return to his home in safety.’” ^18-16

**18:17** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “Did I not tell you that he would not prophesy good fortune for me, but only misfortune?” ^18-17

**18:18** Then [Micaiah] said, “Indeed, hear now the word of the LORD! I saw the LORD seated upon His throne, with all the host of heaven standing in attendance to the right and to the left of Him. ^18-18

**18:19** The LORD asked, ‘Who will entice King Ahab of Israel so that he will march and fall at Ramoth-gilead?’ Then one said this and another said that, ^18-19

**18:20** until a certain spirit came forward and stood before the LORD and said, ‘I will entice him.’ ‘How?’ said the LORD to him. ^18-20

**18:21** And he replied, ‘I will go forth and become a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’ Then He said, ‘You will entice with success. Go forth and do it.’ ^18-21

**18:22** Thus the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours; for the LORD has decreed misfortune for you.” ^18-22

**18:23** Thereupon Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek, and exclaimed, “However did the spirit of the LORD pass from me to speak with you!” ^18-23

**18:24** Micaiah replied, “You will see on the day when you try to hide in the innermost room.” ^18-24

**18:25** Then the king of Israel said, “Take Micaiah and turn him over to Amon, the governor of the city, and to Prince Joash, ^18-25

**18:26** and say, ‘The king’s orders are: Put this fellow in prison, and let his fare be scant bread and scant water until I come home safe.’” ^18-26

**18:27** To which Micaiah retorted, “If you ever come home safe, the LORD has not spoken through me.” He said further, “Listen, all you peoples!”[^3] ^18-27

**18:28** The king of Israel and King Jehoshaphat of Judah marched against Ramoth-gilead. ^18-28

**18:29** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “I will disguise myself and go[^4] into the battle, but you, wear your robes.” So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they went into the battle. ^18-29

**18:30** The king of Aram had given these instructions to his chariot officers: “Do not attack anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel.” ^18-30

**18:31** When the chariot officers saw Jehoshaphat, whom they took for the king of Israel, they wheeled around to attack him, and Jehoshaphat cried out and the LORD helped him, and God diverted them from him. ^18-31

**18:32** And when the chariot officers realized that he was not the king of Israel, they gave up the pursuit. ^18-32

**18:33** Then a man drew his bow at random and hit the king of Israel between the plates of the[^5] armor and he said to his charioteer, “Turn around and get me behind the lines; I am wounded.” ^18-33

**18:34** The battle raged all day long,[^6] and the king remained propped up in the chariot facing Aram until dusk; he died as the sun was setting. ^18-34

[^1]: Cf. 1 Kings 22.
[^2]: A shortened form of Micaiah.
[^3]: Cf. Mic. 1.2.
[^4]: Infinitives used for finite verb; cf. note at 1 Kings 22.30.
[^5]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^6]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
