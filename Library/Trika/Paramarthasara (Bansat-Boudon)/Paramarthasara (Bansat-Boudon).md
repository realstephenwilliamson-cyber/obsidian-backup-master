# The Paramārthasāra of Abhinavagupta and its Commentary by Yogarāja

Lyne Bansat-Boudon, with Kamalesha Datta Tripathi, Routledge (Routledge Studies in Tantric Traditions), 2014.

Converted from the PDF on 2 Oct 2026 by extracting each page's text layer directly (not re-typed, not re-OCR'd) and stripping running page headers/footers. Page breaks are marked `[p. N]` using the book's own printed page numbers, so you can cite the print edition. The PDF itself is [[Abhinavagupta, Yogaraja - An Introduction to Tantric Philosophy (2011).pdf|in Library/Books]].

**What this conversion is and isn't:** the Introduction, Translation, Appendix, Bibliography and both Indexes extract cleanly from the PDF's embedded text layer, with Sanskrit diacritics (ā, ī, ū, ś, ṣ, ṛ, ṁ, etc.) intact and correct — confirmed by spot-checking many pages against the rendered PDF. Footnotes are **not** reformatted as proper Markdown footnotes; they appear inline, after the paragraph that cites them, each starting with its footnote number with no space before the following word (e.g. `227saṃkṣepa —`), exactly as the PDF's own text stream orders them. This is a faithful but unpolished extraction, not a hand-typeset edition — expect occasional stray characters or awkward line breaks where the original PDF's text stream was ambiguous.

**What's excluded:** pp. 356–406, the Sanskrit source-text edition in Devanagari, is omitted. That section was typeset in a legacy pre-Unicode Devanagari font that maps its glyphs into the Unicode Private Use Area; extracting it literally produces unreadable private-use codepoints, not Devanagari text, and there is no reliable way to recover the correct characters from the PDF alone (tested: neither pdftotext nor PyMuPDF extraction, nor fresh Tesseract OCR — including Sanskrit- and Vietnamese-trained models for extended-Latin diacritic recognition — fixes this; it would need either the original word-processor file or a dedicated legacy-font glyph-mapping table, neither of which is available here). The short English essay "On the Sanskrit Text" that introduces that section *is* included (it extracts cleanly) — only the Devanagari verse edition itself is missing. If you want the Sanskrit text of the Paramārthasāra/Yogarāja's commentary, the PDF itself still has it (as an image, effectively) — it's just not reproduced here as searchable text.
