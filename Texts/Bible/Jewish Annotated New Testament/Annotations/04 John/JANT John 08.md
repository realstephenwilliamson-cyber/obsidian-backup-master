# John 8 (JANT notes)

[[JANT John 07|← John 7]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT John 09|John 9 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue John 08|NRSVue]] · [[John 08|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT John - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-1|8:1]]:** *Mount of Olives*, ridge overlooking the Jerusalem Temple; traditionally viewed as an important site in the eschatological period ([[Zechariah 14#^14-4|Zech 14.4]]) and the place from which the Messiah will enter Jerusalem in the end days and the resurrection will begin (e.g., *Tg. of Song* 8.5: “When the dead rise, the Mount of Olives will be cleft and all Israel’s dead will come up out of it”), leading to its popularity as a burial place. It is mentioned in all versions of the triumphal entry, alluding to the event’s messianic significance (see [[Matthew 21#^21-1|Mt 21.1–11]]; [[Mark 11#^11-1|Mk 11.1–11]]; [[Luke 19#^19-28|Lk 19.28–44]]). ^8-1

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-3|3]]:** S*cribes and Pharisees*, not a Johannine phrase (John uses “Pharisees,” but not “scribes”), but frequent in Luke. *Scribes*, only reference in John’s Gospel; the Synoptics closely associate scribes with chief priests (e.g., [[Matthew 02#^2-4|Mt 2.4]]; [[Mark 08#^8-31|Mk 8.31]]; [[Luke 20#^20-19|Lk 20.19]]) and the Pharisees ([[Matthew 23#^23-2|Mt 23.2]]; [[Mark 02#^2-16|Mk 2.16]]; [[Luke 06#^6-7|Lk 6.7]]). ^8-3

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-5|5]]:** [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-10|Lev 20.10]] states that “If a man commits adultery with the wife of his neighbor, both the adulterer and the adulteress shall be put to death.” Roman law did not permit execution for adultery. Jesus’ opponents may have been trying to test his faithfulness to Torah. ^8-5

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-6|6]]:** What Jesus *wrote* is not known, though it may be an allusion to [[Jeremiah 17#^17-13|Jer 17.13]]. *me, as* b Gk *out of his belly* c Other ancient authorities read *for as yet the Spirit* (others, *Holy Spirit*) *had not been given* d Or *the Christ* e Gk *him* ^8-6

### 8.12–29: Confrontation with the Jews

*[[NRSVue John 08#^8-12|Text]], p. 194* ^8-12

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-12|12]]:** *Again*, apparently following 7.52. *Light of the world,* see 1.5,8,9. ^8-12-2

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-13|13]]:** *Testimony is not valid*, see 5.31–47n. ^8-13

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-15|15]]:** *Human standards*, i.e., by appearances, not as God (and Jesus, associated with God) would judge. ^8-15

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-17|17]]:** *Your law*, Jesus dissociates himself from Torah (see 10.34). This, along with the near absence of the association of the term “Jew” (Gk “Ioudaios”) with Jesus, is part of the Gospel’s rhetorical strategy to encourage readers to dissociate from “the Jews.” [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-15|Deut 19.15]] requires the testimony of two witnesses for capital cases. ^8-17

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-18|18]]:** The two witnesses are Jesus and God (v 16). *The Father* (God), and Jesus (the human being) are separate; the doctrine of the Trinity had not yet developed at the time the Gospel was written. ^8-18

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-19|19]]:** More misunderstanding; the Jews ask about Jesus’ paternity, whereas Jesus claims God as his father. ^8-19

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-20|20]]:** *Treasury*, receptacles for offerings ([[Mark 12#^12-41|Mk 12.41]]) to which both men and women had access. Jesus later notes that he has taught publicly in the Temple (18.20). ^8-20

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-21|21]]:** *Going away*, i.e., to death and then to eternal life with the Father. ^8-21

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-22|22]]:** *Kill himself* . . . , as in ch 7, the Jews misunderstand: Jesus is not committing suicide but willingly moving towards his own death (see 3.16). ^8-22

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-24|24,28]]:** *I am he*, a divine claim, alluding to the theophany of [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]. ^8-24

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-24|24]]:** Continuing the trial motif; Jesus judges the Jews and condemns them for their refusal to believe. ^8-24-2

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-25|25]]:** *Why do I speak to you at them* b Or *Lord* c The most ancient authorities lack 7.53—8.11; other authorities add the passage here or after 7.36 or after 21.25 or after [[Luke 21#^21-38|Luke 21.38]], with variations of text; some mark the passage as doubtful. d Gk *according to the flesh all?*, translators’ note *c* makes more sense in context. ^8-25

> [!note] Children of the Devil (JN 8.44) *(p. 194)*
> Jesus accuses the Jews of having the devil as their father because of their murderous intentions and their rejection of his claims to be the Son of God. Some scholars attempt to excuse this language on the grounds that it merely reflects the heightened emotions of an inner-Jewish squab-ble. The integral connection of the verse to the Gospel’s major theological claims, however, argues against this softer reading. [[John 08#^8-44|John 8.44]] participates in the Gospel’s rhetoric of binary opposition, according to which non-Christ-confessing Jews are associated with a full range of negative images — darkness, flesh, death and the devil —in contrast to Christ-confessors, who are identified with light, spirit, life, and God. The verse has played a major role in the history of anti- Semitism.

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-28|28]]:** *Lifted up*, i.e., in the crucifixion (12.32); John’s Gospel transforms crucifixion from a shameful death to Jesus’ exaltation and glorification. ^8-28

### 8.30–59: The conflict sharpens

*[[NRSVue John 08#^8-30|Text]], p. 195* · This section initially appears to be addressed to “the Jews who had believed in him” but as the discussion proceeds, John creates the impression that Jesus is speaking to, and about, all non-believing Jews, not only those who had believed in the past. ^8-30

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-32|32]]:** *The truth will make you free*, Heb “ʾemet,” “truth,” implies reliability or steadfastness. For John, *truth* can only be knowledge of and faith in Jesus’ identity as the Messiah and Son of God. Jesus’ statement could be combined with his claim, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (14.6) to mean “The one who knows me knows the truth and is thereby free.” This statement, and the passage as a whole, plays on the contrast between Isaac and Ishmael (see Gal 4): Isaac as the son of the free woman, Sarah, and Ishmael, as the son of the slave woman, Hagar. Faith in Jesus signifies one’s own freedom and therefore identity as the son of Isaac and heir to the Abrahamic covenant. ^8-32

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-33|33–59]]:** The Jews claim their covenantal relationship with the one God and declare that Jesus contravenes monotheism by claiming divine sonship. Jesus denies their covenantal relationship on the grounds that they reject God’s son. ^8-33

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-33|33]]:** The Jews claim they are children of Abraham (see Gen 12; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1]]) and have never been enslaved. This is often viewed as evidence that the Jews lie, given their slavery in Egypt ([[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-3|Ex 13.3]]), but it is plausible that the reference here is to idolatry, sometimes referred to as enslavement to foreign gods ([[Jeremiah 02#^2-10|Jer 2.10–14]]). ^8-33-2

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-34|34–36]]:** The distinction between *slave* and *son* concerns inheritance. As in ch 2, Jesus claims to be the true “son” who inherits the Temple and all that it symbolizes in contrast to the Jews, who serve in the Temple but do not inherit it. ^8-34

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-39|39]]:** *Abraham’s children*, implies offspring sharing their father’s characteristics, not merely *have heard from your father* physical “descendants” as in v 37. Paternity is shown by behavior. ^8-39

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-41|41–47]]:** In trying to kill Jesus, the Jews show that they are not God’s children but the devil’s. ^8-41

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-41|41]]:** *We are not illegitimate*, perhaps an implied contrast to Jesus’ supposed illegitimacy (Origen, *Cels.* 1.28), possible allusion to [[Malachi 2#^2-10|Mal 2.10]]: “Have we not all one father?” ^8-41-2

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-44|44]]:** *Your father the devil*, this verse is the source of the association of the Jews with Satan that remains prevalent in anti-Semitic discourse (see Introduction). ^8-44

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-48|48–52]]:** The Jews express their inability to believe Jesus’ words, especially the claim that belief overcomes death. ^8-48

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-48|48]]:** *Samaritan*, this comment — and the fact that Jesus does not refute it — has been understood by some to suggest that Jesus was a Samaritan, a point that contradicts Jn 4 and with what we know of the historical Jesus. ^8-48-2

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-51|51]]:** *Death*, here less the end of life but the ultimate death that is the opposite of “eternal life.” ^8-51

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-52|52]]:** *Have a demon*, the Jews throw Jesus’ accusation in 8.44 back at him in much the same way that the Synoptics have the Jewish leaders refer to Jesus as an agent of Beelzebul ([[Matthew 12#^12-22|Mt 12.22–32]]; [[Mark 03#^3-22|Mk 3.22–30]], ; [[Luke 11#^11-14|Lk 11.14–23]]). ^8-52

**[[NRSVue John 08#^8-56|56–58]]:** *Abraham . . . see my day*, in *T. Abr*., God gives Abraham a tour of the heavens and provides him with knowledge of the final judgment before his death. *I am*, allusion to God’s revelation to Moses at the burning bush ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]), also perhaps a claim to preexistence (1.1–3). ^8-56
