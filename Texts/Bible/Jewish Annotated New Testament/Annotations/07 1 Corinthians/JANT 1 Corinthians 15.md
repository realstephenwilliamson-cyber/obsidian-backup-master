# 1 Corinthians 15 (JANT notes)

### 15.1–11: The tradition

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-1|Text]], p. 347* · This chapter reviews and clarifies gospel teachings about the Christ and resurrection. ^15-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-1|1]]:** *Good news*, gospel. ^15-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-2|2]]:** *Saved*, from sin and eternal death. ^15-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-3|3–4]]:** *For our sins*, perhaps evoking [[Isaiah 53#^53-5|Isa 53.5]]; see *Tg. Isa* 53.4, “Then he will pray on behalf of our sins and our iniquities will be forgiven for his sake.” *Scriptures*, what passages Paul invokes is unclear: suggestions include LXX [[LXX Jeremiah 23#^23-5|Jer 23.5]]; LXX [[LXX Hosea 06#^6-2|Hos 6.2]] verse 40 c Gk *my brothers* d Or *gospel* (the Tg. adds “In the day of the resurrection of the dead” to this verse); Jon 1.17 (= LXX 2.1); [[Daniel 07#^7-25|Dan 7.25]]. See also [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-14|1 Thess 4.14]]. ^15-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-3|3]]:** *Handed on . . . received*, see 11.2n. *For our sins*, vicarious atonement, expiatory sacrifice, or propitiatory sacrifice (Isa 53; Ps 22; LXX [[LXX Daniel (Greek) 03#^3-40|Dan 3.40]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 06#^6-27|4 Macc 6.27–29]]; [[LXX 4 Maccabees 17#^17-20|17.20–22]]; *T. Benj.* 3.8; *Mek. Pisḥa* 1 on [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-1|Ex 12.1]]). ^15-3-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-4|4]]:** *Was raised*, by God. ^15-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-5|5]]:** *Cephas*, setting up a chain of male authority (see 1.12n.; [[Luke 24#^24-34|Lk 24.34]]), unlike other Gospel accounts of the resurrected Jesus appearing first to women ([[Matthew 28#^28-9|Mt 28.9]]; [[Mark 16#^16-9|Mk 16.9]]). *The twelve* disciples of Jesus. ^15-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-6|6]]:** *Appeared*, the sole witness for this appearance to 500. ^15-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-7|7]]:** *James*, the “brother of the Lord \[Jesus\]” (9.5; [[Mark 06#^6-3|Mk 6.3]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-18|Gal 1.18–19]]). *Apostles*, see 1.1n. ^15-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-8|8–9]]:** *Untimely born*, like a stillbirth, referring to either Paul’s physical appearance or early hostility to the gospel ([[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-6|Phil 3.6]]; see also [[Acts 08#^8-1|Acts 8.1–3]]; [[Acts 09#^9-1|9.1–5]]; [[Acts 22#^22-4|22.4]]; [[Acts 26#^26-9|26.9–11]]). ^15-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-10|10]]:** *Grace*, divine favor, as with “spiritual gifts” (12.1). *I am what I am*, asserting modesty. ^15-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-11|11]]:** *I or they*, other apostles. *Proclaim*, 2.4; see also LXX [[LXX Isaiah 61#^61-1|Isa 61.1]]; [[Joel 2#^2-1|Joel 2.1]]; Jon 1.2; 3.2; [[Zechariah 09#^9-9|Zech 9.9]]; [[Proverbs 01#^1-21|Prov 1.21]]; [[Daniel 03#^3-4|Dan 3.4]]. ^15-11

### 15.12–19: Centrality of the resurrection

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-12|Text]], p. 348* ^15-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-12|12]]:** *No resurrection*, denial of bodily resurrection (vs. immortality of the soul), a misunderstanding of Jesus’ resurrection as a one-time event rather than as inaugurating the general resurrection (15.20), or the belief that those baptized were already resurrected and therefore no longer subject to rules regarding bodily sanctity. Pharisees, but not all Jews of the Second Temple period, expected bodily resurrection (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.163, 18.14–16, 3.374; *Avot de R. Natan* A 5). ^15-12-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-14|14]]:** *Proclamation*, that Christ “was raised on the third day” (15.4). *Faith,* in their own resurrection. ^15-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-17|17]]:** *In your sins*, unreconciled with God (15.3). ^15-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-18|18]]:** *Died in Christ***,** deceased church members (see [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-16|1 Thess 4.16]]). ^15-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-19|19]]:** *For this life*, to receive only this-worldly benefits (see *2 Bar.* 21.13). ^15-19

### 15.20–28: Christ’s victory

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-20|Text]], p. 348* ^15-20

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-20|20]]:** *First fruits*, gathered at the beginning of the grain harvest ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-10|Lev 23.10]]; [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-2|Deut 26.2]]); used metaphorically here for the sign of the anticipated general resurrection. ^15-20-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-21|21]]:** *Human*, Adam, who brought sin and death into the world (see 15.22n.,vv 45–47); Jesus removed them (see 1.7–8; 11.26). On the Messiah as an anti-type to Adam see [[Romans 05#^5-14|Rom 5.14-15]]; *Gen. Rab.* 8.1. ^15-21

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-22|22]]:** *All die*, Adam’s sin introduced mortality (see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-19|Gen 3.19]]), a widespread interpretation ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 02#^2-24|Wis 2.24]]; *1 En.* 69.11; *L. A. E.* 44.1; *Apoc. Mos.* 14.2; *L. A. B.* 13.8; *Gen. Rab.* 8.11; 16.6; *Sifra* 27a). *Made alive*, resurrected ([[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 07#^7-9|2 Macc 7.9]]; *T. Sim.* 6.7; *T. Jud.* 25.1,4; *T. Zeb.* 10.2; *T. Benj.* 10.7; *1 En.* 51.1–2; *Pss. Sol.* 2.31; 3.12; *m. Sanh.* 10.1; [[Sanhedrin 90#90b|*b. Sanh.* 90b]]–91a; Maimonides, *Commentary on the Mishnah, Sanhedrin* 10.1). ^15-22

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-23|23]]:** *At his coming*, when Christ returns (see 1.7–8). ^15-23

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-24|24]]:** *Hands over*, God assumes sovereignty after the Messiah has *destroyed every ruler* (*1 En.* 53.5; 55.4; *Pss. Sol.* 2.30) *and every authority* (or dominion) *and power*, earthly and demonic forces ([[Isaiah 24#^24-21|Isa 24.21–22]]; *1 En.* 54.5). ^15-24

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-25|25]]:** *Reign*, Christ’s rule precedes *the end* (v 24), when God reigns. Rabbinic tradition similarly distinguishes between the era of messianic rule from the age of God’s kingdom, the world to come ([[Berakhot 34#34b|*b. Ber.* 34b]]). *Under his feet*, [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]. ^15-25

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-26|26]]:** *Death*, perhaps personified (see [[Jeremiah 09#^9-20|Jer 9.20]]), God’s enemy (see *Pss. Sol.* 7.4; *L. A. B.* 3.10; 33.3); Paul’s paraphrase of [[Isaiah 25#^25-8|Isa 25.8]] is closer to the Masoretic text, God “will swallow up death forever,” than the LXX “Death . . . swallowed them \[the nations\] up.” *Destroyed*, through resurrection. ^15-26

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-27|27]]:** [[Psalms 008#^8-7|Ps 8.6]]. *In subjection*, to the Messiah (see 3.22). ^15-27

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-28|28]]:** *To him*, the Messiah. *The one*, God. See “Paul and the Trinity,” p. 326. ^15-28

### 15.29–34: Correct understanding of eschatological wisdom

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-29|Text]], p. 349* ^15-29

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-29|29]]:** *On behalf of the dead*, interpreted as posthumous or proxy baptism, perhaps associated with the custom of washing the dead ([[Acts 09#^9-37|Acts 9.37]]; *m. Shabb.* 23.5). The phrase can be translated “for the sake of the dead”; Mormon tradition cites this verse in support of posthumous baptisms (*Doctrine and Covenants* 128.15–16). ^15-29-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-30|30]]:** *Danger*, hardships experienced by apostles. ^15-30

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-32|32]]:** *Merely human hopes*, or “in human terms.” *Wild animals*, Paul’s opponents (see 16.8). *Let us eat*, [[Isaiah 22#^22-13|Isa 22.13]], invoked sarcastically and caricaturing Epicureanism. ^15-32

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-33|33]]:** *Deceived*, by fatalistic philosophies. *Bad company*, quoting the playwright Menander (d. 292 BCE; *Thaïs*, frag. 218) to impugn those denying resurrection. ^15-33

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-34|34]]:** *To your shame*, believers who *sin* should be shamed into repenting by comparing their improper behavior to that of those who *have no knowledge of God* (6.5). ^15-34

### 15.35–58: The resurrected body

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-35|Text]], p. 349* ^15-35

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-35|35]]:** Rabbinic literature speculates about the form of the resurrected body (whether naked or clothed, reconstituted from dust or repaired, like or unlike a mortal body, etc.); Roman-period Jewish epitaphs suggest that some understood resurrection as purely spiritual and incorporeal ([[Sanhedrin 90#90b|*b. Sanh.* 90b]]–91a; *y. Kil.* 12.3; *Gen. Rab.* 14.5; *Eccl. Rab.* 1.4; *BS* 2.130; *CIJ* 788). ^15-35-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-36|36–38]]:** *Seed*, see 3.8–9. *Dies,* for the same resurrection metaphor, see [[John 12#^12-24|Jn 12.24]]; [[Sanhedrin 90#90b|*b. Sanh.* 90b]]. Some Greek and Roman naturalists thought both human and plant seeds (“sperma*”*) contained a fully-formed embryo (e.g., Anaxagoras, F4–6; Aristotle, *GA* 1.18). ^15-36

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-39|39]]:** See [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-11|Gen 1.11–12]]. ^15-39

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-40|40]]:** *Heavenly*, of celestial material. *Earthly*, of terrestrial substance. *Glory* (see 11.7n.), radiance, often depicted as a garment (see 15.53; [[Romans 13#^13-14|Rom 13.14]]; [[2 Corinthians 05#^5-4|2 Cor 5.4]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-21|Phil 3.21]]; *1 En.* 62.15; *Pss. Sol.* 11.7). Some Jewish writings depict astral objects as bodies encased in light; one midrash describes Adam and Eve’s clothing ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-21|Gen 3.21]]) as “garments of light,” based on a pun of “garments of skin (Heb “ ʿor”) and light (“ ʾor”)” (*1 En.* 18.13–16; 21.3–6; Philo, *Plant.* 3 \[12\]; *Gen. Rab.* 20.12). ^15-40

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-41|41]]:** Belief that human essence consisted of the same ethereal, luminous substance as celestial bodies led to the idea that souls become stars (see [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2–3]]; Cicero, *Somn. Scip.* 3). ^15-41

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-42|42]]:** *Sown*, the body in which one is born. *Imperishable*, unsusceptible to disease, death, and decay. ^15-42

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-43|43]]:** *Dishonor*, correlating conception and birth with death or *weakness* (Philo, *Heir.* 52–58). *Power*, both God’s power, namely Christ (1.24; 2.5), and the manifestation of that power by believers (2.4; 12.10). ^15-43

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-44|44]]:** *Physical* (Gk “psychikon”), composed of earthly matter, inferior to bodies composed of ether, or *spiritual* (Gk “pneumatikon”; 2.14; 6.14; *1 En.* 15.3–7 notes the incommensurability of spiritual and physical bodies). ^15-44

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-45|45]]:** [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]]. God infused primordial Adam with earthly breath-matter (Gk “pnoē”), while the *last Adam*, Christ, was infused with heavenly spirit (Gk “pneuma”) when he was resurrected. *Life-giving*, enlivening believers with divine *spirit*. ^15-45

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-46|46]]:** *Physical* . . . *then the spiritual*, the order in which the resurrected body is formed reverses the order of human creation as presented in the two creation stories of Gen 1 and 2 (see 15.47; Philo, *On the Creation* 134; 141; Plato, *Tim.* 29b–31a). ^15-46

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-47|47]]:** Philo, familiar with Platonic concepts, also understands Gen 2 as describing “earthly man” and Gen 1 as describing the “heavenly man” (*Leg. all.* 1.31; see also *Dreams* 1.33). Some rabbinic writings portray the first human as having two natures, one heavenly and one earthly (*Gen. Rab.* 8.11). ^15-47

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-49|49]]:** *Image*, in rabbinic literature, the “image of God” refers either to humanity’s spiritual aspect or physical resemblance to God (*Gen. Rab.* 8.9; *Lev. Rab.* 34.3; *Deut. Rab.* 11.3; *t. Ber.* 4.1). ^15-49

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-50|50]]:** *Flesh and blood*, a common postbiblical expression for the human body ([[LXX Sirach 14#^14-18|Sir 14.18]]; Philo, *Heir.* 12 \[57\]; [[Sanhedrin 91#91a|*b. Sanh.* 91a]]). The resurrected body, including that of Jesus, is compositionally different from the mortal body (see 15.42n.). The Talmud discusses how God refashions a body from decomposed flesh and blood ([[Sanhedrin 90#90b|*b. Sanh.* 90b]]–91a). ^15-50

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-51|51–52]]:** *Mystery*, see 2.1n; [[Romans 16#^16-25|Rom 16.25]]; 1QMyst frag. 1, col. 1; 1QHa 9.21–25. *We will not all die*, the earthly bodies of living believers will be *changed* into spiritual bodies without experiencing death. *Trumpet*, [[Joel 2#^2-1|Joel 2.1]] (Heb “shofar”), referring to the impending “day of the Lord,” and connected to the resurrection see [[2 Corinthians 05#^5-1|2 Cor 5.1–5]]; [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-13|1 Thess 4.13–18]]; *Pss. Sol.* 11.1; 4Q496 col. 4 frag. 11.3–4; [[Rosh Hashanah 16#16b|*b. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 16b]]; *Tg. Ps. -J.* to [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-15|Ex 20.15]]; see 3.13n. ^15-51

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-53|53]]:** *Put on,* see 15.40n; [[Romans 13#^13-12|Rom 13.12]],[[Romans 13#^13-14|14]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-27|Gal 3.27]]. ^15-53

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-54|54]]:** [[Isaiah 25#^25-7|Isa 25.7]] (see 15.26n.). ^15-54

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-55|55]]:** Compare [[Hosea 13#^13-14|Hos 13.14]]. ^15-55

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-56|56]]:** *Sin*, rebellion against God, which caused *death*. *Law*, because it reveals sin along with its punishments. ^15-56

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 15#^15-57|57]]:** *Victory*, God’s vanquishing sin and death with Jesus’ death and resurrection (see 1.24). ^15-57
