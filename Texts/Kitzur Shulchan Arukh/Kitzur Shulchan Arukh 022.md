# Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 022

← [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 021]] · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh]] · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh 023]] →

*Chapter 22* · [[Kitzur Shulchan Arukh]]

**1.** Preface  
Concerning his many prayers, Moses said of himself: 1) וָאֵשֶׁב בָּהָר אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם—"I sat on the mountain for forty days…" ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^9-9|Deut. 9: 9]]), 2) וְאָנֹכִי עָמַדְתִּי בָּהָר — "I stood on the mountain …" (Ibid. 10: 10), 3) וָאֶתְנַפַּל לִפְנֵי ה׳ —"I prostrated myself before Adonoy." Jews, symbolically, also assume these three positions during prayer by: 1) sitting before the *Amidah* (*Shemoneh Esrei*), 2) standing during the *Amidah* and 3) נְפִילַת אַפַּיִם (lit. falling on the face) after the *Amidah*. This is called *tachanun* (Supplication). (Based on *Tur* 131).Originally *nefilas apayim* was performed by entirely prostrating one's body upon the ground while praying and pleading for one's needs. However, with the destruction of the Temple this practice was discouraged. Also, the Talmud ([[Megillah 22#22a|Megillah 22a]]) states that only a righteous person comparable to Joshua, who is assured of a response to a prayer in this manner, may assume this position in prayer.Therefore, the custom has become to perform *nefilas apayim* only perfunctorily by sitting and tilting one's head downward (*Aruch Hashulchan* 131: 4).  
Upon completing the *shemoneh esrei, tachanun* is said with *nefilas apayim* (falling on the face). Idle talk[^1] is prohibited between *shemoneh esrei* and *tachanun*. ^22-1

**2.** During the *shacharis* [morning prayers] when *tefillin* are worn on the left hand, [*tachanun* is said] leaning on the right [hand] out of respect for the tefillin.[^2] But at the *minchah* prayers the leaning is on the left [hand]. ^22-2

**3.** *Nefilas apayim* is preferably done while sitting. But, when necessary it may be done while standing. After *tachanun Ve'ananchnu lo neida* is said. It is preferable that it be said while sitting, afterwards you should stand [and continue] saying: *mah na'aseh* … ^22-3

**4.** *Nefilas apayim* is done only in a place where a *Seifer Torah* is present. Where there is no *Seifer Torah*, even though there are other sacred books there, *nefilas apayim* is not done but, instead, the psalm is said without *nefilas apayim*. When praying in the synagogue vestibule and the door to the synagogue is open, it is considered as if the *Seifer Torah* is there.[^3] ^22-4

**5.** When praying in the house of a mourner or in the house of the deceased, even if there is no mourner present,[^4] *tachanun* is not said during the seven days of mourning, even at *minchah* of the seventh day. It is customary, that even if the mourners return home they do not say *Tachanun*. If a mourner is present in the synagogue [during prayers] the congregation is not drawn after him and they *do* say *tachanun*, whereas, the mourner, does not. ^22-5

**6.** *Tachanun* is omitted in a synagogue where a circumcision will take place, or if the father of the child to be circumcised, or the *sandik* who holds the child at the circumcision; or the *mohel*[^5] is praying there, even if the circumcision will take place at another location. If the *minchah* prayer is said, in the child's home, before the *bris*[^6] feast or during the feast [there,] too, *[tachanun]* is omitted. But, if [the *mincha*] prayer is said after *birkas hamazon*[^7] [*tachanun*] *is* said. However, the father of the child, the one holding the child and the *mohel* do not say [*tachanun*] even after *birkas hamazon* because it is their holiday[^8] ^22-6

**7.** Likewise, [*tachanun*] is omitted in a synagogue where a groom is present, during all seven days of feasting, provided that the groom was a *bochur*[^9] or the bride a *besulah*.[^10] But if a widower married a widow there are only three days,[^11] after which *[tachanun]* is resumed. On the day of the marriage ceremony [*tachanun*] is said at *shacharis*[^12] prayers, but at the *minchah* preceeding the ceremony it is not said.[^13] ^22-7

**8.** Tachanun is not said on the following days: *Rosh Chodesh*, the fifteenth of *Av*,[^14] the fifteenth of *Shevat*,[^15] during *Chanukah*, the two days of greater *Purim*,[^16] the two days of minor *Purim*,[^17] the thirty-third day of the *Omer*,[^18] during the entire month of *Nissan*,[^19] *Tishah Be'Av*,[^20] the days between Yom Kippur and *Sukkos*, from *Rosh Chodesh* Sivan until the day following the day after *Shavuos*,[^21] the day following *Succos*.[^22] On all these days *tachanun* is also not said at *minchah* on the preceeding days. However, on the day before *erev Rosh Hashanah*, and on the day before *erev Yom Kippur, Tachanun* is said at *Minchah*. On the day before Rosh Hashana [*tachanun*] is said at *selichos*, but after that[^23] *[tachanun]* is omitted. ^22-8

**9.** On Mondays and Thursdays, are especially favorable days [for prayer] because during the forty days the second *luchos*[^24] were received, Moses ascended on a Thursday and descended on a Monday,[^25] therefore, additional supplications are recited and before *tachanun, vehu rachum* is said.[^26] It (*vehu rachum*) is said standing. It must be said with concentration and with deliberation in a pleading manner. On days when *tachanun* is omitted it (*vehu rachum*) is not said. ^22-9

**10.** After *tachanun* [is completed] half-kaddish[^27] is said. On Mondays and Thursdays *Keil erech apayim*[^28] is said while standing. ^22-10
