## Mūla

दशाङ्गुलानि त्रीण्यस्मादेकं पञ्चदशाङ्गुलम् ।
चतुर्भिरधिकैरन्यन्नवमं व्यापकं महत् ॥ ^mula

daśāṅgulāni trīṇy asmād ekaṁ[^25] pañcadaśāṅgulam |
caturbhir adhikair anyan navamaṁ vyāpakaṁ mahat || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, *Mālinī*, January 2024 – April 2024
> p. 29 ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Malini (journal), January-April 2024.pdf#page=31>))
>
> The first (occupies) four finger (spaces), two others eight finger (spaces) each, three, ten each, one, fifteen finger (spaces) and another again (of fifteen fingers) and another of four which is the ninth. (The tenth), which is immense, pervades (them all).[^26] (with 6.19cd–20)

[^25]: K: *evaṁ pañcadaśeti*
[^26]: MV 6/19cd-20 is quoted in TĀv ad 16/135cd-138ab. Here again Abhinava renders the meaning of the *Mālinīvijayottara* accessible to us by paraphrasing the text adding the few necessary details to complete the sense as he does so. He informs us, in his own subtle way, what the *Mālinīviyajottara* does not tell us. In this case it is that the Mantra of which these are the parts are the two alphabet goddesses, namely. Mātṛkā and Mālinī. The former is the common order of the alphabet, and the latter the Trika alphabet. In the *Tantrāloka* he says: '(The teacher) should conjoin the nine Parts (of Mantra) associated with (the phonemes of) Mātāka and Mālinī, beginning with the Part (corresponding to) Earth, into (nine spaces, consisting of) four, eight, eight, ten, ten, ten, fifteen, four, and fifteen (finger-spaces, respectively). The tenth Part, which is called 'Śiva', pervades (all the preceding ones).' TĀ 16/135cd-137ab The finger spaces are thus distributed as follows: 4 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 15 + 15 + 4 = 84. For example, the *pada* of Earth is KṢ, according to the Mātṛkā alphabet, and PH, according to Mālinī, and takes up four fingerspaces. In TĀ 11/51-53, drawning from MV 2/51-57, Abhinava enumerates the mantras, parts, phonemes, worlds and principles as they are accommodated into the spheres of the five Forces, thus listing the contents of the six Paths in relation to one another. There the parts of mantra are ten. These correspondences are tabulated in appendix >>>. The corresponding measurements with respect to the body are not listed by in the *Tantrāloka* 11/51-53. We get them from here and have been added to table. Here we observe an example of how Abhinava's exegesis is supplemented by his source. One wonders how much we have missed by the loss of the Tantras to which he refers that have been lost. Initiation can be imparted by just depositing the Parts of Mantra, without reference to the Paths. The *Mālinīvijayottara* does not say this directly, Abhinava understood this to be implied, or else was told by his Trika teacher Śambhunātha who read the Tantra with him. Thus, Abhinava writes in the *Tantrāloka*: 'If the ten Parts (*pada*) (of the Mantra) mentioned previously (i.e., Parāparā Vidyā) are deposited independently, and (if the teacher wishes that) the initiation be performed by means of them alone, then this is the (manner in which) the deposition is conceived. When the main factor in the initiation is the principles (*tattva*) and the rest (of the Six Paths), the series of Parts is considered to be included there in that way, in accord with (each of) the principles and the rest. When their own predominance applies, (the teacher) should deposit the series of Parts successively (in due order) (krameṇa) (independently) in (the course of) initiation, without referring them to the (path of the) principles and the rest.' TĀ 16/132-135ab This is the lower (Apara) procedure, that is, the projection onto 84 fingers' spaces on the body. Again completing the Mālinīvijaya for us, Abhinava goes on to explain the other two procedures are in relation to it: 'The two procedures, (intermediate and supreme, are derived from this one) by adding to the five Parts, omitting that of Earth, (a space of) twelve fingers (for the intermediate one), and twelve (more for the supreme one).' TĀ 16/137cd-138ab
