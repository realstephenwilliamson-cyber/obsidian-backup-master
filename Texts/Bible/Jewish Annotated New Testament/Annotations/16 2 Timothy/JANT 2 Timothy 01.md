### 1.1–2: Salutation

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 442* · Greco-Roman and Jewish letters typically opened with tripartite salutations in the third person, noting the sender, recipient, and greetings (Gk “chairein”) as in Papyrus Yadin 52; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 13#^13-36|1 Macc 13.36]]. Jewish letters sometimes add “eirēnēn agathēn” “good peace” (NRSV “true peace”; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 01#^1-1|2 Macc 1.1]]). ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Apostle* (Gk “apostolos”; Heb “šālûaḥ”; cf. [[I Kings 14#^14-6|1 Kings 14.6]]), lit., “one sent,” is an authorized representative performing a specific function (e.g., *m. Ber.* 5.5, “A man’s agent \[Heb “šālûaḥ”\] is like himself”). *Promise of life*, anticipating 2.18, reflects the hope of resurrection and realization of the world-to-come (Heb “ʿolam ha-baʾ”). See *1 En.* 48.4, where the Son of Man will become the “light of the Gentiles, and the hope of those who are troubled of heart.” See also LXX [[LXX Psalms 59#^59-5|Ps 59.5]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 02#^2-18|2 Macc 2.18]]. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Timothy*, Gk “honoring God,” “in God’s honor,” or “honored by God.” *Beloved*, denoting a chosen status among Paul’s disciples. Cf. [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|1 Cor 4.17]], “My beloved and faithful child.” [[1 Timothy 01#^1-2|1 Tim 1.2]], “my loyal child in the faith”; *Child*, meaning student or disciple, as frequent in biblical wisdom literature (e.g., [[Proverbs 04#^4-1|Prov 4.1]]); see *b. Sanh.* 19b, “He who teaches the son of his neighbor the Torah, Scripture ascribes it to him as if he had begotten him.” *Grace*, Gk “charis,” Heb “ḥēn,” “graciousness, favor” (e.g., [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-8|Gen 6.8]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-12|Ex 33.12]]). *Mercy*, Gk “eleos,” “compassion,” Heb “ḥesed,” “covenantal loyalty, loving-kindness, steadfast love” (see [[Psalms 025#^25-6|Ps 25.6]]). *Peace*, Gk “eirēnē,” Heb “shalom.” ^1-2

### 1.3–5: Thanksgiving for Timothy’s faith

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-3|Text]], p. 442* ^1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Clear conscience*, Gk “athara syneidēsei” reflects the confluence between faith and practice. The Pastoral Epistles contrast the “good” ([[1 Timothy 01#^1-5|1 Tim 1.5]],[[1 Timothy 01#^1-19|19]]) and “clear” ([[1 Timothy 03#^3-9|1 Tim 3.9]]) conscience of the faithful believer with the “corrupted” ([[Titus 01#^1-15|Titus 1.15]]) or “seared with a hot iron” ([[1 Timothy 04#^4-2|1 Tim 4.2]]) consciousness of the unbelievers. *As my ancestors* (Gk “apo progonōn”), i.e., “in the same way as my (Jewish) ancestors,” affirming the continuity of Jewish beliefs with Paul’s teaching. The reference could also be to Paul’s own parents. ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Tears*, [[Acts 20#^20-36|Acts 20.36–38]]; Josephus, *Life* 420. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Sincere faith*, literally, “an unhypocritical faith in you.” *Faith*, occurring over fifty times in the Pastorals, “faith” (Gk “pistis”) and related words are key components of the theological vocabulary of this corpus. *Faith* appears in virtue lists ([[1 Timothy 01#^1-5|1 Tim 1.5]],[[1 Timothy 01#^1-19|19]]; [[1 Timothy 02#^2-7|2.7]]; [[1 Timothy 04#^4-6|4.6]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-13|2 Tim 1.13]]; [[2 Timothy 02#^2-22|2.22]]; [[2 Timothy 03#^3-10|3.10–11]]) and its meanings include doctrinal statements ([[1 Timothy 04#^4-6|1 Tim 4.6]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-8|5.8]]; See also [[Galatians 01#^1-23|Gal 1.23]]) and fidelity ([[1 Timothy 01#^1-14|1 Tim 1.14]]; [[1 Timothy 02#^2-15|2.15]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-12|5.12]]). The equivalent word in the Tanakh (“emunah”) connotes “trustworthiness.” In rabbinic Judaism, *faith* is related to trust in God. For example, *Mek. Ex.* 14.31 reads, “ . . . whosoever accepts even one single commandment with true faith is deserving of having the Holy Spirit rest upon him . . . our father Abraham inherited both this world and the world to come only as a reward for the faith with which he believed, as it is said: ‘And he believed in the Lord’” ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]). *Faith that lived first in your grandmother*, it is not clear whether the author means that she was a believer in Jesus, or whether she was simply a faithful Jew. *Lois and your mother Eunice*, both Jewish, while Timothy’s father was Greek ([[Acts 16#^16-1|Acts 16.1]]). ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-6|1.6–14]]:** **Exhortation to faithfulness.** ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Rekindle*, lit., “set on flame again.” *Gift of God*, Gk “charisma”; here the commission for service to the gospel. *Laying on of my hands*, see [[1 Timothy 04#^4-14|1 Tim 4.14]] (speaking of the elders \[Gk “presbyteroi”\]). The Heb verb “sāmah” encompasses the symbolic gesture associated with blessings ([[Torah 12 - Vayechi#^48-14|Gen 48.14]]), consecration of a person assuming religious or leadership functions ([[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^8-5|Num 8.5–15]]; [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^27-18|27.18–23]]); and the transfer of God’s spirit (see esp. [[Torah 54 - V'Zot HaBerachah#^34-9|Deut 34.9]], “Joshua son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, because Moses had laid his hands on him.” Rabbinic ordination was later called “semikhah,” lit., “laying \[of hands\],” representing the idea that ordination was passed from teacher to student. In his undisputed letters, Paul does not mention this ritual means of conveying “charisma.” ^1-6-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Spirit of cowardice . . . power*, see [[Torah 52 - Vayeilech#^31-8|Deut 31.8]]; [[Joshua 01#^1-9|Josh 1.9]]; [[LXX Sirach 34#^34-14|Sir 34.14–16]]; [[Romans 08#^8-15|Rom 8.15]]; in Greek thought, having “neither fear nor cowardice” was a virtue of both military and political leaders (Homer, *Iliad*, 2,235; Plato, *Protagoras*, 360). *Love* is the most important spiritual gift in the Pauline tradition ([[Romans 05#^5-5|Rom 5.5]]; [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-31|1 Cor 12.31b–14.1]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-13|2 Tim 1.13]]; [[2 Timothy 02#^2-22|2.22]]; [[2 Timothy 03#^3-10|3.10]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Do not be ashamed*, an important theme (1.12,16; 2.15) and a common form of exhortation, e.g., “Do not be ashamed of the law of the Most High and his covenant,” [[LXX Sirach 42#^42-2|Sir 42.2]]; see also [[LXX Sirach 04#^4-26|Sir 4.26]]; [[LXX Sirach 42#^42-1|42.1]]. *Testimony* . . . *prisoner*, testifying or witnessing (Gk “martyrion”) to Jesus’ crucifixion by Roman authorities and Paul’s imprisonment in Rome would suggest that the followers of the gospel were considered the followers of criminals. *Suffering for the gospel*, in the same way that Jesus and Paul suffered as faithful servants for the gospel (Gk “euangelion,” lit., “good news”; see [[2 Timothy 02#^2-8|2 Tim 2.8–10]]). ^1-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Saved us*, salvation in the Pauline tradition is realized both in hope ([[Romans 08#^8-24|Rom 8.24]]), belief and the seal of the Spirit ([[Ephesians 01#^1-13|Eph 1.13]]; [[Ephesians 02#^2-8|2.8]]; see also [[2 Corinthians 06#^6-2|2 Cor 6.2]]), when believers persevere ([[1 Corinthians 01#^1-18|1 Cor 1.18]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-2|15.2]]) and in a future consummation ([[Romans 13#^13-11|Rom 13.11]]; [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-15|1 Cor 3.15]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|5.5]]; [[2 Corinthians 05#^5-10|2 Cor 5.10]]) *Works . . . grace*, a contrast characterizing Paul’s understanding of salvation based not on human merits or actions, but on God’s grace ([[Romans 03#^3-21|Rom 3.21–27]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-15|Gal 2.15–21]]). See “Paul and Judaism,” p. 633. *Ages began*, before the creation of the world, see [[Ephesians 01#^1-4|Eph 1.4]]. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Revealed*, denoting the setting in motion of the age of salvation; see [[1 Timothy 03#^3-16|1 Tim 3.16]]. *Appearing*, see [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-8|2 Thess 2.8]]; [[1 Timothy 06#^6-14|1 Tim 6.14]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-1|2 Tim 4.1]],[[2 Timothy 04#^4-8|8]]; [[Titus 02#^2-13|Titus 2.13]]. *Savior*, also an epithet of emperors. *Abolished death . . . brought life*, Jesus’ resurrection has nullified the power of death, cf. [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-26|1 Cor 15.26]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-10|2 Cor 4.10]]. ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Sound teaching*, an expression characteristic of the Pastoral Epistles (4.3; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-10|1 Tim 1.10]]; [[1 Timothy 06#^6-3|6.3]]; [[Titus 01#^1-9|Titus 1.9]]; [[Titus 02#^2-1|2.1]]). In his authentic epistles, Paul typically argues his points rather than simply asserting them, as here. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Holy Spirit living in us*, [[Romans 08#^8-9|Rom 8.9]],[[Romans 08#^8-11|11]]; [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|1 Cor 3.16]]. ^1-14

### 1.15–18: Behavior of Paul’s followers

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-15|Text]], p. 443* ^1-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-15|15]]:** *Asia*, a Roman province (modern-day Turkey). *Phygelus and Hermogenes*, not mentioned elsewhere. ^1-15-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Onesiphorus*, see 4.19, lived in Ephesus; although he is not mentioned elsewhere in the NT, he appears in the apocryphal *Acts of Paul and Thecla* (3.2–7; 15.23–26,42), which places him in Iconium. *Chain*, referring to Paul’s imprisonment; see [[Acts 28#^28-20|Acts 28.20]]. ^1-16
