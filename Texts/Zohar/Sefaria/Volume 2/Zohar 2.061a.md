*Volume 2, folio 61a* · Beshalach

*Passage Beshalach 27:358 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 2.060b#^beshalach-27-358|Beshalach 27:358]].*

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Beshalach 27:359–27:363.*

**Beshalach 28:364** AND THE LORD SAID UNTO MOSES, BEHOLD I WILL RAIN BREAD FROM HEAVEN FOR YOU. R. Judah quoted here the verse: *Blessed is he who considereth the poor: the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble* (Ps. 41, 2). ‘These words’, he said, ‘have been applied to a man who is lying dangerously ill. Such a one is a prisoner of the King-his neck yoked and his feet in chains. On either side warders keep guard over him. His limbs war with one another, and he is unable to eat.’ ^beshalach-28-364

**Beshalach 28:365** But in his helplessness a guardian angel is appointed to watch over him and to intercede on his behalf before the King, recalling all his virtues and any good deed that he may have done. Happy is then the counsellor who teaches the afflicted one the way of life so that he may be delivered from judgement and be brought back to his Lord; ^beshalach-28-365

**Beshalach 28:366** he becomes an intercessor for him above. And what will be his reward? “The Lord will deliver him in time of trouble”.’ ^beshalach-28-366

**Beshalach 28:367** R. Hiya said: ‘I have often wondered at the words, “For the Lord heareth the poor” (Ps. 69, 34). Does He then hear only the poor?’ R. Simeon replied: ‘These words signify that the poor are indeed nearer to the King than all others, for it is written, “a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise” (Ps. 51, 18) and no one in the world is so broken in heart as the poor man. Mark this! Most human beings appear before the Holy One in body and in soul, but the poor man presents himself before the Throne of the Most High in soul only, and the Holy One is nearer to the soul than to the body.’ ^beshalach-28-367

**Beshalach 28:368** At one time there lived in the neighbourhood of R. Jesse a poor man of whom no one took any notice; and to beg he was ashamed. One day he fell ill, and R. Jesse went to visit him. And as he sat by the sick man’s bedside the Rabbi heard a voice saying: “Wheel,[^1] wheel, a soul is flying to me before its rightful time has come! Woe unto his fellow townsmen that none were found among them to sustain him, that he might live!” R. Jesse, having heard these words, stood up and put into the mouth of the sick man the water of a certain herb, bidding him drink; and this made him so to sweat that the illness left him and he recovered. ^beshalach-28-368

[^1]: i. e. the wheel of destiny.
