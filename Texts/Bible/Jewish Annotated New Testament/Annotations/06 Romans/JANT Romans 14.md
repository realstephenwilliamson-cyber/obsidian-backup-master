### 14.1–23: God’s faithfulness proclaimed by all together

*[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-1|Text]], p. 315* · Paul continues to instruct these non-Jews on how they should live within the Jewish communities of Rome. He refers to those Jews as “weak” or “stumbling” in faithfulness to the revelation of Christ, in contrast to themselves and Jews such as Paul who are “strong” or “able” to express this faithfulness. Christ-following Gentiles are to respect the expressions of righteousness incumbent upon these Jews. Otherwise, they risk harming the very ones for whom they should be living graciously in faithfulness to Christ. ^14-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-1|1]]:** *Welcome*, carries the sense of “bring in as a partner.” *Weak* (Gk “astheneō”) is connected to “stumbling”/“offense” (lit., “trap”) in the LXX, where it translates Heb “kashal” and cognates ([[Psalms 009#^9-4|Ps 9.3]]; [[LXX Psalms 26#^26-2|26.2]]\[LXX\]; 106.12\[LXX\]; [[Jeremiah 06#^6-21|Jer 6.21]]; [[Jeremiah 18#^18-15|18.15]],[[Jeremiah 18#^18-23|23]]; [[Daniel 11#^11-14|Dan 11.14]],[[Daniel 11#^11-19|19]],[[Daniel 11#^11-33|33–35]]; [[Hosea 04#^4-5|Hos 4.5]]; 5.5; [[Hosea 14#^14-2|14.1]],[[Hosea 14#^14-10|9]]; cf. 1QH 9.25–27, where it is also contrasted with “might/power” \[Heb “g-b-r”\] in a way similar to the contrast Paul is drawing with cognates of Gk “dynamai”; see also [[Judges 05#^5-31|Judg 5.31]]; [[II Kings 18#^18-20|2 Kings 18.20]]; [[I Chronicles 29#^29-11|1 Chr 29.11]]). The translation “stumbling” (cf. [[Romans 09#^9-32|Rom 9.32–33]]) explains how these Gentiles are to view Jews who are not Christ-followers. *Weak in faith*, Jews who are stumbling with respect to faithfulness (i.e., to Christ). ^14-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-2|2]]:** *Eat only vegetables*, Torah-observant Jews might eat vegetables when the meat available is deemed inappropriate ([[Daniel 01#^1-8|Dan 1.8–16]]; [[Daniel 10#^10-3|10.3]]; [[LXX Tobit 01#^1-10|Tob 1.10–11]]; [[LXX Judith 10#^10-5|Jdt 10.5]]; [[LXX Judith 12#^12-1|12.1–2]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 05#^5-27|2 Macc 5.27]]; Josephus, *Life* 14); see v 14. The meat might be seen offered to an idol, or was not properly prepared. ^14-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-6|6–8]]:** *Day* is not specified; it could be the Sabbath or other feast days, but also could reflect disputes regarding correct observance based on differing calendars. ^14-6

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-9|9]]:** *Lord of both the dead and the living*, i.e., ruler of all, since he has conquered death. ^14-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-10|10]]:** See 2.1–16. ^14-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-11|11]]:** Paul conflates and adapts [[Isaiah 49#^49-18|Isa 49.18]] and 45.23. *Shall give praise*, or “shall confess.” ^14-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-13|13]]:** *Put a stumbling block*, avoiding the appearance of idolatry to respect the sensibilities of others; see [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-33|Ex 23.33]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-12|34.12]]; [[Judges 02#^2-3|Judg 2.3]]; [[Judges 08#^8-27|8.27]]; [[Jeremiah 03#^3-3|Jer 3.3]]; [[Hosea 04#^4-17|Hos 4.17]]; [[Zephaniah 1#^1-3|Zeph 1.3]]; Ps 106\[LXX 105\].36; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-11|Wis 14.11]]; 1QS 2.11–12,17. cf. [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-14|Lev 19.14]], one shall not “put a stumbling block before the blind,” developed in *y. Demai.* 3.23 ( [[Romans 11#^11-7|Rom 11.7–10]]). *Of another*, lit., “of the brother (or sister).” ^14-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-14|14]]:** *Unclean*, lit., “profane/common” (see “Food That Is ‘Profane’,” p. 316). ^14-14

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-15|15]]:** Cf. [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-14|Lev 19.14]], the commandment to avoid putting a stumbling block before the disabled. *Is being injured*, or “is being harassed.” ^14-15

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-16|16]]:** *Be spoken of as evil*, lit., “be blasphemed.” ^14-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-17|17]]:** *Kingdom of God*, the Epistle’s only reference to this concept ( [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-20|1 Cor 4.20]]). ^14-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-19|19]]:** See [[Psalms 034#^34-15|Ps 34.14]]; *m. Avot* 1.12. ^14-19

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-20|20]]:** *Clean*, or pure, fit for eating; *fall*, lit., “stumble.” ^14-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-21|21]]:** *Wine* could be associated with idolatrous activities involved in the production or with a libation offered to the gods ([[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-38|Deut 32.38]]), and thus prohibited ([[Daniel 01#^1-8|Dan 1.8]]; [[Daniel 05#^5-4|5.4]],[[Daniel 05#^5-23|23]]; *m. Avod. Zar.* 2.3; *b. Avod. Zar.* 30b,31a,36a). ^14-21

**[[NRSVue Romans 14#^14-23|23]]:** Some manuscripts insert here the doxology in 16.25–27. *Have doubts*, lit., “cause disputes.” ^14-23

> [!note] Food That Is “Profane” (ROM 14.14) *(p. 316)*
> Paul recognizes that all things are by nature not created profane (*koinon*, “common”; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-47|1 Macc 1.47]],[[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-62|62]]), for the creation was pronounced good (Gen 1). The categories of common/profane versus holy (set apart to God) are not intrinsic to the food itself; rather, its valuation is imputed by personal or community convention (*nomos*). According to Jews, God establishes for Israel what is profane and what is to be set apart (holy) to God ([[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-41|Lev 11.41–45]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-2|19.2]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-25|20.25–26]]). This distinction does not invalidate the purity laws of Judaism; indeed, it is recognized in scripture ([[Psalms 024#^24-1|Ps 24.1]]; [[Psalms 050#^50-1|50.1]], which Paul cites in [[1 Corinthians 10#^10-26|1 Cor 10.26]] to make a similar point), and in rabbinic literature (*Gen. Rab.* 44.1; *Sifra Aharei* 93d). Paul is not abrogating Torah. Instead, Paul is making halakhic decisions for Gentile Christ-followers.
