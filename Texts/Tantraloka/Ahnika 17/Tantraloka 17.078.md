## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 08 - Chapter Seventeen#^pg192|p. 172]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=192>))
>
> The fetter which is the Impurity of the individual soul is present (in a subtle form) within Sadāśiva, for it is said that there are two fetters within it, namely, (the residual trace of) worldly experience (bhoga) and the aptitude (adhikāra) (for it). However, (the Impurity of) Māyā extends here (up to) the Night (of Māyā) (niśā). When the Impurity called that of the individual soul, which is in the topknot (śikhā) that flows downwards, has been burnt (away), the disciple, as is proper, should bathe, and the teacher should rinse his mouth himself. (with 17.76cd–78ab)
>
> Then, (the teacher), thinking of all that needs to be known (prameya) taught previously, having conjoined the disciple’s body etc. with his own body and vital breath etc., should recollect that everything (viśva) is present within his own body and vital breath etc. (Then, his) intellect firm and mind undistracted, meditating by means of the method (prakriyā) taught (previously) on the two paths of time and space (as) present within the vital breath, the breath (as) present within (Śiva’s) power, and that that (power rests) within consciousness, (he should then meditate on how) that pure consciousness, which is iva, is not separate from the disciple’s consciousness, and is undivided from the fire of Mantra and the rest. Meditating (thus,) with (the same) application (prayoga) (of mantra etc.) as before on Ñiva, with differentiated energies and without them (sakalaniṣkala) or as both together, he should cast a full (oblation into the fire) with a completely tranquil mind (praśāntakaranṇa). (with 17.78cd–82)
