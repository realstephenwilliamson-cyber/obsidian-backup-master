8\. AT THE TIME WHEN RECOURSE IS HAD TO MEDITATION THERE

EXISTS NO NOTION OR ABSORBING INTEREST.

For "meditation" is one's whole being undividedly intent on a specific subject of meditation, and the essence of (Prakāśa) is in the absence of distinctions. Man is attracted by entities that are distinguishable. As a result of fixing his attention thereon, an intensive kind of knowledge on the matter gradually arises. This cannot apply here (to Prakāśa) because of the homogeneous nature of Prakāśa who is absolute fulfillment, (and) because there is no connection between meditator and meditated upon. In our doctrinal school, it is the Śakti resplendent in the reflection that is supreme "I" who is to be desired - because She is the abode of rest.
