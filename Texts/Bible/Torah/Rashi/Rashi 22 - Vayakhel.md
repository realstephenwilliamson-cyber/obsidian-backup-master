# Rashi 22 - Vayakhel

← [[Rashi 21 - Ki Tisa]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 23 - Pekudei]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-1|Exodus 35:1–38:20]]* · Text: [[Torah 22 - Vayakhel]]

## Chapter 35

### 35:1

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-1|Exodus 35:1]]

**And Moshe assembled —** on the day following Yom Kippur when he came down from the mountain. It (וַיַּקְהֵל) is in the causative form, for one does not assemble people, literally, with one’s hands but, rather, they are “caused to be assembled” by means of his saying so. The correct version of this in Onkelos is: וְאַכְנִישׁ — “He caused that they assemble.”

### 35:2

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-2|Exodus 35:2]]

**Six days.** He first admonished them concerning Shabbos before commanding them about the works of the *mishkon,* saying, in effect, that it does not supercede the Shabbos.

### 35:3

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-3|Exodus 35:3]]

**You must not kindle a fire.** Some of our Sages say: Kindling a fire, a לַאו — a negative precept, “went out” (i.e., was singled out to indicate this new law). Others say it “went out” (i.e., was singled out) to separate [the *melachos* of Shabbos].

### 35:4

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-4|Exodus 35:4]]

**This is the word that Adonoy has commanded —** me, to tell you.

### 35:5

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-5|Exodus 35:5]]

**Who is generous of heart.** Since it is his heart that inspires him [to give] he is called “generous of heart.” I have already explained the contributions to the *mishkon* and its work in the place where they were commanded.

### 35:11

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-11|Exodus 35:11]]

**The *mishkon.*** The lower drapes which are visible on the inside are referred to as *mishkon.*

**Its tent.** This refers to the covering of the goat-hair drapes which serve as a roof.

**And its cover.** The covering of the rams’ skins and the *tachash*-skins.

### 35:12

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-12|Exodus 35:12]]

**And the partition screen.** This was the *paroches* of the partition Anything that serves as a shield whether horizontally, whether vertically can be referred to as מָסָךְ or סְכַךְ . Similarly: “(שַׂכְתָּ) You have protected him [from above],” [or:] “Behold I (שָׂךְ) will protect your road.”

### 35:13

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-13|Exodus 35:13]]

**The show bread.** I have explained that it was so called because it had a face in two directions for it was made like an open box.

### 35:14

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-14|Exodus 35:14]]

**And its vessels.** [I.e.] its tongs and its scoops.

**Its lamps.** *Loces* in Old French; the scoops into which the oil and wicks are put.

**Oil for the light.** It, too, required talented people for it is different from other oils, as is explained in Tractate Menachos: He allows it to ripen on top of the olive tree and it thereby is crushed and [yet remains] clear.

### 35:15

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-15|Exodus 35:15]]

**The screen for the entrance.** The curtain on the eastern side where there were no planks nor drapes.

### 35:17

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-17|Exodus 35:17]]

**Its pillars and its sockets.** Here you have חָצֵר referred to both as masculine and feminine. There are many such instances.

**And the screen for the entrance of the enclosure.** A curtain spread on the eastern side over the 20 middle *amohs* of the width of that courtyard. For the total width was 50 *amohs* of which 15 *amohs* of the northern end were closed as well as on the southern end as it is said: “and 15 *amohs* of curtain for one end.”

### 35:18

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-18|Exodus 35:18]]

**The stakes.** To insert them and to tie into them the ends of the drapes into the ground, so that they will not sway in the wind.

**And their ropes.** Ropes with which to tie.

### 35:19

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-19|Exodus 35:19]]

**The covering cloths** — with which to cover the ark, the *shulchan,* the *Menorah,* and the altars when they had to be removed for the journeys.

### 35:22

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-22|Exodus 35:22]]

**And women.** [עַל הַנָּשִׁים means] *with* the women and close to them.

**Bracelets.** A round golden ornament worn on the arm. It is called a צָמִיד — “bracelet.”

**And buckles.** A golden ornament worn against a woman’s private parts. Our Sages explained the derivation of the name כוּמָז as כָּאן מָקוֹם זִמָּה — “here is the place of lewdness.”

### 35:23

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-23|Exodus 35:23]]

**Every man who had with him —** greenish-blue wool, *or* dark red wool, *or* crimson wool, *or* rams’ skins *or tachash* skins — they all brought.

### 35:26

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-26|Exodus 35:26]]

**Spun the goats’ hair.** This was a craft of special skill for they spun it while it was still on the animals’ backs.

### 35:27

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-27|Exodus 35:27]]

**And the leaders [of the tribes] brought.** R. Noson said: Why did the נְשִׂיאִים see fit to contribute to the dedication of the altar first whereas for the works of the *mishkon* they were not the first to contribute? But the נְשִׂיאִים thought as follows: “Let the public-at-large contribute whatever they will contribute, and whatever will be lacking we will supply.” Once the public supplied all that was needed, as it is said: “And the work was sufficient,” the נְשִׂיאִים said, “what is left for us to do?” So “they brought the *shoham*-stones, etc.” It is for this reason that they contributed to the altar dedication first. And since, at first, they were somewhat lax, there is a letter missing, here, from their name and it is written וְהַנְּשִֹאִם (without a י' after the שֹ').

### 35:30

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-30|Exodus 35:30]]

**Chur.** He was Miriam’s son.

### 35:34

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^35-34|Exodus 35:34]]

**And Oholiov.** He was from the Tribe of Dan, one of the lowliest of the Tribes, one of the sons of [Yaakov’s] maidservants. Yet God equates him to Betzalel regarding the works of the *mishkon,* and he (Betzalel) is from the greatest of the Tribes. This tends to fulfill what is stated: “He does not recognize the rich over the poor.”

## Chapter 36

### 36:5

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^36-5|Exodus 36:5]]

**More than is needed for the tasks.** [Meaning:] more than what was needed for the work.

### 36:6

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^36-6|Exodus 36:6]]

**And the people stopped.** [וַיִּכָּלֵא is] an expression meaning “withholding.”

### 36:7

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^36-7|Exodus 36:7]]

**The material was enough for all the work.** [Meaning:] the work of bringing was sufficient for the workers of the *mishkon.* For all the “work” of the *mishkon* — to make it and to have some left over.

**And some was left over.** As in “(וְהַכְבֵּד) *to harden* his heart,” [or:] “(וְהַכּוֹת) *to strike* Moav.”

## Chapter 37

### 37:1

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^37-1|Exodus 37:1]]

**And Betzalel made.** Because he extended himself for the work more than the other wise men, it (the work) bears his name.

## Chapter 38

### 38:7

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^38-7|Exodus 38:7]]

**Hollow boards.** נְבוּב means “hollow.” Similarly: “Its thickness was four fingers (נְבוּב) *hollow.”*

**Hollow boards.** The boards of acacia wood were on all sides [thereby creating] the hollow in the middle.

### 38:8

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^38-8|Exodus 38:8]]

**From the mirrors of the women who had gathered.** The daughters of Israel possessed mirrors into which they would look when adorning themselves. Even those they did not withhold from bringing as a contribution for the *mishkon.* However, Moshe found them repulsive since their purpose is to incite the evil inclination. God said to him: Accept [them], for these are dearer to me than everything else because through them the women raised huge multitudes in Egypt. When their husbands were exhausted from their crushing labor they (the women) would go and bring them food and drink and feed them. They would then take the mirrors and each one would look at herself and her husband in the mirror, and entice him with words, saying, “See! I am more beautiful than you,” thereby awakening their husbands’ desire and they would cohabit with them. They conceived and gave birth there. As is stated: “Under the apple-tree did I arouse you.” That is the intent of what is said: “By the mirrors of the צֹבְאוֹת .” The wash-basin was made out of them for it serves to bring about peace between a man and his wife, by giving to drink from the waters inside it to anyone whose husband expressed jealousy and she, nevertheless, concealed herself [with another man]. You can know yourself that these were actual mirrors, for it is stated: “The total copper donation was seventy *kikar,* etc. and he made from it, etc.” whereas the wash-basin and its base are not mentioned there. Hence you have learned that the copper of the wash-basin was not from the donated copper. Thus did Rabbi Tanchuma explain it. Onkelos also translates it this way: בְמֶחְזְיַת of the women [בְּמֶחְזְיַת] being the Aramaic for “mirrors,” *mireors* in Old French. We find the like in Yeshaiyahu: “וְהַגִּלְיֹנִים — “and the mirrors” which is translated מֶחְזְיָתָא .

**Who had gathered —** to bring their contribution.

### 38:18

[[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^38-18|Exodus 38:18]]

**Corresponding to the curtains of the enclosure.** [Meaning:] Like the measurement of the curtains of the courtyard.
