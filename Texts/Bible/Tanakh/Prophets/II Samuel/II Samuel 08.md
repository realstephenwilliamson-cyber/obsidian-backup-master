## Chapter 8

**8:1** [^1]Some time afterward, David attacked the Philistines and subdued them; and David took Metheg-ammah[^2] from the Philistines. ^8-1

**8:2** He also defeated the Moabites. He made them lie down on the ground and he measured them off with a cord; he measured out two lengths of cord for those who were to be put to death, and one length for those to be spared.[^3] And the Moabites became tributary vassals of David. ^8-2

**8:3** David defeated Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, who was then on his way to restore his monument[^4] at the Euphrates River. ^8-3

**8:4** David captured 1,700 horsemen and 20,000 foot soldiers of his force; and David hamstrung all the chariot horses, except for 100 which he retained. ^8-4

**8:5** And when the Arameans of Damascus came to the aid of King Hadadezer of Zobah, David struck down 22,000 of the Arameans. ^8-5

**8:6** David stationed garrisons in Aram of Damascus, and the Arameans became tributary vassals of David. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went. ^8-6

**8:7** David took the gold shields[^5] carried by Hadadezer’s retinue and brought them to Jerusalem; ^8-7

**8:8** and from Betah and Berothai, towns of Hadadezer, King David took a vast amount of copper. ^8-8

**8:9** When King Toi of Hamath heard that David had defeated the entire army of Hadadezer, ^8-9

**8:10** Toi sent his son Joram to King David to greet him and to congratulate him on his military victory over Hadadezer—for Hadadezer had been at war with Toi. [Joram] brought with him objects of silver, gold, and copper. ^8-10

**8:11** King David dedicated these to the LORD, along with the other silver and gold that he dedicated, [taken] from all the nations he had conquered: ^8-11

**8:12** from Edom,[^6] Moab, and Ammon; from the Philistines and the Amalekites, and from the plunder of Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah. ^8-12

**8:13** David gained fame when he returned from defeating[^7] Edom[^8] in the Valley of Salt, 18,000 in all. ^8-13

**8:14** He stationed garrisons in Edom—he stationed garrisons in all of Edom[^9]—and all the Edomites became vassals of David. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went. ^8-14

**8:15** David reigned over all Israel, and David executed true justice among all his people. ^8-15

**8:16** Joab son of Zeruiah was commander of the army; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder; ^8-16

**8:17** Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar[^10] were priests; Seraiah[^11] was scribe; ^8-17

**8:18** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was commander of[^12] the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and David’s sons were priests. ^8-18

[^1]: This chapter is reproduced, with some variations, in 1 Chron. 18.
[^2]: If not a place name, meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: I.e., he repeatedly doomed twice the number he spared.
[^4]: On yad in this sense, cf. 18.18; 1 Chron. 18.3; 1 Sam. 15.12. Others “dominion.”
[^5]: Or “quivers.”
[^6]: So several mss., Septuagint, and 1 Chron. 18.11–13; and cf. v. 14 below. Printed editions and most mss. read “Aram.”
[^7]: 1 Chron. 18.12 and Ps. 60.1 read differently.
[^8]: So several mss., Septuagint, and 1 Chron. 18.11–13; and cf. v. 14 below. Printed editions and most mss. read “Aram.”
[^9]: This phrase is lacking in 1 Chron. 18.13.
[^10]: Emendation yields “Abiathar son of Ahimelech,” cf., e.g., 20.25; 1 Sam. 22.20.
[^11]: “Sheva” in 20.25; “Shavsha” in 1 Chron. 18.16.
[^12]: So Targum (cf. 20.23; 1 Chron. 18.17); Heb. “and.”
