## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 09 - Chapter Eight, part 7#^pg317|p. 299]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=317>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 8.294cd–295ab.
>
> The Supreme Lord, although full (and perfect in every respect) pūrna),), assumes the state of that individual soul who, as such (evam), is ignorant that that is his own nature. Thus, there are various types (citra) of individual soul. Dependence (paratantra) is said to be the characteristic of all (types of) fetters, (both) those that have been described and those that have not. As they are (progressively) all purified there within the individual soul, he is freed from phenomenal existence. (with 8.293–294)
>
> 1) Above the individual soul is the Principle of Natural Law (niyati). The ten Śaṁkaras reside there, resplendent as gold. 2) There are ten Śivas who are extremely white in the Principle of Time. Each of them is surrounded by ten million and sixteen thousand (deities, who serve as their entourage). 3) (Next comes) the world of (the eight) Vīreśas, and as another, the (ten) teachers and disciples in the Principle of Attachment (rāga). 4) The world of Impure Knowledge shines brilliantly with the nine powers. They are said to range from Vāmā up to Manomanī. 5) The best of worlds, that of the three Mahādevas, is the Principle of the Force (kalā) (of limited agency). (with 8.294–298ab)
