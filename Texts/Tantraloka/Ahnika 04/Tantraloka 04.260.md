## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 11 - Chapter Four, part 10#^pg378|p. 365]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=378>))
>
> Entry (praveśa) into the sacred meeting grounds (kṣetra) and the sacred seats, both primary (pīṭha) and secondary (upapīṭha), is taught (in Kula Tantras) in order to quell obstacles (to the success of one’s practice). Now, in the case of one who propitiates (a particular) Mantra (deity) and the like, (residence in such places) is taught (as a means), in order to attain that (mastery through it and the benefits it bestows). (with 4.259cd–260ab)
>
> (However, in the Matatantras,) travel to the sacred meeting grounds (kṣetra) and other (such places) is prohibited, so that (one may) realize the (perfect all-embracing) plenitude (pūrnatva) of one’s own nature as being the nature of all things, and so this too has been explained (in the same way). (with 4.260cd–261ab)

*Lead-in:* अन्यत्र चात्र निषेधः कृतः -- इत्याह --

> क्षेत्रादिगमनाभावविधिस्तु स्वात्मनस्तथा ॥
> वैश्वरूप्येण पूर्णत्वं ज्ञातुमित्यपि वर्णितम् ।

आदिशब्दात् पीठादेर्ग्रहणम् । "तथा"इति प्रागुक्तेन प्रकारेण । तदुक्तम् --

ङातः किञ्चिदपास्यं प्रक्षेप्तव्यं च नात्र किञ्चिदपि ।

परिपूर्णे सत्यात्मनि किं नु क्षेत्रादिपर्यटनैः ॥"

इति । इह पुनरेतदुभयमपि नास्तीति प्रागेवोक्तम्, इत्याह -- "इत्यपि वर्णितम्"इति । तदुक्तम् --

"इह सर्वात्मकेकस्मात्तद्विधिप्रतिषेधने ।"(४ ।२५७)

इति ॥ २६० ॥
