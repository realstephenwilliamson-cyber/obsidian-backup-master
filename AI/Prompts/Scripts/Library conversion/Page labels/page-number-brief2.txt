READING PRINTED PAGE NUMBERS, v2 (28 Oct 2026, later session: adds the "also" field for pages printing two numbers; readers were also told that a scan may show two pages side by side, given as "4–5"). Reading task; you change nothing but your own output file.

Each image is one page of a scanned or digital book, cut down to two strips: the TOP of the page (above the red line) and the
BOTTOM of the page (below the red line). Book page numbers are normally printed in one of those strips: in the running head at the
top (left or right corner, or centred) or at the foot of the page.

For each image, say which page number is printed on the page, exactly as printed: arabic ("137") or roman ("xiv"). Rules:
- Only the page's own page number. Ignore footnote numbers, chapter or section numbers ("CHAPTER 7", "§ 4"), verse or line numbers,
  years, figure or plate numbers, numbers inside a running title, and numbers in the body text.
- Some pages print two different page numbers (one in the head, one at the foot). Then give the foot number in "printed" and
  the other in "also", with where it is in "note". Otherwise leave "also" empty.
- If the strips show no page number at all (a chapter opening, a blank page, a plate, a title page), answer "none".
- If you see a number but cannot read it for certain, answer "unclear" and say what you see in "note".
- Make sure each image has actually loaded before you answer. If an image does not load or is blank white, answer "noimage".
- Read each digit carefully (3 / 8, 1 / 7, 5 / 6, iii / m, vi / vii are easy to confuse); zoom in your mind on the number itself.

Output: a JSON Lines file at the path given in your task, one line per image, in the order listed:
{"id": "e03", "printed": "113", "also": "", "where": "top-right", "note": ""}
"where": top-left / top-centre / top-right / bottom-left / bottom-centre / bottom-right / "" (for none or noimage).
Check before finishing that there is one line per image and each parses as JSON. Reply with one short line of counts
(numbers read, none, unclear, noimage). Do not paste the readings into the reply.
