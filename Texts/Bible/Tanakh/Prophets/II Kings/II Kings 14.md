# II Kings 14

Compare: [[NRSVue 2 Kings 14|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 14

**14:1** In the second year of King Joash son of Joahaz of Israel, Amaziah son of King Joash of Judah became king. ^14-1

**14:2** He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem; his mother’s name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem. ^14-2

**14:3** He did what was pleasing to the LORD, but not like his ancestor David; he did just as his father Joash had done. ^14-3

**14:4** However, the shrines were not removed; the people continued to sacrifice and make offerings at the shrines. ^14-4

**14:5** Once he had the kingdom firmly in his grasp, he put to death the courtiers who had assassinated his father the king. ^14-5

**14:6** But he did not put to death the children of the assassins, in accordance with what is written in the Book of the Teaching of Moses, where the LORD commanded, “Parents shall not be put to death for children, nor children be put to death for parents; a person shall be put to death only for his own crime.”[^1] ^14-6

**14:7** He defeated ten thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt, and he captured Sela in battle and renamed it Joktheel, as is still the case. ^14-7

**14:8** Then Amaziah sent envoys to King Jehoash son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu of Israel, with this message: “Come, let us confront[^2] each other.” ^14-8

**14:9** King Jehoash of Israel sent back this message to King Amaziah of Judah: “The thistle in Lebanon sent this message to the cedar in Lebanon, ‘Give your daughter to my son in marriage.’ But a wild beast in Lebanon went by and trampled down the thistle. ^14-9

**14:10** Because you have defeated Edom, you have become arrogant. Stay home and enjoy your glory, rather than provoke disaster and fall, dragging Judah down with you.” ^14-10

**14:11** But Amaziah paid no heed; so King Jehoash of Israel advanced, and he and King Amaziah of Judah confronted each other at Beth-shemesh in Judah. ^14-11

**14:12** The Judites were routed by Israel, and they all fled to their homes. ^14-12

**14:13** King Jehoash of Israel captured King Amaziah son of Jehoash son of Ahaziah of Judah at Beth-shemesh. He marched on Jerusalem, and he made a breach of four hundred cubits in the wall of Jerusalem, from[^3] the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate. ^14-13

**14:14** He carried off all the gold and silver and all the vessels that there were in the House of the LORD and in the treasuries of the royal palace, as well as hostages; and he returned to Samaria. ^14-14

**14:15** The other events of Jehoash’s reign, and all his actions and exploits, and his war with King Amaziah of Judah, are recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Israel. ^14-15

**14:16** Jehoash slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel; his son Jeroboam succeeded him as king. ^14-16

**14:17** King Amaziah son of Joash of Judah lived fifteen years after the death of King Jehoash son of Jehoahaz of Israel. ^14-17

**14:18** The other events of Amaziah’s reign are recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Judah. ^14-18

**14:19** A conspiracy was formed against him in Jerusalem and he fled to Lachish; but they sent men after him to Lachish, and they killed him there. ^14-19

**14:20** They brought back his body on horses, and he was buried with his fathers in Jerusalem, in the City of David. ^14-20

**14:21** Then all the people of Judah took Azariah, who was sixteen years old, and proclaimed him king to succeed his father Amaziah. ^14-21

**14:22** It was he who rebuilt Elath and restored it to Judah, after King [Amaziah] slept with his fathers. ^14-22

**14:23** In the fifteenth year of King Amaziah son of Joash of Judah, King Jeroboam son of Joash of Israel became king in Samaria—for forty-one years. ^14-23

**14:24** He did what was displeasing to the LORD; he did not depart from all the sins that Jeroboam son of Nebat had caused Israel to commit. ^14-24

**14:25** It was he who restored the territory of Israel from Lebo-hamath to the sea of the Arabah, in accordance with the promise that the LORD, the God of Israel, had made through His servant, the prophet Jonah son of Amittai from Gath-hepher. ^14-25

**14:26** For the LORD saw the very bitter plight of Israel, with neither bond nor free[^4] left, and with none to help Israel. ^14-26

**14:27** And the LORD resolved not to blot out the name of Israel from under heaven; and he delivered them through Jeroboam son of Joash. ^14-27

**14:28** The other events of Jeroboam’s reign, and all his actions and exploits, how he fought and recovered Damascus and Hamath for Judah in Israel,[^5] are recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Israel. ^14-28

**14:29** Jeroboam slept with his fathers, the kings of Israel, and his son Zechariah succeeded him as king. ^14-29

[^1]: Deut. 24.16.
[^2]: I.e., in battle.
[^3]: Heb. “at.”
[^4]: See note at 1 Kings 14.10.
[^5]: Emendation yields “for Israel.”
