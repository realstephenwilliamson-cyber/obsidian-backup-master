# Colossians 4 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Colossians 03|← Colossians 3]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue Colossians 04|NRSVue]] · [[Colossians 04|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Colossians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-1|4.1]]:** *Treat your slaves justly and fairly*, requirements on the proper treatment of slaves in the Tanakh are based on two principles, that Israelite slaves are human beings who deserve humane treatment (generally speaking, the laws of [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-39|Lev 25.39–43]] and [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-12|Deut 15.12–18]]), and that slaves are property which must be handled with probity ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^21-20|Ex 21.20–27]]) ^4-1

### 4.2–6: Final admonitions

*[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-2|Text]], p. 417* ^4-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-2|2]]:** *Keeping alert*, Gk “grēgoreō,” appears throughout the NT as the proper attitude for prayer ([[Mark 13#^13-35|Mk 13.35]],[[Mark 13#^13-37|37]]; [[Acts 20#^20-31|Acts 20.31]]; 1 Cor 16.31; [[Revelation 16#^16-15|Rev 16.15]]; etc.). ^4-2-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-3|3]]:** *Door for the word*, used by Paul ([[1 Corinthians 16#^16-9|1 Cor 16.9]]; [[2 Corinthians 02#^2-12|2 Cor 2.12]]) for an opportunity to travel to proclaim the gospel. *Mystery*, see 1.26–27n.; here a synonym for “word of the Christ” (see 3.16). *Prison*, see 4.10. ^4-3

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-5|5]]:** *Making the most* (Gk “exagora-zomenoi”), a word from the realm of commerce (“agora,” “forum, marketplace”) meaning “buy back.” The phrase occurs only in the Deutero-Paulines (here and [[Ephesians 05#^5-16|Eph 5.16]]), whose audiences have been given an opportunity (see translators’ note *a*); they can better acquit themselves in the final judgment by behaving appropriately now. ^4-5

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Seasoned with salt*, making speech interesting or witty (Plutarch, *On Talkativeness* 514ef, who is not entirely enamored of the quality). ^4-6

### 4.7–18: Closing greetings

*[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-7|Text]], p. 417* · The author mentions names from Paul’s letters, especially Philemon and Acts. ^4-7

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-7|7]]:** *Tychicus*, see [[Acts 20#^20-4|Acts 20.4]]; [[Ephesians 06#^6-21|Eph 6.21]]; [[2 Timothy 04#^4-12|2 Tim 4.12]]; [[Titus 03#^3-12|Titus 3.12]]. *Fellow servant*, lit., “fellow-slave”; see 1.7n. ^4-7-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-9|9]]:** *Onesimus*, the subject of Philem. ^4-9

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-10|10]]:** *Aristarchus*, [[Acts 19#^19-29|Acts 19.29]]; [[Acts 20#^20-4|20.4]]; [[Acts 27#^27-2|27.2]], and Philem 24. *Fellow prisoner*, the term is borrowed from Paul, who uses it to describe Epaphras in Philem 23, and in [[Romans 16#^16-7|Rom 16.7]] of Andronicus and Junia. The author of Colossians maintains the fiction that he is Paul, imprisoned in Philippi. *Mark*, Philem 24; see also [[Acts 12#^12-12|Acts 12.12]],[[Acts 12#^12-25|25]]; perhaps 13.13; 15.37–39; possibly [[1 Peter 05#^5-13|1 Pet 5.13]]. *Cousin*, Acts closely associates Mark with Barnabas ([[Acts 12#^12-25|Acts 12.25]]; [[Acts 15#^15-37|15.37]],[[Acts 15#^15-39|39]]); the author of Colossians concludes that they were cousins. Christian tradition, via Papias of Hierapolis (second century), makes Mark Peter’s disciple and author of the Second Gospel (Eusebius, *Hist. Eccl*. 39.15). *Barnabas*, Paul’s mentor (see [[Acts 09#^9-27|Acts 9.27]]; [[Acts 11#^11-22|11.22]],[[Acts 11#^11-30|30]]; [[Acts 14#^14-12|14.12]]); both [[Acts 15#^15-36|Acts 15.36–39]] and [[Galatians 02#^2-13|Gal 2.13]] suggest he and Paul parted on poor terms; Colossians, with its theme of reconciliation, presents them in fellowship. ^4-10

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-11|11]]:** *Jesus . . . Justus*, double names (one Heb, one Gk or Lat) elsewhere in the NT: Saul *you are* (Heb “Shaʾul”) and Paul ([[Acts 13#^13-9|Acts 13.9]]); John (“Ioannēn/Yoanan”) and Mark (“Marcus”) ([[Acts 12#^12-12|Acts 12.12]]). *The circumcision*, Jewish members of the Jesus movement. Colossians distinguishes Jew from Greek, and circumcision from foreskin (cf. 2.11n.). ^4-11

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-12|12]]:** *Epaphras*, see 1.7n. In Philem 23, Epaphras is Paul’s “fellow prisoner”; here that distinction goes to Aristarchus. *Servant* (Gk “doulos”), slave. *Wrestling*, *mature*, see 1.28n. *Laodicea . . . Hierapolis*, cities near Colossae. ^4-12

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-14|14]]:** *Luke*, [[2 Timothy 04#^4-11|2 Tim 4.11]]; Philem 24. Only here in the NT is Luke identified as a physician. *Demas*, [[2 Timothy 04#^4-10|2 Tim 4.10]]; Philem 24. ^4-14

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-15|15]]:** *Nympha*, owner of the house church, site of local gathering and worship (see [[Romans 16#^16-5|Rom 16.5]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-9|1 Cor 16.9]]). Her name, meaning “bride,” sounds generic, but two Jewish women with this name lived in the fourth and the fifth centuries ce Sicily (*CIJ* 651–2). ^4-15

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-16|16]]:** *Letter from Laodicea*, this letter is not known, but might be Eph. ^4-16

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-17|17]]:** *Archippus*, in Philem 2, Paul called Archippus “fellow soldier.” Colossians makes him the recipient of a private message, “See that you complete the task. . . .” ^4-17

**[[NRSVue Colossians 04#^4-18|18]]:** The author, adding verisimilitude, includes Paul’s fictive signature (cf. [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-21|1 Cor 16.21]]; [[Galatians 06#^6-11|Gal 6.11]]; [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-17|2 Thess 3.17–18]]). *Chains*, or “imprisonment,” cf. [[Philippians 01#^1-7|Phil 1.7–14]] and Col 4.3n.,10n. *Grace be with you*, the ending of the letter is truncated as compared to Paul’s own letters, but all of Paul’s letters, the Deutero-Paulines, and the Pastorals end with this phrase. ^4-18
