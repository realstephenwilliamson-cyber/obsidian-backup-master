## Mūla

ज्वालादिलिङ्गं चान्यस्य कपालादि तु मे निजम् ।
आदिशब्दात्तपश्चर्यावेलातिथ्यादि कथ्यते ॥ ^mula

jvālādiliṅgaṃ cānyasya kapālādi tu me nijam |
ādiśabdāttapaścaryāvelātithyādi kathyate || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 11 - Chapter Four, part 10#^pg383|p. 370]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=383>))
>
> (Similarly, members of a Kula and others distinguish between their own deities and those of others, reflecting that) “that (iconic) form (rūpa) belongs to another (school), whereas this is my own, upon which I should meditate.” “A trident and the like (are the) sectarian marks (liṅga) of a different (tradition), whereas the skull and the like are (those of) my own.” (with 4.263cd–264ab)
>
> The expression (in the Mālinīvijayottara) ‘and the rest’ (refers to other similar matters that set its teaching apart from that of others, such as the practice of) austerity (tapas), observances (caryā), (auspicious) times of the day (velā), the lunar days (when special rites are performed) and so forth (that vary in differing schools and so are all rejected as relative). (with 4.264cd)
