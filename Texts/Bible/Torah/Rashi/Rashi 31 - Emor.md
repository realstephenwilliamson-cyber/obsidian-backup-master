# Rashi 31 - Emor

← [[Rashi 30 - Kedoshim]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 32 - Behar]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-1|Leviticus 21:1–24:23]]* · Text: [[Torah 31 - Emor]]

## Chapter 21

### 21:1

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-1|Leviticus 21:1]]

**Speak to the kohanim.** Say, and you shall say — to warn the adults regarding the minors.

**The sons of Aharon.** Perhaps those unfit [are also included in this prohibition]? The verse says:

**The kohanim. The sons of Aharon —** even those who have a [physical] blemish are implied.

**The sons of Aharon,** and not the daughters of Aharon.

**Let him not defile himself among his people,** while the dead body is still “among his people,” excluding a *mes mitzvoh*.

### 21:2

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-2|Leviticus 21:2]]

**Except for his kin.** “His kin” [refers] to none other than his wife.

### 21:3

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-3|Leviticus 21:3]]

**To whom he is closely related —** to include the betrothed [sister].

**Who has not yet been married,** [referring to the marriage’s] consummation.

**For her, he may be defiled.** [It is] a [positive] command.

### 21:4

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-4|Leviticus 21:4]]

**A husband may not defile himself among his people to become profaned.** He may not defile himself for an illegitimate wife, through whom he becomes unfit [for the *kehunoh*], while she is still with him. And thus the plain meaning of the verse is [as follows]: “A husband may not defile himself” — “with his flesh,” while [the corpse is] yet “among his people” — when there are [people available who can] bury her, [that is,] she is not a *mes mitzvoh*. And regarding what flesh [= wife] did I speak? Regarding one [through] which “he becomes profaned” — to become unfit from his *kehunoh*.

### 21:5

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-5|Leviticus 21:5]]

**They shall not make baldness** for a deceased person. Were not Israelites also prohibited such [conduct]? However, because it was stated regarding Israelites: “[You shall not make any baldness] ’between your eyes’ [for the dead],” Perhaps he is not liable [for plucking out hair] on [any part] of the head [other than “between the eyes”]? The verse says: “on their head” — and [the law of] Israelites is derived from [the law] of *kohanim* by [means of] a *gezeroh shovoh*: “baldness” (קָרְחָה) is mentioned here and “baldness’ (קָרְחָה) is mentioned regarding Israelites — just as here [any part of] the entire head [is implied], so too further on the entire head is implied, wherever he makes a bald spot on the head. And just as further on [the prohibition involves mourning] for a deceased person, so, here [mourning] for a deceased person [is implied].

**And the corners of their beards they shall not shave.** Because regarding an Israelite it is said, “you shall not destroy [by shaving the corner of your beard],” perhaps for plucking with a tweezer or with plane-like tools he is liable? Therefore it is said, “they shall not shave” — he is liable only for something which is called “shaving” and which has in it “destructiveness,” and that [is accomplished only with] a razor.

**They shall not make gashes in their flesh.** Because it is said regarding an [ordinary] Israelite, “A gash for the dead shall you not make,” perhaps [this means that if he] made five gashes, he is liable for only one? The verse says: “They shall not make gashes” — to make him liable for each and every gash. for this word [= “gashes”] is superfluous [and intended] for midrashic exposition, for it should have been written: “They shall not gash,” and I would know that [the verb “gash” refers to] gashes.

### 21:6

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-6|Leviticus 21:6]]

**They shall be holy.** The court shall sanctify them against their will in this.

### 21:7

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-7|Leviticus 21:7]]

**A harlot,** [A woman] who has had sexual relations with an Israelite who is forbidden for her to marry, under the penalty of *koreis* [for doing so], or a נָתִין, or a מַמְזֵר .

**A profaned woman,** [that is, a woman] who was born of a marriage [between a *kohein*] and one [forbidden] to the *kehunoh*, as for example the daughter of a widow [illegally married] to a *Kohein Godol*, or the daughter of a divorced woman [illegally married] to an ordinary *kohein*. So too one who became invalid [for intermarriage] with the *kehunoh* because she had sexual relations in one of the marriages forbidden to a *kohein*.

### 21:8

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-8|Leviticus 21:8]]

**You shall make him holy —** [even] against his will, for if he does not wish to divorce [his illegal wife], beat him and punish him until he does divorce [her].

**He shall be holy unto you,** treat him with sanctity, to take precedence in all matters [of sanctity], and to bless first at a meal.

### 21:9

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-9|Leviticus 21:9]]

**Who profanes herself by fornication,** when she becomes profaned through fornication, as when she had [some] marital tie with a husband and fornicated, whether [this tie was through] betrothal or marriage. Our Rabbis disagreed about the matter, [but] all agree that the verse does not speak regarding a free woman.

**She profanes her father.** She has profaned and degraded his honor, for they say of him: “Cursed be he that he caused this one to be born; cursed be he that he raised this one”!

### 21:10

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-10|Leviticus 21:10]]

**Shall not let [his hair] grow wild,** it shall not grow wild in mourning. And what is [the measure] of “growing wild”? More than thirty days.

### 21:11

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-11|Leviticus 21:11]]

**And adjacent to any dead body, etc.** [That is,] in[to] a tent [which is over a] dead body.

**Dead body** [literally, “souls of a dead body”] — to include a quarter [of a לוֹג] of blood from a dead body, which causes impurity in a “tent.”

**For his father and his mother he shall not become [ritually] impure.** This comes only to permit him [to become impure] for a *mes mitzvoh*.

### 21:12

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-12|Leviticus 21:12]]

**He shall not depart from the Sanctuary.** He does not follow the coffin. Furthermore, our Rabbis learned from here that a *Kohein Godol* may offer [a sacrifice] while he is an *onein*, and this is its meaning: Even if his father and his mother died, he is not required to leave the Sanctuary, but performs the Temple-service.

**And he shall not profane. . .the Sanctuary,** He does not profane the Temple-service with this, since Scripture permitted him [to remain there]; thus, [we learn,] that an ordinary *kohein* who performs [the Temple-service] while he is an *onein does* profane [it].

### 21:14

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-14|Leviticus 21:14]]

**And a profaned woman,** born of [a marriage] forbidden to the *kehunoh*.

### 21:15

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-15|Leviticus 21:15]]

**He shall not profane his offspring —** thus, if he marries one [of those] women invalid [for marriage with *kohanim*], his offspring from her is profaned from the status of the holiness of the *kehunoh*.

### 21:17

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-17|Leviticus 21:17]]

**The bread of his God,** “the food” of his God. Every meal is called “food,” as [in the verse] “he made a great feast.”

### 21:18

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-18|Leviticus 21:18]]

**For any man who has a blemish shall not approach.** It is not proper that he approach; [the word “approach”] is like [that in the verse] “[If you offer the lame or sick, is it not evil?] Offer it now to your governor; [will he be pleased with you?].”

**A man with a sunken nose,** [one] whose nose is sunken between the two eyes, [so that] he can paint both his eyes with one stroke.

**A man who has disproportionate limbs,** [one who has] one limb larger than the other — one eye large and one eye small, or one thigh is longer than the other.

### 21:20

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-20|Leviticus 21:20]]

**One with overhanging eyebrows.** *Sorzilos* in Old French, [denoting one whose] eyebrows — their hair is long and lies [over his face].

**Or with a cataract,** who has a membrane in his eyes, which is called *toyle* [in Old French], similar [to the use of the word in the verse]: “Who spreads out [the heavens] like a membrane.”

**Or a disarrangement [in the eye],** something which confuses the eye, such as a white streak which stretches from the white [of the eye] and intersects the iris — the circle which surrounds the black [of the eye], [that] which is called *prunelle* in Old French. This streak intersects the circle and enters the black [of the eye]. The Targum of תְּבַלוּל is חִילִּיז, a word [related to] חִלָּזוֹן, for that streak is similar to a worm, and so the Sages of Israel termed it, [when describing] the blemishes [which can befall] a firstling: worm, snake, wart.

**Scurf [= gorov] or scabs [= yallefes].** These are types of boils. “Scurf” is [what is called elsewhere] *cheres*, a boil [which is] dry within and without [on the surface]; “scab” is the Egyptian lichen. And why is it called *yallefes*? Because it continues to cling [to the body] until the day of death, and it is moist without and dry within, and in another place *gorov* is called *shechin* — moist without and dry within, as it is said, “and with the scab [= *gorov*] and with the itch [= *cheres*].” When [the word] “scurf” [= *gorov*] [appears] near *cheres*, [then] “scab” [= *yallefes*] is called “scurf” [= *gorov*], [but] when it [= *gorov*] [appears] near “scab” [= *yallefes*], *cheres* is called “scurf” [= *gorov*] — so is it explained in Bechoros.

**Or one with crushed testicles,** [which is,] according to the Targum: מְרֵס פַּחֲדִין, whose פַּחֲדִין are מְרוּסָסִין — his testicles are crushed. [The word] פַּחֲדִין is related [to its meaning in the verse]: “All the sinews of his testicles are knit together.”

### 21:21

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-21|Leviticus 21:21]]

**Any man who has a blemish —** [this comes] to include other blemishes.

**He has a blemish,** while “he has a blemish,” he is unfit; therefore, if the blemish passes, he is fit.

**The bread of his God,** every food is called “bread.”

### 21:22

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-22|Leviticus 21:22]]

**Of the most holy [sacrifices] —** these are the most holy sacrifices.

**And from the holy, he may eat.** These are the sacrifices of lesser sanctity. If sacrifices of greater sanctity are mentioned, why are sacrifices of lesser sanctity [also] mentioned? If it had not been said, I would have thought: A blemished person may eat of the most holy sacrifices, since we find that they were permitted to a non-*kohein*, [as when] Moshe ate the flesh of the installation-sacrifices. But [I might think] that of the breast and thigh of sacrifices of lesser sanctity he may not eat, since we do not find that a non-*kohein* had a part in them — therefore sacrifices of lesser sanctity are mentioned. So is it explained in [the Maseches of] Zevochim.

### 21:23

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-23|Leviticus 21:23]]

**However, [he may not enter] beyond the curtain,** to sprinkle seven sprinklings on the curtain.

**And to the altar,** the outer [one]. And both [the curtain and the altar] had to be written, and [the reason] is explained in Toras Kohanim.

**He shall not profane My holy things.** For if he did perform [the service], his service is profaned and becomes invalid.

### 21:24

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-24|Leviticus 21:24]]

**Moshe spoke** this *mitzvah*.

**To Aharon and to his sons and to all of Bnei Yisroel —** to warn the court regarding the *kohanim*.

## Chapter 22

### 22:2

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-2|Leviticus 22:2]]

**And let them keep away,** [The word] נְזִירָה refers to “separation,” and so too [the verse] says: “And he separates himself from following Me,” “They are gone away backward,” [thus implying here:] they shall separate [themselves] from holy things in the days of their impurity. And let them keep away from the holy offerings of Bnei Yisroel, which they sanctify for Me, and not profane My Holy Name. Reverse the verse and expound it.

**Which they sanctify for Me,** [this redundancy comes] to include the holy offerings of the *kohanim* themselves.

### 22:3

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-3|Leviticus 22:3]]

**Any person who shall draw near.** This [expression of] “drawing near” refers to eating, and so we have found that the prohibition of eating holy flesh while [in a state of ritual] uncleanness is expressed by [a word implying] “touching”: “Anything holy she shall not touch” — which is a prohibition for one who eats [the flesh], and our Rabbis derived it from a *gezeroh shovoh*. And it is impossible to say that he is liable for touching, since *koreis* is mentioned regarding eating in [the *Sidroh*] of *Tzav es Aharon:* Two *kerisos* are [mentioned] next to one another, and if he is liable for touching, it would not have been necessary to make him liable for eating± so is it expounded in Toras Kohanim: Is then a *kohein* who [merely] touches [sacrificial flesh when he is unclean] liable [to *koreis*]? If so, why does the verse say: “who draws near” [rather than “who eats”]? [It is said in order to teach that] once it is fit to be offered [= brought near], they [= those who eat it] are not liable for uncleanness unless that which makes it permissible is offered. And if you say: Why are three *kerisos* regarding impurity of *kohanim*? [They] were already expounded in Maseches Shevu’os: One is [to teach] a general rule [regarding all sacrifices], one [teaches the rule regarding] a particular case, etc. [and one to teach regarding a sliding-scale sacrifice, which is recorded only to prescribe atonement for the uncleanness of the Temple and its holy food].

**While his impurity is on him —** and the impurity of a *human being* is on him. Perhaps the verse speaks of the impurity of flesh — “and the impurity of the [sacrificial] flesh is on it”? — And regarding a clean [*kohein*] who ate unclean [flesh], does scripture speak? [No.] You are forced to learn from what is implied in it. Of one whose impurity passes away from him, does the verse speak. And this refers to a *human being* whose purification is by immersion.

**Shall be cut off.** Perhaps [he shall be uprooted] from this side [of the world] to the [other] side [of the world]? [Perhaps] he will be cut off [= exiled] from his [native] place and be settled in another place? The verse says: **“I am Adonoy” —** [he will be cut off] from wherever I am.

### 22:4

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-4|Leviticus 22:4]]

**Anything defiled by the dead,** [referring] to one who becomes [ritually] impure by [contact] with the dead.

### 22:5

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-5|Leviticus 22:5]]

**Any creeping creature that causes impurity to him,** in the minimum size which is capable of causing impurity, [that is, the size of] a lentil.

**Or any person,** a dead body.

**Who causes impurity to him,** in [the] minimum size to cause impurity, and that is [the volume of] an olive’s-bulk.

**[With] whatever impurity he has,** this includes one who touches a *zov*, or a *zovoh*, or a *niddoh*, or a woman who has just given birth.

### 22:6

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-6|Leviticus 22:6]]

**Anyone touching [that person] —** any of these [ritually] unclean [persons].

### 22:7

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-7|Leviticus 22:7]]

**And afterwards, he may eat of the holy things.** [This] is expounded in [Maseches] Yevomos [as concerning] *terumoh*, which [*kohanim*] are permitted to eat at sunset.

**Of the holy things.** but not *all* the holy things.

### 22:8

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-8|Leviticus 22:8]]

**An animal which has died on its own, or an animal that was fatally maimed, he must not eat to defile himself with it.** [This verse] warns regarding the matter of uncleanness. That if he ate the carcass of a [ritually] clean bird, which has no [rule of] uncleanness of touch and carrying, except for the uncleanness [caused by] eating as soon as it is in the gullet — he is forbidden to eat holy things. [Furthermore,] it was necessary to state *u’trefah*, [to indicate that the word *neveiloh*] [refers to] an animal whose species includes a *trefah* — excluding the carcass of an *unclean* bird which does not include a *trefah* within its species.

### 22:9

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-9|Leviticus 22:9]]

**They shall keep My charge** not to eat *terumoh* while [in a state of] bodily uncleanness.

**And die because of it.** This has taught us that [violation of this prohibition] is [punishable] by death at the hands of Heaven.

### 22:10

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-10|Leviticus 22:10]]

**No stranger (non-kohein) may eat holy things.** The verse speaks of *terumoh*, for the whole passage speaks of it.

**One indentured to a kohein or one hired —** [that is,] the indentured servant of a *kohein* or his hired hand. That is why [the word] “ תּוֹשַׁב “ is vocalized with a פַּתַּח, because it is connected [to the following word]. And what is a תּוֹשָׁב ? This is [a Hebrew servant] who [has had his ear] pierced, who belongs [to his master] until the Jubilee year. And what is a שָׂכִיר ? One who belongs [to his master] for [a period] of years, who leaves [his master’s service] in six years. The verse has come and taught you here, that he does not belong bodily to his master [which would allow him to] eat *terumoh*.

### 22:11

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-11|Leviticus 22:11]]

**If a kohein shall purchase a person [as a slave],** a Canaanite slave who belongs bodily [to his master].

**And the [slave] born in his house,** these are the children of the maidservants. [Furthermore, the law that] the wife of a *kohein* may eat *terumoh* [is derived] from this verse, for she too is an acquisition obtained with his money. and [this law] is also learned from another verse: “Every [ritually] clean person in your house [may eat of it]” — in Sifrei.

### 22:12

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-12|Leviticus 22:12]]

**To a non-*kohein*,** [that is,] to a Levite or an Israelite.

### 22:13

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-13|Leviticus 22:13]]

**Widowed or divorced —** from the non-*kohein*.

**And she has no children** from him.

**And she returns —** therefore, if she has children from him, she is forbidden [to eat] *terumoh* as long as the children live.

**No non-kohein may eat of it,** [this repetition] comes only to exclude an *onein* [from the prohibition] — for he is permitted [to eat] *terumoh*. I said to you: A non-*kohein* [is forbidden to eat *terumoh*], but not an *onein*.

### 22:14

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-14|Leviticus 22:14]]

**If a man eats of holy things —** *terumah*.

**And give to the kohein the holy thing.** Something which is fit to become holy — that he not pay him in coin, but in common [i.e., non-holy] produce, which becomes *terumoh*.

### 22:15

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-15|Leviticus 22:15]]

**They shall not profane, etc.** To feed them to strangers.

### 22:16

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-16|Leviticus 22:16]]

**They burden them[selves]** [they] themselves will bear the sin — **when they eat their holy things,** that were separated for the sake of *terumoh* and became holy and were forbidden to them [= the strangers]. And Onkelos who translated “in their eating [of it] in impurity,” translated it thus unnecessarily.

**They burden them[selves].** This is one of three [mentions of the word] *es* in the Torah which R. Yishmo’el would expound [as though the verses] were speaking of the person himself; and so [too]: “on the day in which the time of his *nezirus* is fulfilled, he shall bring him” — he shall bring himself; and so [too]: “and he buried him in the valley” — he buried himself. So is it expounded in Sifre.

### 22:18

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-18|Leviticus 22:18]]

**Vows —** [he says:] “I am obligated [to bring this sacrifice].”

**Free-will-offerings,** [he says:] “Behold, this [I will bring].”

### 22:19

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-19|Leviticus 22:19]]

**To gain acceptance —** bring something fit to ingratiate yourselves before Me, that [the offering] should be acceptable for you, *apayement* in Old French. And what is fitting for acceptance?

**An unblemished male of cattle, sheep or goats.** But a bird burnt-offering does not require an unblemished state and masculinity, and is not invalidated by a blemish, but [rather] by loss of a limb.

### 22:21

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-21|Leviticus 22:21]]

**To fulfill an articulated vow,** to express [clearly] by his speech.

### 22:22

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-22|Leviticus 22:22]]

**Blind,** [this is] the noun [denoting] the blemish, “blindness” in a feminine form. It shall not have the blemish of blindness.

**Or broken-limbed** it shall not be.

**Or cracked,** an eye-lid which is split or nipped, and so [too if] its lip is split or nipped.

**Wart.** *Verue* in Old French.

**Scurf,** a type of lichen and so [too] scab, and the expression of “scab” is like [= related to] “And Samson took hold [of the two middle pillars].” [It is so called because] it keeps its hold on him [= the sick peron] to the day of his death, for it has no cure.

**You shall not bring —** [this is mentioned] three times, [once] to forbid its designation [as an offering], [once to forbid] its slaughter, [and once to forbid] the sprinkling of its blood.

**And as a fire-offering you shall not place —** a prohibition [against] burning them [on the altar].

### 22:23

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-23|Leviticus 22:23]]

**Soru’o.** [where] one limb is larger than the other.

**Or kolut.** [an animal] whose hooves are uncloven.

**You shall make it as a free-will-offering —** for the repair of the Temple.

**[But] for [the fulfillment of] a vow —** for the altar,

**it will not be favorably accepted.** What dedicated thing comes to gain acceptance? Say: this is the dedicated [animal intended] for the altar.

### 22:24

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-24|Leviticus 22:24]]

**And crushed, mangled, torn or cut off —** [referring to the animal’s] testicles or organ.

**Crushed,** its testicles are crushed by hand.

**Mangled,** cut off to a greater extent [than the cutting described] by “crushed.”

**Torn,** torn by hand until the threads by which they hang are separated, but they are still in the pouch, and the pouch is not cut off.

**Cut off,** cut off with an instrument, but they are still in the pouch.

**And crushed,** its Targum is “and which is bruised,” this is its meaning in Aramaic — an expression of “crushing.”

**And mangled,** its Targum is “and which is smashed,” as [in the verse]: “The great house shall be smitten into fragments” — into small pieces, and so [too in the Talmudic expression]: “a reed broken into small pieces.”

**In your land shall you not do** this thing, to castrate any animal or beast, even a [ritually] unclean one — therefore it is said, “in your land,” to include all that is in your land, for it is impossible to say that they were commanded regarding castration only in the land [of Israel], for [the prohibition of] castration is an obligation [pertaining to] the person, and every obligation [pertaining to] the person applies both in the land and outside the land.

### 22:25

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-25|Leviticus 22:25]]

**From a stranger’s hand [you shall not bring],** that he brought a sacrifice by the hand of a *kohein*, to offer it to Heaven. **You shall not bring** on his behalf a blemished [animal]. And even though blemished [animals] were not forbidden as an offering from the sons of Noach, except if they were missing a limb, this applies only to altars in the fields, but [animals brought] on the altar of the Tabernacle you should not bring, though unblemished [offerings] you may accept from them. Therefore it was said above: “Any man” — to include non-Jews who may make vows and bring free-will-offerings as [do] Israelites.

**Wounds,** wounds, (quoting Targum).

**They will not be favorably accepted for you,** to atone for you.

### 22:27

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-27|Leviticus 22:27]]

**When it is born,** excluding a Caesarian section.

### 22:28

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-28|Leviticus 22:28]]

**It and its offspring.** [This rule] applies to a female [animal], for it is forbidden to slaughter the mother [together with] the son or daughter [on the same day]. It does not apply to male [animals], and it is permitted to slaughter the father and the son.

**It and its offspring,** even its offspring and it are implied [in the expression].

### 22:29

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-29|Leviticus 22:29]]

**That it be favorably accepted for you shall you sacrifice it.** [That is, at] the outset of its slaughtering, be careful that it should bring acceptance for you, and what is it that assures its acceptance?

### 22:30

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-30|Leviticus 22:30]]

**On that day [that it is sacrificed] it may be eaten.** This comes to warn that the slaughtering should be for this purpose — do not slaughter it in order to eat it tomorrow, for if you have invalidating thoughts, it will not be acceptable for you. Another interpretation: **That it be favorably accepted for you —** consciously; from here [we learn] that one who is preoccupied [and in this state slaughters an offering], it is invalid in regard to the slaughter of holy offerings. And even though it detailed [this rule] in regard to [sacrifices] eaten for two days [and the night between], [the verse] again detailed [it here], regarding [those] eaten for one day — that their slaughter should be with the intention to eat them in their [proper] time. **On that day [that it is sacrificed] you should eat it.** This comes only to warn that the slaughter should be for this purpose, for if [it comes] to set a time of eating, [that] was already written: “The flesh of his peace-offering for thanksgiving [shall be eaten on the day of his offering].”

**I am Adonoy.** Know Who decrees [this] thing, and let it not be trivial in your eyes.

### 22:31

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-31|Leviticus 22:31]]

**You shall preserve —** this [refers] to study.

**And fulfill —** this [refers] to action.

### 22:32

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-32|Leviticus 22:32]]

**You shall not profane,** to transgress My words intentionally. Since it is said, “and you shall not profane,” why does the verse say, “and I will be sanctified”? Surrender yourself [to martyrdom] and sanctify My Name! Perhaps [this command applies even when] he is alone? The verse says: **Among Bnei Yisroel.** And when he surrenders himself, he should surrender himself in order to die, for whoever surrenders himself [to martyrdom] for the sake of a miracle, a miracle is not performed for him. For so we find in [the case of] Chananiyoh, Misho’el and Azariyoh, who did not surrender themselves for the sake of a miracle, as it is said, “But if not [= if He does not save us], be it known to you, o King, [that we will not serve your gods], etc.” — whether He saves [us] or whether he does not save [us], it should be known to you, etc. [= that we will not serve your idols]

### 22:33

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-33|Leviticus 22:33]]

**Who brought you out —** for this purpose.

**I am Adonoy,** faithful to give reward.

## Chapter 23

### 23:2

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-2|Leviticus 23:2]]

**Speak to Bnei Yisroel, [and say to them], the appointed times.** Regulate the appointed times so that [the] Israel[ites] should become practiced in them. [We learn from this] that [the Sanhedrin] proclaims a leap year for those living in the Exile who have left their homes to ascend [to Jerusalem] for the festival, [but] have not yet reached Jerusalem.

### 23:3

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-3|Leviticus 23:3]]

**Six days.** What is the connection between Shabbos and the festivals? To teach you that whoever profanes the festivals is considered as though he profaned the *Shabbasos*, and whoever keeps the festivals is considered as though he kept the *Shabbasos*.

### 23:4

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-4|Leviticus 23:4]]

**These are the appointed times of Adonoy.** Above [the verse] speaks of proclaiming a leap year, and here it speaks of sanctification of the month.

### 23:5

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-5|Leviticus 23:5]]

**In the afternoon,** from six hours onward.

**A Pesach (offering) for Adonoy,** [referring] to bringing the sacrifice whose name is Pesach.

### 23:8

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-8|Leviticus 23:8]]

**You shall bring a fire-offering, etc.** These are the additional sacrifices which are mentioned in Parshas Pinchos. And why are they mentioned here? To say that the[se] additional sacrifices do not impede one another, [since it states]:

**You shall bring a fire-offering to Adonoy,** [bring it] under any circumstance if there are no oxen [available], bring rams, and if there are no oxen or rams, bring lambs.

**[On each of] seven days.** Wherever שִׁבְעַת, (“seven”) is said, it is a noun — a week of days, *seteyne* in Old French, and so too every expression of “eight of,” “six of.” “five of,” “three of.”

**You shall not do any work of labor.** Even that work which is considered as necessary labor, which cause[s] monetary loss by its neglect, such as an opportunity which is lost. So have I understood from Toras Kohanim, which teaches: “Perhaps even [during] the intermediate days of the festival the work of labor should be forbidden? Etc.

### 23:10

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-10|Leviticus 23:10]]

**First fruits of your harvest —** that it should be the first of the harvest.

**An *omer*,** a tenth of an *ephoh* — such was its name, as: “[when] they measured [it] by the *omer*.”

### 23:11

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-11|Leviticus 23:11]]

**He shall wave.** Every waving [denotes that] he moves [it] horizontally away from him and toward him, vertically upwards and downwards; horizontally away from him and toward him — to prevent destructive winds, [and] vertically upwards and downwards — to prevent harmful dews.

**Be favorably accepted for you.** If you bring [it] according to this rule, it will be favorably accepted for you.

**On the day after the day of rest,** the day after the first festival day of Pesach, for if you say that [this refers] to the Shabbos of Creation, you [cannot] know which [Shabbos] is meant.

### 23:12

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-12|Leviticus 23:12]]

**You shall prepare. . .a lamb,** [which] comes as an obligation in connection with the *omer* [meal-offering].

### 23:13

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-13|Leviticus 23:13]]

**Its meal-offering,** the meal-offering [which accompanies] its wine-offering.

**Two tenths —** it was double.

**Its wine-offering is one fourth of a *hin*.** Even though its meal-offering was double, its wine-offering was not double.

### 23:14

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-14|Leviticus 23:14]]

**Parched grain,** flour made from tender grain, which is dried in an oven.

**Tender grain,** those are the parched grains, which are called *greneds* [in Old French].

**In all your dwelling places.** The Sages of Israel disagreed about this. Some learned from this that [the prohibition] of חָדָשׁ applies outside the land [of Israel], and some say that this comes only to teach [us] that we were not commanded regarding חָדָשׁ until after taking possession and settling [the land], after conquering and dividing [it among the tribes].

### 23:15

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-15|Leviticus 23:15]]

**From the day after the day of rest,** the day after the festival.

**Seven complete weeks shall they be.** [This] teaches that one should begin [to] count at night, for if not so, they [= the seven weeks] are not complete.

### 23:16

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-16|Leviticus 23:16]]

**The seventh week,** as its Targum: “the seventh week.”

**Until the day after the seventh week, you shall count [fifty days].** But not including [the day] until which you count — and those are the forty-nine days [referred to].

**Fifty days, and you shall bring a new meal-offering for Adonoy.** Bring it on the fiftieth day. I say this is its Midrash, but its plain meaning is: “Until the day after the seventh week — which is the fiftieth day — you should count” [and then bring the offering]. [This] is an inverted verse.

**A new meal-offering.** “That is the first meal-offering which is brought from *chodosh*, and if you say: “But the *omer* meal-offering has [already] been brought”! [The *omer* meal-offering] is not like other meal-offerings, for it comes from barley.

### 23:17

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-17|Leviticus 23:17]]

**From your dwelling places —** and not from outside the land [of Israel].

**Bread wave-offering,** the bread [to be] raised, which is raised [= offered] for the Most High, and this is the “new meal-offering” mentioned above.

**First fruit-offering,** first of all the meal-offerings — even of the “jealousy” meal-offering which comes from barley, may not be brought from *chodosh* before the “two breads.”

### 23:18

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-18|Leviticus 23:18]]

**Along with the bread,** for the sake of the bread, an obligation of the bread.

**With their meal-offerings and wine-offerings,** according to the rule of meal-offerings and wine-offerings explained regarding every animal [sacrifice] in the *poroshoh* of wine-offerings: three tenths for each ox and two tenths for each ram, and a tenth for each lamb — this is the meal-offering. [As for] the wine-offerings: half a *hin* for each ox, a third of a *hin* for each ram, and a fourth of a *hin* for each lamb.

### 23:19

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-19|Leviticus 23:19]]

**You shall prepare one he-goat.** Perhaps the seven lambs and the he-goat mentioned here are identical with the seven lambs and he-goat mentioned in חוּמַּשׁ הַפְּקוּדִים ? When you reach the [listing] of oxen and rams, [you see] that it is not so. You must conclude from this [that] these are [a group] unto themselves and these are [a group] unto themselves. These are brought for the sake of the bread, and these are for the additional sacrifices.

### 23:20

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-20|Leviticus 23:20]]

**The kohein shall wave them, a wave-offering.** [This] teaches that they require waving [while] alive. Perhaps all of them? The verse says: “with the two lambs.”

**They shall be holy.** Because peace-offerings brought by an individual [are classified] as holy things of a lesser degree, it was necessary to state of communal peace-offerings, that they are holy things of a greater degree.

### 23:22

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-22|Leviticus 23:22]]

**When you reap.** [The verse] repeats [the prohibition] so that a transgressor violates two prohibitions. Said R. Avdimi son of R. Yosef: Why did Scripture place it [= this agricultural prohibition] in the midst of the festival [laws], Pesach and Shevu’os on one side, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, and Sukkos on the other? [In order] to teach you that whoever gives gleanings, the forgotten sheaf and the corner of the field to the poor person [in a] proper [manner], is considered as though he built the Temple and brought his sacrifices within it.

**You shall leave,** leave [it] before them and they will gather [it], and you must not help any one of them [in preference to anyone else].

**I am Adonoy, your God,** faithful to give a reward.

### 23:24

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-24|Leviticus 23:24]]

**A remembrance of the sounding [of the shofor].** A mention of Biblical verses [which relate] to [Divine] Remembrances and Biblical verses [which relate to the] *shofor* — to remember for your sake the binding of Yitzchok for whom a ram was brought in his stead.

### 23:25

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-25|Leviticus 23:25]]

**You shall bring a fire-offering,** the additional offerings mentioned in חוּמַשׁ הַפְּקוּדִים .

### 23:27

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-27|Leviticus 23:27]]

**Indeed.** Every time “however” and “indeed (except),” [are mentioned] in the Torah [they represent] a limitation. [Here, the limitation is this:] [Yom Kippur] atones for those who repent, but does not atone for those who do not repent.

### 23:30

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-30|Leviticus 23:30]]

**I will cause [that person] to perish.** Because [the verse] says “cutting off” everywhere [else], and I do not know what that is, [here] when it says, “I will cause [that person] to perish” it teaches that *koreis* is nothing other than destruction.

### 23:31

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-31|Leviticus 23:31]]

**Any work, etc.** [This is repeated to teach that one who does work on Yom Kippur] transgresses many prohibitions, or to [teach that] work at night is prohibited, just as working during the day [is prohibited].

### 23:35

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-35|Leviticus 23:35]]

**A holy assembly.** Sanctify it with fine clothing and with prayer, and in regard to other festivals, [sanctify them] with food, drink, fine clothing and prayer.

### 23:36

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-36|Leviticus 23:36]]

**It is [a day] of convocation.** I have kept you with me, like a king who invited his children for a feast for a certain number of days, when the time came for them to leave, he said: “My children, I beg of you, remain with me one more day; your leaving is difficult for me.”

**Any work of labor,** even work which is labor for yourselves, that if you do not do it, there will be a loss [to you] in the matter —

**You shall not do.** Perhaps [this applies to] the intermediate days of the festival, that [all] work or labor should be forbidden? The verse says: “It [is a day of convocation].”

### 23:37

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-37|Leviticus 23:37]]

**A burnt-offering and a meal-offering,** a meal-offering [which accompanies] the wine-offering which is brought with the burnt-offering.

**Each on its assigned day,** a quantity fixed in Chumash Hapekudim.

**Each on its assigned day —** therefore, if the day has passed, the sacrifice is cancelled.

### 23:39

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-39|Leviticus 23:39]]

**Indeed, on the fifteenth day, you shall celebrate —** [bringing] a peace-offering [as] sacrifice for the pilgrim’s sacrifice. Perhaps this [sacrifice] supersedes Shabbos? The verse says: “However,” [which is a limitation]; since it has [a supplementary period] for repayment all seven [days of the festival].

**When you gather the produce of the land —** that this seventh month should come at the time of harvest. From here [we learn] that they were commanded to intercalate the years, for if it were not for this addition of a month, at times [Sukkos would fall] during the middle of the summer or the winter.

**Beautiful,** which remain on its tree from year to year — and this is the אֶתְרג [= citron].

**A branch of palm trees.** [Since the word כַּפּוֹת, “branches” appears] without a *vov*, this teaches that there is only one [palm branch].

**Boughs of thick-leaved trees,** whose branches are intertwined like ropes and like cords, and this is the הֲדַס [= myrtle] which is shaped like a braid.

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-42|Leviticus 23:42]]

## Chapter 24

### 24:2

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-2|Leviticus 24:2]]

**Command Bnei Yisroel.** This is the section concerning the *mitzvoh* of the [Tabernacle] lamps. And the section in the *poroshoh* of Ve-Atoh Tetzavveh, was mentioned [there] only for the sake of the Order of the Work of the Tabernacle, to explain the purpose of the *menorah*, and so it implies: You will in the end command Bnei Yisroel regarding this.

**Pure, pressed olive oil.** Three oils come forth from the olive: [the oil of] the first [pressing] is called “pure” — and [these three oils] are explained in [Maseches] Menochos, and *Toras Kohanim*.

**Continually,** from night to night, like [the expression] “a continual burnt-offering,” which [comes] only from day to day.

### 24:3

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-3|Leviticus 24:3]]

**The Curtain of Testimony,** which is before the Ark, which is called “Testimony.” And our Rabbis expounded [this as referring] to the westernmost lamp, which is testimony for all the world’s inhabitants that the Divine Presence dwells in the midst of Israel. [This is because the *Kohein Godol*] places as much oil in it as for the other lamps, [but] from it he would begin [to light the lamps] and with it he would end.

**Aharon shall arrange it [so that it burns] from evening until morning.** He should arrange it in a suitable arrangement according to the length of each night; the Sages estimated [this] at half a *log* for each lamp, and this was fit for even the [long] nights of the Teves season, and this measure was set for them [for the entire year].

### 24:4

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-4|Leviticus 24:4]]

**The pure menorah,** [that is, the menorah] which is [made of] pure gold. Another interpretation: [He should arrange the lamps] on the purity of the menorah, [by] first purifying it and cleaning it of its ashes.

### 24:6

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-6|Leviticus 24:6]]

**Six in each stack —** six loaves in one stack.

**The pure table,** [that is, the table made] of pure gold. Another interpretation: [The loaves should be placed] on the “purity” of the table, that the supportsshould not raise the [lowest loaves of] bread above the table.

### 24:7

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-7|Leviticus 24:7]]

**You shall put on [each] stack,** on each one of the two stacks; there were two bowls of frankincense [in all], a fistful [of frankincense] for each one [= bowl].

**And it shall be —** this frankincense,

**as a memorial portion for the bread,** because of the bread itself nothing was offered to the all-Highest except that the frankincense was burnt when the bread was removed from the Table every Shabbos; and it is the memorial portion for the bread, for by means of it, it [= the bread] is remembered Above, similar to the fistful which is the memorial portion for the meal-offering.

### 24:9

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-9|Leviticus 24:9]]

**It shall belong —** this meal-offering, for any [offering] that comes from crops is [considered in the category of] meal-offering.

**And they shall eat it,** referring to the bread, which is a masculine [noun].

### 24:10

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-10|Leviticus 24:10]]

**The son of an Israelite woman went out.** From where did he go out? R. Levi says: He went out of his [everlasting] world. R. Berechiah says: From the section above he set out [to ridicule]. He mocked [the showbread], saying: “’On each and every Shabbos he shall arrange them!’ It is the custom of a king to eat fresh bread every day, is it then [his way to eat] a cold loaf nine days old”? In astonishment. A teaching states: He went out guilty from Moshe’s court. He had come to pitch his tent in the midst of the tribe of Dan. They asked him: “What right have you to be here”? He said to them: “I am of the descendants of Dan.” They said to him: “Each man [shall camp] by his own flag, [that bears] the signs of their father’s house,” it is written He entered Moshe’s court and went out guilty. [whereupon] he stood up and blasphemed [= cursed].

**And he was the son of an Egyptian man —** he was the Egyptian whom Moshe killed.

**Among Bnei Yisroel —** [this] teaches that he was converted.

**They argued in the encampment —** regarding matters [concerning] the encampment.

**And the Israelite man —** the one against him, who protested against his pitching his tent.

### 24:11

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-11|Leviticus 24:11]]

**Clearly pronounced,** as its Targum: “and he pronounced,” that he clearly pronounced the Proper Name, and blasphemed — it [the Name he used] is the Explicit Name he heard at Sinai.

**The name of his mother was Shelomis, daughter of Divri.** It is to the credit of Israel that this verse publicized this one, to say that she alone was a wayward woman.

**Shlomis.** [She was called this] because she would chatter, “Be well”! “Be well” “Be well”! babbling [many] words, asking [about] everyone’s health.

**Daughter of Divri.** She was a chatterer, speaking with every person [who came her way], and therefore she became corrupted.

**Of the tribe of Dan.** [This] tells that an evil person causes disgrace to himself, disgrace to his father, disgrace to his tribe. Similarly, “Oholiov son of Achisomoch, of the tribe of Dan” — a credit to him, a credit to his father, a credit to his tribe.

### 24:12

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-12|Leviticus 24:12]]

**They placed him [in the guardhouse] —** by himself, and they did not place the wood-gatherer with him, though the two of them were [in custody] during the same period of time. They knew that one who gathered wood [on Shabbos] was [condemned] to death, as it is said, “anyone who profanes it shall be put to death,” but the execution itself had not been explained to them. Therefore it is said, “For it had not been explained [to them] what should be done to him.” But regarding the blasphemer [the verse] says: “to explain to them” — for they did not know if he was liable to death or not.

### 24:14

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-14|Leviticus 24:14]]

**Those who heard him. —** These are the witnesses.

**All —** [this comes] to includes the judges.

**Their hands.** They say to him: “Your blood is on your head, and we are not punished for your death, for you caused [it] to yourself.

**The entire congregation,** in the presence of the entire congregation. (From here) we learn that a man’s agent is like the man himself.

### 24:15

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-15|Leviticus 24:15]]

**Shall bear [the burden] of his sin —** with *koreis*, when there is no warning.

### 24:16

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-16|Leviticus 24:16]]

**Whoever pronounces clearly —** he is not guilty until he pronounces the Name clearly, and not one who blasphemes by one of [God’s] titles.

**And whoever pronounces.** “Pronounces” here is an expression of “cursing,” as [in the verse] “Why should I curse”?

### 24:17

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-17|Leviticus 24:17]]

**Any man who kills.** Since it is said, “If one strikes a man and [the victim dies]” I [know that this applies] only when he killed a man, from where [do I know] [if he killed] a woman or minor? The verse [here] says: “any human being.”

### 24:20

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-20|Leviticus 24:20]]

**So shall it be done to him.** Our Rabbis explained that [this] does not [mean] placing an actual blemish [on him], but [rather] the payment of money. We appraise [his worth] as a slave [being sold in a slave-market]. Therefore [יִנָתֵן,] “giving” is written regarding it — something which is given from hand to hand.

### 24:21

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-21|Leviticus 24:21]]

**Whoever smites an animal shall pay for it,** above (verse 18), [it] spoke of one who kills an animal, and here it speaks of one who causes a wound to it.

**Whoever smites a person shall be put to death,** even if he did not kill him, but [merely] caused a wound, for here [the word] “soul” does not appear. And of one who smites his father or his mother. [this] verse speaks, and [this verse is placed here] to compare [this case] to one who smites an animal: Just as one who smites an animal [when it is] alive [is liable], so [too] one who smites his father [when he is] alive [is liable] — excluding one who smites [his father or mother] after [their] death. Since we find that one who curses [one’s father and mother even] after death, is liable to the death[-penalty], it was necessary to state [the law] regarding one who smites [a parent’s body after death] that he is exempt [from the death penalty]. [The comparison goes the other way as well:] Just as [for wounding] an animal, [one must pay compensation], [for] if there is no wound there is no payment, so [too] one who strikes his father is not liable [to the death-penalty] until he causes a wound to him.

### 24:22

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-22|Leviticus 24:22]]

**For I am Adonoy, your God,** the God of all of you; just as I attach My Name to you, so I attach it to converts.

### 24:23

[[Torah 31 - Emor#^24-23|Leviticus 24:23]]

**And Bnei Yisroel did —** [they carried out] the complete *mitzvoh* of stoning mentioned elsewhere [on the blasphemer]: casting [the condemned man down from a height], the heaping of stones [on him], and hanging [his body afterwards].
