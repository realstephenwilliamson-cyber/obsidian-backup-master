## Mūla

मत्स्येषु वेदाः सूत्राणीत्येवं स्याच्चतुरस्रकम् ।
एकस्मात्प्रभृति प्रोक्तं शतान्तं मण्डलं यतः ॥ ^mula

matsyeṣu vedāḥ sūtrāṇītyevaṃ syāccaturasrakam |
ekasmātprabhṛti proktaṃ śatāntaṃ maṇḍalaṃ yataḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. XI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 11 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v11 04 - Chapter Thirty-One, part 1#^pg84|p. 66]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 11.pdf#page=84>))
>
> Then, by means (of a thread) which is half the length of the area (covered by) the oval (drawn initially), one should, starting from the centre, mark (in the same way the intermediate) directions. (Then,) by means of the two threads, lifted up from the two directions (i.e. east to west and north to south), an oval (is to be drawn that extends) to each corner (in the intermediate directions). In this way, (four) threads (that pass through) the four (extremities of the four) ovals form a square. (with 31.6–7ab)
>
> The mandala is said to begin with one and end with a hundred. Thus, it is said in the Siddhātantra that in that section (tatpītha), there are a hundred mandalas. Amongst them, three mandalas are clearly considered to be the main (mukhya) ones, namely, one with a (single) trident in the middle, one with three tridents, and one with nine tridents. (with 31.7cd–9ab)
