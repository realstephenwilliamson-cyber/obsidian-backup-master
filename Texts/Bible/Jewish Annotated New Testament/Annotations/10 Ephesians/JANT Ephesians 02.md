### 2.1–10: Rebirth from sin through Christ

*[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 390* ^2-1

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Dead through the trespasses and sins*; the association of death and sin may be a product of the fact that, in biblical tradition, sin is a cause of impurity, which is associated with death, also a source of impurity; see [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-16|Lev 16.16]] and Num 19. In rabbinic tradition, a Gentile who leaves idolatrous practices behind is “as one separating from the grave” (*m. Ed*. 5.2). ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Ruler of the power of the air*, see 6.11**–**12 and [[Colossians 01#^1-13|Col 1.13]]. See also the fallen angels in *1 En*. 6.2**–**6 and the angel of darkness in 1QS 3.20**–**21, both of whom lead their human followers astray. Such malevolent supernatural powers are not a significant part of the rabbinic tradition. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-3|3]]:** *Passions of our flesh*, the carnal, as opposed to the spiritual, which *by nature* lead us astray; see the rabbinic “yetzer ha-raʿ ” (impulse toward evil), which is often associated with carnal lust (e.g., *b. Sukk.* 52a**-**b), though it is also defended as necessary for the settlement of the world by leading a man to take a wife, build a home, and have children (*Gen. Rab.* 9.7). ^2-3

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-4|4–5]]:** *Rich in mercy . . . great love . . . by grace*, God’s salvation, the author claims, is an expression of love, *not the result of works* (v 9); God’s quality of mercy is also notable in key settings in the Tanakh; see, e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]]**–**7 and [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^14-18|Num 14.18]]. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-5|5–6]]:** *Made us alive together with Christ . . .* , see 1.20; believers are joined to the experience of Christ, as the body to the head (1.22**–**23). ^2-5

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-6|6]]:** *Heavenly places*, the heavenly throne is amply referenced in the Tanakh ([[Isaiah 06#^6-1|Isa 6.1]]; [[Ezekiel 01#^1-26|Ezek 1.26]], and elsewhere) and, following such visions, in [[Revelation 04#^4-2|Rev 4.2]]**–**11. Rabbinic teachings also suggest that the righteous will, in the world to come, sit by God’s side and enjoy the splendor of the divine presence; see *b. Ber*. 17a. ^2-6

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Grace in kindness*, 2.4–5n. ^2-7

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-8|8–9]]:** *Have been saved*, in the undisputed Pauline epistles, salvation is generally a future event, not a present status. *The gift of God—not the result of works*, see 2.4**–**5n. *So that no one may boast*, cf. [[Romans 03#^3-27|Rom 3.27]]. ^2-8

> [!note] The ‘Wall of Hostility,’ ‘Abolishing the Law,’ and ‘One New Humanity’ (eph 2.14–15) *(p. 391)*
> Jewish law separated observant Jews from others. [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-6|Exodus 19.6]] declares that Israel should be a “holy nation,” a notion reinforced at [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-2|Lev 19.2]] and elsewhere. “Holiness” (Heb *q-d-sh*) demands separation (the early rabbinic midrash, *Sifra*, translates [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-2|Lev 19.2]], “you shall be holy,” as “you shall be separate”). The Torah’s dietary laws (Lev 11), in particular, would have created such a separation, while later extensions of the law—forbidding eating any Gentile food (e.g., [[Daniel 01#^1-8|Dan 1.8]]**–**12), extending new impurities to Gentiles (*Sifra*, *zavim* 1.1 to [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^15-1|Lev 15.1]]) and others—would have made the wall even higher. That such separation led to enmity toward Jews on the part of some Gentiles is best exemplified by Tacitus, who wrote, “Jews are extremely loyal toward one another . . . but toward every other people they feel only hate and enmity. They sit apart at meals and they sleep apart . . . ” (*Hist*. V, 1**–**2). Some Jews also felt enmity toward Gentiles, as exemplified in *m. Avod. Zar*. 2, which declares (among other things), “we may not keep an animal in the inns of Gentiles, for they are suspected of bestiality, and a woman should not be alone with them, for they are suspected of adultery, and a \[Jewish\] man should not be alone with them, for they are suspected of murder.” By abolishing those laws that differentiate between Jews and Gentiles, such as the separating mark of circumcision, Gentiles may be “brought near” (v 13) and a divided humanity made one.

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Created in Christ Jesus for good works*, works (equivalent to Heb “mitzvot”) are not excluded by Ephesians but they are a product of one’s salvation, not the cause. *Our way of life*, lit., “in them we should walk,” like Heb “halakhah,” “the path one is to walk.” ^2-10

### 2.11–22: Uniting Jews and Gentiles in Christ

*[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-11|Text]], p. 392* ^2-11

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Called “the uncircumcision*,*” m. Ned*. 3.11, citing [[Jeremiah 09#^9-25|Jer 9.26]], remarks that all Gentiles are called uncircumcised (with a derogatory connotation), whether or not they are actually circumcised. ^2-11-2

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Aliens . . . and strangers*, probably equivalent to biblical Heb “ger” (resident alien) and “nokhri” (foreigner). *Aliens from the commonwealth*, as opposed to citizens (see 2.19). *Covenants of promise*, the promise of eternal divine favor; see Gen 15 and 17. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Far off . . . brought near*, possibly alluding to [[Isaiah 57#^57-19|Isa 57.19]], which equates those far and near in God’s healing peace; see 2.17. *The blood of Christ*, the crucifixion, see 1.7n. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Broken down the dividing wall*, see “The Wall of Hostility,*”* p. 391. ^2-14

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Abolished the law*, cf. [[Matthew 05#^5-17|Mt 5.17]]**–**18. ^2-15

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-17|17]]:** *Proclaimed peace to you who were far*, see 2.13n. ^2-17

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-18|18]]:** *In one Spirit*, because the flesh, representing genetic descent, no longer matters. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue Ephesians 02#^2-19|19–22]]:** *Household . . . grows into a holy temple*, Christ, the head and cornerstone, along with the church, the body and foundation, replace the physical Temple in Jerusalem; cf. [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-16|1 Cor 3.16]]**–**17; [[1 Peter 02#^2-4|1 Pet 2.4]]**–**5. In rabbinic Judaism, the Temple destroyed in 70 CE was never consciously replaced, though studying its parameters (outlined in *m. Midd.* ) and its service (in *m. Tamid* and other tractates) would serve until its hoped-for restoration. ^2-19
