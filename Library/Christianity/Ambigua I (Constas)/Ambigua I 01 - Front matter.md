> [!note] Fresh OCR of the page images
> Each PDF page is one book page: the Greek text on the left-hand pages, the English translation on the right, so the two languages alternate page by page.
> - The Greek keeps its accents and breathings. About one or two Greek words in a hundred are misread, some in Latin letters ("Kai" for καὶ, "Adyov" for Λόγου).
> - The English is reliable but has lost its italics. The small raised note numbers are often lost or read as "!" or "?".
> - The large first letter of each Ambiguum is often misread ("Fox Saint Gregory's" for "From Saint Gregory's", "E" and "K τοῦ" for "Ἐκ τοῦ").
> - The section numbers in the margins are often missing. Where they were read, they stand inside the text at the end of the line beside them, and on the Greek pages they can break a paragraph in two.
> - Each chapter note starts with the page pair where its Ambiguum begins, so it may open with the end of the Ambiguum before.
> - Notes to the Translation: some note numbers are missing or misread, and longer numbers often stand after the first line of their note.
> - Notes to the Text: these keep the PDF's earlier OCR layer, because the fresh OCR misplaces their numbers. Its Greek lacks breathings and has some wrong accents, and some English words are split ("T h is").
> - The PDF lacks printed pages 4–5 and 12–13 (Greek and English).
>
