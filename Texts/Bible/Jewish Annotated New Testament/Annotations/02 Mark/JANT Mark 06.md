# Mark 6 (JANT notes)

### 6.1–13: Second rejection in Jesus’ hometown and the mission of the twelve

*[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 81* · ([[Matthew 10#^10-1|Mt 10.1–14]]; [[Matthew 13#^13-53|13.53–58]]; [[Luke 04#^4-16|Lk 4.16–30]]; [[Luke 09#^9-1|9.1–6]]). ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-3|3]]:** *Brother . . . sisters*, Christian tradition has sometimes explained these siblings as children of Joseph and a wife other than Mary, or as cousins. The Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek words for “brother” and “sister” can mean relative ([[LXX Tobit 07#^7-9|Tob 7.9]]). The context suggests close family members, and the virginal conception of Jesus is not mentioned in Mark ([[Matthew 01#^1-18|Mt 1.18–25]]; [[Luke 01#^1-34|Lk 1.34–35]]). The point, as in 3.19b–30, is that Jesus’ family and hometown rejected him (see Introduction). ^6-3

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-4|4]]:** *Prophets are not without honor*, the Renaissance Hebrew expression “There is no prophet in his own city” (“Ein navi beʾiro”) may derive from this verse. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-5|5–6a]]:** Lack of faith limits Jesus’ power. ^6-5

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-6|6b–13]]:** Jesus bestows upon the disciples his power over *unclean spirits*; as *m. Ber.* 5.5 says, “A person’s representative is as the person himself.” ^6-6

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-7|7]]:** *Two by two*, perhaps to ensure that there would be two witnesses in accordance with [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-6|Deut 17.6]]; this would be relevant for the *testimony* in v 11. ^6-7

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Anointed with oil*, common medical practice ([[Isaiah 01#^1-6|Isa 1.6]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 1.657). *and of Mary* c Or *stumbled* ^6-13

### 6.14–29: Beheading of John the Baptist

*[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-14|Text]], p. 82* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-1|Mt 14.1–12]]; [[Luke 09#^9-7|Lk 9.7–9]]). The Jewish historian Josephus (*Ant.* 18.116–19) indicates that John the Baptist was a well-known, respected figure ([[Mark 11#^11-30|Mk 11.30–32]]). Mark presents John as a prophet who condemns *Herod* (v 16) Antipas for violating Torah ([[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-16|Lev 18.16]]) by marrying his *brother’s wife* (v 18). ^6-14

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-15|15]]:** *Elijah*, [[Mark 08#^8-28|Mk 8.28]]. ^6-15

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-16|16]]:** *John . . . has been raised,* Herod is reminded of John the Baptist, since their preaching was similar and they both attracted crowds. The idea of the resurrection of many was a common, though not universal Jewish belief (Ezek 37; [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 15#^15-12|2 Macc 15.12–16]]; [[Mark 12#^12-18|Mk 12.18]]). What is unusual is the suggestion that a particular individual has been raised from the dead, unless this is understood as the beginning of a larger process. Yet here it is only presented as Herod’s speculation. This account is likely based on the actual execution of John, although a somewhat different account is found in Josephus, *Ant.* 18.116–19, where the execution occurs in Machaerus, a fortress on the Dead Sea. ^6-16

### 6.30–44: Feeding of the 5,000

*[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-30|Text]], p. 82* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-13|Mt 14.13–21]]; [[Luke 09#^9-10|Lk 9.10–17]]; [[John 06#^6-1|Jn 6.1–13]]). Mark depicts two miraculous feedings, one in predominantly Jewish territory and the other (8.1–10) in a predominantly Gentile area. *Herodias herself* Matthew includes both; Luke and John have only one. The narratives evoke the Exodus, and especially Elisha’s miracles (Ex 16–18; [[II Kings 04#^4-42|2 Kings 4.42–44]]). ^6-30

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-33|33]]:** Improbably, the people see them leave by boat, recruit others from *all the towns*, run ahead, and yet arrive at the deserted place ahead of them (see also the crowds in 3.7–12). This description emphasizes the kingdom’s miraculous nature. ^6-33

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-34|34]]:** *Sheep without a shepherd*, [[I Kings 22#^22-17|1 Kings 22.17]]. Like Moses, Jesus is called out of his preferred isolation (v 31) by *compassion* for the people (e.g., Ex 3–4) who will abandon him. *Shepherd,* [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^27-17|Num 27.17]]; [[Isaiah 40#^40-11|Isa 40.11]]. ^6-34

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-41|41]]:** *Taking . . . blessed . . . broke . . . gave*, are especially close to words used in the Last Supper ([[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22]]–25n.); thus the miraculous feeding foreshadows the Eucharist while also suggesting the banquet prophesied at [[Isaiah 25#^25-6|Isa 25.6]] (cf. 1QM 2.11–22; *1 En.* 10.18–19). ^6-41

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-43|43]]:** *Twelve baskets* suggest the twelve disciples as well as the eschatological gathering of the twelve tribes ([[Luke 22#^22-28|Lk 22.28–30]]). ^6-43

### 6.45–52: Walking on the water

*[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-45|Text]], p. 83* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-22|Mt 14.22–33]]; [[John 06#^6-15|Jn 6.15–21]]). Each of Mark’s feeding miracles is joined with a water miracle, evoking the Exodus (e.g., God parting the waters \[[[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-19|Ex 14.19–31]]\], God feeding the people in the wilderness \[[[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-13|Ex 16.13–21]]\]). ^6-45

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-49|49]]:** *A ghost*, (Gk “phantasma”), a vague apparition. ^6-49

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-52|52]]:** The disciples’ misunderstanding is a serious condition, akin to that of Pharaoh who oppressed the Israelites (Ex 7–11). *Hearts . . . hardened*, as with Pharaoh (e.g., [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^7-14|Ex 7.14]]) but also the people ([[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^17-1|Ex 17.1–7]]; [[Psalms 095#^95-8|Ps 95.8]]). ^6-52

### 6.53–56: A summary passage

*[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-53|Text]], p. 83* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-34|Mt 14.34–36]]). See 1.29–34n. ^6-53

**[[NRSVue Mark 06#^6-56|56]]:** *Fringe of his cloak*, the blue threads (Heb “tzitzit”) God commanded Israelite males to wear at the corners of their cloaks ([[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-37|Num 15.37–40]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-12|Deut 22.12]]). Jesus observes Torah. **7.1–23. Washing of hands and the commandment of God** ([[Matthew 15#^15-1|Mt 15.1–20]]). The Torah’s rules for kosher food and ritual purity were, along with male circumcision, contentious areas for Jesus’ followers (see [[Acts 15#^15-19|Acts 15.19–20]]). ^6-56
