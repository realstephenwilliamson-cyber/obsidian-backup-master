# Panchang: the five limbs

*Pañcāṅga* means "five limbs": every panchang gives the **vāra**, **tithi**, **nakṣatra**, **yoga** and **karaṇa** of the day (Himalayan Academy). Home's date box also gives the **māsa** and **saṃvat**. The words in the box link here; the computation behind the [[Panchang 2026 (Hickory)|Hickory tables]] is described at the top of each table.

The Hindu day runs from sunrise to sunrise. Each limb in the table is the one current at sunrise, with the time it ends (24-hour, Hickory time). Anything after "then" begins later in the same day.

## Vāra

The weekday ("solar weekday", Himalayan Academy), counted from sunrise. In the table: Ravivāra (Sunday), Somavāra, Maṅgalavāra, Budhavāra, Guruvāra, Śukravāra, Śanivāra (Saturday).

## Tithi

The lunar day: the time the Moon takes to move 12° ahead of the Sun. There are thirty in a lunar month, fifteen in each fortnight (**pakṣa**):

- **Śukla pakṣa**, the bright (waxing) fortnight, from new moon to full moon, ends with **Pūrṇimā** (full moon);
- **Kṛṣṇa pakṣa**, the dark (waning) fortnight, from full moon to new moon, ends with **Amāvāsyā** (new moon).

In each fortnight: Pratipadā (1), Dvitīyā, Tṛtīyā, Caturthī, Pañcamī, Ṣaṣṭhī, Saptamī, Aṣṭamī, Navamī, Daśamī, Ekādaśī (11), Dvādaśī, Trayodaśī, Caturdaśī (14), then Pūrṇimā or Amāvāsyā.

A tithi is not the same length as a solar day. The tithi current at sunrise names the day. One can begin and end between two sunrises, so that no sunrise falls in it; the table then marks its observance on the day it falls in.

## Nakṣatra

The lunar mansion: which of 27 equal parts of the sidereal zodiac (13°20′ each) the Moon is in ("the constellation the moon is aligned with", Himalayan Academy). In order: Aśvinī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, Rohiṇī, Mṛgaśirā, Ārdrā, Punarvasu, Puṣya, Āśleṣā, Maghā, Pūrva Phalgunī, Uttara Phalgunī, Hasta, Citrā, Svātī, Viśākhā, Anurādhā, Jyeṣṭhā, Mūla, Pūrva Āṣāḍhā, Uttara Āṣāḍhā, Śravaṇa, Dhaniṣṭhā, Śatabhiṣā, Pūrva Bhādrapadā, Uttara Bhādrapadā, Revatī.

## Yoga

"A particular angle of the sun and moon" (Himalayan Academy). It is computed from the sum of the Sun's and Moon's sidereal longitudes, in 27 steps of 13°20′: Viṣkambha, Prīti, Āyuṣmān, Saubhāgya, Śobhana, Atigaṇḍa, Sukarman, Dhṛti, Śūla, Gaṇḍa, Vṛddhi, Dhruva, Vyāghāta, Harṣaṇa, Vajra, Siddhi, Vyatīpāta, Varīyān, Parigha, Śiva, Siddha, Sādhya, Śubha, Śukla, Brahman, Indra, Vaidhṛti.

## Karaṇa

Half a tithi ("half-day", Himalayan Academy): 6° of the Moon's movement ahead of the Sun, so sixty in a month. Seven movable karaṇas repeat eight times through the month: Bava, Bālava, Kaulava, Taitila, Gara, Vaṇija, Viṣṭi. Four fixed ones fill the remaining places: Kiṃstughna (the first half of Śukla Pratipadā) and Śakuni, Catuṣpāda and Nāga (the last three, at the end of the dark fortnight).

## Māsa

The lunar month. The tables use **amānta** months, which run from new moon to new moon. Each is named for the sign the Sun is in, sidereally, at the new moon that begins it: Caitra, Vaiśākha, Jyeṣṭha, Āṣāḍha, Śrāvaṇa, Bhādrapada, Āśvina, Kārttika, Mārgaśīrṣa, Pauṣa, Māgha, Phālguna. A month in which the Sun enters no new sign is **Adhika** (intercalary), and the month after it takes the same name. In the dark fortnight the table also gives the **pūrṇimānta** name, for calendars whose months run from full moon to full moon and so are already in the next month.

## Saṃvat

The year count. **VS** is the Vikrama Saṃvat (the Gregorian year + 57 from Caitra), **Śaka** the Śaka era (− 78), and **Saptarṣi** the Kashmiri Laukika era (+ 3076). All three turn at Caitra Śukla Pratipadā. The table gives all three; Home shows VS and Saptarṣi.
