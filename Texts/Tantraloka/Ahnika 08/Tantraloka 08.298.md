## Mūla

कलायां स्यान्महादेवत्रयस्य पुरमुत्तमम् ।
ततो माया त्रिपुटिका मुख्यतोऽनन्तकोटिभिः ॥ ^mula

kalāyāṃ syānmahādevatrayasya puramuttamam |

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 09 - Chapter Eight, part 7#^pg319|p. 301]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=319>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 8.298cd–299ab.
>
> 1) Above the individual soul is the Principle of Natural Law (niyati). The ten Śaṁkaras reside there, resplendent as gold. 2) There are ten Śivas who are extremely white in the Principle of Time. Each of them is surrounded by ten million and sixteen thousand (deities, who serve as their entourage). 3) (Next comes) the world of (the eight) Vīreśas, and as another, the (ten) teachers and disciples in the Principle of Attachment (rāga). 4) The world of Impure Knowledge shines brilliantly with the nine powers. They are said to range from Vāmā up to Manomanī. 5) The best of worlds, that of the three Mahādevas, is the Principle of the Force (kalā) (of limited agency). (with 8.294–298ab)
>
> Then comes Māyā which, primarily consisting of three cavities, is strewn with endless billions of vaginal cavities (bhagabila). According to the Sivatanuśāstra, the eight Mahādevas ending with Anguṣṭhamātra reside within Māyā as the Lords of eight Wheels. Such is also the opinion of the Mālinīmata. (with 8.298cd–300ab)
