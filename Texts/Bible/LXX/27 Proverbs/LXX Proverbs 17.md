# LXX Proverbs 17

← [[LXX Proverbs 16]] · [[LXX]] · [[LXX Proverbs 18]] →

*Proverbs · [[LXX]]*

**17:1** Better is a morsel with pleasure in peace, than a house *full* of many good things and unjust sacrifices, with strife.  ^17-1

**17:2** A wise servant shall have rule over foolish masters, and shall divide portions among brethren.  ^17-2

**17:3** As silver and gold are tried in a furnace, so are choice hearts with the Lord.  ^17-3

**17:4** A bad man hearkens to the tongue of transgressors: but a righteous man attends not to false lips.  ^17-4

**17:5** He that laughs at the poor provokes him that made him; and he that rejoices at the destruction of another shall not be held guiltless: but he that has compassion shall find mercy.  ^17-5

**17:6** Children's children are the crown of old men; and their fathers are the glory of children. 6a The faithful has the whole world full of wealth; but the faithless not even a farthing.  ^17-6

**17:7** Faithful lips will not suit a fool; nor lying lips a just man.  ^17-7

**17:8** Instruction is to them that use it a gracious reward: and whithersoever it may turn, it shall prosper.  ^17-8

**17:9** He that conceals injuries seeks love; but he that hates to hide *them* separates friends and kindred.[^1]  ^17-9

**17:10** A threat breaks down the heart of a wise man; but a fool, though scourged, understands not.  ^17-10

**17:11** Every bad man stirs up strifes: but the Lord will send out against him an unmerciful messenger.  ^17-11

**17:12** Care may befall a man of understanding; but fools will meditate evils.  ^17-12

**17:13** Whoso rewards evil for good, evil shall not be removed from his house.  ^17-13

**17:14** Rightful rule gives power to words; but sedition and strife precede poverty.  ^17-14

**17:15** He that pronounces the unjust just, and the just unjust, is unclean and abominable with God.  ^17-15

**17:16** Why has the fool wealth? for a senseless man will not be able to purchase wisdom. 16a He that exalts his own house seeks ruin; and he that turns aside from instruction shall fall into mischiefs.  ^17-16

**17:17** Have thou a friend for every time, and let brethren be useful in distress; for on this account are they born.  ^17-17

**17:18** A foolish man applauds and rejoices over himself, *as he* also that becomes surety would make himself responsible for his own friends.  ^17-18

**17:19** A lover of sin rejoices in strifes;  ^17-19

**17:20** and the hard-hearted man comes not in for good. A man of a changeful tongue will fall into mischiefs;[^2]  ^17-20

**17:21** and the heart of a fool is grief to its possessor. A father rejoices not over an uninstructed son; but a wise son gladdens his mother.  ^17-21

**17:22** A glad heart promotes health; but the bones of a sorrowful man dry up.  ^17-22

**17:23** The ways of a man who unjustly receives gifts in *his* bosom do not prosper; and an ungodly man perverts the ways of righteousness.  ^17-23

**17:24** The countenance of a wise man is sensible; but the eyes of a fool *go* to the ends of the earth.  ^17-24

**17:25** A foolish son *is a cause of* anger to his father, and grief to her that bore him.  ^17-25

**17:26** *It is* not right to punish a righteous man, nor *is it* holy to plot against righteous princes.  ^17-26

**17:27** He that forbears to utter a hard word is discreet, and a patient man is wise.  ^17-27

**17:28** Wisdom shall be imputed to a fool who asks after wisdom: and he who holds his peace shall seem to be sensible.  ^17-28

[^1]: 17:9 Comp. *Heb.*
[^2]: 17:20 *Or,* meets not with good men.
