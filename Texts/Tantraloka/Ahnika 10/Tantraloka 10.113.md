## Mūla

प्रमाता स्वकतादात्म्यभासिताखिलवेद्यकः ।
शिवः प्रमाता नो मेयो ह्यन्याधीनप्रकाशता ॥ ^mula

pramātā svakatādātmyabhāsitākhilavedyakaḥ |
śivaḥ pramātā no meyo hyanyādhīnaprakāśatā || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 11 - Chapter Ten, part 2#^pg385|p. 366]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=385>))
>
> When the object of knowledge is the Consciousness Deconditioned (subject), whose coverings have been completely destroyed, only Mantra (perceivers), whose consciousness is about to awake, are the subjects. When these Mantra (perceivers) are the objects of knowledge, then the subject is (Mantreśvara), their Lord, within whom the fullness of consciousness is in the process of arising. When he becomes an object, the subject is the Great Lord of Mantra, within whom the full (and complete) form (of consciousness) has dawned. (Finally,) when (he has) become an object, the subject is Śiva, who, supreme and self-luminous, manifests all objectivity as one with Himself. (with 10.110–113ab)
>
> Śiva is (always) a subject, and (never) an object. Objectivity (meyatā) is a manifest state of being which depends on another (reality to become manifest). That is not the case there. (Śiva) is the Lord Who is His own Light. (with 10.113cd–114ab)

## Viveka

*Lead-in:* ननु तस्मिन्नपि विज्ञेयतां प्राप्ते कः प्रमाता ? -- इत्याशङ्क्याह --

> शिवः प्रमाता नो मेयो ह्यन्याधीनप्रकाशता ॥
> मेयता सा न तत्रास्ति स्वप्रकाशो ह्यसौ प्रभुः ।

परप्रकाशत्वं नाम मेयत्वं तच्चास्य स्वप्रकाशत्वान्न विद्यते, इति कथं शिवस्यापि प्रमात्रन्तरवन्मेयता भवेत् -- इत्यर्थः ॥ ११३ ॥
