### 10.1–11: A new angel heralds the final trumpet

*[[NRSVue Revelation 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 555* · The *mighty angel*\[’s\] descent to declare the imminence of the seventh trumpet serves to draw out the completion of the eschatological catastrophes. ^10-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 10#^10-1|1–3]]:** *Cloud* and *seven thunders*, traditional images of storm theophanies (see [[Psalms 018#^18-7|Ps 18.6–15]]; 29), suggestive also of the pillars of cloud and fire of [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^13-21|Ex 13.21–23]]. The angel straddles *land* and *sea* to signify God’s dominion and judgment over both zones (see 10.6; 20.13). ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 10#^10-4|4]]:** What John was to transcribe is unclear; it may be related to the revelation of *the mystery of God* (v 7). ^10-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 10#^10-7|7]]:** *Mystery of God*, refers to elements of divine plan that have yet to be disclosed to humans, e.g., [[Daniel 02#^2-18|Dan 2.18–19]]; [[Romans 16#^16-25|Rom 16.25–26]]; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-51|1 Cor 15.51]]. *Prophets*, here including John the author. Prophets are often designated as God’s *servants* in the Hebrew Bible (e.g., [[II Kings 09#^9-7|2 Kings 9.7]]; [[Jeremiah 07#^7-25|Jer 7.25]]; [[Daniel 09#^9-6|Dan 9.6]],[[Daniel 09#^9-10|10]]). ^10-7

**[[NRSVue Revelation 10#^10-8|8–11]]:** In close imitation of [[Ezekiel 02#^2-8|Ezek 2.8–3.4]], John receives a *scroll* to *eat*, allowing God’s (written) warnings to come from his mouth. Like Ezekiel’s scroll, the sweetness of its taste reflects its divine origin, though John’s scroll assumes bitterness from the horrific decrees he must announce. ^10-8
