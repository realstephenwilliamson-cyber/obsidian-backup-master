# Proverbs 25

Compare: [[NRSVue Proverbs 25|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 25

**25:1** These too are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of King Hezekiah of Judah copied: ^25-1

**25:2** It is the glory of God to conceal a matter,  
And the glory of a king to plumb a matter. ^25-2

**25:3** Like the heavens in their height, like the earth in its depth,  
Is the mind of kings—unfathomable. ^25-3

**25:4** The dross having been separated from the silver,  
A vessel emerged for the smith. ^25-4

**25:5** Remove the wicked from the king’s presence,  
And his throne will be established in justice. ^25-5

**25:6** Do not exalt yourself in the king’s presence;  
Do not stand in the place of nobles. ^25-6

**25:7** For it is better to be told, “Step up here,”  
Than to be degraded in the presence of the great.  
Do not let what your eyes have seen ^25-7

**25:8** Be vented rashly in a quarrel;  
Think[^1] of what it will effect in the end,  
When your fellow puts you to shame. ^25-8

**25:9** Defend your right against your fellow,  
But do not give away the secrets of another, ^25-9

**25:10** Lest he who hears it reproach you,  
And your bad repute never end. ^25-10

**25:11** Like golden apples in silver showpieces[^2]  
Is a phrase well turned. ^25-11

**25:12** Like a ring of gold, a golden ornament,  
Is a wise man’s reproof in a receptive ear. ^25-12

**25:13** Like the coldness of snow at harvesttime  
Is a trusty messenger to those who send him;  
He lifts his master’s spirits. ^25-13

**25:14** Like clouds, wind—but no rain—  
Is one who boasts of gifts not given. ^25-14

**25:15** Through forbearance a ruler may be won over;  
A gentle tongue can break bones. ^25-15

**25:16** If you find honey, eat only what you need,  
Lest, surfeiting yourself, you throw it up. ^25-16

**25:17** Visit your neighbor sparingly,  
Lest he have his surfeit of you and loathe you. ^25-17

**25:18** Like a club, a sword, a sharpened arrow,  
Is a man who testifies falsely against his fellow. ^25-18

**25:19** Like a loose tooth and an unsteady leg,  
Is a treacherous support in time of trouble. ^25-19

**25:20** Disrobing on a chilly day,  
Like vinegar on natron,  
Is one who sings songs to a sorrowful soul. ^25-20

**25:21** If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat;  
If he is thirsty, give him water to drink. ^25-21

**25:22** You will be heaping live coals on his head,  
And the LORD will reward you. ^25-22

**25:23** A north wind produces rain,  
And whispered words, a glowering face. ^25-23

**25:24** Dwelling in the corner of a roof is better  
Than a contentious woman in a spacious house.[^3] ^25-24

**25:25** Like cold water to a parched throat  
Is good news from a distant land. ^25-25

**25:26** Like a muddied spring, a ruined fountain,  
Is a righteous man fallen before a wicked one. ^25-26

**25:27** It is not good to eat much honey,  
Nor is it honorable to search for honor.[^4] ^25-27

**25:28** Like an open city without walls  
Is a man whose temper is uncurbed. ^25-28

[^1]: Lit. “Lest.”
[^2]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^4]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
