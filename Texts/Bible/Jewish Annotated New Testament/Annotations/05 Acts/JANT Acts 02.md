### 2.1–41: Holy Spirit and Peter’s preaching

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 223* · The events occur during Shavuot (“Weeks,” [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-15|Lev 23.15–21]]), a harvest festival (*m. Bik.* 3; *b. Shabb.* [[Shabbat 86#86b|86b]]) and in later Jewish tradition the time when Moses received the Torah (*b. Pesah.* [[Pesachim 68#68b|68b]]). ^2-1

### 2.1–13: Sending of the Spirit

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 223* · . The act parallels Jesus’ reception of the Spirit, [[Luke 03#^3-21|Lk 3.21–22]]. ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Pentecost*, Gk “fiftieth,” seven full weeks plus one day (50 days) after Pesach (Passover), the name of the festival used by Greek-speaking Jews ([[LXX 2 Maccabees 12#^12-32|2 Macc 12.32]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 6.299). *Were all together*, Philo reports that the Therapeutae, a contemplative group of Egyptian Jews, gathered together to celebrate Shavuot (cf. Philo, *Cont. Life* 65–66). ^2-1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-3|3]]:** *Tongues, as of fire,* this image ([[Isaiah 05#^5-24|Isa 5.24]]; *1 En.* 14.8–25; 71.5) and fire in general M E S O P O TA M I A PA R T H I A M E D I A E L A M *A ra b i a n D e s e r t* ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-2|Ex 3.2]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^14-24|14.24]]; [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-18|19.18]]; [[Isaiah 05#^5-24|Isa 5.24]]) symbolize divine presence. ^2-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Filled with the Holy Spirit*, fulfills Jesus’ prediction in 1.5. *Other languages*, speaking in known languages rather than speaking in tongues, as in [[1 Corinthians 14#^14-6|1 Cor 14.6–19]]. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-5|5]]:** *Devout Jews from every nation*, Shavuot, one of the three pilgrimage festivals (along with Pesach \[Passover\] and Sukkot \[Booths\]), attracted many Jews from all over to Jerusalem. *Living*, suggesting these Jews are not pilgrims but reside in Jerusalem. ^2-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Galileans*, rabbis sometimes treated Galileans as mediocre scholars and inarticulate (*y. Shabb.* 15d; *b. Eruv.* [[Eruvin 53#53a|53a]]-b). ^2-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-9|9–11]]:** A list of nations, adding geographic detail to the expression *every nation under heaven* (v 5), and foreshadowing the spread of Christianity. ^2-9-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-10|10]]:** *Proselytes,* converts to Judaism. ^2-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-13|13]]:** *New wine*, the suggestion that people are drunk and thus speaking gibberish seems to contradict the previous indication that the people were heard speaking in known languages (v 4). Some Christians were known to speak in ways that made no rational sense ([[1 Corinthians 14#^14-23|1 Cor 14.23]]; Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 5.16.7). Similar forms of ecstatic or esoteric speech can be found in Jewish texts (*T. Job* 48–50) and in certain Greek cults, particularly that of Dionysus (Euripides, *Bacch.* 142; Livy 39.8.5–8; Plutarch, *Mor.* 364E). ^2-13

### 2.14–36: Peter’s speech

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-14|Text]], p. 224* ^2-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-14|14–21]]:** Teaching on Pentecost.  ^2-14-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-17|17–21]]:** See [[Joel 3#^3-1|Joel 2.28–32]]\[MT 3.1–5\]. Rabbinic literature understands Joel as referring to the world to come (*Num. Rab.* 15.25; *Deut. Rab.* 6.14; *Tanh. Miqqetz* 10). Luke adds *in the last days*, presenting the plan as now realized in the giving of the Spirit. *Slaves*, indicates the inclusion of all orders of society. ^2-17

### 2.22–36: Jesus the Messiah

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-22|Text]], p. 224* ^2-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-22|22]]:** *Israelites*, designation used only in invocations (3.12; 5.35; 13.16; 21.28). *Deeds of power, wonders, and signs*, common expressions for proof of divine activity ([[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-34|Deut 4.34]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-22|6.22]]; [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^26-8|26.8]]; [[Isaiah 08#^8-18|Isa 8.18]]; [[Jeremiah 32#^32-20|Jer 32.20]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-43|Ps 78.43]]; [[Daniel 03#^3-31|Dan 4.1–3]]\[MT 3.31–33\]). *A man*, emphasizing the human nature of Jesus. ^2-22-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-23|23]]:** *Definite plan,* Jesus’ death was the result of both divine plan and Jewish action (3.13; 4.10; 5.30; 7.52; 13.27). Divine involvement does not absolve human responsibility. *Crucified and killed,* Luke-Acts regularly identifies Jewish leaders ([[Luke 23#^23-13|Lk 23.13–23]]; [[Acts 04#^4-8|Acts 4.8–10]]; [[Acts 05#^5-27|5.27–30]]) or Jews in general ([[Acts 02#^2-23|Acts 2.23]],[[Acts 02#^2-36|36]]; [[Acts 03#^3-12|3.12–15]]; [[Acts 07#^7-51|7.51–52]]; [[Acts 10#^10-39|10.39]]; [[Acts 13#^13-27|13.27–28]]) as those responsible for killing Jesus, but see 4.25–30, which includes non-Jews as well. *Outside the law*, may refer to the Romans (who are not subject to Torah) or Jews (who transgressed against God; cf. [[Luke 22#^22-37|Lk 22.37]]). ^2-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-24|24]]:** *God raised him up,* according to Luke, the Jews who killed Jesus acted in opposition to God (2.36; 3.15; 4.10; 5.30,38–39; 10.39–40; 13.29–30). ^2-24

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-25|25–28]]:** See [[Psalms 016#^16-8|Ps 16.8]]**–**11. ^2-25

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-25|25]]:** The superscription to this Psalm attributes it to David. *Lord*, here a reference to Jesus, later confirmed through the interpretation of Ps 110 (v 34). ^2-25-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-27|27]]:** *Hades*, Heb “Sheol,” ultimate destination after death according to earlier biblical texts ([[Isaiah 38#^38-10|Isa 38.10]],[[Isaiah 38#^38-18|18]]; [[Psalms 006#^6-6|Ps 6.5]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-49|89.48]]; 116.3; [[Job 07#^7-9|Job 7.9]]). Hellenistic Jewish writings employed the Greek term ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 02#^2-1|Wis 2.1]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 16#^16-13|16.13]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-23|2 Macc 6.23]]; *Pss. Sol*. 16.2), and came to imagine Sheol/Hades as a place of judgment and punishment (*1 En.* 63.10; *t. Yoma* 4.11), in contrast to a blessed afterlife represented in various forms, such as eternal life ([[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]), bodily resurrection ([[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-9|2 Macc 6.9]]; *2 Bar.* 50.1–4; *L. A. B.* 3.10; *b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 90#90b|90b]]), paradise (*4 Ezra*\[2 Esd\] 7.44), and the world to come (*m. Sanh.* 10.1–6). ^2-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-29|29]]:** *Tomb*, a site identified as the tomb of David was known in antiquity (Josephus, *J.W.* 1.61; *Ant.* 7.393; 13.249), but it is not the present-day tomb of David on Mt. Zion, a tradition that begins in the Middle Ages. For Peter, David’s earthly burial excludes the possibility of him being the enthroned Lord referred to in the Psalm. Rather, David was speaking of Jesus, who was raised from the dead and seated at God’s right hand, thus confirming him as Lord (vv 31–32). ^2-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-30|30]]:** *Prophet,* See Josephus, *Ant.* 6.166; 7.334; 8.109; 11QPsa 27.11; *y. Sot.* 9.24b. *One of his descendants,* see [[II Samuel 07#^7-12|2 Sam 7.12–13]]. Jesus is a descendant of David ([[Luke 03#^3-31|Lk 3.31]]; [[Luke 18#^18-38|18.38-39]]). ^2-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-31|31]]:** *Abandoned to Hades*, see [[Psalms 016#^16-10|Ps 16.10]]. ^2-31

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-32|32]]:** *Witnesses*, see 1.8n. ^2-32

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-33|33–35]]:** See [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]; the Tanakh passage most quoted or alluded to in Luke-Acts ([[Luke 20#^20-42|Lk 20.42–43]]; [[Luke 22#^22-69|22.69]]; [[Acts 02#^2-33|Acts 2.33–35]]; [[Acts 05#^5-31|5.31]]; [[Acts 07#^7-55|7.55–56]]) and the NT. Christian traditions understand the Psalm as speaking about Jesus ([[Mark 12#^12-36|Mk 12.36]]; [[Hebrews 01#^1-13|Heb 1.13]]; *1 Clem.* 36.5; *Ep. Barn.* 12.10; *Apoc. Pet.* 6). Jewish traditions used the image of heavenly enthronement as a way to designate figures favored by God (*T. Job* 33.2–9) or imbued with divine authority (*1 En.* 51.3; 55.4; 61.8; 69.29), and interpreted the verse itself in relation to Abraham (*b. Ned.* [[Nedarim 32#32b|32b]]; *b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 108#108b|108b]]; *Lev. Rab.* 25.6), David (*b. Sanh.* [[Sanhedrin 38#38b|38b]]), or the Messiah (*Gen. Rab.* 85.9; *Seder Eliyahu Rab.* 18, sec. 90; *Midr. Pss. Sol.* 18, sec. 29). ^2-33

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-36|36]]:** *Made . . . Lord and Messiah*, Luke again (vv 23–24) contrasts God’s affirmation of Jesus as Messiah and the Jews who rejected him. ^2-36

### 2.37–41: Teaching on repentance

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-37|Text]], p. 226* · Repentance is a central teaching (3.19; 5.31; 8.22; 11.18; 17.30; 20.21; 26.20). ^2-37

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-38|38]]:** *Repent*, turn from what one has been and done; the reference is general, not to a specific act. As suggested in this verse and elsewhere, repentance is incomplete without also coming to believe in Jesus as the Messiah (3.19; 5.31; 11.18). *Be baptized,* some Jews associated ritual immersion with repentance ([[Isaiah 01#^1-16|Isa 1.16]]; [[Psalms 051#^51-9|Ps 51.7]]\[HB v 9\]; [[Luke 03#^3-3|Lk 3.3]]; *Sib. Or.* 4.162–70; 1QS 3.6–9). *Sins may be forgiven*, unspecified, but likely including the killing and rejection of Jesus (v 23). *Holy Spirit*, possession of the Holy Spirit marks one with divine favor and acceptance into the people of God (2.41; 10.48; 19.5; see also [[Matthew 28#^28-19|Mt 28.19]]). The relation between baptism and receiving the Holy Spirit varies in Acts (8.16; 10.44; 18.26; 19.5–6). **40**: *Save,* a key concept in Acts, connoting rescue from sin and falsehood (as here) and entry into the reign of God (4.12; 11.14; 13.47; 15.1; 16.30–31). *Corrupt generation*, see [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-5|Deut 32.5]]; [[Psalms 078#^78-8|Ps 78.8]]. ^2-38

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-41|41]]:** Such summaries appear regularly (4.32–35; 5.12–16; 6.11; 9.31). The passive voice suggests that divine direction brought about the community’s astounding growth. *Three thousand*, many in Jerusalem become believers (2.47; 4.4; 5.14; 6.1; 21.20). ^2-41

### 2.42–47: Early community life

*[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-42|Text]], p. 226* ^2-42

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-42|42]]:** *Teaching and fellowship*, *to the breaking of bread and the prayers,* the community is constituted through shared instructions and rituals. ^2-42-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-43|43]]:** *Wonders and signs,* activities usually associated with God (see 2.22n.), but here and elsewhere in Acts with humans empowered by God (4.30; 6.8; 14.3). ^2-43

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-44|44]]:** *Had all things in common*, another characteristic of the community (4.36–5.11; 6.1–6); communal ownership existed among the Jews at Qumran (1QS 1.11) and was highly valued in philosophical teachings (Aristotle, *Eth. nic.* 9.8; Cicero, *Off.* 1.16.51). ^2-44

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-46|46]]:** *Temple*, where the apostles often congregate for prayer and teaching (3.1–10; 5.21,42; 21.26–30; 22.17). ^2-46

**[[NRSVue Acts 02#^2-47|47]]:** Summary depicting church growth (see 4.32–35; 5.12–16; 6.7; 9.31; 16.5; 19.20). ^2-47
