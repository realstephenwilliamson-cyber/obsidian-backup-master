The Kramastotra 

by 

Abhinavagupta 

Abhinavagupta also wrote a *Kramastotra* (published by K.C. Pandey (1963: 948- 951) in which he describes the Twelve Kālīs. After his already concise description in chapter four of the Tantrāloka, he describes them again briefly in his *Tantrasāra,* *Tantroccaya Tantravaṭadhanikā*. However, nowhere does he help us as much to understand them than here in his *Kramastotra*. Here and at the end of his *vivṛtvimarśinī* on the *Īśvarapratyabhijñā* he does us the great favour of noting the date in which he completed the work. 

*ayaṁ duḥkhavrātavrataparigame pāraṇavidhir mahāsaukhyasāraprasaraṇarase durdinam idam |* *yadanyanyakkṛtyā viṣamaviśikhaploṣaṇaguror vibhoḥ stotre śaśvat pratiphalati ceto gatabhayam* || 1 || 

This is the procedure to cross over (to the liberated state) once understood the manner of life which is (this) mass of suffering; (when experiencing) the savour of the essence of the expansion of Great Bliss that, having reduced this bad day (of Māyā), which is other than that, to naught, so that the mind, free from fear, is ever reflected in the hymn to the Omnipresent Lord who is the teacher that burns up the rugged path (of fettered life). (1) 

*vimṛśya svātmānaṁ vimṛśati punaḥ stutyacaritaṁ tathā stotā stotre prakaṭayati bhedaikaviṣaye |* *vimṛṣṭaś ca svātmā nikhilaviṣayajñānasamaye tad itthaṁ tvatstotre 'ham iha satataṁ yatnarahitaḥ* || 2 || 

Having reflected on himself, the one who lauds (You) he reflects again on (Your) laudable conduct (*carita*), and reveals it in the hymn that has as its subject the oneness of duality; and one’s own Self is contemplated when knowledge of all the objects of sense (*viṣaya*) (arises). In this way, I am always here in your hymn, free of exertion. (2) 

*anāmṛṣṭaḥ svātmā na hi bhavati bhāvapramitibhāj anāmṛṣṭaḥ svātmety api hi na vinā 'marśanavidheḥ |* *śivaś cāsau svātmā sphuradakhilabhāvaikasarasas tato 'ham tvatstotre pravaṇahṛdayo nityasukhitaḥ* || 3 || 

One’s own Self, ignored (*anāmṛṣṭi*), does not rightly know existence. (Indeed) without the practice of awareness (there would be no knowledge) even that one’s own Self is being ignored. And Śiva is one’s own Self in the oneness of feeling (*rasa*) with all of radiantly manifesting existence and so, my heart dispossed to your *stotra*, I am constantly made joyful (*sukhita*). (3) 

*[p. 2]* ^pg2

*vicitrair jātyādibhramaṇaparipāṭīparikarair avāptam sārvajñaṁ hṛdayaṁ yad ayatnena bhavatā |* *tad antas tvadbodhaprasarasaraṇībhūtamahasi sphuṭam vāci prāpya prakaṭaya vibhoḥ stotram adhunā* || 4 || 

You have effortlessly attained the heart of the omniscient one by virtue of the many varied discernments of the series of (worldly) errors such as caste and the like. Having attained that clearly within the great power of speech which serves as the path for the flow of your awakened consciousness (bodha) now make the pervasive Lord’s hymn clear (and lucid). (4) 

*vidhunvāno bandhābhimatabhavamārgasthitim imāṁ rasīkṛtyānantastutihutavahaploṣitabhedām |* *vicitrasvasphārasphuritamahimārambharabhasāt piban bhāvān etān varada madamatto 'smi sukhitaḥ* || 5 || 

Shaking off this abiding state on the path of phenomenal existence which is (rightly) considered to be bondage, and having turned it into the savour (*rasa*) (of aesthetic delight), burnt away duality with the fire of endless praise (*stuti*) by the impetuosity of the exertion of the glory that has flashed forth as his own wonderfully varied expansion whilst drinking these phenomena (bhāva), O bestower of boons, I, made joyful (*sukhitaḥ*), am mad with inebriation (*mada*). (5) 

*bhava prājyaiśvaryaprathitabahuśakter bhagavato vicitraṁ cāritraṁ hṛdayam adhiśete yadi tataḥ |* *kathaṁ stotraṁ kuryād atha ca kurute tena sahasā śivaikātmyaprāptau śivanatir upāyaḥ prathamakaḥ* || 6 || 

If the wonderfully varied activity (caritra) of the Lord who possesses many powers that have spread forth from (His) copious sovereignity over phenomenal existence, come to rest in the Heart, then how is it possible to compose a hymn? Or if one does so with (the power of that victorious) audacity (*sahasa*), when attained oneness with Śiva, the first (and foremost) means (to realise Him) is to bow with reverence (to Him). (6) 

*jvaladrūpaṁ bhāsvat pacanam atha dāhaṁ prakaṭanam vimucyānyad vahneḥ kim api ghaṭate naiva hi vapuḥ |* *stuve saṁvidraśmīn yadi nijanijāṁs tena sa nuto bhaven nānyaḥ kaścid bhavati parameśasya vibhavaḥ* || 7 || 

Inflammed and shining, cooking, burning and making visible - nothing else at all apart from the body of fire can do that. If I praise each one of my own rays of consciousness, that which thus bows (in reverence) is none other at all than the Supreme Lord’s glory. (7) 

*vicitrārambhatve galitaniyame yaḥ kila rasaḥ paricchedābhāvāt paramaparipūrṇatvam asamam |* 

*[p. 3]* ^pg3

*svayaṁ bhāsāṁ (> bhāsan*) *yogaḥ sakalabhavabhāvaikamayatāviruddhair dharmaughaiḥ paracitir anarghocitaguṇā* || 8 || 

That aesthetic savour (*rasa*), (experienced) when restrictions fall away, in the state of the wonderfully varied beginning, is unconditioned and so is supreme plenitude without equal. Self-luminous union (*yoga*), it is the oneness which is the essential nature of all phenomenal existence that, with (its) streams of harmonious attributes, is supreme consciousness that is priceless and delightful. (8) 

*itīdṛkṣair rūpair varada vividhaṁ te kila vapur vibhāti svāṁśe 'smin jagati gatabhedaṁ bhagavataḥ |* *tad evaitat stotuṁ hṛdayam atha gīr bāhyakaraṇaprabandhāś ca syur me satatam aparityaktarabhasaḥ* || 9 || 

O bestower of boons, Your body shines in many ways with such forms within this universe of the Lord which is as aspect (*aṁśa*) of Himself in which He (as Your body) is free of duality. May my heart, speech and series of outer senses be constantly engaged in praising that itself, without (ever) giving up the force (of my intention). (9) 

*tavaivaikasyāntaḥ sphuritamahaso bodhajaladher vicitrormivrātaprasaraṇaraso yaḥ svarasataḥ |* *ta evāmī sṛṣṭisthitilayamayasphūrjitarucāṁ śaśān.kārkāgnīnāṁ yugapad udayāpāyavibhavāḥ* || 10 || 

The wonderfully varied mass of waves that flows forth spontaneously (by its own delight) as the aesthetic savour (*rasa*) of the ocean of the oneness of Your consciousness whose glory flashes forth within. These are the glorious expressions (*vibhava*) that simultaneously arise and fall away of the Moon, Sun and Fire that are the flashing vibrant radiance which is emanation, persistence and merger. (10) 

*ataś citrācitrakramataditarādisthitijuṣo vibhoḥ śaktiḥ śaśvad vrajati na vibhedaṁ katham api |* *tad etasyāṁ bhūmāv akulam iti te yat kila padaṁ tadekāgrībhūyān mama hṛdayabhūr bhairava vibho* || 11 || 

Thus the state of pervasive Lord is both various and not (at all), dynamic (krama) and (yet, at the same time) its opposite, (even so His) power never becomes divided in any way at all. On that plane is your condition (*pada*) (known as) Akula, O Bhairava! Pervasive Lord! may my heart be concentrated on that! (11) 

*amuṣmāt saṁpūrnāt vata rasamahollāsasarasān nijāṁ śaktiṁ bhedaṁ gamayasi nijecchāprasarataḥ |* *anarghaṁ svātantryaṁ tava tadidam atyadbhutamayīṁ bhavacchaktiṁ stunvan vigalitabhayo 'haṁ śivamayaḥ* || 12 || 

From this completely full lake of the great outpouring of aesthetic savour (*rasa*) You divide Your own power by the extending flow (*prasara*) of Your will. This is Your 

*[p. 4]* ^pg4

sinless freedom made of extraordinary wonder. As I praise Your power, I who am Śiva am free of fear. (12) 

*idantāvad rūpaṁ tava bhagavataḥ śaktisarasaṁ kramābhāvād eva prasabhavigalatkālakalanam |* *manaḥśaktyā vācāpy atha karaṇacakrair bahir atho ghaṭādyais tadrūpaṁ yugapad adhitiṣṭheyam aniśam* || 13 || 

Such is this Your nature Lord, the essence of power, that, because it is devoid of succession (*krama*), is the intensely powerful (*prasabha*) falling away of the passage (*kalana*) of time. May I constantly take the support of that nature simultaneously along with the power of the mind, speech, the wheels of the senses and (so too by means of common) outer things like jars. (13) 

*kramollāsaṁ tasyāṁ bhuvi viracayan bhedakalanāṁ svaśaktīnāṁ deva prathayasi sadā svātmani tataḥ |* *kriyājñānecchākhyāṁ sthitilayamahāsṛṣṭivibhavāṁ trirūpaṁ bhūyāsaṁ samadhiśayituṁ vyagrahṛdayaḥ* || 14 || 

Deploying the outpouring of succession on that plane You unfold the formation of relative distinctions (*bhedakalanā*) between Your own powers within Yourself so that the agitated heart may preside over (and rest in) the triple nature which is the glorious power (*vibhava*) of persistence, merger and the great emanation called action, knowledge and will. (14) 

The Sphere of Objectivity[^1] 

1\) Sṛṣṭikālī (emantion of objectivity) 

*purā sṛṣṭir līnā hutavahamayī yātra vilaset parollāsaunmukhyaṁ vrajati śaśisaṁsparśasubhagā |* *hutāśendusphārobhayavibhavabhāg bhairavavibho taveyaṁ sṛṣṭyākhyā mama manasi nityaṁ vilasatām* || 15 || 

Initially, the supreme (*parā*) emanation is merged (in consciousness). She is made of fire that here, beautiful with its contact with the Moon, enters a state of propense expectancy (*aunmukhya*) towards the supreme outpouring (of manifestation). O pervasive lord Bhairava, who possesses the glorious power (*vibhava*) of the expansion of both Fire and Moon, this is Your (Kālī) called Sṛṣti. May she always play in my mind. (15) (1) 

2\) Raktākālī (persistence of objectivity) 

[^1]: The original sources may divided the twelve Kaaliis into three groups of four but they nowhere equate the three groups with the sphere of objectivity, that of perception and subjectivity. This is Abhinava’s understanding of their domains. Although Abhinava this hymn Abhinava does not refer to this exegetical very much, nonetheless for the sake of clarity and coherence with the account as presented by Jayaratha in chapter four, I remind the reader of these domains

*[p. 5]* ^pg5

*visṛṣṭe bhāvāṁśe bahir atiśayāsvādavirase yadā tatraiva tvaṁ bhajasi rabhasād raktimayatām |* *tadā raktā devī tava sakalabhāveṣu nanu māṁ kriyād raktāpānakramaghaṭitagoṣṭhīgataghṛṇam* || 16 || 

Once the aspect of (phenomenal) existence has been emitted externally and the most excellent (inner) savouring (*āsvāda*) (of consciousness has become) insipid, if then You assume with force the nature of attachment (*rakti*) You are then the goddess Raktā in all Your emotive states (*bhāva*). May You indeed feel for me the tenderness (*ghṛṇa*) (experienced by) the assembly (gathered) for the rite of drinking Raktā (*raktāpānakrama*). (16) (2) 

3\) Sthitināśakālī (withdrawal of objectivity) 

*bahir vṛttiṁ hātuṁ citibhuvam udārāṁ nivasituṁ yadā bhāvābhedaṁ prathayasi vinaṣṭormicapalaḥ |* *sthiter nāśaṁ devī kalayati tadā sā tava vibho sthiteḥ sāṁsārikyāḥ kalayatu vināśaṁ mama sadā* || 17 || 

In order to destoy the outer state (*vṛtti*) and to reside on the noble plane of consciousness if You reveal the oneness of phenomenal existents, the fickle play of the waves (of phenomenal existence) destroyed, and the Goddess brings about the destruction of persistence, then may She, O pervasive lord, always bring about the destruction of the persistence of my transmigratory existence. (17) (3) 

4\) Yamakālī (the inexplicable state of objectivity) 

j*agatsaṁhāreṇa praśamayitukāmaḥ svarabhasāt svaśan.kātan.kākhyaṁ vidhim atha niṣedhaṁ prathayasi |* *yamaṁ sṛṣṭvetthaṁ tvaṁ punar api ca śan.kāṁ vidalayan mahādevī seyaṁ mama bhavabhayaṁ saṁdalayatām* || 18 || 

Desiring to bring to rest (all things) by withdrawing the universe by Your own force, You cause injunction and prohibition, which is called the affliction of one’s own doubt, to be perceived, having emitted Yama (the restriction of rules and regulations) even as You are then again engaged in destroying doubt. May this great goddess destroy my fear of phenomenal existence (*bhava*). (18) (4) 

The Sphere of Perception 

5\) Saṁhārakālī (emanation of perception) 

*vilīne śan.kaughe sapadi paripūrṇe ca vibhave gate lokācāre galitavibhave śāstraniyame |* *anantaṁ bhogyaughaṁ grasitum abhito laṁpaṭarasā vibho saṁhārākhyā mama hṛdi bhidāṁśaṁ praharatu* || 19 || 

*[p. 6]* ^pg6

When the flux of doubts has dissolved away and (Your) glory is completely full (and perfect) in a moment, when worldly life (*lokācāra*) has ceased, and the regulations and the power of scriptural injunction has fallen away, O pervasive Lord, may (the Kālī) called Saṁhāra (who delights in) the aesthetic savour of one who is greedy (*laṁpaṭarasā*) to gobble up the endless flux of objects of enjoyment (completely) all around, strike down (every) part of duality in my heart. (19) (5) 

6\) Mṛtyukālī (persistence of perception) 

*tad itthaṁ devībhiḥ sapadi dalite bhedavibhave vikalpaprāṇāsau pravilasati mātṛsthitir alam |* *ataḥ saṁhārāṁśam nijahṛdi vimṛśya sthitimayī prasannā syān mṛtyupralayakaraṇī me bhagavatī* || 20 || 

When the goddesses have in this way destroyed the power of duality in an instant, the abiding state of the perceiver, of which the (very) life is conceptualization (*vikalpa*), can (*alam*) play joyfully. Thus having contemplated the aspect of withdrawal within in one’s heart, She who is my Goddess (*bhagavatī*) who destroys death, and nature is persistence, is pleased. (20) (6) 

7\) Bhadra/Rudrakālī (the withdrawal of perception) 

*tad itthaṁ te tisro nijavibhavavisphāraṇavaśād avāptāḥ ṣaṭcakrakramakṛtapadaṁ śaktaya imāḥ |* *kramād unmeṣeṇa pravidadhati citrāṁ bhavadaśām imābhyo devībhyaḥ pravaṇahṛdayaḥ syāṁ gatabhayaḥ* || 21 || 

The plane created in this way by the Krama of the six wheels (of the first six Kālīs) are these, Your three energies, that have been attained by the unfolding of Your own glorious power (*vibhava*). By unfolding in due order (*kramāt*) (Bhadrakālī) bestows the strange (and wonderful) state (*daśā*) in the world. May I, whose heart is inclined to devotion, be freed from fear by these goddesses. (21) 

*imāṁ rundhe bhūmiṁ bhavabhayabhidātan.kakaraṇīm imāṁ bodhaikāntadrutirasamayīm cāpi vidadhe |* *tad itthaṁ saṁrodhaṁ drutim atha vilupyāśubhatatīr yatheṣṭaṁ cācāraṁ bhajati lasatāṁ sā mama hṛdi* || 22 || 

I am blocking this plane which causes mental distress due to the duality and fear of phenomenal existence and so too I ordain this (state) which is the innerwardly melting juice (of the aesthetic delight) (*rasa*) at one with awakened consciousness. Having destroyed in this way blockage, flux and the inauspicious mass of sacrificial rites (*tati*), she behaves (freely) as she wishes, may She play in my heart. (22) (7) 

8\) Mārtaṇḍakālī (the inxplicable state of perception) 

*kriyābuddhyakṣādeḥ parimitapade mānapadavīm avāptasya sphāraṁ nijanijarucā saṁharati yā |* 

*[p. 7]* ^pg7

*iyaṁ mārtaṇḍasya sthitipadayujaḥ sāram akhilam haṭhād ākarṣantī kṛśatu mama bhedaṁ bhavabhayāt* || 23 || 

She who withdraws (each time), in accord with her own desire, the expansion of the plane of the means of knowledge within the plane of limitation of the senses of knowledge and action of one who has attained (that), draging by force (*haṭhāt*) the entire essence of Mārtaṇḍa who is on the plane of persistence. May she drag my duality out of the fear of phenomenal existence. (23) (8) 

(The Sphere of Subjectivity) 

9\) Paramārkakālī (The emanation of subjectivity) 

*samagrāsakālīṁ*[^2] *kramavirahitā sātmani muhur niveśyānantāntarbahalitamahāraśminivahā |* *parā divyānandaṁ kalayitum udārādaravatī prasannā me bhūyāt hṛdayapadavīṁ bhūṣayatu ca* || 24 || 

Having penetrated within Herself instantly at each moment (*muhur*) the Kālī who, devoid of succession, consumes everything equally, is a multitude of great rays that, endless, inwardly grow compact (and strong). May she, the Supreme (Goddess) Parā who is the receptical of (the most) exaulted respect, be pleased with me and adorn the plane of (my) heart. (24) (9) 

10\) Kālāgnirudrakālī (the persistence of subjectivity) 

*pramāṇe saṁlīne śivapadalasadvaibhavavaśāt śarīraprāṇādir mitakṛtakamātṛsthitimayaḥ |* *yadā kālopādhiḥ pralayapadam āsādayati te tadā devī yāsau lasati mama sā syāc chivamayī* || 25 || 

When the means of knowledge has merged (into consciousness) by the glorious power that is playing on Śiva’s plane, if the body and the vital breath etc and the adjunct of time, which is the abiding state (*sthiti*) of the limited, artificial perceiver, attains the play of destruction, may the goddess who flashes forth playfully (*lasati*) then, be for me of Śiva’s nature. (25) (10) 

11\) Mahākālī (the withdrawal of subjectivity) 

*prakāśākhyā saṁvit kramavirahitā śūnyapadato bahirlīnātyantam prasarati samācchādakatayā |* *tato 'py antaḥsāre galitarabhasād akramatayā mahākālī seyaṁ mama kalayatāṁ kālam akhilam* || 26 || 

Consciousness called Light, devoid of succession, that from the plane of the Void is merged externally and flows forth extensively as that which covers over (and obscures 

[^2]: Read *samagraasakaalii.m* for *samagraamak.saalii.m*

*[p. 8]* ^pg8

all), but even so, falling away into the inner essence with great force as free of succession, may this Mahākālī generate (and process) all my time. (26) (11) 

12\) Mahācaṇḍograkālī (the inexplicable state of subjectivity) 

*tato devyāṁ yasyāṁ paramaparipūrṇasthitijuṣi kramaṁ vicchidyāśu sthitimatirasāt saṁvidadhati |* *pramāṇaṁ mātāraṁ mitim atha samagraṁ jagad idaṁ sthitāṁ kroḍīkṛtya śrayatu mama cittaṁ citim imām* || 27 || 

Then have suddenly cut away the Krama within the goddess who possesses the supreme completely full abiding state, by the aesthetic savour (*rasa*) of the consciousness (*mati*) of persistence, ordains the means of knowledge, perceiver, perception and all this universe having encompassed (them all), may she reside in my mind (*citta*) which is this abiding (*sthitā*) consciousness (*citi*). (27) (12) 

*anargalasvātmamaye maheśe tiṣṭhanti yasmin vibhuśaktayas tāḥ |* *taṁ śaktimantaṁ praṇamāmi devaṁ manthānasaṁjñaṁ jagadekasāram* || 28 || 

I bow to the god called Manthāna (Bhairava), the one essence of the universe Who is the possessor of those energies of (the Lord’s) glorious power that abide within the Great Lord whose is my own unfettered Self. (28) 

*itthaṁ svaśaktikiraṇaughanutiprabandhān ākarṇya deva yadi me vrajasi prasādam |* *tenāśu sarvajanatāṁ nijaśāsanāṁśusaṁśāntitākhila-tamaḥpaṭalāṁ vidheyāḥ* || 29 || 

O God, having heard in this way the continuing series of praises of the current of the rays of your own powers, if You become propitious to me may you thus quickly quell all the veil of darkness of everybody with the rays of Your own teachings. (29) 

*ṣaṭṣaṣṭinām eke varṣe navamyām asite 'hani |* *mayā 'bhinavaguptena mārgaśīrṣe stutaḥ śivaḥ* || 

*iti śrī abhinavaguptapādācāryakṛtaṁ kramastotraṁ sampūrṇam* || 

I, Abhinavagupta praised Śiva on the ninthday of the dark (fortnight) in (the month of) Mārgaśīrṣa during the so called sixty sixth year. (30) 

Thus the *Kramastotra* composed by the venerable Abhinavagupta is complete.
