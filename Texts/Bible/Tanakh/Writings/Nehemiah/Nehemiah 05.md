## Chapter 5

**5:1** There was a great outcry by the common folk and their wives against their brother Jews. ^5-1

**5:2** Some said, “Our sons and daughters are numerous; we must get grain to eat in order that we may live!” ^5-2

**5:3** Others said, “We must pawn our fields, our vineyards, and our homes to get grain to stave off hunger.” ^5-3

**5:4** Yet others said, “We have borrowed money against our fields and vineyards to pay the king’s tax. ^5-4

**5:5** Now we are as good as[^1] our brothers, and our children as good as theirs; yet here we are subjecting our sons and daughters to slavery—some of our daughters are already subjected—and we are powerless, while our fields and vineyards belong to others.” ^5-5

**5:6** It angered me very much to hear their outcry and these complaints. ^5-6

**5:7** After pondering the matter carefully, I censured the nobles and the prefects, saying, “Are you pressing claims on loans made to your brothers?” Then I raised a large crowd against them ^5-7

**5:8** and said to them, “We have done our best to buy back our Jewish brothers who were sold to the nations; will you now sell your brothers so that they must be sold [back] to us?” They kept silent, for they found nothing to answer. ^5-8

**5:9** So I continued, “What you are doing is not right. You ought to act in a God-fearing way so as not to give our enemies, the nations, room to reproach us. ^5-9

**5:10** I, my brothers, and my servants also have claims of money and grain against them; let us now abandon those claims! ^5-10

**5:11** Give back at once their fields, their vineyards, their olive trees, and their homes, and [abandon] the claims for the hundred pieces of silver, the grain, the wine, and the oil that you have been pressing against them!” ^5-11

**5:12** They replied, “We shall give them back, and not demand anything of them; we shall do just as you say.” Summoning the priests, I put them under oath to keep this promise. ^5-12

**5:13** I also shook out the bosom of my garment and said, “So may God shake free of his household and property any man who fails to keep this promise; may he be thus shaken out and stripped.” All the assembled answered, “Amen,” and praised the LORD.  
The people kept this promise. ^5-13

**5:14** Furthermore, from the day I was commissioned to be governor in the land of Judah—from the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes until his thirty-second year, twelve years in all—neither I nor my brothers ever ate of the governor’s food allowance. ^5-14

**5:15** The former governors who preceded me laid heavy burdens on the people, and took from them for bread and wine more than[^2] forty shekels of silver. Their servants also tyrannized over the people. But I, out of the fear of God, did not do so. ^5-15

**5:16** I also supported the work on this wall; we did not buy any land, and all my servants were gathered there at the work. ^5-16

**5:17** Although there were at my table, between Jews and prefects, one hundred and fifty men in all, beside those who came to us from surrounding nations; ^5-17

**5:18** and although what was prepared for each day came to one ox, six select sheep, and fowl, all prepared for me, and at ten-day intervals all sorts of wine in abundance—yet I did not resort to the governor’s food allowance, for the [king’s] service lay heavily on the people. ^5-18

**5:19** O my God, remember to my credit all that I have done for this people! ^5-19

[^1]: Lit. “our flesh is as good as the flesh of.”
[^2]: Lit. “after”; meaning of Heb. uncertain.
