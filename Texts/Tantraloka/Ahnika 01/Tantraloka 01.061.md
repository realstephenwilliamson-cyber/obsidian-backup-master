## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. I
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 01 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v01 06 - Chapter One, part 5#^pg202|p. 188]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 01.pdf#page=202>))
>
> By virtue of His ubiquity, He is all-pervading. As he is eternal, He is free of beginning and end. As He is omniform, He manifests the wonderful variety of things, both sentient and insentient. (with 1.60cd–61ab)
>
> Thus, His pluriform nature is taught, for example, in the Dīkṣottara. Śiva, conjoined with the Point (bindu) and Sound (nāda) etc., is said to be of six kinds, (as World (bhuvana), (Iconic) Form (vigraha), Light (jyotiṣ), Space (kha), (Unstruck) Sound (śabda) and Mantra. (with 1.61cd–62)
