Śrīkaṇṭha declares his Devotion to the Goddess and implores her for the 

Teachings 

Śrīkaṇṭha said: 

In this way you have attained, in accord with (its) own (true) nature, the plane of the Śāmbhava Command (*śāmbhavājñāpada*), beyond the tranquil (state of deep sleep) (*śāntātīta*)<sup>1</sup> which is (both) supreme (transcendence) and inferior (immanence), by abandoning all duality.<sup>2</sup> (1) 

Thus, you, mother,<sup>3</sup> are my teacher. By virtue of (your) wisdom (*yukti*), forgive me (whatever I may have done) wrong (*ayukta*).<sup>4</sup> You are the sole deity in the heart of the mothers, Siddhas and Rudras. You are my energy, Rudra's mother<sup>5</sup> in the Western House. I do not desire sexual pleasure (*kāma*) here (from you).<sup>6</sup> I desire the Command and am greedy for the Transmission (*krama*).<sup>7</sup> (2-3) 

I am your proper (*samyak*) disciple, attentive and of lucid intellect. Tell me the Kula tradition (*kulāmnāya*) that has come down through the transmission of the series (of teachers) (*pāramparyakrama*)), the great arising of the lineage (*santāna*) which is the current (*ogha*) of the transmission, divine and human.<sup>8</sup> (4-5ab) 

O mistress of the gods, (tell) me of the eight signs (of attainment) (*pratyaya*)<sup>9</sup> and the school (*mata*) of the division beginning with Kā (*kādibheda*).<sup>10</sup> Give me, O supreme mother, what is called (the knowledge of) the seventy million (verses).<sup>11</sup> (5cd-6ab) 

In the same way (impart the teaching transmitted through) the little garland of letters (*akṣaramālikā*) written in the *Vāyupurāṇa* and other (such scriptures). (Give me), the God of the gods, who is devoid of the signs of attainment (*pratyaya*) and (your empowering) Command, the final purport (*niścaya*) (of the teachings). A state of uncertainty prevails (*vaikalpyaṁ bhavate*) (within me) and so I beg (for the empowering) Command (transmitted) in the Western (tradition).<sup>12</sup> (6cd-7)

(Thus) the Command (the god) possesses of the venerable (goddess) (*śrīkārā*) operates again in the Tradition of the Masters (*gurvāmnāya*). The goddess's (*śāmbhavī*) Command is there (and) Śrīnātha obtains (it) in the Age of Strife. In order to liberate everybody, he will come down into the world. This truth is the ancient purport (of the scripture) also (realised) by practising devotion to you. It is the *Śrīmata*, that is, the Western Tradition (*paścimāmnāya*) that has come through the transmission of the root lineage (*mūlānvaya*).<sup>13</sup> (8-10ab) 

Tell (me this) Transmission (*krama*), the Tradition of the Masters, the Kaulika Tradition endowed with vibrant energy (*sasphura*)<sup>14</sup> that is free of the sphere of *rajas* and devoid of (the six) types (*jāti*) and *praṇava*.<sup>15</sup> (10cd-11ab) 

Having heard the auspicious discourse of the god, the goddess who resides in the Liṅga of Fire (*tejoliṅga*),<sup>16</sup> inhabited by an intense Command (*tīvrājñā*),<sup>17</sup> uttered thus a mighty speech (*mahāvāṇī*). (11cd-12ab) 

The venerable Vakrā said: 

That which is this Western path is the Śrīkrama, the most excellent; it is the abode of all the Siddhas and, in a special way (*saviśeṣa*), a Śāmbhava (tradition). O Śambhu, it is a (Tradition of the) Lion (*siṁhaka*)<sup>18</sup> and brings about possession (*āveśa*)<sup>19</sup> by just recalling (it). (12cd-13) 

(The goddess) Jyeṣṭhā, who is the sequence of seventeen (lunar energies) has come (here) by means of the sequence (*krama*) of masters and disciples by the grace of their Command and ours.<sup>20</sup> O god, how will you become their disciple? O god, by transgressing Jyeṣṭhā's Command, the Command declines. One who is devoid of the Command is lamentable and considered to be outside the clan (*gotra*).<sup>21</sup> You are the lord of all the gods, I have seen none greater than you. (Moreover) I am within your (very) form,<sup>22</sup> so how can I be (your) teacher? (14-17ab) 

Śrīkaṇṭha said: 

 Kaulinī’s<sup>23</sup> Command pulses radiantly also by virtue of the transmission through the series (of teachers) (*pāramparyakrama*) (just as it does when it is first transmitted). If (that ultimate) reality (*padārtha*) has been realised (*dṛṣta* lit. 'perceived'), than what defect can there be in the Kula?<sup>24</sup> There is (only) one essence in (both) Kula and Kaula<sup>25</sup> and (so) I am, once again, their disciple. (17cd-18)

O goddess, the one who is the eldest (*jyeṣṭhā*)<sup>26</sup> in due order (*anukrama*) on this, the Path of the Current (of the teaching) (*ughamārga*) in the Kula of the Eldest One (*jyeṣṭhākula*) (is you) not me. Grace (me)! (19) 

You are the saviour, my venerable teacher, I worship you in the midst of the three (transmissions). I see no defect here. The Command of the teacher is its own light.<sup>27</sup> (20) 

The Command is the five-fold energy located in the womb (*yoni*) of the Liṅga (*liṅgayoni*). (These five aspects are) mantra, Vidyā, the supreme gesture (*parāmudrā*), the Yoginī and the deity of the Kula (*kuladevatā*). (21) 

Here within it (*atrodare*)<sup>28</sup> is the entire universe, the Transmission (*krama*) as well as the Great Transmission (*mahākrama*). O great goddess, (I am the one who) desires the Command here in (this) life. O supreme mother! having entered that (Liṅga),<sup>29</sup> give me, for the sake of (my) devotion, the knowledge which is the Great Transmission.<sup>30</sup> (22-23ab) 

Devotion in the disciple and the teacher's Command - if both have been attained then this Kaulikī Command descends (into the world).<sup>31</sup> Its sacred abode (*āśrama*) is this Liṅga whose form is your aggregate of energies (*śaktikula*). (23cd-24) 

The authority in the tradition of the transmission through the series (of teachers) (*pāramparyakramāmnāya*) that has come through the Transmission is the Liṅga of the Command (*ājñāliṅga*), greatly accomplished (*mahāsiddha*), it is free of delusion and thought constructs. Its scripture (*śāstra*) and Tantra is this one (consisting) of a hundred and twenty-five thousand (verses).<sup>32</sup> (25-26ab) 

O great goddess, (I have) approached (you with devotion) permit me to play (with you). O goddess, I have been told (by you) that no one is my equal. O fair one! the darling of (this devotee) who has come to take refuge! You have proclaimed the entire descent of the (Western) House onto the top of (mount) Meru on the head. (26cd-28ab)

One should tell (only) one's own disciple of the mortal incarnation (*martyāvatāra*) (of the deity in the world) during the Age of Strife. During the sacrifice he (will) bestow accomplishment and, worshipped, grant boons. Once all have received libation and have been initiated, (the deity) will descend (into the world). (Whatever) one earns illegally will be fruitless and bring about the transgression of the Command. (Thus he will be) worried every day.<sup>33</sup> (So) once performed a (purifying) sacrifice in (one's own) home (*gṛhayāga*), that tradition (*gṛha*) should be embraced (*saṁgrāhya*) again.<sup>34</sup> (28cd-31ab) 

Śrīkaṇṭha said: 

Then the god, having deeply pondered (*mathitvā* lit. \`churned') the Supreme Principle,<sup>35</sup> began (to intone) with divine praise and blissful voice, a divine hymn that enlightens as to the entire meaning (of the scripture). Thus there, in the Western (tradition) he composed with devotion a composition in prose (*daṇḍaka*) with seed-syllables (called) the *Mālinīstava*. (31cd-33ab) 

His is this speech of the deity (*devatāvāṇī*) which, by virtue (of the goddess's) Command (*ādeśa*), is endowed with the great current (of the teaching). In this way, the form of the Mālinīkula<sup>36</sup> is accompanied by that (Speech). Possessing twelve divisions,<sup>37</sup> it is viewed (with grace) by the Command of (the goddess's divine) Form (*mūrti*).<sup>38</sup> (33cd-34) 

Thus, by (this) great hymn the lord will get the Command. Then Śrīkaṇṭha, the yogi, approached (the goddess with devotion) in order (to receive) the grace of (her) Command. By means of this kind of offering, with the flowers of speech heralded by hymns of praise and with sacrifices of breath control and the like, (he performed) austerities for a period of a hundred divine years. (Then), with (his) mind solely intent on worship, the god praised (the goddess) Bhuvanamālinī ('She who wears the worlds as her garland')<sup>39</sup> with many kinds of hymns. (35-37)

Be victorious,<sup>41</sup> O goddess Mālinī,<sup>42</sup> you who are pure!<sup>43</sup> The destroyer of impurity! O goddess, (you) are the lord (*prabhu*), the power of knowledge,<sup>44</sup> you are the intellect and you increase the radiant energy (*tejas*) (of consciousness).<sup>45</sup> (38) 

(You) are the mother of all living beings<sup>46</sup> residing in this world of transmigratory existence. (You are) the mother, the goddess Virāvalī.<sup>47</sup> O dear one, be compassionate!<sup>48</sup> (39)
