### 6.1–8: Piety

*[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 22* ^6-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Piety* (Gk “dikaiosynē”), lit., “righteousness” (in LXX for Heb “tzedakah”); see 1.19n.; 3.15; 5.6,10,20; 6.33; 9.13; 21.32; 25.37,46; see also [[I Samuel 16#^16-7|1 Sam 16.7]]; [[Romans 02#^2-28|Rom 2.28–29]]; *To be seen by them*, on practicing righteousness in private, see *b. Sukk.* 49b: the Torah enjoins one to “walk humbly” regarding matters of public (and private) prayer and ritual. *Reward*, see *m. Avot* 1.3 (“Be not like servants who serve their master for the sake of receiving a reward”). *Father in heaven*, see 5.45n. ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-2|2]]:** *Alms,* see Deut 14.28–29; 15.11; 19.24; [[LXX Tobit 04#^4-7|Tob 4.7]]; [[LXX Tobit 12#^12-8|12.8]]; see also *b. Git*. 11b; almsgiving is an increasingly valued practice in Hellenistic and rabbinic Judaism. *Trumpet*, Jews did not, contrary to popular Christian preaching, have trumpets announce donations. *Hypocrite*, originally a Gk term for “actor,” came to mean one who seeks public praise or is deceitful; Matthew uses it to refer usually to Pharisees (6.2,5,16; 7.5; 15.7; 22.18; 23 passim; see [[Habakkuk 2#^2-5|Hab 2.5]]; [[Proverbs 21#^21-24|Prov 21.24]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-25|2 Macc 6.25]]; *m. Avot* 1.3; *b. Sanh*. 103a; *Pss. Sol*. 4.1–6,20); the rabbis use the term “hypocrite” to refer to one of four classes of people (along with liars, scoffers, and slanderers) who will not bask in the glory of the divine presence, in Heb “Shekhinah.” The polemic likely reflects rivalries in Matthew’s own time. *Synagogues*, see 4.23n. ^6-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-4|4]]:** *Alms may be done in secret*, the twelfth-century Jewish philosopher Maimonides advocated secret almsgiving as the second-highest of his eight levels of “tzedakah”, or charity (the highest is supporting a fellow Jew through financial means or other forms of assistance). See also *b. Sukk*. 49b where the rabbis discuss “gemilut chasadim,” the idea that “deeds of loving kindness” can supersede charity. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-5|5–6]]:** See [[Luke 18#^18-10|Lk 18.10–14]]. *Whenever you pray*, traditionally, Jews prayers are said three times daily: morning, late afternoon, and early evening ([[Daniel 06#^6-11|Dan 6.10]]; see also [[Acts 03#^3-1|Acts 3.1]]; [[Acts 10#^10-2|10.2–3]],[[Acts 10#^10-30|30]]; *Did*. 8.3; *m. Ber*. 4.1; *t. Ber*. 3.6; *b. Ber*. 26b). Jesus is not forbidding public prayer but condemning those who seek attention by their manner of praying (see 23.5–7; [[Isaiah 26#^26-20|Isa 26.20]]). ^6-5

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-7|7]]:** *Empty phrases*, *b. Ber.* 55a condemns verbosity in prayer. ^6-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-8|8]]:** *Your Father knows*, the Jewish philosopher Philo indicated that God already knows the reason(s) for one’s prayers, but still desires to be sought out for assistance. See Philo, *Life of Moses* 2.217. ^6-8

### 6.9–15: The “Our Father” or

*[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-9|Text]], p. 23* · **“Lord’s Prayer”** ([[Luke 11#^11-2|Lk 11.2–4]]). ^6-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-9|9]]:** *Father in heaven*, see 5.45n. *Hallowed be your name*, see [[Psalms 105#^105-3|Ps 105.3]], “Glory in his holy name,” the Aramaic “Kaddish” prayer (from Heb “qadosh,” “holy,” e.g., [[Isaiah 06#^6-3|Isa 6.3]], source of the prayer), which became more common during the Talmudic era (*b.* Yebam. 79a; *b*. *Sot.* 49a; *Sifre Deut*. 32.3; *Midr. Ps*. 25.13), begins, “May his great name grow exalted and glorified” (see [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-32|Lev 22.32]]; Isa 23.23; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-23|Ezek 36.23]]; [[Psalms 113#^113-2|Ps 113.2]]; cf. [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-3|Deut 32.3]]; Jn 17; *Did*. 8.2; 9.2–4; 1QM 11.15). ^6-9-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-10|10]]:** *Your will be done*, the rabbis emphasize obedience to divine will (*m. Avot* 1.11; *t. Ber*. 3.7; *b. Ber*. 29b; *b. Meg*. 29b; *b. Yoma* 53b; 86b). *As it is in heaven*, angels have no independent power (e.g., Job 1–2). ^6-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-11|11]]:** *Daily bread*, likely reflecting an unknown Aramaic original, possibly “leḥma maḥar,” meaning “bread for tomorrow” or the eschatological banquet. ^6-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-12|12]]:** *Debts,* sins were considered “debts” ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-1|Deut 15.1–2]]; see also *Gen. Rab*. 85.2; 92.9; *Ex. Rab*. 25.6; 31.1; *Pesiq. Rab.* 11.23; 51.8). Cf. [[Luke 11#^11-4|Lk 11.4]], which uses “sin.” See 18.23–35n. ^6-12

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Time of trial*, or testing, see [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-3|2 Thess 3.3]]; [[James 01#^1-13|Jas 1.13]]. *Evil one*, Satan. *B. Ber.* 60b offers examples of similar prayers. ^6-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-14|14]]:** *Father will also forgive you*, God forgives those who forgive others (5.21–48; 18.35; see also [[LXX Sirach 28#^28-1|Sir 28.1–8]]). ^6-14

### 6.16–18: Fasting

*[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-16|Text]], p. 23* · A form of Jewish religious devotion associated with mourning, repentance, and self-discipline; Jesus fasted (4.1; see also 9.14–17; [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^16-34|Lev 16.34]]; [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^29-7|Num 29.7–11]]; [[II Samuel 12#^12-22|2 Sam 12.22–23]]; [[I Kings 21#^21-27|1 Kings 21.27–29]]; [[Isaiah 58#^58-3|Isa 58.3–7]]; [[Jeremiah 14#^14-11|Jer 14.11–12]]; [[Joel 1#^1-14|Joel 1.14]]; [[Joel 2#^2-15|2.15]]; Jon 3.5; [[Zechariah 08#^8-19|Zech 8.19]]; [[Daniel 09#^9-3|Dan 9.3]]; [[LXX Judith 08#^8-5|Jdt 8.5]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 03#^3-47|1 Macc 3.47]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 3.47; *t. Taʾan* 2.4). ^6-16

### 6.19–34: Wealth

*[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-19|Text]], p. 23* · ([[Mark 04#^4-21|Mk 4.21–23]]; [[Luke 12#^12-33|Lk 12.33–34]]; [[Luke 11#^11-33|11.33–34]]; [[Luke 16#^16-13|16.13]]; [[Luke 12#^12-22|12.22–31]]). ^6-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-19|19–20]]:** *Treasures on earth*, some Jewish traditions decry obsession with material wealth ([[Proverbs 11#^11-4|Prov 11.4]]; [[LXX Sirach 31#^31-8|Sir 31.8–11]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 2.50; *y. Peʾah* 1.1; *1 En*. 63.10; 94.8; 96.4; 97.8; 1QS 10.18–19; 11.2; CD 4.17; 8.7). *Treasures in heaven* ([[Luke 12#^12-19|Lk 12.19–21]]), form, *For the kingdom and the power and the glory are yours forever. Amen.* d Other ancient authorities add *openly* e Gk *eating* f Gk *mammon* g Other ancient authorities lack *or what you will drink* h Or *add one cubit to your height* eschatological reward accumulated through right practice (see *2 Bar*. 24.1; *T. Job* 33; *b. B. Bat. 11a*). ^6-19-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-21|21]]:** See 5.8n. ^6-21

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-22|22]]:** *Full of light,* see 5.6; [[Luke 16#^16-8|Lk 16.8]]. Jewish sources connect light and spiritual goodness ([[Isaiah 60#^60-20|Isa 60.20]]; [[Habakkuk 3#^3-3|Hab 3.3–4]]; [[Psalms 056#^56-14|Ps 56.13]]; 104.2; [[Proverbs 15#^15-30|Prov 15.30]]; [[Daniel 02#^2-22|Dan 2.22]]; [[LXX Tobit 10#^10-5|Tob 10.5]]; [[LXX Tobit 11#^11-14|11.14]]). ^6-22

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-24|24]]:** *Wealth*, lit., “mammon,” Gk “mamonas,” transliteration of Aram “mamona,” a word that came to mean “riches” but which originally was derived from “ ʾaman,” “trust, reliance,” meaning “that in which \[other than God\] one places one’s trust”; see [[Luke 16#^16-9|Lk 16.9]],[[Luke 16#^16-11|11]],[[Luke 16#^16-13|13]]. ^6-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-25|25]]:** [[Luke 10#^10-41|Lk 10.41]]; [[Luke 12#^12-11|12.11]]; [[Philippians 04#^4-6|Phil 4.6]]. The promise recalls [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^28-1|Deut 28.1–14]]. ^6-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-26|26]]:** *Birds . . . Are you not of more value? B. Qidd.* 82b makes a similar argument, stating that if animals (were created to) serve humankind without trouble, then how much more must humans serve God without trouble. For “qal vahomer” (“light and heavy”) rabbinic arguments, see *m. Qidd*. 4.14; *b. Pesah*. 66a; *b. Sanh*. 17a; *Gen. Rab*. 92.7. See also 10.24; 12.10–12; 20.26–28. ^6-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-29|29]]:** *Solomon*, David’s son (1.6–7). ^6-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-30|30]]:** *Little faith*, a frequent charge against the disciples (8.26; 14.31; 16.8; 17.20). ^6-30

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-33|33]]:** See 1.19n.; [[Mark 10#^10-29|Mk 10.29–30]]; [[Luke 18#^18-29|Lk 18.29–30]]. ^6-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 06#^6-34|34]]:** *Today’s trouble is enough,* see *b. Yebam*. 63b, “Do not worry about tomorrow’s troubles. . . .” ^6-34
