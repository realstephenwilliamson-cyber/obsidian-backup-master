## Chapter 14

**14:1** But the LORD will pardon Jacob, and will again choose Israel, and will settle them on their own soil. And strangers shall join them and shall cleave to the House of Jacob. ^14-1

**14:2** For peoples shall take them[^1] and bring them to their homeland; and the House of Israel shall possess them[^2] as slaves and handmaids on the soil of the LORD. They shall be captors of their captors and masters to their taskmasters. ^14-2

**14:3** And when the LORD has given you rest from your sorrow and trouble, and from the hard service that you were made to serve, ^14-3

**14:4** you shall recite this song of scorn over the king of Babylon:  
How is the taskmaster vanished,  
How is oppression[^3] ended! ^14-4

**14:5** The LORD has broken the staff of the wicked,  
The rod of tyrants, ^14-5

**14:6** That smote peoples in wrath  
With stroke unceasing,  
That belabored nations in fury  
In relentless pursuit. ^14-6

**14:7** All the earth is calm, untroubled;  
Loudly it cheers. ^14-7

**14:8** Even pines rejoice at your fate,  
And cedars of Lebanon:  
“Now that you have lain down,  
None shall come up to fell us.” ^14-8

**14:9** Sheol below was astir  
To greet your coming—  
Rousing for you the shades  
Of all earth’s chieftains,  
Raising from their thrones  
All the kings of nations. ^14-9

**14:10** All speak up and say to you,  
“So you have been stricken as we were,  
You have become like us! ^14-10

**14:11** Your pomp is brought down to Sheol,  
And the strains of your lutes!  
Worms are to be your bed,  
Maggots your blanket!” ^14-11

**14:12** How are you fallen from heaven,  
O Shining One, son of Dawn![^4]  
How are you felled to earth,  
O vanquisher of nations! ^14-12

**14:13** Once you thought in your heart,  
“I will climb to the sky;  
Higher than the stars of God  
I will set my throne.  
I will sit in the mount of assembly,[^5]  
On the summit of Zaphon:[^6] ^14-13

**14:14** I will mount the back of a cloud—  
I will match the Most High.” ^14-14

**14:15** Instead, you are brought down to Sheol,  
To the bottom of the Pit.[^7] ^14-15

**14:16** They who behold you stare;  
They peer at you closely:  
“Is this the man  
Who shook the earth,  
Who made realms tremble, ^14-16

**14:17** Who made the world like a waste  
And wrecked its towns,  
[^8]Who never released his prisoners to their homes?” ^14-17

**14:18** All the kings of nations  
Were laid, every one, in honor  
Each in his tomb; ^14-18

**14:19** While you were left lying unburied,  
Like loathsome carrion,[^9]  
Like a trampled corpse  
[In] the clothing of slain gashed by the sword  
Who sink to the very stones of the Pit. ^14-19

**14:20** You shall not have a burial like them;  
Because you destroyed your country,  
Murdered your people.[^10]  
Let the breed of evildoers  
Nevermore be named! ^14-20

**14:21** Prepare a slaughtering block for his sons  
Because of the guilt of their father.[^11]  
Let them not arise to possess the earth!  
Then the world’s face shall be covered with towns. ^14-21

**14:22** I will rise up against them—declares the LORD of Hosts—and will wipe out from Babylon name and remnant, kith and kin—declares the LORD— ^14-22

**14:23** and I will make it a home of bitterns,[^12] pools of water. I will sweep it with a broom of extermination—declares the LORD of Hosts. ^14-23

**14:24** The LORD of Hosts has sworn this oath:  
“As I have designed, so shall it happen;  
What I have planned, that shall come to pass: ^14-24

**14:25** To break Assyria in My land,  
To crush him on My mountain.”[^13]  
And his yoke shall drop off them,  
And his burden shall drop from their[^14] backs. ^14-25

**14:26** That is the plan that is planned  
For all the earth;  
That is why an arm is poised  
Over all the nations. ^14-26

**14:27** For the LORD of Hosts has planned,  
Who then can foil it?  
It is His arm that is poised,  
And who can stay it? ^14-27

**14:28** This pronouncement was made in the year that King Ahaz died: ^14-28

**14:29** Rejoice not, all Philistia,  
Because the staff of him that beat you is broken.  
For from the stock of a snake there sprouts an asp,  
A flying seraph[^15] branches out from it. ^14-29

**14:30** The first-born of the poor shall graze[^16]  
And the destitute lie down secure.  
I will kill your stock by famine,[^17]  
And it shall slay the very last of you. ^14-30

**14:31** Howl, O gate; cry out, O city;  
Quake, all Philistia!  
For a stout one is coming from the north  
And there is no straggler in his ranks.[^18] ^14-31

**14:32** And what will he answer the messengers of any nation?  
That Zion has been established by the LORD:  
In it, the needy of His people shall find shelter. ^14-32

[^1]: I.e., the House of Jacob.
[^2]: I.e., the peoples.
[^3]: Reading marhebah with 1QIsa (cf. Septuagint). The traditional reading madhebah is of unknown meaning.
[^4]: A character in some lost myth.
[^5]: I.e., the assembly of the gods in council.
[^6]: The abode of the gods; cf. Ps. 48.3.
[^7]: A region of the netherworld reserved for those who have not received decent burial; cf. Ezek. 32.21 ff.
[^8]: Emendation yields “Who chained to his palace gate / All the kings of nations? / Yet they were all laid in honor…” The practice of chaining captive chieftains to gates is attested in Mesopotamia.
[^9]: So several ancient versions; cf. postbiblical neṣel, “putrefying flesh or blood.”
[^10]: Emendation yields “…countries, / Murdered peoples.”
[^11]: Heb. “fathers.”
[^12]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^13]: Heb. “mountains”; for the designation of the entire land of Israel as the Lord’s mountain, cf. 11.9.
[^14]: Heb. “his.” The last two lines of this verse would read well after v. 26.
[^15]: Others “fiery serpent”; cf. Num. 21.6, 8.
[^16]: Emendation yields “The poor shall graze in his pasture.” This line and the next would read well after v. 32.
[^17]: Emendation yields “It shall kill your offspring with its venom (zar‘ekh berosho).”
[^18]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain; the rendering “stout one” is suggested by the Syriac ‘ashsh™†n.
