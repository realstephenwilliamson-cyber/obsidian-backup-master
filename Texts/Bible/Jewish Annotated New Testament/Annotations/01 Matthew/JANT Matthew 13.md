### 13.1–53: Parables of the kingdom

*[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 35* ^13-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-1|1–3]]:** Introduction to the parables ([[Mark 04#^4-1|Mk 4.1]]). ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Parables*, Gk “parabolē,” meaning “comparison” (cf. Heb “mashal”; see “Midrash and Parables,” p. 707). Parables appear in various forms throughout Jewish literature ([[II Samuel 12#^12-1|2 Sam 12.1–7]]; [[Isaiah 05#^5-1|Isa 5.1–7]]; [[LXX Sirach 01#^1-24|Sir 1.24]]; [[LXX Sirach 03#^3-29|3.29]]; [[LXX Sirach 20#^20-20|20.20]]; [[LXX Sirach 39#^39-2|39.2]]; [[LXX Sirach 47#^47-17|47.17]]; *1 En*. 1.2–3; 37–71; *T. Job* 18.7–8; *m. Sot*. 9.15; *t. Sot.* 5.9; *b. Ber.* 61b). ^13-3

### 13.3–9: Parable of the sower

*[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-3|Text]], p. 35* · ([[Mark 04#^4-2|Mk 4.2–9]]; [[Luke 08#^8-4|Lk 8.4–8]]). Cf. *Gos. Thom*. 9. ^13-3-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-3|3]]:** *Sower*, here, likely Jesus (13.37). “Sowing” related to doing the work of God ([[Jeremiah 31#^31-27|Jer 31.27–28]]; [[Ezekiel 36#^36-9|Ezek 36.9]]; [[Hosea 02#^2-23|Hos 2.21–23]]; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 9.31; *2 Bar*. 32.1). *Seeds*, later identified as “the word of the kingdom” (13.19). *Path . . . rocky ground. . . thorns*, do not change their nature. ^13-3-3

### 13.10–23: Jesus explains his teaching

*[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-10|Text]], p. 35* · ([[Mark 04#^4-10|Mk 4.10–12]]; [[Luke 08#^8-9|Lk 8.9–10]],[[Luke 08#^8-18|18]]; [[Luke 19#^19-26|19.26]]; [[John 12#^12-40|Jn 12.40]]). ^13-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-11|11]]:** *Secrets*, indicating a divine plan previously unrevealed, which typified Jewish apocalyptic literature of the Second Temple Period ([[Daniel 02#^2-18|Dan 2.18]],[[Daniel 02#^2-19|19]],[[Daniel 02#^2-27|27]]; 1QpHab 7.8; 1QS 3.23; 1QM 3.9). ^13-11

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-13|13]]:** *Nor do they understand*, the message is open to a select few (e.g., the disciples; see 13.16–17) having faith in Jesus. ^13-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-14|14–15]]:** [[Isaiah 06#^6-9|Isa 6.9–10]]; [[Luke 08#^8-10|Lk 8.10]]; [[John 12#^12-37|Jn 12.37–41]]; [[Acts 28#^28-26|Acts 28.26–27]]; an admonition aimed at anyone who fails to grasp Jesus’ teachings. Even though Isa 6 refers to the prophet’s immediate audience, it is understood here as being fulfilled over seven hundred years later. ^13-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-16|16–17]]:** See [[Luke 10#^10-23|Lk 10.23–24]]. ^13-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-18|18–23]]:** [[Mark 04#^4-13|Mk 4.13–20]]; [[Luke 08#^8-11|Lk 8.11–15]]. Jewish parables often included explanations (e.g., Isa 5; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 4.13-18,20–21). ^13-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-19|19]]:** *Evil one*, see 8.16n.; 6.13; 13.19,38; [[John 17#^17-15|Jn 17.15]]; *Jub.* 11.11–12; *Apoc. Abr.* 13. ^13-19

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-24|13.24–30]]:** **Parable of the wheat and the weeds** ([[Mark 04#^4-13|Mk 4.13–20]]; [[Luke 08#^8-11|Lk 8.11–15]]), see. *Gos. Thom*. 57. ^13-24

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-25|25]]:** *Enemy*, the devil (v 39). ^13-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-30|30]]:** *Both of them grow together*, unfaithful people in the church will be uprooted in the eschaton. *Harvest*, metaphor for final judgment ([[Jeremiah 51#^51-33|Jer 51.33]]; [[Hosea 06#^6-11|Hos 6.11]]; [[Joel 4#^4-13|Joel 3.13]]). ^13-30

### 13.31–52: Parables of mustard seed and yeast

*[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-31|Text]], p. 36* · ([[Mark 04#^4-30|Mk 4.30–34]]; [[Luke 13#^13-18|Lk 13.18–21]]), see *Gos. Thom*. 8,20,76,96,109. ^13-31

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-31|31–32]]:** *Mustard seed*, symbolizing smallness (17.20; *m. Nidd*. 5.2; *m. Tehar.* 8.8, referring to pieces of dough “as small as mustard seed”; cf. [[Luke 17#^17-6|Lk 17.6]]), although the plant can grow as high as five feet. *Becomes a tree*, technically, in botanical terms, a bush; Jesus is utilizing hyperbole to refer to the growth of the kingdom of heaven ([[Ezekiel 17#^17-23|Ezek 17.23]]; [[Ezekiel 31#^31-5|31.5]]; [[Daniel 04#^4-17|Dan 4.17–19]]). ^13-31-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-33|33]]:** *Yeast*, which permeates and expands. *Three measures*, approximately 60 pounds; like the previous parable, an account of unexpected exaggeration. ^13-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-35|35]]:** [[Psalms 078#^78-2|Ps 78.2]] (also utilizing LXX, [[LXX Psalms 77#^77-2|Ps 77.2]]). ^13-35

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-37|37]]:** *Son of Man*, see 8.20n. ^13-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-42|42]]:** See 8.12n.; 22.13. *Furnace*, represents judgment ([[Daniel 03#^3-6|Dan 3.6]]; *1 En*. 54.6; *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 7.36). ^13-42

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-43|43]]:** [[Daniel 12#^12-3|Dan 12.3]]. ^13-43

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-44|44]]:** See [[Proverbs 02#^2-4|Prov 2.4]]; [[LXX Sirach 20#^20-30|Sir. 20.30]]; *2 Bar*. 6.7–9. According to rabbinic sources, once a person acquired property, all contents belonged to the purchaser (see *m. B. Bat.* 4.8–9) ^13-44

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-45|45]]:** *Pearls*, Jewish sources relate pearls to piety and Torah study (*Pesiq. Rab*. 23.6; *Avot de R. Natan* 18 A; cf. *Acts Pet*. 20). ^13-45

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-48|48]]:** Fishermen in the Sea of Galilee would have had to separate kosher and non-kosher fish from their nets. ^13-48

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-52|52]]:** *Scribe . . . trained for the kingdom of heaven*, elsewhere, scribes are condemned (ch 23), suggesting this positive reference relates to Matthew’s own scribes (see 5.17–20; 8.18; 9.3; 13.52; 15.1; 20.18; 21.15; 23). *What is new and what is old* (see 9.17n.), implies that older Torah teachings are still valid, but Jesus’ new interpretations must be heeded as well. The rabbis also utilized “old” and “new” to refer to the teachings of the Torah and the scribal interpretations of those teachings, respectively (see *b. Eruv*. 21b). ^13-52

### 13.53–58: Rejection in Nazareth

*[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-53|Text]], p. 37* · ([[Mark 06#^6-1|Mk 6.1–6]]; [[Luke 04#^4-16|Lk 4.16–30]]; [[John 04#^4-44|Jn 4.44]]). See 12.46–50n. ^13-53

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-54|54]]:** *Hometown,* Nazareth, see 2.23n*.* ^13-54

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-55|55]]:** *Carpenter* (Gk “tektōn”), a builder, not just a woodworker. ^13-55

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-57|57]]:** *Took offense*, Hebrew prophets were also rejected ([[Jeremiah 11#^11-21|Jer 11.21–23]]; [[Jeremiah 12#^12-6|12.6]]; Am 7.10–17). *Prophets are not without honor*, this gave rise to the Hebrew expression in the renaissance, “ ʿein navi beʾiro,” a person is not considered a prophet in his own city. ^13-57

**[[NRSVue Matthew 13#^13-58|58]]:** *Did not do many deeds*, Matthew makes the lack of miracles a matter of volition, not capability (contrast [[Mark 06#^6-5|Mk 6.5]]). ^13-58
