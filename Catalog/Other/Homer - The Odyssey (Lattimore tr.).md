## About

An ancient Greek epic poem traditionally attributed to Homer. Odysseus, king of Ithaca, takes ten years to come home after the Trojan War, while suitors court his wife Penelope and his son Telemachus goes in search of news of him.

- **Author:** Homer, by ancient tradition. Wikipedia says most scholars now take the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* to have been composed independently, within long oral traditions. World History Encyclopedia reports that some think Homer wrote the *Odyssey* late in life and others give it to a different poet. The debates over authorship are known as the Homeric Question.
- **Date:** Wikipedia gives around the 8th or 7th century BC, with no consensus. World History Encyclopedia gives the 8th century BC, adding that some scholars prefer the 6th. Britannica places Homer himself, with a question mark, in the 9th or 8th century BC.
- **Form:** Homeric Greek in dactylic hexameter; Wikipedia counts 12,109 lines in 24 books. The book divisions were probably made long after composition; World History Encyclopedia credits them to scholars at Alexandria.
- **Story:** the action covers about the last six weeks of the ten years (Britannica; World History Encyclopedia says 42 days), with the earlier wanderings told in flashback. Odysseus loses all his companions, comes home in disguise, wins an archery contest, kills the 108 suitors and is reunited with Penelope.
- **Lattimore:** Richmond Lattimore (1906–1984), professor of Greek at Bryn Mawr College from 1935 to 1971, published *The Odyssey of Homer* (New York: Harper & Row) in 1965, after his *Iliad* of 1951.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia counts it among the most significant works of the Western canon; a 2018 BBC Culture poll of experts ranked it literature's most enduring narrative, and Edith Hall says it has been regarded as "the very birthplace of literary fiction". Joyce's *Ulysses* (1922) is among its many later reworkings.
