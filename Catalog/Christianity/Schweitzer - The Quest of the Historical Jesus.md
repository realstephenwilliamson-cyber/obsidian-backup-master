## About

Albert Schweitzer's (1875–1965) critical history of the attempts, from the late 18th century on, to write a life of Jesus. He shows each portrait reflecting its author's own age, and argues that Jesus must be understood through the late Jewish eschatology he held: he expected history to end soon, with God's final judgment.

- **1905–06:** written in 1905, before Schweitzer began medical studies, and published at Tübingen by J. C. B. Mohr in 1906 as *Von Reimarus zu Wrede: eine Geschichte der Leben-Jesu-Forschung* (418 pages).
- **1910:** W. Montgomery's English translation of the first edition, *The Quest of the Historical Jesus: A Critical Study of Its Progress from Reimarus to Wrede*. Gathercole (2000) says it was reprinted many times and that the first edition has had the most influence in English-speaking scholarship.
- **1913:** the second German edition, retitled *Geschichte der Leben-Jesu-Forschung* and enlarged to 650 pages. Two new chapters (22–23) answer Drews's denial that Jesus existed and survey about 40 scholars' replies; Schweitzer concludes that Jesus' existence is "exceedingly likely".
- **2001:** Fortress Press's "first complete edition" in English, edited by John Bowden, 608 pages. Wikipedia describes it as the expanded second edition, translated by Montgomery, J. R. Coates, Susan Cupitt and Bowden; the publisher's page quotes a review saying it is based on the ninth German edition (1984).
- **Argument:** Gathercole reads two aims: a critical one, replacing the liberal German Jesus with an apocalyptic prophet, and a dogmatic one, to destroy dogmatically what had been re-established critically. Fortress's page notes that the book also treats the messianic secret and the nature of the kingdom of God.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says it made Schweitzer's reputation and is said to have halted historical-Jesus research for decades, until the "Second Quest" of Ernst Käsemann, Günther Bornkamm and others. It reports Bart Ehrman's view that Schweitzer's apocalyptic Jesus has held since.
