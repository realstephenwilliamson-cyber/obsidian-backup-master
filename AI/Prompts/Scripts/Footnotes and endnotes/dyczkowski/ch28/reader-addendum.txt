CHAPTER TWENTY-EIGHT (vol. 10, notes v10-02 to v10-07): what is different from the brief (2 Nov 2026)

1. The OCR lost the footnote numbers in this chapter, so a pre-pass made the definitions from the page images: it found
   each footnote's start on the image (a line at the margin of the footnote zone that begins with a raised superscript
   number), read the superscript where it could (digit templates), numbered the starts through the chapter (the readings
   as anchors, the rest by sequence), and made `[^n]: ` at the matching place in the note, often splitting a line where
   the converter had run a page's footnotes together. pages.txt shows, under each page, what the pre-pass did
   ("pre-pass:" lines) and its flags. Your TASK.txt names the numbers the pre-pass expects on your pages and any gaps.
2. CHECK EVERY DEFINITION ON YOUR PAGES against the image: its label must be the printed superscript number at the start of
   that footnote, and the definition must begin where that footnote begins. Fix a wrong label or a wrong start with def
   ops (brief, op 2). Where the pre-pass missed a footnote (a gap in the numbers; a page "with no footnote defined" may
   still hold one), make it: old = its first words (with any garble mark just before them), new = `[^n]: ` + the same
   words, or "\n\n[^n]: " + the words when it starts in the middle of a line. Use the printed number when you are sure
   of it, else a LETTER label (e.g. `57b`, after the footnote before it); split two printed footnotes run together in
   one definition the same way. Some definitions may still begin with a garble mark the pre-pass left (e.g.
   `[^95]: " Above`, `[^127]: 1” The`): remove the mark with a def op.
3. LABELS = PRINTED NUMBERS. The print numbers this chapter's footnotes through the chapter, from 1 on its first page
   (PDF 18) to several hundred; the note must use exactly the printed numbers. Do not renumber anything that is right.
   In per_note give "printed" (the superscript number on the page) for EVERY footnote of your pages, also those whose
   label you did not change; the lead relabels letter labels to the printed numbers afterwards.
4. CARRY-OVERS. This chapter's footnotes are long: many run over one or more pages, and some pages hold nothing but
   footnote text (e.g. PDF 19). A carried-over part is plain text at the top of the next page's block (before that page's
   first definition, or the page's only text), often after the page's body text. Join each paragraph of it with a move op
   (brief, op 4): `after` = the last line of that footnote's text before it (its definition line, its last same-page
   continuation, or the line of the previous carried paragraph when it runs over several pages: chains are allowed;
   several paragraphs moved after the same line keep their order). Body text (the translation and Jayaratha's
   commentary, set in the larger type) that continues on the next page is NOT moved.
5. Same-page further paragraphs of a footnote (Sanskrit quotations, their translations, new paragraphs) that are not
   indented: indent them (op 3). Body text the OCR put among the footnotes, or footnote text inside a body line: report,
   don't fix. Headings (`####`) that the converter put between the body and the footnotes stay where they are.
6. YOUR PAGES ONLY. Another reader may have the other pages of the same note.txt. Write ops only for lines that lie on
   your pages (from your first page's marker line to the line before the marker of the page after your last). A move
   op's `after` may point to a line before your range (a footnote carried over from the page before yours).
7. ACROSS NOTES. The chapter is split into six notes (02 is only PDF 18; 03 = PDF 19-58, 04 = 59-98, 05 = 99-138,
   06 = 139-178, 07 = 179-216). Obsidian joins a call and its definition only inside one note. So:
   - a footnote defined on your pages whose call is printed in the previous or next note (e.g. on the last page of the
     previous note): undo its definition back to the plain `n\. ` form with a def op and report it (number, pages);
   - text at the top of the first page of a note that carries over a footnote defined in the previous note (e.g. PDF 19,
     the rest of footnote 2): leave it where it is and report it;
   - a call on your pages whose footnote is defined in another note: add nothing, report it.
8. The calls are superscript numbers in the body (bold verse translations and the commentary), sometimes at the very
   start of a paragraph (e.g. PDF 18, "²Thus"), and in the verse translations often just before the verse number in
   parentheses: write `[^n] (1)` with the space (brief: Obsidian reads `[^n](…)` as a link).
