### 4.1–5: Eschatological judgment

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 328* ^4-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-1|1]]:** *Servants* (Gk “hupēretēs,” lit., “under-rowers,” those on the bottom deck) . . . *stewards* (Gk “oikonomos,” “one who oversees the household”), see 3.5,9. ^4-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-3|3–4]]:** *Judge*, human verdicts are a *small thing*, as opposed to final judgment (see 3.13n.; 11.31–32). Paul’s use of juridical language draws from the biblical concept of God as cosmic judge ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-25|Gen 18.25]]; [[Psalms 009#^9-9|Ps 9.8]]; [[Psalms 058#^58-12|58.11]]; 82.8; 94.2; 96.13; 98.9; see also [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-1|1 Cor 6.1–8]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-13|9.13–14]]). For the beginning of the verse, see [[Job 27#^27-6|Job 27.6]]. *I am not aware . . . not thereby acquitted*, Paul here clarifies that personal insight into one’s own actions or character must defer to the *Lord who judges* (see [[Romans 07#^7-15|Rom 7.15]],[[Romans 07#^7-18|18–19]],[[Romans 07#^7-23|23]]). ^4-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-5|5]]:** *Do not pronounce judg-men*t, see [[Matthew 07#^7-1|Mt 7.1]]; [[Luke 06#^6-37|Lk 6.37]]. *Bring to light*, reveal the *hidden* end-time scenario. *Disclose*, God’s far superior knowledge (see [[Psalms 044#^44-22|Ps 44.21]] \[HB v 22\]; [[Proverbs 24#^24-12|Prov 24.12]]). *Purposes of the heart*, see [[Jeremiah 11#^11-20|Jer 11.20]]. ^4-5

### 4.6–21: Apostolic admonitions

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-6|Text]], p. 328* ^4-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Have applied* (Gk “meteschēmatisa,” lit., “transformed” or “disguised,” see [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-13|2 Cor 11.13-15]]), is unclear; how is “all this” changed into or disguised as Paul and Apollos? *Apollos*, see 1.12n.; 3.4–6,11,22; 16.12. *Nothing beyond*, teaching secret knowledge not derived from Scripture, such as what “false” apostles ([[2 Corinthians 11#^11-13|2 Cor 11.13]]; see *1 En*. 104.10–11) might convey. *Puffed up*, or “inflated” (4.18–19; 5.2; 8.1; 13.4), arrogance based on knowledge (see [[Romans 15#^15-4|Rom 15.4]]; Philo, *Leg. Gai.* 86; 4QCatenaa frag. 5; *Sib. Or.* 3.738–9). ^4-6-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-7|7]]:** *Sees anything different*, “distinguishes between” or “renders judgment on,” employing a form of the same verb (Gk “diakrinō”) used in 6.5; 11.29,31; 14.29. Paul scolds the Corinthians for judging others and touting knowledge as earned rather than as a *gift*. ^4-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-8|8]]:** *Rich*, possibly the wealth of the gospel, whose benefits the Corinthians have already enjoyed as holy ones (saints) who have won favorable divine judgments. Jewish texts use wealth metaphorically for abundance, applied to wisdom and righteousness ([[Proverbs 14#^14-24|Prov 14.24]]; [[LXX Tobit 04#^4-21|Tob 4.21]]; [[LXX Sirach 30#^30-15|Sir 30.15]]; *m. Avot* 4.1; [[Nedarim 41#41a|*b. Ned*. 41a]]). Paul is being ironic: the Corinthians presume they are *kings*, when the kingdom has not yet come. See [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-21|Wis 6.21]]. ^4-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-9|9]]:** *Death . . .  spectacle*, the public nature of the apostles’ work exposed them to ridicule and assaults (4.11), like criminals tortured and killed in the amphitheater. *To angels*, Paul imagines a cosmic arena (see 11.10n.). ^4-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-10|10]]:** *Fools*, used both ironically (1.21,25; 2.14; 3.18–19) as here, and straightforwardly (1.18,23,27). Contrast 1.27–29: the Corinthians’ hubris is antithetical to God’s call. ^4-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-11|11–13]]:** Lists of tribulations are common rhetorical devices corroborating the admirable steadfastness of the sufferer (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.151–53; Plutarch, *Mor.* 326 D–E; *2 En.* 66.6; *m. Pesah.* 10.5; *m. B. Kamma* 1.1,4). See also Isa 53; [[Matthew 05#^5-3|Mt 5.3–11]]; 39–45; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-7|2 Cor 4.7–12]]. ^4-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|12]]:** *Work of our own hands*, the Bible commends handiwork ([[Proverbs 12#^12-24|Prov 12.24]]; [[Ecclesiastes 03#^3-13|Eccl 3.13]]), and rabbis practiced trades (*m. Avot* 2.2; 4.5; *t. Qidd.* 1.11; [[Kiddushin 29#29a|*b. Qidd.* 29a]]; for Paul, see 16.19; [[Acts 18#^18-1|Acts 18.1–3]]); generally, the Greek elite disparaged manual labor. ^4-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-14|14]]:** *Children*, common term for disciples (see [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^14-1|Deut 14.1]]; [[Psalms 103#^103-13|Ps 103.13]]; [[Proverbs 22#^22-6|Prov 22.6]]; *m. B. Metz.* 2.11). ^4-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-15|15]]:** *Guardians*, (Gk “paidagōgos,” whence “pedagogue”) of young children, see [[Galatians 03#^3-24|Gal 3.24–25]]. *Father*, (Gk “pater”; see [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-11|1 Thess 2.11–12]]; Philem 10), evoking the responsibilities of the Roman male head of household, including shaping his children’s moral character. [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-7|Deut 6.7]]; [[Proverbs 03#^3-12|Prov 3.12]]; [[Kiddushin 22#22a|*b. Qidd.* 22a]]; 29a, et al. list moral instruction among a father’s duties. *B. Sanh.* 19b compares teaching Torah to fathering a child. ^4-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-16|16]]:** *Imitators*, Jewish and Greek teachers encouraged their disciples to use “mimesis” or “imitation” (Xenophon, *Mem.* 1.6.3; [[Berakhot 62#62a|*b. Ber.* 62a]], see also [[Luke 06#^6-40|Lk 6.40]]). ^4-16

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-17|17]]:** *Timothy*, 16.10–11; [[Acts 16#^16-3|Acts 16.3]]; [[Romans 16#^16-21|Rom 16.21]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-1|2 Cor 1.1]],[[2 Corinthians 01#^1-19|19]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-19|2.19]],[[Philippians 02#^2-22|22]]; [[1 Thessalonians 01#^1-1|1 Thess 1.1]]; [[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-2|3.2]],[[1 Thessalonians 03#^3-6|6]], and the addressee of 1 and 2 Timothy. *Child*, continuing the familial metaphor ([[Philippians 02#^2-22|Phil 2.22]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-2|1 Tim 1.2]]; [[2 Timothy 01#^1-2|2 Tim 1.2]]). *Ways*, see [[Jeremiah 21#^21-8|Jer 21.8–14]]; [[LXX Sirach 15#^15-11|Sir 15.11–17]]; *Did*. 1,5–6. ^4-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-18|18]]:** *Arrogant*, see 4.6n. ^4-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-19|19–20]]:** *Talk*, Philosophical rhetoric vs. God’s *power*. ^4-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-19|19]]:** *If the Lord wills*, an expression of divine providence found in prayer formulas; still used col-loquially as an invocation by religious Jews today ([[LXX 1 Maccabees 03#^3-60|1 Macc 3.60]]; [[Hebrews 06#^6-3|Heb 6.3]]; [[James 04#^4-15|Jas 4.15]]; *t. Ber.* 3.7; [[Berakhot 29#29b|*b. Ber.* 29b]]). ^4-19-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 04#^4-21|21]]:** *Stick*, for pedagogical discipline ([[Proverbs 13#^13-24|Prov 13.24]]; Diogenes Laertius \[third century\], *Vit*. *Phil*. 6). ^4-21
