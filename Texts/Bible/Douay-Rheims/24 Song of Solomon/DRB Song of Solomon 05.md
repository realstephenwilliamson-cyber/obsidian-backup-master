# Song of Solomon 5 (Douay-Rheims)

*Canticle of Canticles 5 in the Douay-Rheims*

[[DRB Song of Solomon 04|← Song of Solomon 4]] · [[Douay-Rheims|index]] · [[DRB Song of Solomon 06|Song of Solomon 6 →]]

**5:1** Let my beloved come into his garden, and eat the fruit of his apple trees. I am come into my garden, O my sister, my spouse, I have gathered my myrrh, with my aromatical spices: I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk: eat, O friends, and drink, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved. ^5-1

**5:2** I sleep, and my heart watcheth; the voice of my beloved knocking: Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled: for my head is full of dew, and my locks of the drops of the nights. ^5-2

**5:3** I have put off my garment, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them? ^5-3

**5:4** My beloved put his hand through the key hole, and my bowels were moved at his touch. ^5-4

**5:5** I arose up to open to my beloved: my hands dropped with myrrh, and my fingers were full of the choicest myrrh. ^5-5

**5:6** I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved: but he had turned aside, and was gone. My soul melted when he spoke: I sought him, and found him not: I called, and he did not answer me. ^5-6

**5:7** The keepers that go about the city found me: they struck me: and wounded me: the keepers of the walls took away my veil from me. ^5-7

**5:8** I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you tell him that I languish with love. ^5-8

**5:9** What manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, O thou most beautiful among women? what manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, that thou hast so adjured us? ^5-9

**5:10** My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands. ^5-10

**5:11** His head is as the finest gold: his locks as branches of palm trees, black as a raven. ^5-11

**5:12** His eyes as doves upon brooks of waters, which are washed with milk, and sit beside the plentiful streams. ^5-12

**5:13** His cheeks are as beds of aromatical spices set by the perfumers. His lips are as lilies dropping choice myrrh. ^5-13

**5:14** His hands are turned and as of gold, full of hyacinths. His belly as of ivory, set with sapphires. ^5-14

**5:15** His legs as pillars of marble, that are set upon bases of gold. His form as of Libanus, excellent as the cedars. ^5-15

**5:16** His throat most sweet, and he is all lovely: such is my beloved, and he is my friend, O ye daughters of Jerusalem. ^5-16

**5:17** Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou most beautiful among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside, and we will seek him with thee? ^5-17
