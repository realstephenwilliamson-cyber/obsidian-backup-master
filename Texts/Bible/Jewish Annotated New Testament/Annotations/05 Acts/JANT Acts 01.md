### 1.1–14: Introduction

*[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 221* ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-1|1]]:** Like the Gospel of Luke, Acts opens with a formal, conventional address (see [[Luke 01#^1-3|Lk 1.3]]). *Theophilus*, may have been Luke’s benefactor, although the name, meaning “lover of God,” may symbolize all readers who display such character. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-2|2–4]]:** The verses share key themes with the Gospel: Holy Spirit, resurrection, kingdom of God, Jerusalem, and God’s promises. *Taken up to heaven*, a reward granted to those faithful to God ([[II Kings 02#^2-9|2 Kings 2.9–11]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-58|1 Macc 2.58]]). *Holy Spirit*, the power of God working in a human life (cf. [[Psalms 051#^51-13|Ps 51.11]] \[HB v 13\]). *Apostles*, “ones sent out,” ambassadors or representatives. ^1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Forty days*, [[Luke 24#^24-50|Lk 24.50–51]] describes the ascension as taking place on the same day as the resurrection. The number 40 often represents divinely ordained periods ([[Torah 02 - Noach#^7-4|Gen 7.4]]; [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-35|Ex 16.35]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-18|24.18]]; [[Torah 44 - Devarim#^2-7|Deut 2.7]]; [[II Samuel 05#^5-4|2 Sam 5.4]]). ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Father*, not a common designation for God in the Hebrew Bible, but more frequent in later Jewish sources; see e.g., [[Jeremiah 03#^3-19|Jer 3.19]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-27|Ps 89.26]]; [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|Sir 23.1]]; *m. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 3.8. ^1-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-5|5]]:** *John the Baptist*, see [[Luke 03#^3-1|Lk 3.1–20]]; baptism with the Holy Spirit occurs in 2.1–4. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Restore the kingdom to Israel,* the two on the road to Emmaus express a similar understanding of the Messiah’s role ([[Luke 24#^24-21|Lk 24.21]]). Traditional Jewish expectation held that the Messiah would restore Israel’s freedom ([[Luke 24#^24-21|Lk 24.21]]; *Pss. Sol.* 17; the 14th and 15th benedictions in the daily Jewish prayer called the Amidah/Eighteen Benedictions). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-7|7–8]]:** Jesus’ response serves two purposes: first, he indicates that the apostles’ understanding is faulty, since redemption is found not in political change, but in the bestowing of the Spirit; second, the response justifies Jesus’ failure to return, an issue that concerned many early Christians ([[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-1|1 Thess 5.1–10]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Witnesses*, Gk “martyres,” whence the English “martyr.” “Witness” is an important theme in Acts, by which believers become living testimony to Jesus’ great acts and his resurrection (1.22; 2.32; 3.15; 5.32; 10.39,41; 13.31; 22.15; 26.16). *Jerusalem . . . ends of the earth*, the narrative’s geographic progression: Jerusalem (chs 1–7); Judea and Samaria (chs 8–11), ends of the earth (chs 13–28); Acts concludes with Paul in Rome, the capital of the empire. *Ends of the earth*, refers to God’s universal rule ([[Isaiah 40#^40-28|Isa 40.28]]; [[Isaiah 49#^49-6|49.6]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-10|52.10]]; [[Zechariah 09#^9-10|Zech 9.10]]; [[Psalms 002#^2-8|Ps 2.8]]; [[Psalms 019#^19-5|19.4]]) and may also connote the ethnic progression of God’s promise from Jews to Gentiles (see [[Luke 24#^24-47|Lk 24.47]]). ^1-8

### 1.9–12: The ascension

*[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-9|Text]], p. 222* · Within the NT, only Luke-Acts reports this event. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Cloud*, common symbol for divine presence ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-15|Ex 24.15]]; [[Daniel 07#^7-13|Dan 7.13]]). ^1-9-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Two men in white*, similar figures appear in Jesus’ tomb ([[Luke 24#^24-4|Lk 24.4]]; [[John 20#^20-12|Jn 20.12]]). ^1-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Come in the same way*, [[Zechariah 14#^14-4|Zech 14.4]] identifies the Mount of Olives as the site where the Lord (Heb “YHWH”) will appear. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Olivet*, Mount of Olives, across the Kidron Valley to the east of Jerusalem. *Sabbath day’s journey*, restrictions on travel during the Sabbath appear in the DSS (1,000 cubits \[CD 10.21\]; 2,000 cubits when pasturing animals \[CD 11.5\]) and rabbinic tradition (2,000 cubits \[*m. Eruv.* 4.3; 5.7; [[Eruvin 51#51a|*b. Eruv.* 51a]]; *Mek. Vayassa* 6\]); 2,000 cubits is approximately 1 km (0.6 mi). ^1-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-13|13]]:** The same names, in a different order and minus Judas Iscariot, appear in [[Luke 06#^6-14|Lk 6.14–16]]. *Zealot*, possibly identifying him as a member of a group aiming at violent revolution but more likely an epithet indicating ardent commitment. ^1-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-14|14]]:** *His* \[Jesus’\] *brothers*, [[Mark 06#^6-3|Mk 6.3]] names four brothers; one, James, becomes a church leader (12.17; 15.13; 21.18; [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-7|1 Cor 15.7]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-9|Gal 2.9]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 20.200). ^1-14

### 1.15–26: Appointment of Matthias

*[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-15|Text]], p. 222* ^1-15-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Scripture . . . fulfilled*, God’s plan, knowable through the correct reading of scripture, included both Judas’s betrayal and the appointment of Matthias. The reference is to no particular text but to scripture generally. *Guide*, [[Luke 22#^22-3|Lk 22.3]]–-4, 47–48 identifies Judas as leading the chief priests and others to arrest Jesus. ^1-16

### 1.18–20: Judas’ death

*[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-18|Text]], p. 222* · Contrast [[Matthew 27#^27-3|Mt 27.3–10]], where Judas regretted his actions and died by hanging himself. ^1-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-18|18]]:** The manner of Judas’s death suggests divine judgment (cf. the death of Herod Agrippa in 12.23). ^1-18-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-19|19]]:** *Hakeldama . . . Field of Blood*, the location is given first in a transliteration of the Aram “hekal dama.” ^1-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-20|20]]:** From two biblical laments of the individual ([[Psalms 069#^69-26|Ps 69.25]] and 109.8) understood as referring to Jesus. ^1-20

*[[NRSVue Acts 01#^1-21|Text]], p. 222* ^1-21
