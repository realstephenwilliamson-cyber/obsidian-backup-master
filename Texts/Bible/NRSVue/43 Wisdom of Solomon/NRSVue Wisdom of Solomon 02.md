# Wisdom of Solomon 2 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Wisdom of Solomon 01|← Wisdom of Solomon 1]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Wisdom of Solomon 03|Wisdom of Solomon 3 →]]

**2:1** For they reasoned unsoundly, saying to themselves, “Short and sorrowful is our life, and there is no remedy when a life comes to its end, and no one has been known to return from Hades. ^2-1

**2:2** For we were born by mere chance, and hereafter we shall be as though we had never been, for the breath in our nostrils is smoke, and reason is a spark kindled by the beating of our hearts; ^2-2

**2:3** when it is extinguished, the body will turn to ashes, and the spirit will dissolve like empty air. ^2-3

**2:4** Our name will be forgotten in time, and no one will remember our works; our life will pass away like the traces of a cloud and be scattered like mist that is chased by the rays of the sun and overcome by its heat. ^2-4

**2:5** For our allotted time is the passing of a shadow, and there is no return from our death, because it is sealed up and no one turns back. ^2-5

**2:6** “Come, therefore, let us enjoy the good things that exist and make use of the creation to the full as in youth. ^2-6

**2:7** Let us take our fill of costly wine and perfumes, and let no flower of spring pass us by. ^2-7

**2:8** Let us crown ourselves with rosebuds before they wither. ^2-8

**2:9** Let no meadow be free from our revelry; everywhere let us leave signs of enjoyment, because this is our portion, and this our lot. ^2-9

**2:10** Let us oppress the righteous poor man; let us not spare the widow or regard the gray hairs of the aged. ^2-10

**2:11** But let our might be our law of right, for what is weak proves itself to be useless. ^2-11

**2:12** “Let us lie in wait for the righteous man, because he is inconvenient to us and opposes our actions; he reproaches us for sins against the law and accuses us of sins against our training. ^2-12

**2:13** He professes to have knowledge of God and calls himself a child of the Lord. ^2-13

**2:14** He became to us a reproof of our thoughts; the very sight of him is a burden to us, ^2-14

**2:15** because his manner of life is unlike that of others, and his ways are strange. ^2-15

**2:16** We are considered by him as something base, and he avoids our ways as unclean; he calls the last end of the righteous happy and boasts that God is his father. ^2-16

**2:17** Let us see if his words are true, and let us test what will happen at the end of his life, ^2-17

**2:18** for if the righteous man is God’s child, he will help him and will deliver him from the hand of his adversaries. ^2-18

**2:19** Let us test him with insult and torture, so that we may find out how reasonable he is and make trial of his forbearance. ^2-19

**2:20** Let us condemn him to a shameful death, for, according to what he says, he will be protected.” ^2-20

## Error of the wicked

**2:21** Thus they reasoned, but they were led astray, for their wickedness blinded them, ^2-21

**2:22** and they did not know the secret purposes of God, nor hoped for the wages of holiness, nor discerned the prize for blameless souls, ^2-22

**2:23** for God created us for incorruption and made us in the image of his own eternity,^[Other ancient authorities read *nature*] ^2-23

**2:24** but through an adversary’s^[Or *a devil’s*] envy death entered the world, and those who belong to his company experience it. ^2-24
