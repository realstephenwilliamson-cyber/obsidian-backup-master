# 1 Corinthians 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT 1 Corinthians 02|1 Corinthians 2 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01|NRSVue]] · [[1 Corinthians 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT 1 Corinthians - Introduction]]

### 1.1–3: Salutation

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-1|Text]], p. 322* ^1-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Apostle*, “one sent out”; not all regard Paul as such (9.2; see also 12.28; 15.9). *Sosthenes*, perhaps the synagogue leader in [[Acts 18#^18-17|Acts 18.17]]. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-2|2]]:** *Church* (Gk “ekklēsia”) *of God*, compare Israel as the “assembly of God” (translated from Heb “qahal”; see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^4-10|Deut 4.10]]; [[Judges 20#^20-2|Judg 20.2]]; [[I Kings 08#^8-14|1 Kings 8.14]], etc.) *Sanctified* . . . *saints* (Heb “meqadesh”), church members as set apart or chosen by God (see 6.11; LXX [[LXX Leviticus 20#^20-8|Lev 20.8]]). *Called*, suggesting election. *Call on the name*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-26|Gen 4.26]]; [[Joel 3#^3-5|Joel 2.32]] \[HB 3.5\]; [[Psalms 099#^99-6|Ps 99.6]]; [[Psalms 105#^105-1|105.1]]. *Lord*, the *Christ* (Gk for Heb “mashiaḥ”) who sets believers apart. Jewish uses of “Lord” as a messianic title are rare (see *Pss. Sol.* 17.32), and absent in the Tanakh. ^1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Grace . . .  peace* (Gk “charis,” whence “charity”. . . “eirēnē,” whence “irenic”; Heb “ḥen . . . shalom”; [[Torah 35 - Nasso#^6-24|Num 6.24–26]]), standard Pauline greetings invoking divine favor and well-being, traditional in ancient Greek and Roman letter-writing (e.g., [[LXX 2 Maccabees 01#^1-1|2 Macc 1.1]]). *Father*, an increasingly common Jewish usage ([[Isaiah 63#^63-16|Isa 63.16]]; [[Isaiah 64#^64-7|64.8]]; [[LXX Tobit 13#^13-4|Tob 13.4]] \[*Sinaiticus* A\]; [[LXX Judith 09#^9-12|Jdt 9.12]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 14#^14-3|Wis 14.3]]; [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|Sir 23.1]],[[LXX Sirach 23#^23-4|4]]; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 02#^2-21|3 Macc 2.21]]; Philo, *Decalogue* 64; *Praem.* 166; Josephus, *Ant.* 7.380; *b. Taʾan.* 25b;), familiar today from the later “Avinu Malkeinu” (“Our Father, Our King”) prayer recited on High Holy Days and other occasions, and other prayers. ^1-3

### 1.4–9: Thanksgiving

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-4|Text]], p. 322* ^1-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Give thanks*, standard Greek and Roman epistolary greeting. *In Christ Jesus*, the death of the Messiah (*Christ*) Jesus freed his followers from being punished for their sins and enabled them to enter the kingdom of God, an eschatological transformation of the world that Paul believes Jesus inaugurated ([[1 Corinthians 15#^15-3|1 Cor 15.3]],[[1 Corinthians 15#^15-24|24]]). ^1-4-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Speech and knowledge*, speaking in tongues, rapid vocalization of unrecognizable speech-like sounds (14.2) and claiming special knowledge (Gk “gnosis,” 8.1) created factions. Paul’s thanksgiving is ironic, since such gifts created problems for the community. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-6|6]]:** *Testimony* about God’s saving power, the gospel message. ^1-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Spiritual gift* (Gk “charisma”), e.g., prophecy and speaking in tongues (12.1–31), enabled by the spirit of God that “dwells in” them ([[Romans 08#^8-9|Rom 8.9]]). *Revealing* (Gk “apokalypsis,” whence “apocalypse”), referring to end-time knowledge. ^1-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Blameless*, not in the sense of Heb “tamim” (unblemished or perfect; metaphorically: righteous or moral), but legally nonindictable in the final judgment as measured by Torah-faithfulness ([[Philippians 01#^1-10|Phil 1.10]]), an idea unique to Paul in the NT and rare in Jewish writings ([[LXX 3 Maccabees 05#^5-31|3 Macc 5.31]]). *Day of our Lord*, compare the “day of *the* Lord,” referring to the end-time, in [[Isaiah 13#^13-6|Isa 13.6]],[[Isaiah 13#^13-9|9]]; [[Jeremiah 25#^25-33|Jer 25.33]]\[LXX 32.33\]; Am 5.18,20; and elsewhere. ^1-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-9|9]]:** *Faithful* (Heb “neʾeman”), Jewish literature emphasizes God’s fidelity to maintaining the covenant with Israel and fulfilling its promises (e.g., [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-9|Deut 7.9]]; [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-4|32.4]]; [[Psalms 089#^89-3|Ps 89.2]]; *Deut. Rab*. 3.3). *Fellowship* (Gk “koinonia”) or communion, the community’s shared rituals and values. Philo uses the same word for the sharing of sacrificial meat at the Jerusalem Temple and to describe Essene fellowship (*Spec. Laws* 1.221, *Good Person* 84,91; see also Josephus *Ant.* 14.214). The related Heb terms “yaḥad” (“oneness, unity”; 1QS 5.1) and “ḥavurah” (“companionship, friendship”; *m. Demai* 2.2–3) refer to groups that shared meals. ^1-9

### 1.10–17: Unity

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-10|Text]], p. 323* ^1-10

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-10|10]]:** *Brothers* \[*and sisters*\], the letter is replete with kinship language, common for ancient religious associations and trade guilds ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Lev 19.17]]; [[LXX 3 Maccabees 04#^4-12|3 Macc 4.12]]; Josephus, *J.W.* 2.122; *b. Yebam.* 45b). *Name*, perhaps a reference to baptism in the name of Jesus as opposed to other teachers, with whom various factions within the community identify, and thus an implicit appeal for unity (see 1.12–13). ^1-10-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-11|11]]:** *Chloe’s people*, family, slaves, and/or employees of a female-headed household. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-12|12]]:** *Apollos*, Paul’s Alexandrian Jewish colleague, well regarded by Corinthian believers ([[1 Corinthians 03#^3-4|1 Cor 3.4–6]]; [[1 Corinthians 03#^3-22|3.22]]; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-6|4.6]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-12|16.12]]; [[Acts 18#^18-24|Acts 18.24–19.1]]). *Cephas*, Gk transliteration of Aram “kepha,” “rock,” translated into Gk as “Peter” (see [[Matthew 16#^16-18|Mt 16.18]]). ^1-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-13|13]]:** *Baptized*, Gk term meaning “dip,” “immerse” (see [[Matthew 28#^28-19|Mt 28.19]]; [[Acts 08#^8-16|Acts 8.16]]). Ritual immersion was common in Jewish practice: immersion pools existed at the base of the Jerusalem Temple, in Hasmonean and Herodian palaces, in domestic, oil-production, and public contexts at Arbel, Gamla, Qeren Naftali, Qumran, Sepphoris and Yodefat, etc., for ritual purification (1QS 3.4–5,9; Josephus, *Life* 2; *Ant.* 18.116–17); the followers of Jesus adapted this practice into an initiation rite (see “Baptism and Eucharist,” p. 674). *In the name*, a Semitic expression also found in Hellenistic financial documents; the same phrase appears regarding transmission of rabbinic opinions (Heb “beshemʾomro,” *m. Avot* 6.6). ^1-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Crispus*, a Jew with a Latin name, a synagogue leader (Act 18.8). *Gaius*, a wealthy Roman, often identified with Titius Justus ([[Acts 18#^18-7|Acts 18.7]]), who provided the venue for the Corinthian church ([[Romans 16#^16-23|Rom 16.23]]), possibly his home or meeting hall, which could have accommodated twenty-five to seventy-five people. ^1-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-16|16]]:** *Household*, including slaves. *Stephanas*, see 16.15. ^1-16

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-17|17]]:** *Gospel* (Gk “euangelion,” “good news,” whence “evangelist”), often used for political announcements (e.g., of a tax relief), and by Jesus’ followers to announce God’s message. In the Tanakh, “good news” (Heb “bʾsorah tovah”) indicates welcome tidings ([[II Samuel 18#^18-27|2 Sam 18.27]]; [[II Kings 07#^7-9|2 Kings 7.9]]; [[Isaiah 52#^52-7|Isa 52.7]]). *Eloquent wisdom*, philosophical speculation or clever speech. ^1-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-18|1.18–31]]:** **Divine vs. human wisdom.** ^1-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-18|18]]:** *The cross*, of Jesus’ crucifixion, regarded by non-believers, “those who are perishing” eschatologically speaking ([[2 Corinthians 02#^2-15|2 Cor 2.15]]; [[2 Corinthians 04#^4-3|4.3]]), as *foolishness* (Gk “mōria,” whence “moronic”), since this disgraceful means of execution was reserved for the lower classes. *Are being saved*, Paul’s authentic epistles regard salvation from death as a future event. ^1-18-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-19|19]]:** *It is written* (Heb “kakatuv”), standard Jewish formula for citing scripture (e.g., [[Joshua 08#^8-31|Josh 8.31]]; [[I Kings 02#^2-3|1 Kings 2.3]]; [[II Kings 14#^14-6|2 Kings 14.6]]; [[Daniel 09#^9-13|Dan 9.13]]; [[Ezra 03#^3-2|Ezra 3.2]]; 1QS 5.17; 8.14; CD 7.19; 11QMelch 2; *m. Yoma* 3.8; *t. Yoma* 2.1). Paul cites LXX [[LXX Isaiah 29#^29-14|Isa 29.14]] (see [[Matthew 15#^15-8|Mt 15.8–9]]; [[Mark 07#^7-6|Mk 7.6–7]]), God’s promise to rescue Israel despite false counsel by *the wise*, but reinter-prets Isaiah’s “amazing things” as related to the Christ. The Isaiah Pesher (4QpIsac frags. 16–17) and *L. A. B* 53.13 also interpret [[Isaiah 29#^29-13|Isa 29.13–16]] eschatologically. ^1-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-20|20]]:** Paraphrasing [[Isaiah 19#^19-12|Isa 19.12]]; [[Isaiah 33#^33-18|33.18]]; [[Isaiah 44#^44-25|44.25]]. Jewish exegesis customarily combined biblical verse segments for elucidation. *This age*, Jewish tradition (e.g., *m. Avot* 2.7) distinguished this age (Heb “olam ha-zeh”) from the eschatological age to come (“olam ha-ba,” a term also referring to personal, spiritual afterlife). ^1-20

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-21|21]]:** Paul denigrates human wisdom as opposed to the *wisdom of God*, Christ (see 1.24). In postbiblical Judaism, divine wisdom (Gk “sophia”; Heb “ḥoch-mah”) is often equated with Torah or natural law ([[LXX Sirach 01#^1-26|Sir 1.26]]; [[LXX Sirach 04#^4-24|4.24]]; [[LXX Sirach 06#^6-37|6.37]]; [[LXX Sirach 19#^19-20|19.20]]; [[LXX Baruch 03#^3-9|Bar 3.9–14]]; [[LXX Baruch 04#^4-1|4.1]]; Philo, *Dreams* 1.167–77; *Gen. Rab.* 1.1). In Stoic philosophy, “sophia*”* can refer to a divine quality mystically accessible to humans seeking immortality ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 01#^1-6|Wis 1.6]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 06#^6-9|6.9]]–-23; 7.23–28). *Proclamation*, that salvation is a matter of belief in the gospel (*those who believe*) rather than of philosophy or human wisdom (see 2.5). ^1-21

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-22|22]]:** *Signs*, miraculous events (1 *En.* 82.16; *Sib. Or.* 3.335). Characterization of Jews seeking signs supported medieval stereotypes of Jews as unable to accept Christian truth (Aquinas, *Summa Theologica* 3.47.5; see 3.1–3nn.). Signs can refer to prophecy, whether true ([[I Samuel 10#^10-7|1 Sam 10.7]],[[I Samuel 10#^10-9|9]]; [[II Kings 20#^20-8|2 Kings 20.8–9]]; [[Isaiah 07#^7-11|Isa 7.11]],[[Isaiah 07#^7-14|14]]) or false ([[Isaiah 44#^44-25|Isa 44.25]]). The Bible is somewhat ambivalent about whether or not it is good to seek divine signs (see [[Isaiah 07#^7-1|Isa 7.1–17]]). ^1-22

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-23|23]]:** *Stumbling block* (Gk “skandalon,” whence “scandal”; see [[Romans 09#^9-33|Rom 9.33]]; [[Romans 11#^11-1|11.1–10]]; Ps 69); Jesus’ ignominious and premature death, which hindered belief in his messiahship; some rabbinic writings, however, record a tradition that one of the two anticipated messiahs, the messiah ben Joseph, would die prior to the coming of the king-ruler messiah ben David (*b. Sukk.* 52a; *Sefer Zerubbabel*). *Foolishness,* Gentiles regarded Jesus’ crucifixion as a disqualification for kingship. ^1-23

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-24|24]]:** *Called*, see 1.2n. ^1-24

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-25|25–31]]:** Jewish wisdom literature commonly contrasts foolishness and wisdom (e.g., [[Proverbs 10#^10-1|Prov 10.1]]; [[LXX Sirach 20#^20-31|Sir 20.31]]) and notes that divine wisdom surpasses human knowledge and sometimes subverts human plans (e.g., [[Proverbs 19#^19-21|Prov 19.21]]). ^1-25

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-25|25–26]]:** God’s kingdom inverts hierarchies (in Jewish literature: Hannah’s prayer, as expanded in the Greek \[LXX 1 Kings ([[II Samuel 02#^2-8|2 Sam 2.8–10]] = 4QSama)\]; [[Proverbs 29#^29-23|Prov 29.23]]; 1QHa 9.35–39; *Lev. Rab.* 1.5; see also [[Jeremiah 09#^9-22|Jer 9.22-3]]; in Christian literature: [[Matthew 18#^18-4|Mt 18.4]]; [[Matthew 19#^19-30|19.30]]; [[Matthew 20#^20-16|20.16]]; [[Matthew 23#^23-12|23.12]] and parallels; [[Luke 01#^1-46|Lk 1.46–55]]; [[James 04#^4-6|Jas 4.6–10]]). ^1-25-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-26|26]]:** Paul suggests most Corinthians were uneducated, plebeian, and socially uninfluential. ^1-26

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-27|27]]:** *To shame*, see [[Psalms 006#^6-11|Ps 6.10]]; HB v 11; 31.17; 35.4, etc., where God shames Israel’s enemies by defeating them. ^1-27

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-29|29]]:** *No one*, lit., “all flesh” (Heb “kol basar”), all humanity. *Boast*, a Pauline concern (see also 1.31; 3.21; 4.7; 13.3; 15.31). Paul, like the Tanakh (see esp. [[Jeremiah 09#^9-22|Jer 9.22–23]], quoted below, and see *T. Jud.* 13.2 Pseudo-Phocylides, *Sent.* 53; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.311), condemns boasting. ^1-29

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-30|30]]:** *He,* God. *Life,* eternal life attained through resurrection by those who are *in Christ Jesus*, united with God’s incarnate *wisdom*. *Righteousness*, justification (see 6.11) *sanctification*, holiness, *redemption,* rare word (Gk “apolutrōsis”) conveying rescue from death by resurrection (see [[Romans 08#^8-23|Rom 8.23]]). Paul invokes [[Jeremiah 09#^9-23|Jer 9.23–24]] (love, justice, righteousness). ^1-30

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 01#^1-31|31]]:** [[Jeremiah 09#^9-23|Jer 9.23]] (see also LXX [[LXX 1 Kingdoms 02#^2-10|1 Kings 2.10]]). ^1-31
