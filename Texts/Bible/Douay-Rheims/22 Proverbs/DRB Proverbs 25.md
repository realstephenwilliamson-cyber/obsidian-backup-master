# Proverbs 25 (Douay-Rheims)

[[DRB Proverbs 24|← Proverbs 24]] · [[Douay-Rheims|index]] · [[DRB Proverbs 26|Proverbs 26 →]]

**25:1** These are also parables of Solomon, which the men of Ezechias king of Juda copied out. ^25-1

**25:2** It is the glory of God to conceal the word, and the glory of kings to search out the speech. ^25-2

**25:3** The heaven above, and the earth beneath, and the heart of kings is unsearchable. ^25-3

**25:4** Take away the rust from silver, and there shall come forth a most pure vessel: ^25-4

**25:5** Take away wickedness from the face of the king, and his throne shall be established with justice. ^25-5

**25:6** Appear not glorious before the king, and stand not in the place of great men. ^25-6

**25:7** For it is better that it should be said to thee: Come up hither; than that thou shouldst be humbled before the prince. ^25-7

**25:8** The things which thy eyes have seen, utter not hastily in a quarrel: lest afterward thou mayst not be able to make amends, when thou hast dishonoured thy friend. ^25-8

**25:9** Treat thy cause with thy friend, and discover not the secret to a stranger: ^25-9

**25:10** Lest he insult over thee, when he hath heard it, and cease not to upbraid thee. Grace and friendship deliver a man: keep these for thyself, lest thou fall under reproach. ^25-10

**25:11** To speak a word in due time, is like apples of gold on beds of silver. ^25-11

**25:12** As an earring of gold and a bright pearl, so is he that reproveth the wise, and the obedient ear. ^25-12

**25:13** As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to him that sent him, for he refresheth his soul. ^25-13

**25:14** As clouds, and wind, when no rain followeth, so is the man that boasteth, and doth not fulfill his promises. ^25-14

**25:15** By patience a prince shall be appeased, and a soft tongue shall break hardness. ^25-15

**25:16** Thou hast found honey, eat what is sufficient for thee, lest being glutted therewith thou vomit it up. ^25-16

**25:17** Withdraw thy foot from the house of thy neighbour, lest having his fill he hate thee. ^25-17

**25:18** A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour, is like a dart and a sword and a sharp arrow. ^25-18

**25:19** To trust to an unfaithful man in the time of trouble, is like a rotten tooth, and weary foot, ^25-19

**25:20** And one that looseth his garment in cold weather. As vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to a very evil heart. As a moth doth by a garment, and a worm by the wood: so the sadness of a man consumeth the heart. ^25-20

**25:21** If thy enemy be hungry, give him to eat: if he thirst, give him water to drink: ^25-21

**25:22** For thou shalt heap hot coals upon his head, and the Lord will reward thee. ^25-22

**25:23** The north wind driveth away rain, as doth a sad countenance a backbiting tongue. ^25-23

**25:24** It is better to sit in a corner of the housetop, than with a brawling woman, and in a common house. ^25-24

**25:25** As cold water to a thirsty soul, so is good tidings from a far country. ^25-25

**25:26** A just man falling down before the wicked, is as a fountain troubled with the foot, and a corrupted spring. ^25-26

**25:27** As it is not good for a man to eat much honey, so he that is a searcher of majesty, shall be overwhelmed by glory. ^25-27

**25:28** As a city that lieth open and is not compassed with walls, so is a man that cannot refrain his own spirit in speaking. ^25-28
