### 5.1–21: The faithfulness of God in Christ

*[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 296* · The privileges these Gentiles now have should lead to celebration and service rather than judging others. God’s Spirit will enable them to live righteously even if they are not circumcised. Christ (through resurrection) broke a chain of death that began with Adam; now those in Christ are to live responsibly, not groping for self-satisfaction, but assured that God will provide. ^5-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-1|1–11]]:** This passage is either the voice of a Christ-following Gentile dialogue partner (“we” and “us”), commenting on what Paul has argued about their equal standing with Jews, or it is Paul speaking inclusively for them. Paul explains who they now are and how they are to walk in the footsteps of Abraham before he was circumcised (i.e., before circumcision \[or Torah\]; 4.12). ^5-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-1|1]]:** *We have*, or let us have; see translators’ note *b*. ^5-1-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-2|2]]:** *Grace,* or “favor/benefaction”; *and we*, other ancient authorities add “by faith/faithfulness”; see 1.5. *Sharing the glory*, the full redemption experienced at the eschaton. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-3|3]]:** *But we*, other ancient authorities have “but let us.” *Boast in our sufferings*, because they lead to endurance, then character, then hope. ^5-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-5|5]]:** *Does not disappoint us*, lit., “is not ashamed.” *Holy Spirit*, capitalized to indicate its divine origin (1.4; 8.2). Later Trinitarian doctrine is not necessarily implied here. ^5-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-6|6]]:** *Still weak*, or helpless. *Right time*, or appointed time (Gk “kairos”). ^5-6

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-9|9]]:** *Justified by his blood*, see 3.25; *Wrath of God*, “of God,” not in Gk. ^5-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-10|10]]:** *Enemies,* lit., “alienated ones”; these non-Jews were formerly alienated from God, worshipping idols, as Paul described in 1.18–32. *Will be saved,* protected, kept safe from divine wrath at the final judgment. ^5-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-12|12–17]]:** Adam and Christ are presented as opposites, as type and anti-type: Adam as sinner (similarly, [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 02#^2-24|Wis 2.24]]; 2 Esd \[*4 Ezra*\] 3.21,26; *2 Bar.* 54.15), versus Christ as obedient. *Law* (“nomos”) here does not likely refer to Torah, which was not given until Moses. The principle is that one cannot “miss the mark” (sin or err) until a mark has been set by a convention (“nomos”). Once it has been set and missed, the consequence is distance (death) from the one the convention’s ideals express (God). Once Adam sinned, death became a reality for all his descendants, whether they sinned like Adam, or not (e.g., Christ). ^5-12

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-12|12]]:** *Through one man*, Adam (Gen 3). *Death came*, was introduced. ^5-12-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-13|13]]:** *Sin . . . law,* sin (errors) existed before moral conventions were established, but it could not be judged apart from those conventions. *Before the law,* no article in Gk; or before convention; *no law,* or no convention. ^5-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Transgression*, or sidestepping; Gentiles are \[metaphorically\] sidestepping circumcision yet becoming members of Abraham’s family. *Type*, a model. ^5-14

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-15|15–18]]:** *Free gift*, benefaction, see 1.5; *free* not in Gk; *trespass*, misstep; *grace*, or favor. ^5-15

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-18|18]]:** *Led to condemnation*, led to mortality, or a death sentence; *justification and life,* “and” not in Gk; lit., “justified living.” ^5-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-20|20]]:** *Trespass multiplied*, results in more missteps. ^5-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 05#^5-21|21]]:** *Eternal life*, see 2.7n. ^5-21
