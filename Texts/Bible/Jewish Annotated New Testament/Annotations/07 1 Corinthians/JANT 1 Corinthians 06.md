### 6.1–8: Lawsuits and grievances

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-1|Text]], p. 330* ^6-1

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-1|1]]:** *Saints*, (see 1.2n.) are accountable to the “law of Christ” (9.21), not Roman law. *Court*, Jewish communities outside Judea constituted semi-autonomous legal bodies (see [[Philippians 03#^3-20|Phil 3.20]]) that adjudicated noncapital offenses. However, there is no evidence for such a court in Corinth. Rabbinic literature suggests Jews were expected to settle noncapital intra-Jewish violations of Jewish norms in local Jewish courts (*m. Sanh*. 1–3; *b. Git*. [[Gittin 88#88b|88b]]), yet Egyptian papyri suggest that some Jews did settle divorce cases in Gentile courts (*BGU*.*IV.*1102). ^6-1-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-2|2]]:** *Judge*, i.e., in the final judgment (see LXX [[LXX Daniel (Greek) 07#^7-22|Dan 7.22]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-8|Wis 3.8]]; [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 09#^9-12|9.12]]; [[LXX Sirach 04#^4-15|Sir 4.15]]; 1QpHab 5.4). In some Jewish accounts, Israel judges the Gentiles (*Jub*. 32.19; *T. name of the Lord Jesus* b Other ancient authorities add *Jesus* c Gk *brother Abr*. 13.6; see also [[Daniel 07#^7-21|Dan 7.21–22]]; Rabbi David Kimchi \[twelfth–thirteenth centuries\] on [[Isaiah 11#^11-14|Isa 11.14]] and 14.1). ^6-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-3|3]]:** *Angels*, are judged by the saints, or the righteous ([[Daniel 04#^4-14|Dan 4.14]]; [[2 Peter 02#^2-4|2 Pet 2.4]]; Jude 6–15; *1 En*. 14.21–24, 15; 19; 21; 41.9; 46.7; *y. Shabb*. 6.10 \[8d\]; *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 38#38b|38b]]). ^6-3

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-4|4]]:** *Those . . . no standing*, pagans. ^6-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-6|6]]:** *Believer*, lit., “brother”; pagan philosophers were also appalled that siblings would sue each other (see Plutarch, *Mor*. 481B). ^6-6

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-7|7–8]]:** Paul accuses church members of wronging each other by using the Roman judiciary system. ^6-7

### 6.9–11: Vice lists

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|Text]], p. 331* · See 5.11n. *Fornicators* (Gk “pornoi”), translated elsewhere as *sexually immoral persons* (5.9–11). *Male prostitutes* (Gk “malakoi”), lit., “soft,” referring to moral and physical weakness as well as sexual passivity or penetrability. *Sodomites* (Gk “arsenokoitai,” a rare term combining “male” and “bed”), better translated “males who bed males,” echoing [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-22|Lev 18.22]] and 20.13 (see also [[Romans 01#^1-27|Rom 1.27]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-10|1 Tim 1.10]]; *Sib. Or.* 2.73), since Paul’s Greek neologism has nothing to do with Sodom (Gen 19) and the term “sodomite” does not appear in the Tanakh. ^6-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-10|10]]:** *Inherit the kingdom*, see 15.50; [[Matthew 25#^25-34|Mt 25.34]]; [[Matthew 19#^19-29|19.29]]; [[Mark 10#^10-17|Mk 10.17]]; [[Luke 10#^10-25|Lk 10.25]]; [[Luke 18#^18-18|18.18]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-21|Gal 5.21]]. ^6-10

> [!note] Freedom from the Law *(p. 331)*
> Paul’s discussion of freedom from the Law has sometimes been misconstrued as an antinomian repudiation of Torah commandments. Believers are not free from or above the law, — only from sin — but, through their baptism, they have become “slaves to righteousness” ([[Romans 06#^6-18|Rom 6.18]]). Believers, therefore, are no longer condemned by the guilty verdict rendered to Gentiles under the law of Moses, the “curse of the law” ([[Galatians 03#^3-13|Gal 3.13]]). Paul does not reject Torah per se, nor does he reject the idea of Jews following the specific commandments given to Jews (e.g., circumcision, dietary regulations). But he does reject Gentiles practicing such traditions. With Christ’s death and resurrection, Gentiles are now incorporated into the community in which the Law is inscribed on believers’ hearts ([[Romans 02#^2-15|Rom 2.15]]; [[Romans 06#^6-17|6.17]]; [[2 Corinthians 01#^1-22|2 Cor 1.22]]; [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-3|3.3]]; [[Galatians 04#^4-6|Gal 4.6]]). Furthermore, Paul did not understand the freedom vis-à-vis the Law obtained in Christ as a license to immorality, as others apparently did (see [[Romans 02#^2-12|Rom 2.12–14]]; [[Romans 06#^6-15|6.15–18]]; [[Romans 08#^8-2|8.2]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-1|Gal 5.1]]): freedom from sin meant rather slavery “to righteousness” ([[Romans 06#^6-18|Rom 6.18]]; [[Galatians 04#^4-31|Gal 4.31]]).
>
> Slaves, in the broader Roman context, were not protected by imperial legislation; thus they were vulnerable to abuse and exploitation. Freed slaves were subject to certain laws but were denied the full range of protections and privileges, while citizens were bound to and protected by the laws of the state. In Paul’s allegorical schema of the old Mosaic covenant in the pre-Christ age, Gentiles were slaves and Jews were citizens. Christ freed Gentiles from sin so that they would no longer be slaves but free (7.22), transforming the exclusively Jewish Torah covenant into Gentile-inclusive *Christ’s law* (9.21).
>
> For Second Temple-period and rabbinic Jews, obedience to the Law, or Torah, served various functions, such as a vehicle for maintaining the divine covenant and a demonstration of faithfulness to God, as reflected in the rabbinic aphorism “only one who engages in Torah study is truly liberated” (*m. Avot* 6.2; see also [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-20|1 Macc 2.20–27]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-18|2 Macc 6.18–7.42]]; *T. Moses* 9.6). Some Jews predicted that all the commandments would be rendered obsolete in the end-time, which may relate to Paul’s view of freedom from the Law. For example, Rav Joseph interprets the halakhah that cloth of mixed fibers ([[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-19|Lev 19.19]] and [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-9|Deut 22.9–11]]) may be used as a burial shroud to mean that Israel will not be held accountable for such violations of Torah injunctions in the eschatological age, when the dead have been resurrected (*b. Nidd.* [[Niddah 61#61b|61b]]).
>
> In a similar eschatological vein, Paul envisions that believers will be “kings” once the kingdom of God has been fulfilled (4.8). In discussing the relationship of kings to the law of the state, Greek philosophers such as Dio Chrysostom (first century ce) wrote that kings could choose to follow the law without being subject to it (3 *Regn*. \[*Or.* 3\] 10; 1 *Serv. lib.* \[*Or*. 14\] 7–18; *Regn. tyr*. \[*Or*. 62\] 2). By contrast, the Bible mandates that kings obey “this law and these statutes” ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-19|Deut 17.19–20]]).

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-11|11]]:** *Used to be*, their pagan immorality should have been expunged by their being *washed* (baptized). *Sanctified*, purified from sinful ways and set apart from idolatrous Gentiles; the Gk (“hēgiathētē”) corresponds to the Heb “qiddesh” or rarely “kipper” ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-14|Ex 19.14]]; [[Torah 20 - Tetzaveh#^29-36|29.36]]; [[Torah 26 - Shmini#^11-44|Lev 11.44]]; see also 1.30 and 2.16). *Justified* (from Heb root “tz-d-q”), vindicated or acquitted before God; the term draws on Roman juridical and Jewish prophetic language ([[Isaiah 43#^43-9|Isa 43.9]],[[Isaiah 43#^43-26|26]]; [[Isaiah 45#^45-25|45.25]]; [[Micah 6#^6-11|Mic 6.11]]). ^6-11

### 6.12–20: Bodily purity

*[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-12|Text]], p. 332* · This passage delineates how believers should properly guard their bodily purity by avoiding sins of the flesh. ^6-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-12|12]]:** *All things are lawful*, a Corinthian slogan (see 7.1; 10.23), consistent with some Greek philosophical views (Epictetus, *Diatr.* 41.1; Dio Chrys., 3 *Regn*. \[*Or.* 3\] 10). Cynics and Essenes are purported to have flouted cultural norms by publicly performing private acts (like defecation) or breaking the law to demonstrate their freedom from the power of the bodily passions (Philo, *Hypoth.* 11.3; Dio Chrys., *Virt*. \[*Or*. 8\] 36; Diogenes Laertius, *Vit. Phil.* 6.72**-**73;). ^6-12-2

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-13|13]]:** *Food is meant for the stomach . . . food*, perhaps another Corinthian slogan; the claim may have developed in relation to eating meat offered to idols (8.13; 10.25). *Destroy*, in the resurrection. *Fornication* (Gk “porneia”), sexual immorality (5.1). Paul is concerned that fornication, by polluting the physical body, would render believers ineligible for resurrection. ^6-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-14|14]]:** See 15.51–52. ^6-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-15|15]]:** The church is the “body of Christ” (see 12.12–27). Jewish literature often portrays Israel as a body ([[Isaiah 10#^10-18|Isa 10.18]]; *y*. *Ned.* 9.4; *Mek. R. Shimon bar Yohai* on [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-6|Ex 19.6]]). ^6-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-16|16]]:** *The two*, in LXX [[LXX Genesis 02#^2-24|Gen 2.24]] but not in the Heb. Like Philo (*Giants* 13–15), Paul interprets immoral intercourse as corrupting a person’s nature and invalidating that person’s dedication to Christ. ^6-16

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-17|17]]:** *United*, still echoing [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-24|Gen 2.24]]. ^6-17

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-18|18]]:** *Shun fornication*, see [[Proverbs 05#^5-3|Prov 5.3]]; [[Proverbs 06#^6-23|6.23–7.27]]; [[LXX Sirach 09#^9-6|Sir 9.6]]; [[LXX Sirach 19#^19-2|19.2]]; a Jewish teaching found in *T. Reuben* 5.5, which uses the same phrase to describe Joseph’s response to the sexual advances of Potiphar’s wife ([[Torah 09 - Vayeshev#^39-12|Gen 39.12]]). *Against the body itself*, whose purity must be guarded (LXX [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-17|Sir 23.17]]). ^6-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-19|19]]:** *Temple* (see 3.16–17n.), container for God’s Holy Spirit. ^6-19

**[[NRSVue 1 Corinthians 06#^6-20|20]]:** *Bought*, ransomed by Christ from their captivity to sin ([[Matthew 20#^20-28|Mt 20.28]] || [[Mark 10#^10-45|Mk 10.45]]; see also [[Isaiah 50#^50-1|Isa 50.1–2]]). *Price*, Jesus’ crucifixion (see 1.30; 15.3). *Glorify God,* by remaining pure *in your body*. ^6-20
