# Zohar 2.206b

← [[Zohar 2.206a]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 2.207a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 6.05 - Parashat Va-Yaqhel#^z2-206b|Parashat Va-Yaqhel, at 2:206b]]

*Volume 2, folio 206b* · Vayakhel

*Passage Vayakhel 17:227 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 2.206a#^vayakhel-17-227|Vayakhel 17:227]].*

**Vayakhel 17:228** It behoves the reader to concentrate all his mind on the words he reads, and to realize that he is the messenger of his Master, charged with the duty of communicating these words to the whole congregation, he being in the place of heaven to them. Hence, whoever would go up to read in the Torah should previously rehearse his reading at home, or else not read at all. This we learn from the giving of the Law on Mount Sinai, of which we read, first, “Then did he see it and declare it; he prepared it, yea, and searched it out” (Job. 28, 27), and then, “And unto man he said: Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is Wisdom” (*Ibid.* 28, 28). ^vayakhel-17-228

**Vayakhel 17:229** It is forbidden to the reader to break off anywhere save where Moses indicated a pause. Neither may he in reading the portion of one week add part of the portion of another week. ^vayakhel-17-229

**Vayakhel 17:230** The inner reason of this is as follows. Each weekly lesson is at its conclusion adorned with a crown, and presents itself before God. At the conclusion of their yearly cycle they all present themselves, thus crowned, before the Holy One, blessed be He, each one announcing: I belong to the Sabbath so-and-so, and to congregation so-and-so. ^vayakhel-17-230

**Vayakhel 17:231** At that moment the angel Youfiel, the great chief, is called for, who presents himself accompanied by the fifty-three[^1] legions under his charge. These legions superintend the reading of the Law, each one having to preside over the reading on a particular Sabbath assigned to it. It is thus forbidden us to disarrange the Lessons and thereby cause the overlapping of one legion with another, even by so much as a hairbreadth, by so much as a single word or even a single letter; but each must be kept within its own limits as fixed by the Holy One, blessed be He. ^vayakhel-17-231

**Vayakhel 17:232** All the legions thus present themselves, each one standing guard over the weekly Lesson under its charge. Each portion thus decorated with a crown, after its reading has been completed by the congregation, is taken up by its superintending legion and brought before the presence of the Holy One, blessed be He, in all its several words. These words declare: “We are such-and-such a section, completed by such-and-such a congregation in such-and-such manner.” ^vayakhel-17-232

**Vayakhel 17:233** If the reading of them has been completed in the proper manner, the words ascend and themselves are woven into a crown to adorn the Divine Throne, with its superintending legion standing guard over it. The same is repeated with each separate Sabbath lesson in turn until they are all joined and become interwoven into one single crown. Hence, happy is the portion of whoever completes the reading of the weekly portion of each and every Sabbath in the proper manner, and in accordance with the divisions fixed on high. ^vayakhel-17-233

**Vayakhel 17:234** On Sabbath we have to read in the Book of the Law twice: once in the morning and a second time at dusk. For the late afternoon is the time when judgement hangs over the world, hence it is necessary for us to intertwine the Left with the Right,[^2] seeing that the Torah proceeded from the two sides, as it is written: “At his right hand was a fiery law unto them” (Deut. 33, 2), implying both the right and the left. Hence the reading of the Law at dusk should comprise ten verses or more, but not the entire portion, as the complete portion is of the right, and the right prevails only until the time of afternoon service. ^vayakhel-17-234

**Vayakhel 17:235** We have also to read the Law on the second and the fifth days of the week, as on these days the higher grades descend below, those grades that represent the main principles of the Torah. ^vayakhel-17-235

**Vayakhel 17:236** The esoteric significance of the matter is this. These superior grades represent one portion of the Torah, but from them there emanate nine grades,[^3] which form a unity, and hence have their counterpart in the nine persons called to read the Law: to wit, three on Sabbath at *Minha*, and three each on the second and thc fifth days of the week. ^vayakhel-17-236

**Vayakhel 18:237** Similarly we read in the Book of R. Yeba the Venerable: “At Minha-time on Sabbath there is an awakening of the mysterious forces of the Left, and the lower ‘Sabbath-Point’ within that left side receives the mystery of the Torah. At that moment, therefore, it receives from the domination, as it were, of the left side, ^vayakhel-18-237

**Vayakhel 18:238** the side which is esoterically represented by nine, and hence the nine persons who read the Law, to wit, six on weekdays and three on Sabbath at the moment when the left side bestirs itself.” Happy is the portion of whoever is privileged to do honour to the Sabbath; happy is he in the two worlds, in this world and in the future world. ^vayakhel-18-238

[^1]: The traditional division of the Pentateuch is into fifty-four sections. Some say that the sections *Nizabim* and *Vayelekh* are, by the Zohar, counted as one; others, that *V’zoth Habbrakhah* is here excluded, as not belonging to any Sabbath.
[^2]: i.e. Rigour with Clemency.
[^3]: The grades of *Nezah, Hod*, and *Yesod*, each with its three sections.
