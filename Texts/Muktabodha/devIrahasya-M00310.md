# devīrahasya

**Catalog number:** M00310  
**Main title:** devī rahasya with pariśiṣṭas  
**Editor(s):** ram chandra kak, harrabaṭṭa śastrī  
**Published:** Unknown, Srinigar, India, 1941

## Description

The Devīrahasya and Uddhārakośa are published together in one volume. Edited by
Rāma candra Kāka and Haribhaṭṭa śāstrī, it was published originally published in Kashmir in 1941. The Devīrahasya is considered to be a part
of the Rudrayāmala. It appears to be have been composed in Kashmiri as it refers
repeatedly to the goddesses Jvālāmukhī, śārikā, Mahārājñī,
śāradā, Bhidā, and Bālā who have shrines in the Kathmandu Valley where
they were very popular. The words Arabic words phirangahā and safak found on pages
307 and 317 indicate that it was composed when the Muslims were already establized in
the Valley.

The Devīrahasya is divided into three parts, as follows.
A) The Pūrvārdha (First Half). This consists of  twenty-five
paṭalas that deal with (1) Initiation. (2) Mantras of form so the
Goddess. (3) Mantras of śiva. (4) Mantras of Viṣṇu. (5)
Utkelana or unlocking of Mantras. (6) Vitalizing of Mantras. (7)
Methods to remove curses attached to Mantras. (8) The mental utterance of
Mantras (pārāyaṇa). (9) How to use particular seed syllables
(bīja) uttered , before and after particular Mantras. (10)
Preliminary practice of Mantras. (11) The sacrificial rite performed for the
preliminary practice of Mantras. (12) Diagrams used for worship
(yantra). (13) How to wear amulets containing those diagrams.
(14) The Rṣ.i, metre etc of Mantras. (15, 16) Worship in
cremation grounds. (17, 18) Purification of the impliments used for
worship. (19) The origin of wine. (20) Ritual ingestion of wine.
(21) Prayers and hymns dedicated to śākta sādhakas (vīra).
(22) Purification of wine. (23) Purification of the consort.
(24) Different kinds of rosary and their purification. (25) Purification
etc., of the diagrams.

B) The uttarārdha (Second Half) otherwise called Rahasyavijaya
consists of thirty-five chapters embodying the Pañcāṅgas, Paṭala,
Kavaca, Paddhati Sahasranāma and Stotra of Gaṇeśa, Sūrya,
Lakṣhmīnārāyaṇa, śiva and Durgā.
The appendix (Pariśiṣṭha) gives the Pañcāṅgas of Jvālāmukhī,
śārikā, Mahārajñī and Bālā the family goddesses of the Kashmiri paṇḍits.

B) The Uddhārakośa (Treasure of Mantras) which is in the
appendix. Written in the form of a dialogue between Dakṣināmūrti and his disciple
Akṣobhya, it is a collection of quotations from no less than forty-seven
Tantrika works. It is in seven sections. 1) The Mantras of the goddesses
Tripurā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Tārā, Bhuvaneśvarī,
Mātaṅgī, śarikā, Rāñjī, Bhīdā and Jvālāmukhī along with some
seed syllable Mantras and their names. 2) The Mantras of Bhadrakālī,
Turī, chinnamastā, Dakṣiṇākālikā, śyāmā and Kālarātrī. 3)
The Mantras of Vajrayoginī, Vārāhī, śāradā, Kāmeśvarī,
Gaurī, Annapurṇā and Kulvāgīśvarī along with those of Ganeśa,
Vatuka, Kumāra, Mṛtyuñjaya, Kārtaviryārjuna, Sugrīva and
Hanuman. 4) The Mantras of the nine planets. 5) Names of the letters of the
Sanskrit alphabet. 6) The Mantras of Bhavānī, Bagalāmukhī, Indrākṣī
and Khecarī. 7) Descriptions of the goddesses, gods and planets mentioned in
the preceding sections.
