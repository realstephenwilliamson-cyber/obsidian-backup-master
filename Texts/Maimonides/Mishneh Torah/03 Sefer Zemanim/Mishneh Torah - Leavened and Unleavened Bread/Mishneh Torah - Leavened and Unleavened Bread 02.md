**1.** It is a positive commandment from the Torah to destroy chametz before the time it becomes forbidden to be eaten, as[^1] \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-15|Exodus 12:15]]] states: "On the first day, destroy leaven from your homes." On the basis of the oral tradition, it is derived that "the first day" refers to the day of the fourteenth.  
Proof of this matter is the verse from the Torah \[[[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-25|Exodus 34:25]]]: "Do not slaughter the blood of My sacrifice with chametz," i.e., Do not slaughter the Pesach sacrifice while chametz exists [in your possession]. The slaughter of the Pesach sacrifice was on the fourteenth after midday. ^2-1

**2.** What is the destruction to which the Torah refers? to nullify chametz within his heart[^2] and to consider it as dust,[^3] and to resolve within his heart that he possesses no chametz at all:[^4] all the chametz in his possession being as dust and as a thing of no value whatsoever.[^5] ^2-2

**3.** According to the Sages' decree,[^6] [the mitzvah[^7] involves] searching for chametz[^8] in hidden places and in any holes [within one's house],[^9] seeking it[^10] and removing it from all of one's domain.[^11]  
Similarly, according to the Sages' decree, we must search [with the intent to] destroy chametz by candlelight, at night,[^12] at the beginning of the night of the fourteenth [of Nisan].[^13] [They instituted the search at that time][^14] because all people are at home at night,[^15] and the light of the candle is good for searching.[^16]  
A study session should not be fixed for the end of the thirteenth of Nisan.[^17] Similarly, a wise man should not begin to study at this time,[^18] lest he become involved,[^19] and thus be prevented from searching for chametz at the beginning of the time.[^20] ^2-3

**4.** We do not search [for chametz] by the light of the moon, the light of the sun,[^21] or the light of a torch;[^22] only by the light of a candle.[^23] To what does this apply? to the holes and hidden places. However, for a porch[^24] which has much light,[^25] searching it by the light of the sun is sufficient.[^26]  
The middle of a courtyard[^27] does not need to be searched, because birds are found there, and they eat all the chametz which falls there. ^2-4

**5.** A hole[^28] in the middle[^29] of [the wall of] the house[^30] between a person and his colleague [should be searched by both individuals],[^31] each searching to the extent his hand reaches. [Afterwards,] each must nullify in his heart [any chametz in] the remaining portion.[^32]  
[This applies to a hole in a wall separating two Jewish homes.] However, a hole between [the home of] a Jew and a gentile should not be searched at all, lest the gentile[^33] fear that the Jew is casting spells against him.[^34] All that is necessary for him to do is to nullify it within his heart.[^35]  
Any place where chametz is not brought in does not need to be searched.[^36] ^2-5

**6.** The upper[^37] and lower holes[^38] [in the wall] of a house,[^39] the roof of a balcony,[^40] a cow stall, chicken coop,[^41] hayloft, wine cellars or storage rooms for oil when supplies are not taken from them [in the midst of a meal],[^42] and storage rooms for large fish,[^43] need not be searched unless one brought chametz into them.[^44]  
In contrast, storage rooms for beer, storage rooms for wine from which supplies are taken [in the midst of a meal], storage rooms for salt, candles, small fish, wood, and brine,[^45] the middle holes in a wall,[^46] and all similar places must be searched, for chametz is usually brought into them.[^47] However, if a person knows that he did not bring chametz into these places, they do not have to be searched.[^48]  
When searching a wine cellar,[^49] [all that is necessary to] search are the two outer rows—i.e., the highest row and the one below it. ^2-6

**7.** We do not suspect that a weasel dragged chametz into a place where it is not usually brought.[^50] Were we to suspect [that chametz would be taken] from house to house, we would also have to suspect [that chametz might be taken] from city to city. There is no end to the matter.[^51]  
A person who checked on the night of the fourteenth[^52] and placed ten loaves of chametz [on the side][^53] and [later] found [only] nine must suspect [that chametz is present in his home,] and [hence], must search a second time,[^54] for definitely it was taken by a weasel or mouse.[^55] ^2-7

**8.** Similarly, a person who saw a mouse enter the house with chametz in its mouth after he searched [for chametz] must search a second time.[^56] [This applies] even if he found crumbs in the middle of the house; we do not necessarily presume that it already ate the bread in this place and that these are its crumbs.[^57] Rather, we suspect that it left the bread in a hole or window, and that these crumbs were [originally] located there.[^58] Hence, he must search again.[^59]  
If he does not find anything, he must search through the entire house.[^60] If he found the bread[^61] that the mouse took when he entered,[^62] he need not search [further].[^63] ^2-8

**9.** [A person who] saw an infant enter an already checked house[^64] with bread in his hand, followed him inside and discovered crumbs, need not search [again].[^65] We may confidently assume that he ate the bread and that these crumbs fell from him while eating.[^66] Infants generally crumble food while eating, though mice do not.[^67]  
If he does not find any crumbs, he must check [again].[^68] ^2-9

**10.** Nine piles of matzah and one of chametz were placed [aside].[^69] A mouse came and took [something from one of the piles][^70] and entered a house that had been checked.[^71] If we do not know whether it took either chametz or matzah, the house must be checked [again],[^72] for every instance where [a doubt arises and the presence of both the permitted and forbidden substances] is fixed, [is judged] as if they[^73] were present in equal amounts.[^74] ^2-10

**11.** There were two piles: one of chametz and one of matzah, and two houses: one that had been searched and one that had not been searched. Two mice came; one took chametz and one took matzah [and they entered the houses] without our knowing which house the mouse holding the chametz entered...[^75]  
Similarly, there were two houses which had been checked, with one pile of chametz before them. A mouse took [from the pile and entered a house]. However, we do not know which house he entered...[^76]  
or we saw which one he entered, a person followed him, checked [for chametz], and did not find anything...[^77]  
or he checked and found bread...[^78]  
Similarly, when there were nine piles of matzah and one of chametz, and a loaf became separated from the piles, and we do not know whether it was chametz or matzah: If a mouse took the loaf that became separated and entered the house that had been checked:  
In[^79] all of these cases, there is no need to check a second time,[^80] because the presence of the forbidden substance is not fixed.[^81] ^2-11

**12.** A person who placed chametz in one corner and discovered it in another...[^82]  
or who put aside nine loaves and found ten...[^83]  
or a mouse came and took the chametz, and there is a doubt whether he entered this house or not...[^84]  
In all these cases, he must search [the house again]. ^2-12

**13.** A mouse entered a house with a loaf[^85] in its mouth. Afterwards, a mouse left there with a loaf in its mouth; we presume that the same mouse who entered originally was the one which ultimately left, and [the owner] need not search [again].[^86] If the first mouse which entered was black and the one which left was white,[^87] he must search [again].[^88]  
If a mouse entered with a loaf in its mouth and a weasel left there with a loaf in its mouth, he must search [again].[^89] If a weasel left there with a mouse and a loaf in its[^90] mouth, he does not have to search [again].[^91] [We may presume] that this is the loaf which was in the mouse's mouth.[^92]  
If a snake enters a hole with a loaf in its mouth, there is no obligation to bring a snakecharmer to remove it.[^93] ^2-13

**14.** When chametz is [discovered] on a very high beam, [the owner] is obligated to bring a ladder[^94] and remove it, for it might fall from the beam.[^95] If chametz is in a pit,[^96] [the owner] is not obligated to bring it up.[^97] All that is necessary is for him to nullify [ownership over it] in his heart.[^98] ^2-14

**15.** A block of yeast which was designated to be used as a seat:[^99] If its surface was coated with mortar,[^100] it is [considered as] destroyed and we are permitted to keep it [on Pesach].[^101]  
The dough in the cracks of a kneading trough: If a size of an olive [of dough] exists in one place,[^102] one is obligated to destroy [the dough under all circumstances].[^103] Should there be less than that amount:[^104] If it[^105] serves to reinforce the broken pieces of the kneading trough or to plug a hole, it is [considered] negligible[^106] because of its minimal size.[^107] If not,[^108] one is obligated to destroy it.[^109]  
Two [quantities of dough,[^110] each] half the size of an olive were found in separate places, and a string of dough connects them:[^111] We check whether the [pieces of] dough themselves are lifted up when the string of dough is picked up. If they are, one is obligated to destroy [them]. If not, one is not obligated to destroy [them].[^112] ^2-15

**16.** To what does the above[^113] apply? to [pieces of dough stuck to] a kneading trough. However, [if a similar situation is discovered] within a house,[^114] one is obligated to destroy [the dough] even if, when the [connecting] thread [of dough] is lifted up, the pieces of dough are not raised up with it.[^115] [This stringency was instituted] because the [smaller portions] may sometimes be brought together.[^116]  
If half of an olive size [of dough] was found in a house and another half in the second storey;  
or[^117] if half of the size of an olive was found in a house and another half in [the adjoining] porch;  
or[^118] if half of the size of an olive was found in a room[^119] and another half in an inner[^120] room;  
Since these portions of dough which are less than the size of an olive are found stuck to the walls, beams, or floors [of the house],[^121] one is not obligated to destroy them. All that is necessary is to nullify them in one's heart.[^122] ^2-16

**17.** A person who rents out a house on the fourteenth [of Nisan]:[^123] Behold, [the tenant may operate] under the presumption that it has been searched[^124] and he need not search.[^125]  
[Furthermore,] even if we must assume that the person who rented out the house did not search [it],[^126] should a woman or a minor say: "We have searched it," they are believed,[^127] for everyone's statements are accepted with regard to the destruction of chametz.[^128][^129]  
Everyone is acceptable to search [for chametz], even women, slaves, and minors.[^130] The latter applies only when the minor has sufficient understanding to search.[^131] ^2-17

**18.** A person who rents[^132] a house to a colleague: If[^133] the fourteenth [of Nisan][^134] falls before [the landlord] gave the keys to [the tenant],[^135] the landlord is responsible for searching.[^136] If the fourteenth falls after the keys have been transferred,[^137] the tenant is responsible for checking.[^138]  
When a person rents out a house under the presumption that it has been searched and [later, the tenant] discovers that it has not been searched,[^139] [the tenant] is responsible for searching it[^140] and [cannot nullify the transaction by claiming that it was carried out] under false premises.[^141] This applies even when it is customary[^142] to hire people to search, since, behold, he is performing a mitzvah.[^143] ^2-18

**19.** A person who sets out to sea or one who leaves in a caravan within thirty days [of Pesach] is obligated to search[^144] [for chametz].[^145] [If he leaves] before thirty days [prior to Pesach],[^146] he is not obligated to search. However, if he intends to return before Pesach,[^147] he must search before departing,[^148] lest he [be delayed and] return Pesach eve at nightfall,[^149] when he will have no opportunity to destroy [the chametz].[^150] However, if he does not intend to return,[^151] he does not have to search.[^152]  
Similarly, a person who makes his house a storage room:[^153] If he does so[^154] within thirty days [of Pesach], he is required to search and then bring in the goods he wants to store.[^155] Over thirty days before Pesach: If he intends to remove the goods before Pesach, he is required to search[^156] and then bring in the goods he wants to store; if he does not intend to remove the goods before Pesach, he does not have to search.[^157] ^2-19
