## Chapter 4

**4:1** I further observed[^1] all the oppression that goes on under the sun: the tears of the oppressed, with none to comfort them; and the power of their oppressors—with none to comfort them. ^4-1

**4:2** Then I accounted those who died long since more fortunate than those who are still living; ^4-2

**4:3** and happier than either are those who have not yet come into being and have never witnessed the miseries that go on under the sun. ^4-3

**4:4** I have also noted that all labor and skillful enterprise come from men’s envy of each other—another futility and pursuit of wind! ^4-4

**4:5** [True,]  
The fool folds his hands together[^2]  
And has to eat his own flesh. ^4-5

**4:6** [But no less truly,]  
Better is a handful of gratification  
Than two fistfuls of labor which is pursuit of wind. ^4-6

**4:7** And I have noted this further futility under the sun: ^4-7

**4:8** the case of the man who is alone, with no companion, who has neither son nor brother; yet he amasses wealth without limit, and his eye is never sated with riches. For whom, now, is he amassing it while denying himself[^3] enjoyment? That too is a futility and an unhappy business. ^4-8

**4:9** [^4]Two are better off than one, in that they have greater benefit from[^5] their earnings. ^4-9

**4:10** For should they fall, one can raise the other; but woe betide him who is alone and falls with no companion to raise him! ^4-10

**4:11** Further, when two lie together they are warm; but how can he who is alone get warm? ^4-11

**4:12** Also, if one attacks, two can stand up to him. A threefold cord is not readily broken! ^4-12

**4:13** Better a poor but wise youth than an old but foolish king who no longer has the sense to heed warnings. ^4-13

**4:14** For the former can emerge from a dungeon to become king; while the latter, even if born to kingship, can become a pauper.[^6] ^4-14

**4:15** [However,] I reflected about all the living who walk under the sun with[^7] that youthful successor who steps into his place. ^4-15

**4:16** Unnumbered are the multitudes of all those who preceded them;[^8] and later generations will not acclaim him either.[^9] For that[^10] too is futile and pursuit of wind. ^4-16

**4:17** Be not overeager to go[^11] to the House of God: more acceptable is obedience than the offering of fools, for they know nothing [but] to do wrong. ^4-17

[^1]: Cf. 3.16.
[^2]: I.e., does not work; cf. Prov. 6.10; 24.33.
[^3]: Lit. “am I amassing…myself.”
[^4]: 4.9–5.8 consists of a series of observations, each of which is introduced by some slight association with what precedes. The theme of 4.4–8 is not resumed until 5.9.
[^5]: Emendation yields “hope for”; cf. 2.20.
[^6]: Taking rash as a verb; cf. Ps. 34.11.
[^7]: I.e., “the contemporaries of.”
[^8]: And so never heard of the gifted youth.
[^9]: For despite his wisdom, he too will be forgotten; cf. 2.16.
[^10]: I.e., the advantage of wisdom over folly.
[^11]: Lit. “Guard your foot when it [or, you] would go.”
