> [!note] Diatribe against the Jews *(p. 421)*
> First Thess 2.14b–16 reflects the tension that sometimes occurred between those Jews who did not accept Jesus as Messiah (see “Jewish Responses to Believers in Jesus,” p. 730) and those who did. These verses present a succinct summary of classical Christian anti-Judaism: the Jews killed Jesus, persecuted his followers, and threw them out of the synagogues; God has therefore rejected and punished them. The harshness of these words raises questions about Paul’s attitude toward his fellow Jews.
>
> Because the Greek word for Jews, *Ioudaioi*, means both “Jews” and “Judeans,” Paul’s invective may be directed at this geographically limited group (see “*Ioudaios*,” p. 596), but readers through the centuries have understood the reference to be about all Jews, not just those in Judea.
>
> Some scholars argue that these verses are an interpolation written by someone other than Paul. The verses are not consistent with Paul’s comments about Jews in Rom 9–11, nor are they integral to the letter; were they excised, the narrative flow would not be affected. These scholars also suggest that the “wrath” (2.16) refers to the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE, after Paul’s death.
>
> However, no ancient manuscript lacks these verses, and they fit logically and stylistically into the epistle’s context. Paul elsewhere uses strong language about his opponents consistent with his apocalyptic, dualistic worldview (e.g., [[Galatians 05#^5-2|Gal 5.2–26]]; [[2 Thessalonians 01#^1-5|2 Thess 1.5–12]]). Indeed, some Jews, including Paul himself (see [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]), did attempt to “hinder” ([[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-16|2 Thess 2.16]]) the Gentile mission, most likely because the conversion of Gentiles away from worshiping the gods of Rome made the followers of Jesus politically suspect (not to worship the gods was considered treasonous; see “Paul and Judaism,” p. 633) and so put local Jewish communities in danger. The “wrath” could refer to the death of Herod Agrippa I in 44 CE, the famine in the land of Israel in 47 CE (Josephus, *Ant*. 20.41,101; see [[Acts 11#^11-28|Acts 11.28]]), Claudius’s expulsion of the Jews from Rome in 49 CE, or another tragic event.
>
> In 1965, the Vatican II document “*Nostra Aetate*” (Lat “in our time”) stated, “what happened in His \[Jesus’\] passion cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today.” Several Protestant denominations as well as countless individual Christians have similarly rejected the idea of Jewish communal and perpetual guilt for the crucifixion of Jesus.

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Approved by God*, tested, emphasizing God’s choice of Paul. *Entrusted*, a reference to Paul’s commission, see [[Romans 06#^6-7|Rom 6.7]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-17|1 Cor 9.17]]. *Tests our hearts*, knows even our thoughts; see e.g., [[Jeremiah 11#^11-20|Jer 11.20]]; [[Jeremiah 12#^12-3|12.3]]. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-5|5]]:** *God is our witness*, the language of an oath. ^2-5

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Demands*, financial support, see [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-3|1 Cor 9.3–12]]. *Apostles*, those “sent out,” refers to Jesus’ initial disciples or, more broadly, to Paul and his missionary companions ([[Romans 01#^1-1|Rom 1.1]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-1|1 Cor 1.1]]). *Nurse*, usually understood as a wet-nurse, but in context could also mean “mother.” ^2-7

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Our own selves*, by personally visiting the community. ^2-8

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-9|9]]:** *Labor*, Paul was a leather-worker ([[Acts 18#^18-3|Acts 18.3]]). The rabbis value labor as both providing subsistence and guarding against sin (e.g., *b. Qidd.* [[Kiddushin 29#29a|29a]]: “He who does not teach his son a trade is as though he taught him to be a robber”); among Roman elites, the availability of slaves lead to the devaluation of labor. ^2-9

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Like a father*, see [[Hosea 11#^11-1|Hos 11.1–4]]; [[Psalms 103#^103-13|Ps 103.13]]; [[Proverbs 04#^4-1|Prov 4.1]]; [[LXX Sirach 07#^7-3|Sir 7.3]]; [[LXX Sirach 23#^23-1|23.1-4]]; [[LXX Sirach 39#^39-13|39.13]]; Philo, *Life of Moses* 1.328. ^2-11

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Worthy of God*, i.e., pleasing to God. *Calls you*, in the sense of election; see [[Isaiah 41#^41-9|Isa 41.9]]; [[Isaiah 42#^42-6|42.6]]; [[Isaiah 43#^43-1|43.1]]; [[Isaiah 45#^45-3|45.3]]. *Kingdom*, the expected reign of God. *Glory*, God’s glorious presence (Heb “kavod,” e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-18|Ex 33.18]]). ^2-12

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-13|2.13-16]]:** **Persecuted churches.** See “Diatribe against the Jews,” p. 421 ^2-13

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Imitators,* see 1.6*. Churches*, see 1.1. *Judea*, the Roman province encompassing Jerusalem. *Compatriots*, see 1.6. ^2-14

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Killed . . . prophets*, see e.g., [[Jeremiah 26#^26-20|Jer 26.20–23]]; [[Luke 11#^11-47|Lk 11.47]]; [[Luke 13#^13-34|13.34]]; [[Acts 07#^7-52|Acts 7.52]]; *Lives of the Prophets*; *Ascension of Isaiah.* ^2-15

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-16|16]]:** *Filling up the measure*, [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-16|Gen 15.16]]; [[Daniel 08#^8-23|Dan 8.23]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-14|2 Macc 6.14]]. ^2-16

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-18|18]]:** *Satan*, mentioned in the Gospels as tempting/testing Jesus ([[Matthew 04#^4-10|Mt 4.10]] || [[Mark 01#^1-13|Mk 1.13]]), by Paul also in [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-5|1 Cor 5.5]]; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-5|7.5]]; [[2 Corinthians 02#^2-11|2 Cor 2.11]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-14|11.14]]; etc.). “The Satan” is a member of the heavenly court ([[Job 02#^2-6|Job 2.6]]); for the rabbis, Satan is less the evil agent than one who tests the righteous (*b. Git.* [[Gittin 52#52a|52a]]; *Pesiq. Rab.* 185b–186a). See also 3.5n. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue 1 Thessalonians 02#^2-19|19]]:** *Crown of boasting*, see [[Ezekiel 16#^16-12|Ezek 16.12]]; [[Ezekiel 23#^23-42|23.42]]; [[Proverbs 16#^16-31|Prov 16.31]]; Paul’s language suggests the crown of laurels given to the victor in an athletic contest (see also [[Philippians 03#^3-12|Phil 3.12–14]]). ^2-19
