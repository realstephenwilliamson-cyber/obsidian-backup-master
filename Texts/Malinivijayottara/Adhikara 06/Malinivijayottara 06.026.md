> **6) The Intermediate (Parāpara) Procedure**
>
> One should know that, as before, the Earth principle (occupies) four fingers. One should note that (the principles beginning with Water and) ending with the intellect measure two and half fingers (each). One should know that Fundamental (Nature) (occupies a space of) three fingers and should teach that the rest are as before.[^35] (with 6.25–26ab)

> **7) The Supreme (Para) Procedure**
>
> In the case of the supreme (para) procedure also, Earth is as before and the others (i.e., the principles from Water to the intellect each measure) three fingers and Fundamental (Nature) occupies four units. One should deploy the rest as before.[^37] (with 6.26cd–27ab)

[^35]: Abhinava explains in relation to the lower (Apara) deposition of the thirty-six principles that ' t h i s, t h e intermediate (parāpara) procedure (is such) because it (extends for an) extra twelve fingers, as the principles from Water up to the intellect occupy two fingers and a half and Nature (prakṛti) three.' TĀ 16/106 We have seen that the Worlds are deposited in relation to the thirty-six principles and that in that context the sequential order of the Kaṣcukas is unusual. However, we take the order to be the usual one in the deposition of the thirty-six principles. We may safely assume that is their order here also in the case of the Intermediate measure and Supreme one. The Intermediate (Parāpara) deposition into a space of 96 finger breadths is as follows. Earth – 4 fingers (from the soles of the feet to ankles) Water to Mind – 2 ½ x 22 fingers (from the ankles to 15 fingers above the navel) = 55 fingers Nature – 3 fingers (from 15 fingers above the navel to 18 fingers above the navel) = 3 fingers Puruṣa to Kalā – 3 x 6 fingers (8 fingers above the navel to the palate) = 18 fingers Māyā to Sadāśiva – 4 x 4 fingers (from the palate to the Cavity of Brahmā) = 16 fingers. 4 + 55 + 3 + 18 + 16 = 96 fingers

[^37]: MV 6/24cd-27ab is quoted in TĀv ad 16/101-107, which is a paraphrase of that. In Tantrāloka 16/107 we read that 'the supreme procedure is such as it occupies twenty-four more fingers' (space than the lower procedure), and so comprises one hundred and eight digits, with the principles from Water to the intellect occupying three fingers (each) and Nature four.' The Supreme (Para) deposition in 108 finger breadths is as follows. Earth – 4 fingers (from soles of the feet to the ankles). Water to Mind – 3 x 22 fingers (up to six fingers below the throat) = 66 fingers. Nature – 4 fingers (from ten to six fingers below the throat). = 4 fingers. Puruṣa to Kalā – 3 x 6 fingers (from six fingers below the throat to four fingers below the Cavity of Brahmā) = 18 fingers. Māyā to Sadāśiva – 4 x 4 fingers (to the End of the Twelve) = 16 fingers. This makes 4 + 66 + 4 + 18 + 16 = 108 finger breadths. See appendix for the charts of the layout of the Intermediate and Supreme procedures. Abhinava adds to the presentation here of the three layouts by relating them to the five spheres of Forces (kalā). In that context the extra twelve and twenty-four fingers' spaces are added to the second Force, called Pratiṣṭhī. Thus, he writes (in TĀ 16/147cd-149ab): 'The four (Forces (*kalā*)) beginning with Cessation (*nivṛtti*) each occupy four, forty-six, twenty-two, and twelve (fingers' space, respectively). The Force Beyond Quiescence (*śāntyatīta*) pervades (the others). The other two (procedures), intermediate and supreme, (are formed) by adding twelve (fingers' space), and (then) twelve (more, respectively,) within the second Force.'
