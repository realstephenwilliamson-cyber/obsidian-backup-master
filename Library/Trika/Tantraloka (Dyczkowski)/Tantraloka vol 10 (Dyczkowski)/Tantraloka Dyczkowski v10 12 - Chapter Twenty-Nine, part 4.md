Therefore, he brings about the awakening (of consciousness), repose (within it), and the penetration (of all of them), ‘culminating in the stable rooting (niṣthā)’', right up to the (final) state of repose in the Middle Abode (of consciousness), ‘of the two currents’, that is, the right and left currents (of the breath). Thus, he should take care at all times to abide on the plane of union, which is full (and perfect) consciousness. The meaning is that he should pay careful attention there (in that state). (He should do this) because from that comes the attainment of the formless state (of consciousness, which is such) because it is devoid of the limitations of the form etc. of the differentiated (sakala) (consciousness) of both those two forms, tranquil and emergent, on the plane of (determined and) fixed (distinctions). Thus, because (that consciousness) is devoid of parts (aṁśa), it is ‘devoid of division into measures (mātrā)’, and so it ʻillumines’, that is, makes manifest the one beautifully lucid reality, which is one’s own experience alone (free of form and thought constructs).
