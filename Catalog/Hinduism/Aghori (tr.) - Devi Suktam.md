## About

A hymn of the Rigveda (book 10, hymn 125) in which the goddess Vāc, Speech, proclaims her own power. Wikipedia also gives the names Āmbhṛṇī Sūkta and Vāgāmbhṛṇī Sūkta.

- **Seer:** tradition names the woman seer Vāc Āmbhṛṇī, daughter of the sage Ambhṛṇa, who is both the seer and the deity of the hymn. Wikipedia reports that Sāyaṇa's commentary reads it as Vāk praising herself, but marks the claim as needing a citation.
- **Length and content:** eight verses (Sacred-texts translation). The speaker moves with the Rudras, Vasus and Ādityas, upholds gods such as Soma and Tvaṣṭṛ, feeds all who see and breathe, bends Rudra's bow against the enemy, and pervades earth and heaven.
- **Date:** Wikipedia gives no date for the hymn beyond its place in the Rigveda.
- **Use:** chanted in goddess worship, in daily temple ritual, in Vedic rites such as iṣṭi and homa, and at the end of a recitation of the *Devīmāhātmya* (Wikipedia). AuroBharati (Sri Aurobindo Society) says it has been part of Caṇḍī worship.
- **A shared name:** in chapter 13 of the *Devīmāhātmya* the king and the Vaiśya (merchant) perform austerities while reciting a "Devī Sūkta". AuroBharati says many take this to be the Rigvedic hymn, while some take it to be the hymn of praise in chapter 5 that begins *namo devyai mahādevyai*. A text titled *Devi Suktam* may therefore be either one.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia calls the hymn an important basis of Śāktism, traces the origins of Tantric Śāktism to it, and cites it as Vedic recognition of women's realized consciousness. The article gives little supporting evidence for these claims.
