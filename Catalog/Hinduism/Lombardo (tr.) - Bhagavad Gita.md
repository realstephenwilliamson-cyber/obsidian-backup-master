## About

A Sanskrit dialogue in verse, set within the *Mahabharata*, in which Krishna, serving as charioteer, counsels the warrior Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra just before the great war. Wikipedia calls it the most prominent Hindu sacred text with pan-Hindu influence.

- **Title:** interpreted as "Song of God" (Britannica and Wikipedia), "the word of God", "the words of the Lord", "the Divine Song" or "Celestial Song" (Wikipedia); often styled *Shrimad Bhagavad Gita* as a mark of respect. It is distinct from the *Bhagavata Purana*.
- **Place in the epic:** the sixth book, the *Bhishma Parva*: chapters 6.23–40 in one numbering and 6.25–42 in another, according to recension. Read on its own, it is numbered as 18 chapters.
- **Form:** 700 verses, mostly in the *anushtubh* meter (about 1,400 lines), turning to *tristubh* at dramatic moments. Sanjaya narrates the dialogue to the Kuru king Dhritarashtra.
- **Author and date:** tradition names Vyasa; scholars generally treat it as composite, and Basham argued for three or more authors or compilers. Dates vary: Wikipedia reports the second or first century BCE as most often cited, with estimates from the fifth or fourth century BCE (Upadhyaya) to the second or third century CE (Sargeant). The IEP says it appears to be an interpolation in the epic and dates the *Mahabharata*'s formulation to the start of the Common Era.
- **Teaching:** duty (*dharma*), acting without attachment to results, and three paths: action (*karma*), knowledge (*jnana*) and devotion (*bhakti*). Wikipedia notes a Kashmir recension that does not alter the basic message.
- **Commentaries:** Shankara's (8th century), which gives the count of 700 verses, and those of the Vedanta philosophers Ramanuja and Madhva (IEP).
- **Why it matters:** it is central to Vedanta and Vaishnava traditions. Wikipedia says its prominence is a modern development, which Eric Sharpe dates from the 1880s (prominent after 1900) and Arvind Sharma to the 1920s; the IEP ties its standing as a separate scripture to the forming of a Hindu identity under British rule. Gandhi memorized the last 19 verses of chapter 2 as a companion in his nonviolent movement.
