# Jude 1 (JANT notes)

— · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue Jude 01|NRSVue]] · [[Jude 01|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Jude - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-1|1–2]]:** Salutation.  ^1-1

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-1|1]]:** *Jude* (Heb “Yehudah”), lit., “Jewish man” or “Judean,” the name of several NT figures, including Judas Iscariot ([[Mark 03#^3-19|Mk 3.19]]) and another “Judas” who, along with *James*, is listed as one of the brothers of Jesus ([[Mark 06#^6-3|Mk 6.3]]). [[Luke 06#^6-3|Luke 6.3]] and [[Acts 01#^1-13|Acts 1.13]] refer to “Judas of James”; although this phrase is normally understood as “son of James” it could also be translated as *brother of James*, if *James* is a sufficiently well-known figure. The letter-writer refers to himself only as the *servant* (lit., “slave”) of Jesus; yet since *James* was well known as “the Lord’s brother” ([[Galatians 01#^1-19|Gal 1.19]]) and leader of the Christians in Jerusalem (Acts 15, Gal 4), it is likely the author is also indirectly claiming to be Jesus’ brother. *Those who are called*, or “chosen,” used to describe the special status of the letter’s recipients, also called saints (v 3) or those set apart (see [[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]; [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-2|1 Cor 1.2]]). *Beloved . . . kept safe . . . love*, the standard form of salutation in Greek epistolography is expanded with blessings and prayers for the well-being of the recipients. ^1-1-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-3|3–4]]:** Reason for the letter. An otherwise commendable community must be warned against devious *intruders*, who have *stolen in among* the group but teach immorality. The community is “saved” from being *ungodly* and from *licentiousness*, but also *for the faith . . . entrusted to the saints*. ^1-3

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-3|3]]:** *Salvation we share*, communal salvation was a hope shared in this period by Jews (whose covenant bound them through history) and followers of Jesus (who understood God’s saving acts as binding them together as a new people). ^1-3-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-4|4]]:** *Long ago were designated for this condemnation*, as in many apocalyptic communities, like that at Qumran (which divided humanity into “children of light” and “children of darkness,” 1QS 1.9–11), humanity has already been divided into camps of saved and condemned. *Licentiousness*, accusations against the *intruders* are vague, but suggest sexual immorality. ^1-4

> [!note] Enoch Literature in Second Temple Judaism *(p. 533)*
> The extensive collection of stories known as *1 Enoch*, cited directly in Jude 14, records an elaborate angelology as well as apocalyptic expectations. These stories, framed as revelations to the primordial figure Enoch ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-21|Gen 5.21–24]]), who “walked with God” and was “taken” by God (that is, perhaps did not die a normal human death), circulated widely among Jewish communities in the late Second Temple period, and they remained influential among Jews and Christians in the early centuries ce. Instead of placing the origin of evil with Adam and Eve in Eden, *1 Enoch* interpreted the “sons of God” in [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-2|Gen 6.2]] as fallen angels whose interactions with humanity initiated a division between godly and godless humans that would last until the end of the world. *1 Enoch* survives in ancient Ethiopic texts, although Aramaic fragments were discovered at Qumran. Although a popular strand of thought among various Jews in the Second Temple period, and among several early Christian groups, the interpretation of the angelic “sons of God” fell out of favor in rabbinic interpretation and in later Christian explanations of the origins of evil.

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-5|5–7]]:** Disobedience is punished. Reminders of divine punishment from sacred history; see [[2 Peter 02#^2-4|2 Pet 2.4–6]]. ^1-5

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-5|5]]:** *Destroyed those who did not believe*, see [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^14-35|Num 14.35]]. ^1-5-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-6|6]]:** *And the angels*, refers to common legends of the “fall of angels” based on [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^6-1|Gen 6.1–4]] (see “Enoch Literature in Second Temple Judaism,” p. 533). ^1-6

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-7|7]]:** *Sodom and Gomorrah*, [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-4|Gen 19.4–11]], ties disobedience to “licentiousness” and fiery punishment. Although Jude 7 has traditionally been taken as a condemnation of homosexuality, *sexual immorality* (Gk “porneia”) may be a more general condemnation; *unnatural lust*, Gk “sarkos heteras,” “other flesh” (see translators’ note a), referring to those who had, or wish to have, intercourse with angels. In the Tanakh, the “sin of Sodom” was also seen as injustice and economic exploitation (cf. [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^19-5|Gen 19.5]] with [[Ezekiel 16#^16-49|Ezek 16.49]]). ^1-7

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-8|8–13]]:** Accusations against intruders.  ^1-8

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-8|8]]:** *Dreamers* might imply that the false teachers have replaced apostolic authority (see v 17) with personal visions and revelation. *Glorious ones,* possibly angels, understood as intermediaries (e.g., [[Daniel 09#^9-20|Dan 9.20–22]]) between the divine and human realms, and convey-ors of insight, moral exhortation, and steadfastness among Second Temple Jews. ^1-8-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-9|9]]:** The story of the archangel *Michael* contending *with the devil* for Moses’ body may be drawn from a missing part of the *Assumption* (or *Testament*) *of Moses*. This text probably dates from the first century ce and survives only in an incomplete Latin version; it is an apocalyptic version of Moses’ last words to Joshua. The legend that Michael and the devil fought over Moses’ body, which has no known clear source, is used here to combine references to good and bad angels (see vv 6,8) with overall concern for authority, morality, and truth. ^1-9

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-11|11]]:** Three villains from the Tanakh: *Cain*, fratricide ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-1|Gen 4.1–16]]), also considered in Jewish midrash (*Tg. Ps. -J.* , [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^4-1|Gen 4.1]]) a son of Eve and the devil; *Balaam* (Num 22), a false prophet; *Korah* (Num 16), a rebel against Moses. ^1-11

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-12|12–13]]:** These intruders are selfish *blemishes*, fruitless, destructive, and misleading; cf. [[2 Peter 02#^2-17|2 Pet 2.17–18]]. *Love-feasts*, the only time in the NT this term (Gk “agape”) is used in this sense, although it is used in other early Christian writings (e.g., Tertullian, *Apol*. 39.16) to describe communal meals that were presumably intended to increase the bonds of *love* in the community. *Twice dead*, those baptized who have fallen away; they have died once in baptism (see [[Romans 06#^6-3|Rom 6.3]]), and again by betraying their faith. ^1-12

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-14|14–19]]:** Intruders foretold.  ^1-14

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-14|14]]:** *Enoch*, descendant of Adam ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-18|Gen 5.18–24]]); his prophecy comes from *1 Enoch* 1.9. Jude takes “the Lord” to be Jesus. 1 ^1-14-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-7|7–18]]:** *Predictions of the apostles*, otherwise unattested *for themselves* f Gk *came* (though perhaps meaning those such as [[2 Thessalonians 02#^2-3|2 Thess 2.3]]); *the last time* reinforces the author’s apocalyptic world-view (cf. [[2 Peter 03#^3-3|2 Pet 3.3]]). ^1-7-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-20|20–23]]:** Exhortation. The recipients should hold firm, trust in their reward, and keep those *wavering* from following the *defiled* teaching of the enemies. ^1-20

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-20|20]]:** *Faith*, the teaching of the community, not a relationship of trust. *Pray in the Holy Spirit*, perhaps a reference to the worship practice of ecstatic utterance of words presumed to be from God (cf. [[1 Corinthians 12#^12-3|1 Cor 12.3]],[[1 Corinthians 12#^12-10|10]]). ^1-20-2

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-21|21–22]]:** *Mercy . . . eternal life*: the *love of God* as expressed in the *mercy* of *Jesus* brings the believers into the kingdom (*eternal life*) as opposed to the punishment of *fire* (v 23); *have mercy*, show mercy as you are yourselves shown mercy. ^1-21

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-23|23]]:** *Fire*, a post-mortem punishment also known as “Gehenna of fire” ([[Matthew 05#^5-22|Mt 5.22]]) or “Gehinnom” in later rabbinic writings (*m. Ed*. 2.10); *tunic defiled by their bodies*, the Greek is unclear but the author may be referring to impure contagion from the *defiled* enemies. ^1-23

**[[NRSVue Jude 01#^1-24|24–25]]:** Doxology. An extended, concluding praise of God (cf. [[Romans 16#^16-27|Rom 16.27]]). *Keep you from falling*, unlike the enemies who claim total moral freedom, Jude’s audience accepts reliance upon God; *without blemish*, language of sacrifice, in which both those serving *in the presence* in the Temple ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-17|Lev 21.17–18]]) and the offered animal ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-20|Lev 22.20–21]]) must be free from defect. *God . . . Savior, through Jesus Christ*, God saves by the agency of Jesus; unlike later texts (such as Jn 1) which view Christ as equal to God, here the messiah serves as a mediator of divine salvation. uncertain at several points ^1-24
