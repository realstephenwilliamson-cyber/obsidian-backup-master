## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. IX
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 09 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v09 06 - Chapter Sixteen, part 2#^pg103|p. 83]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 09.pdf#page=103>))
>
> By taking into account whether the (purifying) Mantras are of the immanent deity (sakala) or otherwise (niṣkala), with or without (their auxiliary) limbs etc.,' and by counting (also) the aforementioned fifteen etc. varieties of the object of purification, beginning with the principles etc., the varieties (bheda) (are so many that) it is impossible to enumerate (them all). These are the (primary) varieties (of initiations) that are not mixed (asaṁkīrna) (with other kinds). Moreover, (apart from them) there are many other (secondary) mixed (saṁkīrna) varieties (bheda) (of initiation) that combine? in many ways, because of the division into two and three etc. of the purifying (Mantras) and (their) objects of purification etc. (with 16.191–193ab)
>
> Tf the goal is to attain worldly benefits (bhoga), the wise man should undertake as many rites (kartavya) as possible. What wonder is it that an abundance of causes should serve to give rise to an abundance of fruits? Liberation (is one only), it is certainly not various, thus (the teacher whose) mental dispositions (vāsanā) are firm (and stable) in (executing) this (procedure) may also resort to a brief (simple) rite or just knowledge alone (vijñānamātra) (that he imparts to his disciple directly). (with 16.193cd–195ab)
