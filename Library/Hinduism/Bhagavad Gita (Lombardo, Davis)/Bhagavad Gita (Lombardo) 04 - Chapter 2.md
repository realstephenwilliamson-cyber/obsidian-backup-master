## Chapter Two

Sanjaya went on:

\[1\] Krishna, Slayer of Madhu, spoke
to the despairing Arjuna,
who was overwhelmed with pity
and whose eyes were brimming with tears:

\[2\] “Why has this craven timidity
appeared at this critical hour?
It is ignoble, Arjuna,
and closes the gates of heaven.

\[3\] Son of Pritha, do not give in.
This cowardice is beneath you!
Shake off this vile faint-heartedness
and rise up, O Scourge of the Foe!”

Arjuna answered:

\[4\] “How can I fight back with arrows
against Bhishma in this battle?
Or Drona? Both of them deserve
my respect, O Demon Slayer.

\[5\] Better to beg alms in this world than to kill
   my honored teachers. If I were to kill them,
however greedy for gain they now may be,
   all of my pleasures would be tainted with blood.

\[6\] Nor do I know what would be preferable,
   to win or to lose. The sons of Dhritarashtra
stand here before us in battle formation.
   If I kill them, I will lose my will to live.

[p. 11] \[7\] I am overwhelmed with a sense of pity,
   and my mind is confused as to my duty.
What would be best, I ask you? Tell me clearly.
   Advise me, your disciple, I beseech you.

\[8\] I see nothing to dispel this grief of mine
   that drains my senses dry and saps their power,
not even if I were to obtain royal power
   unrivaled on earth, or lordship of the gods.”

Sanjaya then said:

\[9\] After Arjuna spoke these things
to Krishna, Lord of the Senses,
he added, “I will not fight,
Govinda,” and then fell silent.

\[10\] Then, O Bharata, Lord Krishna,
a smile playing on his lips, spoke
to the despondent Arjuna
there between the opposing armies.

And the Lord said:

\[11\] “You mourn those who should not be mourned,
and yet speak as if you were wise.
But those who truly are wise mourn
neither the living nor the dead.

\[12\] Never have I not existed,
nor have you ever not existed,
neither have any of these lords.
Nor will we ever cease to exist.

\[13\] Just as our embodied being
goes through childhood, youth, and old age,
so too getting another body
is not confusing to the wise.

[p. 12] \[14\] Sensory contact is the cause
of cold and heat, pleasure and pain.
Impermanent, they come and go.
You must just bear them, Bharata!

\[15\] Bull of a Man, whoever remains
the same in happiness and grief,
disturbed by neither, such a sage
is fit for immortality.

\[16\] In the unreal nothing exists,
and in the real nothing ceases.
Both these conclusions have been reached
by those who know the highest truth.

\[17\] Know that what pervades the cosmos
is in fact indestructible.
No one is able to destroy
this imperishable being.

\[18\] Only the bodies of the eternal,
indestructible, embodied Self
are said to die, come to an end.
Therefore, Arjuna, join battle!

\[19\] Whoever thinks this embodied Self
is either the slayer or the slain
simply has not comprehended
that it neither slays nor is slain.

\[20\] Neither is it born, nor does it ever die,
   does not come into or go out of existence.
Unborn, eternal, perpetual, primordial,
   it cannot be slain when the body is slain.

\[21\] One who knows the indestructible,
the eternal, undying, unborn—how [p. 13] can he cause one to be slain?
Whom, Son of Pritha, does he slay?

\[22\] As, after casting aside worn-out garments,
   a person takes and puts on other, new clothes,
so too, after discarding worn-out bodies,
   the *Atman*, Self, encounters other new ones.

\[23\] Weapons cannot cut or slash it,
nor can it be burned by fire;
water does not douse or drown it,
nor can it be withered by wind.

\[24\] Not to be pierced, not to be burned,
neither to be drowned nor withered,
all-pervading and eternal,
it is unmoving and primeval.

\[25\] It is said to be unmanifest,
inconceivable, unchanging.
If you understand it as such,
you are obliged not to lament.

\[26\] And even if you think the Self
is constantly being born or dying,
even then, O Mighty Armed One,
you are obliged not to mourn it.

\[27\] For all who are born, death is certain,
and birth is certain for the dead.
Since this cannot be avoided,
you are obliged not to mourn it.

\[28\] Beginning as unmanifested,
beings then become manifest
and end as unmanifested.
What is there to lament in this?

[p. 14] \[29\] There are some who perceive this as wondrous,
   there are others who say it is wondrous,
and still others who hear it as wondrous.
   But there is no one who understands it.

\[30\] That which indwells every body
is inviolable forever.
You should therefore never lament
any being, O Bharata.

\[31\] Perceiving your own proper duty,
you should not tremble or waver.
Nothing is nobler than rightful war
for those in the warrior class.

\[32\] And if by good luck they attain
to the open gates of heaven,
warriors rejoice, Arjuna,
to have fought in such a battle.

\[33\] If, however, you should refuse
to fight in this rightful battle,
shunning duty and glory both,
you will incur iniquity.

\[34\] Everyone will talk about
your disgrace and shame forever;
ill repute is far worse than death
for whoever has known honor.

\[35\] The great heroes will think that you
have held back through fear of battle,
and among those who esteemed you,
you will become of little worth.

\[36\] And your enemies will say things
that should not be said about you,
[p. 15] mocking you and your prowess.
What suffering could be greater?

\[37\] Slaughtered, you will attain heaven.
Victorious, you will enjoy earth.
Therefore rise up, son of Kunti,
Resolved now to enter battle!

\[38\] Treating pleasure and pain the same,
gain and loss, victory and defeat,
join battle and engage in war!
Doing so you will incur no guilt.

\[39\] *Samkhya* has made this clear to you.
Hear now insight into *Yoga*.
Practicing this, Son of Pritha,
will free you from all *karmic* bonds.

\[40\] Effort is never wasted here,
nor is there any loss of ground.
Even a little of this practice
rescues you from great danger.

\[41\] Arjuna, awareness must be
single-minded and resolute.
A mind whose attention wavers
divides and branches endlessly.

\[42\] The ignorant all deliver
flowery speeches, Arjuna.
They delight in Vedic doctrine
and proclaim there is nothing else.

\[43\] Greedily intent on heaven,
seeking rebirth as *karmic* fruit,
they perform abundant rituals
aimed at pleasure and at power.

[p. 16] \[44\] Those attached to pleasure and power,
those perverted by this language,
do not achieve meditation
fixed in resolute awareness.

\[45\] Indifferent to paired opposites
ignore the three Vedic *gunas*,
remain in goodness, free from greed,
and be completely Self-possessed.

\[46\] As useless as is a wellspring
when the land is inundated,
equally useless are the *Vedas*
for a *brahmin* who knows the Self.

\[47\] Your only right is to action,
never to the fruits of action.
Never give rise to this motive,
but don’t be inactive either.

\[48\] Do all things steadfast in *Yoga*,
without attachment, Arjuna,
the same in failure and success.
*Yoga* is equanimity.

\[49\] Action is far inferior
to cultivation of the mind.
Take refuge in enlightenment.
Pity those seeking action’s fruits.

\[50\] The enlightened cast off in life
all acts of good and evil both.
Therefore yoke yourself to *Yoga*,
equanimity in action.

\[51\] The truly wise, minds enlightened,
who have abandoned action’s fruits,
[p. 17] are freed from rebirth’s bondage
and go to where there is no pain.

\[52\] When your mind is extricated
from the thickets of delusion,
then you will become disgusted
with all the teaching you have heard.

\[53\] Disregarding Vedic doctrine,
your mind deep in meditation,
immovable and firmly fixed—
then you will be one with *Yoga*.”

Then Arjuna said:

\[54\] “What is he like, Krishna, the man
composed in deep meditation,
his mind steady? How might he speak,
how might he sit, how might he move?”

And the Lord answered:

\[55\] “When he leaves behind desires
that come, Arjuna, from the mind,
the Self contented with the Self,
he is called steadfast in wisdom.

\[56\] The man undisturbed by suffering,
not desirous of pleasure,
free from passion, fear, and anger,
steady in thought, is called a sage.

\[57\] Without desire, whatsoever
he encounters, pleasant or not,
without any likes or dislikes—
such a person’s wisdom stands firm.

[p. 18] \[58\] And when he withdraws his senses
from their objects, like a tortoise
withdrawing back into its shell,
such a person’s wisdom stands firm.

\[59\] Sensory objects fade away
if you abstain, but their taste remains.
Taste for them also fades away
when you have seen the Ultimate.

\[60\] Son of Kunti, even the man
who strives for insight and wisdom
has his mind torn away from him
by the violence of the senses.

\[61\] To restrain his senses he should
sit in *Yoga*, intent on Me.
Whoever can control his senses,
such a person’s wisdom stands firm.

\[62\] If you dwell upon sense objects,
attachment to them will be born.
From attachment desire is born,
and anger is born from desire.

\[63\] From anger delusion arises;
from delusion memory fails,
and intellect is then destroyed.
When that is destroyed, one is lost.

\[64\] But without desire and hatred
even when using the senses,
the man self-controlled by the Self
attains tranquility and peace.

\[65\] In tranquility all sorrows
begin to cease and withdraw.
[p. 19] Once the mind is calm and tranquil
its enlightenment is steady.

\[66\] The uncontrolled have no wisdom,
nor ability to meditate.
No meditation means no peace.
Without peace no one is happy.

\[67\] Whenever your mind follows the lead
of the senses as they wander,
your wisdom can be compared to
a ship blown by wind on water.

\[68\] Likewise, warrior Arjuna,
in one whose senses are withdrawn
far from all sensory objects,
wisdom is established firmly.

\[69\] In what is night for all beings
the disciplined man is awake.
When other beings are awake,
it is night for the watchful sage.

\[70\] Like the ocean, full but unmoved
when the rivers pour into it,
be unmoved when desires pour in.
So doing, you will attain peace.

\[71\] The person who casts off desires,
who acts free from craving and lust,
indifferent to “I, my, me,”
that person will arrive at peace.

\[72\] This is the divine, Arjuna.
Attain this, free of delusion,
firm even at the hour of death,
and in *Brahman* reach *nirvana*.”
