# Luke 3 (JANT notes)

### 3.1–6: John the Baptist

*[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 116* · ([[Matthew 03#^3-1|Mt 3.1–6]]; [[Mark 01#^1-2|Mk 1.2–6]]). See Josephus, *Ant*. 18.5.2. Some scholars believe the original version of Luke’s Gospel began here (see Introduction and compare Mk 1). ^3-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Tiberius* ruled 14–37 CE. *Pontius Pilate*, Roman governor of *Judea*, Samaria, and Idumea (26–36 CE). *Herod* Antipas (see 23.6–7) ruled *Galilee* and Perea (4 BCE–29 CE). *Philip*, ruled 4 BCE–34 CE; see 3.19–20. *Lysanias*, Roman tetrarch of Abilene (west of Damascus), ca. 25–30 CE. ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Annas*, high priest 6–15 CE, then deposed by Rome. He was succeeded by his sons, then his son-in-law *Caiaphas* (18–36 CE). On biblical dating by reigning powers, see e.g., [[Isaiah 06#^6-1|Isa 6.1]]; [[Jeremiah 01#^1-1|Jer 1.1–3]]; [[Ezekiel 01#^1-1|Ezek 1.1–3]]. *Word of God came*, Heb “vayhi dvar YHWH el . . . ,” a frequent formula in the Tanakh. *Zechariah*, see 1.5–23,59–80. *Wilderness*, see 1.80n. ^3-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-3|3]]:** *Baptism*, from Gk for “dip,” ritual immersion. Unlike “miqveh” immersion for ritual purity, John’s baptism was apparently a singular event. Josephus (*Ant*. 18.117) states that John’s baptism did not wash away sins; rather, it served as public testimony of repentance. For washing as symbolizing God’s cleansing, see e.g., [[Ezekiel 36#^36-25|Ezek 36.25]]; [[Psalms 051#^51-4|Ps 51.2]]; for washing metaphorically related to removing evil, see [[Isaiah 01#^1-16|Isa 1.16]]. For 1QS 5.7–15, immersion functions also as a rite of initiation; there is no direct evidence of John’s connection to Qumran. “Jewish proselyte baptism” (i.e., immersion as part of the ritual of conversion to Judaism) appears to be a post-70s practice (*b. Yebam*. [[Yevamot 46#46a|46a]]). ^3-3

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-4|4–6]]:** See [[Isaiah 40#^40-3|Isa 40.3–5]] (see also 1QS 8.12–16). For Isaiah, *salvation* was Israel’s return from Babylonian exile to its homeland (“A voice cries out: ‘In the wilderness prepare . . .’ ”). For Luke, John is in the wilderness where he announces eschatological salvation. Cantillation marks (for chanting the text in the synagogue) place “in the wilderness” with “prepare the way”: the Jewish tradition thus also took a broader reading of Isaiah’s original meaning. See also [[Malachi 3#^3-1|Mal 3.1]]. ^3-4

### 3.7–18: John’s teaching

*[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-7|Text]], p. 116* ^3-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-7|7]]:** *Crowds* (see [[Matthew 03#^3-7|Mt 3.7–10]]), addresses John’s invective to Pharisees and Sadducees. *Brood of vipers*, Matthew’s designation for Pharisees, scribes, and Sadducees ([[Matthew 03#^3-7|Mt 3.7]]; [[Matthew 12#^12-34|12.34]]; [[Matthew 23#^23-33|23.33]]). The insult is fierce: vipers were thought to eat their way out of their mother’s body and so kill her. *Wrath to come*, God’s final judgment. ^3-7-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Abraham as . . . ancestor* (see [[Isaiah 51#^51-1|Isa 51.1–2]]; [[John 08#^8-33|Jn 8.33–39]]). Jewish tradition speaks of “zukhut ʾavot,” the “merits of the fathers” (see e.g., *b. Shabb*. [[Shabbat 55#55a|55a]]); see also 1.72–73n. *Stones . . . children*, an Aram (“avnayya . . .benayya”) and Heb (“avanim . . . banim”) pun. John rejects the idea of relying on one’s ancestry as a sign of divine favor. ^3-8

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Share*, caring for the disadvantaged is a major Jewish teaching (see, e.g., [[Isaiah 01#^1-10|Isa 1.10–20]]; [[Isaiah 58#^58-6|58.6–7]]; [[Ezekiel 18#^18-5|Ezek 18.5–9]]; Tob 1.16–17; 4.16; *b. Ber*. [[Berakhot 5#5a|5a]]; *Gen. Rab*. 30; etc.). ^3-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-12|12]]:** *Tax collectors*, most likely Jews in Rome’s employ, regarded as traitors and known for corrupt practices. ^3-12

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-13|13]]:** *Collect no more*, do not overcharge the population (19.8). ^3-13

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-14|14]]:** *Soldiers*, likely locals in service to Herod Antipas (Josephus, *Ant*. 18.5.1). Josephus describes John (*Ant*. 18.117) as, “A good man who commanded the Jews to exercise virtue, both as to righteousness towards one another and piety towards God.” ^3-14

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-15|15]]:** Some regarded *John* as the *Messiah*, a teaching maintained by a religious group known as the Mandaeans. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-16|16–18]]:** ([[Matthew 03#^3-11|Mt 3.11–12]]; [[Mark 01#^1-7|Mk 1.7–8]]). *Baptize you with the Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n.; [[Acts 02#^2-1|Acts 2.1–4]]; the phrase combines references to an initiation rite, an eschatological status, and a change from a profane to a holy state. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Gather the wheat* . . . *fire*, harvest imagery typical of eschatological teaching. ^3-17

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-18|18]]:** *Good news*, see 1.19n. ^3-18

### 3.19–20: John’s imprisonment

*[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-19|Text]], p. 117* · ([[Matthew 14#^14-3|Mt 14.3–4]]; [[Mark 06#^6-17|Mk 6.17–18]]). Herod Antipas divorced the daughter of the Nabatean king in order to marry Herodias, his niece, who had divorced his brother, Herod Philip. Josephus (*Ant*. 18.5.1) provides details. Jewish law prohibited marrying one’s brother’s wife ([[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-16|Lev 18.16]]; [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-21|20.21]]). ^3-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-19|19]]:** *Rebuked*, see [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-17|Lev 19.17]]; *b. Tamid* [[Tamid 28#28a|28a]]; *Sifra* 89a–89b; *Gen. Rab*. 54.3. ^3-19-2

### 3.21–22: Jesus’ baptism

*[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-21|Text]], p. 117* · ([[Matthew 03#^3-13|Mt 3.13–17]]; [[Mark 01#^1-9|Mk 1.9–11]]). ^3-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-21|21]]:** *Praying*, indicating Jewish piety (5.16; 6.12; 9.18,28; 11.1; 22.32,41–46). *Heaven was opened* ([[Isaiah 06#^6-1|Isa 6.1–4]]; [[Ezekiel 01#^1-1|Ezek 1.1]]; *2 Bar*. 22.1), direct divine revelation. ^3-21-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-22|22]]:** *Holy Spirit*, see 1.15n. *Voice came from heaven*, Heb “bat qol” (“daughter of the voice”), a rare means of divine communication (e.g., *b. B. Metz*. [[Bava Metzia 59#59b|59b]]; see also [[Luke 09#^9-35|Lk 9.35]]). *Son*, suggesting 2 Sam 7; [[Psalms 002#^2-7|Ps 2.7]]. *The Beloved*, see [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-1|Gen. 22.1]] (Isaac). For Jewish messianic interpretation of Ps 2, see *Pss. Sol*. 17.23–24; 4QFlor. *Well pleased*, see [[Isaiah 42#^42-1|Isa 42.1]]. ^3-22

### 3.23–38: Jesus’ genealogy

*[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-23|Text]], p. 117* · ([[Matthew 01#^1-1|Mt 1.1–17]]). Luke traces Jesus’ lineage to Adam ([[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-1|Gen 5.1]]), also a “son of God” (v 38; see Philo, *Virtues* 204–05). Matthew’s genealogy goes through Solomon (1.6) rather than Nathan ([[II Samuel 05#^5-14|2 Sam 5.14]]; [[Luke 03#^3-31|Lk 3.31]]). ^3-23

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-23|23]]:** *Thirty*, conventional age of service: [[Torah 10 - Miketz#^41-46|Gen 41.46]] (Joseph); [[Torah 34 - Bamidbar#^4-3|Num 4.3]],[[Torah 35 - Nasso#^4-23|23]]; [[II Samuel 05#^5-4|2 Sam 5.4]] (David); Dionysius, *Ant. rom*. 4.6. *Heli*, [[Matthew 01#^1-15|Mt 1.15–16]] names Joseph’s father Jacob in an allusion *today I have begotten you* e Gk *Salathiel* f Other ancient authorities read *Salmon* g Other ancient authorities read *Amminadab, son of Aram*; others vary widely to Jacob and Joseph of Genesis. Luke’s seventy-seven names (compare Matthew’s three divisions of 14) suggest completion. For *Adam* to *David*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^5-3|Gen 5.3–32]]; [[Torah 02 - Noach#^11-10|11.10–26]]; [[Ruth 4#^4-18|Ruth 4.18–22]]; [[I Chronicles 01#^1-1|1 Chr 1.1–4]],[[I Chronicles 01#^1-24|24–28]]; [[I Chronicles 02#^2-1|2.1–15]]. ^3-23-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 03#^3-38|38]]:** *Son of Adam*, *son of God*, Jewish tradition does not typically refer to Adam as a “son of God.” ^3-38
