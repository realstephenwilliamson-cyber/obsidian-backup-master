## Mūla

काष्ठा सैव परा सूक्ष्मा सर्वदिक्कामृतात्मिका ॥ ^mula

tasyāṃ divi sudīptātmā niṣkampo'calamūrtimān |

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. I
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 01 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v01 11 - Chapter One, part 10#^pg395|p. 381]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 01.pdf#page=395>))
>
> The same is said at the beginning (of a chapter) in the venerable Mataṅgatantra by (indicating various) distinguishing qualities of what is stated (concerning the Lord’s sustaining ground) at the beginning (of this passage, namely,) ‘now, the Lord’s sustaining ground (within which He is made manifest) consists of the rays of His own energies.’ (Thus it continues): ‘(residing in this way) within that light (of His power, He) is well illumined by nature (sudīpta), He wavers not and (so) possesses an immobile form. That same (power) is the supreme level (kāṣṭhā) (of existence), which, subtle and present in every direction (in space,) is nectar. (Every) obscuring covering completely destroyed, She is peaceful and is the intense delight within (that, which is nothing but) reality alone (vastumātra). Wise (sādhvī), without beginning (or) end, (She) is said metaphorically to be the iconic form (mūrti) (of the Lord).’ (with 1.202–204)
