> **The Sacrifice The Large and the Little Jar, Placement and Worship**
>
> There in the beginning (of the rite) one should take a jar without cracks made of gold or the like, that contains all (kinds of) jewels and herbs[^109] and is completely filled with fragrant water, its mouth (adorned) with young mango leaves and a garland and thread fashioned as its necklace. It is covered with with a mark (tilaka) that serves to destroy demons[^110] and is wrapped in a white cloth. It is worshipped with the root Mantra repeated one hundred and eight times.[^111] The small jar (vārdhānī) is also (adorned) like that, however, (unlike the large jar) it is worshipped with the Weapon (mantra).[^112] (with 8.101–103)

[^109]: Read as in the citation of this passage in TĀv and 381cd-388ab (378-385ab) *sarvaratnauṣadhī-* for *sarvamantrauṣadhī-.* We find the same expression below in 11/44ab.
[^110]: The expression *rakṣoghnatilakākrāntaṁ* may also be understood to mean 'covered with white mustard (rakṣoghna) and sesamee (seeds)' which seems to me to be less likely.
[^111]: Read *–saṁjaptamūla-* for *-saṁjaptaṁ mūla*-
[^112]: TĀ 15/381cd-391ab (378-388ab) are a paraphrase of MV 8/101-109. MV 8/101-103 is quoted in TĀv ad 15/381cd- 388ab (378-385ab). Abhinavagupta condenses these into TĀ 15/381-382a.
