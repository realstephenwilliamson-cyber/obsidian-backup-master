### 12.1–11: Anointing at Bethany

*[[NRSVue John 12#^12-1|Text]], p. 202* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-6|Mt 26.6–13]]; [[Mark 14#^14-3|Mk 14.3–11]]; see also [[Luke 07#^7-36|Lk 7.36–38]]). ^12-1

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-1|1]]:** *Bethany*, see 11.1n. ^12-1-2

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-2|2]]:** In [[Luke 10#^10-38|Lk 10.38–42]], Martha serves and Mary sits at Jesus’ feet. ^12-2

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-3|3]]:** *Pound***,** Roman weight of 327.45 grams (11 or 12 ounces). *Nard*, oil of (spike) nard plant whose rhizomes are crushed to extract an aromatic oil used as perfume; see [[Mark 14#^14-3|Mk 14.3]]. *Wiped . . . with her hair*, similar to Luke’s account but different from Mark and Matthew’s story in which the woman anoints Jesus’ head. ^12-3

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-7|7]]:** *Day of my burial*, Jesus interprets Mary’s act as foreshadowing his death. ^12-7

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-8|8]]:** *Always have the poor*, see [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^15-11|Deut 15.11]]. ^12-8

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-11|11]]:** *Jews were deserting*, the leaders are motivated both by political concerns (11.50) and by the number of Jesus’ followers. This formulation suggests that one could no longer be both a “Jew” and follower of Jesus; this does not likely reflect the reality of the period. ^12-11

### 12.12–19: Triumphal entry

*[[NRSVue John 12#^12-12|Text]], p. 203* · ([[Matthew 21#^21-1|Mt 21.1–9]]; [[Mark 11#^11-1|Mk 11.1–10]]; [[Luke 19#^19-28|Lk 19.28–38]]). ^12-12

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-13|13]]:** *Branches of palm trees*, palm fronds are most often associated with the Feast of Tabernacles, when palms were used to build temporary dwellings and in processions ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-40|Lev 23.40]]; [[Nehemiah 08#^8-15|Neh 8.15]]), but there is no way to know whether this detail was transposed from Tabernacles to Passover. [[Matthew 21#^21-8|Mt 21.8]] and [[Mark 11#^11-8|Mk 11.8]] refer to branches but do not specify which kind. *Hosanna . . . Lord*, [[Psalms 118#^118-25|Ps 118.25–26]]; *Hosanna*, Heb “please save” or “save now,” most directly associated with the Feast of Tabernacles (7.37–39n.). *King of Israel*, absent from the psalm. ^12-13

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-14|14]]:** *It is written*, combining and paraphrasing [[Zephaniah 3#^3-16|Zeph 3.16]] and [[Zechariah 09#^9-9|Zech 9.9]]. ^12-14

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-16|16]]:** *They remembered*, see 2.22. ^12-16

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-19|19]]:** *The world*, here, a vague reference to “everyone.” The idea that the “world” has gone after him in this case is to emphasize the Pharisees’ growing concern about Jesus’ popularity, a theme that is expressed also in 11.48. ^12-19

### 12.20–50: End of Jesus’ public ministry

*[[NRSVue John 12#^12-20|Text]], p. 203* ^12-20

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-20|20]]:** *Greeks*, likely Gentiles rather than Greek-speaking Jews (see 7.34-35 and note there). Anticipation of the broadening out of the group of believers that may point to a Gentile component of the Gospel’s intended audience. See Introduction. ^12-20-2

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-23|23]]:** *Hour has come*, perhaps foreshadowing the Gentile (to the “Greeks”) mission. ^12-23

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-24|24]]:** Jesus apparently refuses to meet with the Greeks. *Grain*, see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-36|1 Cor 15.36]]. ^12-24

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-28|28]]:** *Voice . . . from heaven*, a rare form of direct revelation, called a “bat qol,” wages for a laborer c Gk lacks *She bought it* d Other ancient authorities read *with him began to testify that he had called . . . from the dead* “daughter of the voice,” or “echo” in rabbinic Judaism (cf. 11.41–43). See, e.g., [[Eruvin 13#13b|*b. Eruv.* 13b]]. ^12-28

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-29|29]]:** *Thunder*, see the Israelites’ response at Mt. Sinai ([[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-18|Ex 19.18–19]]). Heb “qol,” at Sinai (esp. [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^19-19|Ex 19.19]]), may be voice or thunder. ^12-29

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-31|31]]:** The trial motif concludes. *Ruler of this world*, the devil (14.30; 16.11). ^12-31

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-32|32]]:** *Lifted up*, a double entendre referring to Jesus’ crucifixion, which is also his exaltation. ^12-32

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-34|34]]:** *Law*, here, scripture more generally, perhaps a reference to [[Psalms 089#^89-37|Ps 89.36]], which says that the Davidic line will continue forever. This verse can be read as a reference to the Messiah given the belief that the Messiah will come from the Davidic line. *Son of Man*, see 1.51. ^12-34

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-36|36]]:** *Children of light*, playing on the contrast between light and darkness that begins in the Prologue and continues throughout, e.g., 8.12. ^12-36

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-38|38]]:** [[Isaiah 53#^53-1|Isa 53.1]], a direct quotation of the LXX text of the Suffering Servant Song. ^12-38

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-39|39]]:** *Could not believe*, the Jews’ lack of understanding is seen as evidence of their inability to believe, which in turn must somehow be part of a divine plan, given God’s power over God’s creation. ^12-39

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-40|40]]:** See [[Isaiah 06#^6-10|Isa 6.10]]. In the original, the verbs are in the imperative (“Make . . . blind,” “Harden . . .”). This verse is quoted also in [[Matthew 13#^13-14|Mt 13.14–15]] and [[Mark 04#^4-12|Mk 4.12]] to explain the lack of understanding of the parables. ^12-40

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-42|42]]:** See 9.22. *Pharisees*, did not run synagogues. ^12-42

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-44|44–50]]:** Jesus claims that his authority, offer of eternal life and salvation, and the judgment that he announces, are not from himself but from God. ^12-44

**[[NRSVue John 12#^12-47|47–48]]:** *I do not judge anyone* . . . *word will . . . serve as judge*, the people’s refusal to act in accordance with Jesus’ words is what condemns them; judgment comes from God and is rendered on that basis. ^12-47
