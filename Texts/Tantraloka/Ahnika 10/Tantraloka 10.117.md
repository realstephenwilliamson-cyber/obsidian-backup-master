## Mūla

मानानां हि परो जीवः स एवेत्युक्तमादितः ।
नन्वस्ति स्वप्रकाशेऽपि शिवे वेद्यत्वमीदृशः ॥ ^mula

mānānāṃ hi paro jīvaḥ sa evetyuktamāditaḥ |
nanvasti svaprakāśe'pi śive vedyatvamīdṛśaḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 11 - Chapter Ten, part 2#^pg388|p. 369]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=388>))
>
> Thus, as (iva) is self-luminous, of what use are other vain arguments? We explained at the very beginning that (Śiva) alone is the supreme living essence (jīva) of (all) the means of knowledge. (with 10.116cd–117ab)
>
> Surely, (the opponent objects), although Śiva is self-luminous, He is objectively perceivable (vedyatva) (to some degree). Otherwise, how (could) the common interaction (vyavahāra) like this between (Śiva) the teacher and the taught (be possible)? (with 10.117cd–118ab)
