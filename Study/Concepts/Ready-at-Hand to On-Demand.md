# Ready-at-Hand to On-Demand

> [!abstract] Definition
> The structural transformation whereby cultural memory, liturgical practice, and epistemic authority cease to be inherited through embodied, communal habituation (*ready-at-hand* / *zuhanden*) and instead become externalized data objects retrieved instantaneously via automated technical systems (*on-demand*).

## Philosophical Foundations
1. **Martin Heidegger:** *Being and Time* (§15–18)—the distinction between readiness-to-hand (*Zuhandenheit*—the hammer in unreflective skilled use) and presence-at-hand (*Vorhandenheit*—the broken hammer inspected objectively).
2. **Bernard Stiegler:** [[Tertiary Retention|Tertiary retention]] as the technical constitution of collective memory.

## Diagnostic Application
In [[Cybernetic Anamnesis text 2025-11-05|Cybernetic Anamnesis]], this shift explains why modern digital culture experiences a profound collapse of trust:
- When tradition was "ready-at-hand," it was absorbed tacitly through family, community, liturgy, and bodily practice. One did not evaluate the algorithm; one lived within the practice.
- When cultural memory shifts to "on-demand," citizens query algorithmic intermediaries whose curatorial logic is proprietary and opaque. Because the medium cannot be verified, trust (*attestation*) breaks down.
