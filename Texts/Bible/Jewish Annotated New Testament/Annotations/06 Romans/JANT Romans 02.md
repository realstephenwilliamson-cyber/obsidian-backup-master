### 2.1–16: Human responsibility and divine judgment

*[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 289* · This ch is framed as a dialogue with a hypothetical Gentile who judges others without attending to personal faithfulness. That Gentiles recognize a universal moral standard demonstrates their culpability, not their superiority. People who know that God is just should be concerned with their own intention to do what is right rather than judging the failures of others. Those who use legal means to subvert what is right, as well as those who simply reject doing right, will be judged by the Righteous One, for the benevolent God holds everyone accountable. The ch undermines any notion that Paul separates responsibility to behave faithfully from the idea of belief. ^2-1

> [!note] Diatribe (ROM 2.1) *(p. 289)*
> Diatribe is a Socratic rhetorical technique, involving questions and answers, that is commonly employed by philosophers and rhetoricians such as Cicero, Seneca, and Epictetus. Changes of voice from singular to plural and first to second or third person are regular characteristics. Thus, Paul may be asking the questions, or put- ting them in the mouth of the interlocutor (or interlocutors), or some of each. The diatribe form sometimes makes it difficult to distinguish between Paul’s views and the views he attributes to his interlocutors. Romans was probably delivered by a messenger who carried the letter (perhaps Phoebe; see 16.1) who would read aloud and act out its parts.

> [!note] Law (ROM 2.12) *(p. 289)*
> *Nomos* is usually translated “law” and often understood to mean Torah, “Teaching/Instruction/Guidance.” (See “The Law,” p. 655.) Depending on context, Paul may be referring to laws or traditions in general, or more specifically to a law or tradition of a particular people, whether Jew- ish, Roman, Greek, etc. For example, in ch 4, Paul uses *nomos* to refer to circumcision, but not to Torah more broadly. He uses *nomos* in chs 7 and 8 to refer to the bond of marriage and of sin and death as well as of faith, life, and Christ. The point is that all people are subject to judgment according to some principle that they violate.

### 2.17–29: The responsibility of Jewish teachers

*[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-17|Text]], p. 290* · Turning from a dialogue with a hypothetical Gentile, Paul begins a dialogue with a fictive Jewish teacher of Gentiles. Since Paul is writing to Gentile Christ-followers, this imaginary Jewish interlocutor serves as a rhetorical gambit to emphasize that nobody, not even Jewish teachers, is above God’s judgment — nor would even these teachers be expected to claim to be. At issue is not Jewishness or glorying in and observing Torah, but preten-tiousness and hypocrisy; those who teach Torah are to exemplify the behavior it enjoins. It is not clear that there is any such Jew among Paul’s audience, and the chapter’s rhetorical power derives from the stereotype that Jews are the antithesis of hypocrisy (so this is an atypical Jew with whom Jews would find fault). Paul is teaching non-Jews Jewish values, not criticizing Jewish values, although traditional interpretations suggest otherwise. ^2-17

> [!note] Circumcision and “Works of Law” (ROM 2.25) *(p. 290)*
> “Circumcision,” the removal of the foreskin of the penis, signals for eight-day-old boys born to Jews their identity within a Jewish family. For non- Jews, whom Paul is usually discussing when referring to circumcision, it indicates the adult man’s transformation from Gentile into Jewish identity, i.e., proselyte conversion. This particular rite became a flashpoint for demonstrating commitment to Jewish communal identity, or not, following the Maccabean crises of the 160s bce, when the Greek rulers (and Judeans who were seeking to curry their favor) sought to make it illegal ([[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-10|1 Macc 1.10–15]],[[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-48|48]],[[LXX 1 Maccabees 01#^1-60|60]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-46|2.46]]). Circumcision was widely recognized among Greeks and Romans not only as a central identity marker for male Jews, but also for non-Jews who sought to become identified as Jews (Horace, *Sat.* 1.9.70; Tacitus*, Hist.* 5.5.2; Juvenal, *Satires* 14.96–99; cf. Josephus, *Ant*. 20.38–48).
>
> Paul refers to circumcision as *erg*ōn *nomou*, which is usually translated “works of law.” Paul uses the phrase to indicate the completion of the actions or rites involved in this identity transformation process: “works of the law” means for Paul the rites related to proselyte conversion. Paul insists that Gentiles who turn to God because of the faithfulness of Christ are to remain non-Israelites, and that is the faithful way for them to live. In contrast, Paul expects Jews to circumcise their sons as the faithful way to live. Similarly, for Paul, the Torah was given to Israel to guide behavior to demonstrate the righteousness of God to the nations, but it was not given to the nations. Paul sees circumcision as an advantage for (male) Jews, because it marks them as dedicated to God’s righteousness, and thus it also makes them deeply aware of sin and the need for God’s mercy and forgiveness (cf. 3.1–2,20). (See “Circumcision,” p. 673.)

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-17|17–24]]:** Epictetus, the Stoic-Cynic, offers similar criticism of those who call themselves philosophers but do not live accordingly — with which any philosopher would be expected to agree (*Diatr.* 2.19.19; 3.7.17; 3.22.93–94; 3.24.40–43; 4.8–9); the rabbis criticize hypocrisy similarly: [[Sotah 22#22b|*b. Sot.* 22b]]; *Avot de R. Natan* (A) 29.3. ^2-17-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-17|17]]:** The phrasing is theoretical (“if”), and ambiguous. The fictive interlocutor is portrayed as claiming the title *Jew* and to be a teacher of *those who are in darkness* (v 19), i.e., Gentiles. *Boast of your relation to God*, lit., “boast/exult in God”; *of your relation to*, not in Gk. ^2-17-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-18|18–20]]:** The point is not that one should not exult in God, or that Torah is at fault, but that it is self-evident that those who teach it should observe it. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-23|23]]:** *Boast*, or “glory.” ^2-23

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-24|24]]:** *Name of God is blasphemed* . . ., altered form of [[LXX Isaiah 52#^52-5|Isa 52.5]]\[LXX\]. Isaiah was indicting the nations, not Israel or even, as Paul adapts it, certain hypocritical Israelites. Paul describes the opposite outcome from what should result when Jews announce God’s righteousness to the Gentiles. ^2-24

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-25|25]]:** *Is of value*, lit., “helps.” *Break*, or sidestep (the observing of). *Uncircumcision,* lit., “foreskin.” Paul’s point is not to accuse Jews of hypocrisy but, by introducing a fictive UNJEWISH-like-Jew, to help Gentiles adopt the Jewish value of practicing what one teaches. Paul does not write “uncircumcision” and cognates, but “foreskin” and cognates throughout. ^2-25

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-26|26]]:** *Keep the requirements of the law*, if — without the benefit of Torah, or circumcision, which signifies commitment to Torah — Gentiles observe Torah’s intent by following comparable laws of moral and civil behavior, that would indicate what circumcision is meant to indicate. Paul makes the same point as in 2.14–16. Paul affirms that Torah’s requirements are righteous, although this inference in the Gk is omitted in the NRSV translation. ^2-26

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-27|27]]:** *Will condemn you*, an intentionally righteous Gentile accomplishing the intention of Torah, yet without the benefit of circumcision or Torah guidance (or “literal circumcision”), stands in comparative judgment of an intentionally hypocritical (male) Jew. *Physically uncircumcised,* lit., “foreskinned from birth.” *Keep*, lit., “accomplish the goal.” *Break*, or sidestep \[observance of\]. ^2-27

> [!note] Circumcision of the Heart (ROM 2.29) *(p. 291)*
> The idea that Israelites require a circumcision of the heart in addition to that of the body is a long-standing Jewish ideal related to being humble and repentant; see [[Torah 33 - Bechukotai#^26-41|Lev 26.41]]; [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^10-16|Deut 10.16]]; [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-6|30.6]]; [[Jeremiah 04#^4-4|Jer 4.4]]; [[Jeremiah 09#^9-24|9.25–26]]; 31.33; [[Ezekiel 44#^44-7|Ezek 44.7]] (note also *Jub*. 1.22–24; Philo, *Spec. Laws* 1.6; *QG* 3.46–52; 4Q434 \[= *Barkhi Nafshi* \]1 I, 4; [[Berakhot 29#29a|*b. Ber.* 29a]]). In the case of [[Jeremiah 09#^9-24|Jer 9.25–26]], it is specifically the Israelites who are punished for not having circumcised hearts. Paul seems to be working with the same distinction here: it is a special Jewish responsibility to live according to the ideals of being circumcised in the body, that is, to be circumcised in the heart and thus focused on walking humbly in accord with God’s will. Such a Jewish person is intentionally dedicated to righteousness (with every breath taken), and not simply to making an outward show.

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-28|28]]:** *True circumcision*, the ideals to which being known as a Jew and males having circumcised flesh should bear witness: *For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly* (lit., “For the \[ideal for a\] Jew is not \[seeking validation in\] the public acknowledgement \[that one is called a Jew\])”; *nor is true* (Gk lacks *true*) *circumcision something external and physical*, lit., “nor is (the validation by) circumcision in the visible display in the flesh (what counts most).” ^2-28

**[[NRSVue Romans 02#^2-29|29]]:** Paul’s point is not that Gentiles are the true Jews, or that the foreskinned are the true circumcision; quite the opposite: Jew and circumcision are reserved for Israelites. *Real circumcision* (Gk lacks *real)*; *it is spiritual and not literal*, lit., “by spirit (made manifest in the way one breathes/lives), not by inscription (i.e., not \[merely\] by a cut into the flesh).” Verse 29 could be translated: “Rather, the deepest character of the Jew, even the purpose of circumcision, is about the spirit, the intentions of the heart at work through the way one breathes/lives who is so marked, not (merely) inscribed (in flesh) (as if a mark alone fully defined who one is); the praise (ideally valued) is not from humans, but from God.” ^2-29
