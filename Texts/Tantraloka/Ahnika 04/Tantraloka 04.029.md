## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 02 - Chapter Four, part 1#^pg48|p. 35]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=48>))
>
> Again, the (form) that the Vaiṣṇavas and others (like them) have conceived by their own imagination (saṁkalpa) liberation to be, namely, union (sāyujya) with the Supreme Nature (paraprakṛti), or (by Vedāntins) as the blissful nature (of the Brahman), pure consciousness alone (by idealist – Vijñānavāda – Buddhists), or the extinction of the series (of individual existences) like (blowing out) a lamp (by Vaibhāṣika Buddhists). (They are all) in the state of (perceivers called) ‘Dissolution Deconditioned’ (Pralayākala) (which are of two kinds, namely,) those (in deep sleep) ‘associated with objectivity’ (savedya) (in which it is perceived,) and those (in deep sleep) ‘free of objectivity’ (apavedya) (in which it is not perceived). (with 4.29–30)
