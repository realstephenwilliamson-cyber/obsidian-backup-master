## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 02 - Chapter Seven#^pg21|p. 3]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=21>))
>
> All (Mantras) consisting of seed syllables (bīja) and syllabic groups (piṇḍa) are the (dynamic) pulsing nature (spandanātmatā) of consciousness. Thus, (a Mantra) is said to serve as a means (to attain) Supreme Consciousness (because such is its nature). (with 7.2cd–3ab)
>
> A waterwheel moves a series of machines for raising water located in front of it, and (so) possesses (bhajet) the varied emergent (activity of the) machines (it sets into operation) by (its) single, unified exertion. Similarly, when Mantras arise by the strength of a single (one-pointed and) continuous application (of attention and intention) (anusamidhāna), the deity of those Mantras, because it is at one with them, becomes propitious by virtue of (that) exertion (yatnāt). (with 7.3cd–5ab)
