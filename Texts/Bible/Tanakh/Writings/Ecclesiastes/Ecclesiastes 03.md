## Chapter 3

**3:1** A season is set for everything, a time for every experience under heaven:[^1] ^3-1

**3:2** A time for being born[^2] and a time for dying,  
A time for planting and a time for uprooting the planted; ^3-2

**3:3** A time for slaying and a time for healing,[^3]  
A time for tearing down and a time for building up; ^3-3

**3:4** A time for weeping and a time for laughing,  
A time for wailing and a time for dancing; ^3-4

**3:5** A time for throwing stones and a time for gathering stones,  
A time for embracing and a time for shunning embraces; ^3-5

**3:6** A time for seeking and a time for losing,  
A time for keeping and a time for discarding; ^3-6

**3:7** A time for ripping and a time for sewing,  
A time for silence and a time for speaking; ^3-7

**3:8** A time for loving and a time for hating;  
A time for war and a time for peace. ^3-8

**3:9** What value, then, can the man of affairs get from what he earns? ^3-9

**3:10** I have observed the business that God gave man to be concerned with: ^3-10

**3:11** He brings everything to pass precisely at its time; He also puts eternity in their mind,[^4] but without man ever guessing, from first to last, all the things that God brings to pass. ^3-11

**3:12** Thus I realized that the only worthwhile thing there is for them is to enjoy themselves and do what is good[^5] in their lifetime; ^3-12

**3:13** also, that whenever a man does eat and drink and get enjoyment out of all his wealth, it is a gift of God. ^3-13

**3:14** I realized, too, that whatever God has brought to pass will recur evermore:  
Nothing can be added to it  
And nothing taken from it—  
and God has brought to pass that men revere Him. ^3-14

**3:15** [^6]What is occurring occurred long since,  
And what is to occur occurred long since:  
and God seeks the pursued. ^3-15

**3:16** And, indeed, I have observed under the sun:  
Alongside justice there is wickedness,  
Alongside righteousness there is wickedness. ^3-16

**3:17** I mused: “God will doom both righteous and wicked, for there is[^7] a time for every experience and for every happening.” ^3-17

**3:18** [^8]So I decided, as regards men, to dissociate them [from] the divine beings and to face the fact that they are beasts.[^9] ^3-18

**3:19** For in respect of the fate of man and the fate of beast, they have one and the same fate: as the one dies so dies the other, and both have the same lifebreath; man has no superiority over beast, since both amount to nothing. ^3-19

**3:20** Both go to the same place; both came from dust and both return to dust. ^3-20

**3:21** Who knows if a man’s lifebreath does rise upward and if a beast’s breath does sink down into the earth? ^3-21

**3:22** I saw that there is nothing better for man than to enjoy his possessions, since that is his portion. For who can enable him to see what will happen afterward? ^3-22

[^1]: I.e., all human experiences are preordained by God; see v. 11.
[^2]: Lit. “giving birth.”
[^3]: Emendation yields “wrecking…repairing”; cf. 1 Kings 18.30.
[^4]: I.e., He preoccupies man with the attempt to discover the times of future events; cf. 8.17.
[^5]: I.e., what the author has already concluded (2.24) is good.
[^6]: Meaning of parts of verse uncertain.
[^7]: Shift of a diacritical point yields “He has set.”
[^8]: Meaning of parts of verse uncertain.
[^9]: Contrast Ps. 8.5–6.
