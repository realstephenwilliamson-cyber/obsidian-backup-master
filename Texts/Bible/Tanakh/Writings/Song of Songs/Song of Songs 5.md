# Song of Songs 5

Compare: [[NRSVue Song of Songs 05|NRSVue]]

## Chapter 5

**5:1** I have come to my garden,  
My own, my bride;  
I have plucked my myrrh and spice,  
Eaten my honey and honeycomb,  
Drunk my wine and my milk.  
Eat, lovers, and drink:  
Drink deep of love! ^5-1

**5:2** [^1]I was asleep,  
But my heart was wakeful.  
Hark, my beloved knocks!  
“Let me in, my own,  
My darling, my faultless dove!  
For my head is drenched with dew,  
My locks with the damp of night.” ^5-2

**5:3** I had taken off my robe—  
Was I to don it again?  
I had bathed my feet—  
Was I to soil them again? ^5-3

**5:4** My beloved took his hand off the latch,[^2]  
And my heart was stirred for him.[^3] ^5-4

**5:5** I rose to let in my beloved;  
My hands dripped myrrh—  
My fingers, flowing myrrh—  
Upon the handles of the bolt. ^5-5

**5:6** I opened the door for my beloved,  
But my beloved had turned and gone.  
I was faint because of what he said.[^4]  
I sought, but found him not;  
I called, but he did not answer. ^5-6

**5:7** I met the watchmen[^5]  
Who patrol the town;  
They struck me, they bruised me.  
The guards of the walls  
Stripped me of my mantle. ^5-7

**5:8** I adjure you, O maidens of Jerusalem!  
If you meet my beloved, tell him this:  
That I am faint with love. ^5-8

**5:9** How is your beloved better than another,[^6]  
O fairest of women?  
How is your beloved better than another[^7]  
That you adjure us so? ^5-9

**5:10** My beloved is clear-skinned and ruddy,  
Preeminent among ten thousand. ^5-10

**5:11** His head is finest gold,  
His locks are curled  
And black as a raven. ^5-11

**5:12** His eyes are like doves  
By watercourses,  
Bathed in milk,  
Set by a brimming pool.[^8] ^5-12

**5:13** His cheeks are like beds of spices,  
Banks of[^9] perfume  
His lips are like lilies;  
They drip flowing myrrh. ^5-13

**5:14** His hands are rods of gold,  
Studded with beryl;  
His belly a tablet of ivory,  
Adorned with sapphires. ^5-14

**5:15** His legs are like marble pillars  
Set in sockets of fine gold.  
He is majestic as Lebanon,  
Stately as the cedars. ^5-15

**5:16** His mouth is delicious  
And all of him is delightful.  
Such is my beloved,  
Such is my darling,  
O maidens of Jerusalem! ^5-16

[^1]: In vv. 2–8 the maiden relates a dream.
[^2]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^3]: Many manuscripts and editions read “within me” (‘alai).
[^4]: Change of vocalization yields “because of him.”
[^5]: See note at 3.3.
[^6]: Or “What sort of beloved is your beloved?”
[^7]: Or “What sort of beloved is your beloved?”
[^8]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^9]: Septuagint vocalizes as participle, “producing.”
