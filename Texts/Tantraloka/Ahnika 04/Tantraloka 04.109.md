## Mūla

तत्त्वे चेतः स्थिरं कार्यं तच्च यस्य यथास्त्विति ।
एवं द्वैतपरामर्शनाशाय परमेश्वरः ॥ ^mula

tattve cetaḥ sthiraṃ kāryaṃ tacca yasya yathāstviti |
evaṃ dvaitaparāmarśanāśāya parameśvaraḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. III
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 03 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v03 05 - Chapter Four, part 4#^pg141|p. 128]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 03.pdf#page=141>))
>
> ‘Rather, O mistress of the gods, it is enjoined here as a fixed rule (niyama) that the mind must be firmly fixed on (ultimate) reality, and that that be so for whomever in whatever way it may be (achieved).’ = (with 4.108cd–109ab)
>
> In this way, the Supreme Lord abides somewhere, constantly reflecting on His own stainless (amala) nature, so as to put an end to the awareness of duality (dvaitaparāmarśa). (with 4.109cd–110ab)
