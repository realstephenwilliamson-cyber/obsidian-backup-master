**1.** Where are the head *tefillin* placed?[^1] They should be placed at the point of the skull, the end of the hairline towards the face,[^2] the place where a child's brain [can be felt] to pulsate.[^3]  
Care must be taken to position them in the center,[^4] so that they will be[^5] "between the eyes."[^6] The knot[^7] should be at the top portion of the neck,[^8] the bottom of the skull.[^9] ^4-1

**2.** The arm [*tefillin*] should be tied to one's left arm[^10] at the muscle[^11] - i.e., the bulging flesh of the arm between the shoulder and the elbow.[^12] Thus, if one presses his arm to his ribs, the *tefillah* will be opposite his heart,[^13] thus fulfilling the directive \[[[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-6|Deuteronomy 6:6]]], "And these words... shall be upon your heart."[^14] ^4-2

**3.** A person who places the arm *tefillah* on his palm,[^15] or the head *tefillah* on his forehead,[^16] follows the way of the Sadducees.[^17] A person who makes his *tefillin* rounded like a nut[^18] does not fulfill the mitzvah at all.[^19]  
A left-handed person puts *tefillin* on his right hand, since [figuratively,] it is his left hand.[^20] If he is ambidextrous, he should place them on his left hand[^21] - i.e., his left hand in a literal sense.  
The places where to tie and place[^22] the *tefillin* were received as part of the oral tradition.[^23] ^4-3

**4.** The [absence of the] head *tefillah* does not preclude [wearing *tefillin*] on the arm,[^24] and similarly, the [absence of the] arm *tefillah*[^25] does not preclude [wearing *tefillin*] on the head.[^26] They are two mitzvot, each one to be considered independently.[^27]  
What blessings are recited? On the head *tefillin*, one recites:[^28] "[Blessed are You...] who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning the mitzvah of *tefillin*." On the arm *tefillin*, one recites: "[Blessed are You...] who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to put on *tefillin*."[^29] ^4-4

**5.** When does the above apply? When one puts on only one of them.[^30] If, however, one puts them both on,[^31] one recites only a single blessing, "...to put on *tefillin*."  
One[^32] should tie the *tefillin* on one's arm,[^33] and afterwards put on the head *tefillin*.[^34] When one removes them, the head *tefillin* should be removed, and then the arm *tefillin*.[^35] ^4-5

**6.** [After] reciting the blessing, "...to put on *tefillin*," and tying the *tefillin* on one's arm, one is forbidden to talk[^36] - even to respond to a greeting from one's teacher[^37] - until he puts on the head *tefillin*. If he talks, it is considered to be a transgression.[^38] [In such an instance,] one is required to recite the second blessing, "...concerning the mitzvah of *tefillin*," and then put on the head *tefillin*.[^39] ^4-6

**7.** Whenever a person puts on *tefillin*[^40] - even if he removes them and puts them on several times throughout the day[^41] - he should recite a blessing for them.[^42]  
With regard to all mitzvot: one recites a blessing for them before performing them.[^43] Therefore, one should recite the blessing for the arm *tefillah* after placing it on one's muscle,[^44] before tying it,[^45] since tying it comprises the fulfillment of the mitzvah.[^46] ^4-7

**8.** When a person removes his *tefillin* to place them in a container,[^47] he should not place the arm *tefillah* below and the head *tefillah* above, because when he wants to put them on, he will encounter the head *tefillah* first.[^48] Thus, he will [be forced to] ignore it and take out the arm *tefillah*, since one should not put on the head *tefillah* before the arm *tefillah*.[^49]  
[This is undesirable because] it is forbidden for a person to ignore one mitzvah and proceed to the fulfillment of another mitzvah.[^50] Rather, as soon as a mitzvah comes to a person's hand, he should occupy himself with it.[^51] Therefore, a person should place the arm *tefillin* above, so that he will encounter it first, and thus put on the *tefillin* in the proper sequence.[^52] ^4-8

**9.** A container that was made for *tefillin* to be placed in and which was used[^53] for that purpose[^54] becomes holy. It is forbidden to use it for mundane purposes.[^55]  
If a container was prepared for that purpose, but never used for it,[^56] or if a container was temporarily[^57] used for *tefillin*, but was not prepared for that purpose, it does not become holy. Rather, it is considered an ordinary article as before.[^58]  
It is forbidden to suspend *tefillin*.[^59] [This applies regardless of whether one suspends them] by their straps or from the *tefillah* itself.[^60] One may, however, suspend the bag in which they are placed. ^4-9

**10.** The time for wearing *tefillin* is the day and not the night, as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-10|Exodus 13:10]]] states: "And you shall observe this statute in its appointed time, from day to day." "This statute" refers to the mitzvah of *tefillin*.[^61]  
Similarly, Sabbaths and holidays[^62] are not days on which *tefillin* [are worn],[^63] as \[[[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-9|Exodus 13:9]]] states: "And they shall be a sign for you."[^64] Since the Sabbaths and the holidays are themselves signs,[^65] [the sign of *tefillin* is unnecessary].[^66]  
When does the time to put them on begin? When one can see[^67] a colleague standing four cubits away[^68] and recognize him.[^69] [It continues] until sunset.[^70] ^4-10

**11.** It is permitted for a person who put on *tefillin* before sunset to continue wearing them after nightfall.[^71] They may even remain upon him the entire night.[^72]  
This law, however, is not to be taught in public.[^73] Instead, everyone should be taught not to wear *tefillin* at night and to remove them before sunset.[^74]  
A person who puts on *tefillin* after sunset transgresses a Scriptural prohibition,[^75] as [implied by [[Torah 15 - Bo#^13-10|Exodus 13:10]]]: "And you shall observe[^76] this statute... from day to day."[^77] ^4-11

**12.** When a person is wearing *tefillin* in the midst of a journey and the sun sets, marking the commencement of the Sabbath,[^78] he should cover them with his hand[^79] until he reaches his home[^80] and then, remove them.[^81]  
If he is sitting in the house of study[^82] and the sun sets, marking the commencement of the Sabbath,[^83] he should cover them with his hand until he reaches his home, and then remove them.[^84]  
If there is a house located near the wall [of a city] where they would be safe, he should place them there.[^85] If, however, he did not remove his *tefillin* after sunset because he did not have a place where they would be safe, it is permissible for him to continue wearing them in order to protect them.[^86] ^4-12

**13.** All those who are exempt from the obligation to recite the *Shema*[^87] are exempt from the obligation to wear *tefillin*.[^88] If a minor knows [the importance of] guarding his *tefillin*,[^89] his father should obtain *tefillin* for him,[^90] to educate him regarding the performance of mitzvot.[^91]  
A person with stomach problems and anyone who can contain his excretory functions only with difficulty[^92] are exempt from the obligation to wear *tefillin*.[^93] [In contrast,] all those ritually impure are obligated to wear *tefillin* like one who is pure.[^94]  
A person who suffers discomfort, or someone who is disturbed and cannot concentrate his thoughts, is exempt from the obligation to wear *tefillin*,[^95] since a person who wears *tefillin* is forbidden to divert his attention from them.[^96]  
Priests who are in the midst of [the Temple] service,[^97] Levites who chant[^98] on the platform,[^99] and Israelites while they are attending the Temple [ceremonies][^100] are exempt from the obligation to pray and to wear *tefillin*.[^101] ^4-13

**14.** A person should touch his *tefillin*[^102] [from time to time] during the entire time he is wearing them,[^103] so that he will not divert his attention[^104] from them even for a single moment,[^105] for[^106] their holiness surpasses that of the *tzitz*.[^107] The *tzitz* has God's name [written] upon it only once,[^108] while the head *tefillin*[^109] - and similarly, the arm *tefillin* - contain the name י-ה-ו-ה 21 times. ^4-14

**15.** *tefillin* require a clean body;[^110] i.e., that one should be careful not to release gas while wearing them.[^111]  
Accordingly, it is forbidden to sleep while wearing them[^112] - neither a long sleep nor a nap. If, however, one places a cloth over them[^113] and a woman is not with him,[^114] one may nap while wearing them. What should he do? Place his head between his knees and sleep while sitting.[^115] ^4-15

**16.** A person who has *tefillin* wound around his hand[^116] is permitted to sleep with them. [This applies] even to a long sleep.[^117]  
He may eat only a snack[^118] while wearing them. If, however, he enters to partake of a regular meal, he should remove them[^119] and place them on the table until after he washes his hands [at the conclusion of the meal].[^120] Afterwards, he should put them on and recite grace over his meal while wearing them.[^121] ^4-16

**17.** [The following rules apply with regard to a person] wearing *tefillin* who must use the lavatory:[^122] [He should remove his *tefillin*[^123] but,] while he enters, he should not place them in the holes [of the outer wall of the lavatory] which are near the public domain, lest they be taken by passersby.[^124]  
What should he do? Even if he [merely] has to urinate, he should remove his *tefillin* four cubits away [from the lavatory] and roll them in his clothes like a Torah scroll,[^125] holding them near his heart with his right hand.[^126] He must take care that the strap does not protrude more than a handbreadth from his hand.[^127] Afterwards, he should enter and relieve himself. After leaving the lavatory and walking more than four cubits away,[^128] he should put them on.[^129] ^4-17

**18.** When does the above apply? In a permanent lavatory where drops [of urine] will not sprinkle upon him. In contrast, in a place that is temporarily being used as a lavatory, one should not enter, [holding *tefillin*] wound up in one's hand. Instead, one should remove them [outside the lavatory] and give them to a colleague to guard.  
Urine cannot be expelled [without drops sprinkling] even in a permanent lavatory unless one squats. If [the floor is covered] with soft dust, [drops will not sprinkle] even when one stands. If the floor is hard, one should stand on an incline [and urinate], so that drops will not sprinkle on him. ^4-18

**19.** When a person is wearing *tefillin* and must relieve himself in the evening, when there would be no time left in the day to put them on again after he finishes, he should not enter a lavatory, [holding them] rolled up in his clothes.  
What should he do [instead]? He should remove them and place them in their container if it is a handbreadth in size, or in another container which is not specific for them even though it is not a handbreadth in size. He should hold the container in his hand when he enters the lavatory. Similarly, if he needs to relieve himself at night, he should place them in a container and enter the lavatory, holding the *tefillin* in his hand. ^4-19

**20.** If a person forgot and entered a lavatory while wearing *tefillin*, he should cover them with his hand until he completes expelling the first issue of feces or urine, and then leave the lavatory, remove the *tefillin*, return, and complete relieving himself. Were one to interrupt in the midst of expelling the first outburst of feces or urine, he might become very dangerously ill. ^4-20

**21.** If a person forgot and had intercourse while wearing *tefillin*, he should not hold either the straps or the compartments themselves until he washes his hands. [Then, he should] remove them. [This restriction was instituted] because hands are active. ^4-21

**22.** [The following rules apply to] a person who enters a bathhouse: In the rooms where people stand clothed, it is permitted to put on *tefillin*. In the rooms where some of the people stand naked and some clothed, one need not remove one's *tefillin*, nor, at the outset, should one put *tefillin* on there. In the rooms where [everyone] stands naked, one should remove one's *tefillin* and, needless to say, one should not put them on. ^4-22

**23.** A person should not walk in a cemetery[^130] wearing his head *tefillin*.[^131] Even [outside a cemetery], a person should remove his[^132] *tefillin* if he comes within four cubits of a corpse or a grave,[^133] until he distances himself four cubits from them.[^134]  
A person should not put on *tefillin* [when he is naked]. He must first cover his genitalia[^135] and put on his clothes.[^136]  
A person who is carrying a load on his head should remove his head *tefillin* [and not put it on again] until he puts down his load.[^137] It is even forbidden to wear a handkerchief[^138] around one's head when wearing *tefillin*. One may, however, wear a hat over the *tefillin*.[^139] ^4-23

**24.** When *tefillin* or a Torah scroll are in a room, it is forbidden to engage in sexual relations, unless they are removed or placed into a container, and that container placed into a second container[^140] which is not specific to them.[^141] If, however, the second container designated for them, even ten[^142] containers are considered as a single container.[^143]  
Should a person place [the sacred articles] in two containers, he may place them at the head of his bed,[^144] between a cushion and a pillow,[^145] as long as they are not under his head,[^146] so that he can protect them.[^147] [This applies] even if his wife is together with him in bed.[^148] ^4-24

**25.** The holiness associated with *tefillin* is very great.[^149] As long as a person is wearing *tefillin* on his head and arm, he will be humble and God-fearing and will not be drawn to frivolous behavior or empty speech.[^150] He will not turn his thoughts to evil matters, but rather will direct his heart to words of truth and justice.[^151]  
Accordingly, a person should try to wear [*tefillin*] throughout the entire day,[^152] for this is the mitzvah associated with them.[^153] Among the praises conveyed upon Rav, the student of Rabbenu Hakadosh,[^154] was that he was never seen walking four cubits without [reciting words of] Torah, without *tzitzit*, and without *tefillin*.[^155] ^4-25

**26.** Although it is a mitzvah to wear [*tefillin*] throughout the entire day, it is most important during prayer. Our Sages declared: "Whoever recites the *Shema* without *tefillin* is considered as if he is giving false testimony."  
Whoever does not wear *tefillin* transgresses eight positive commandments, for in each of the four passages contained in the *tefillin* we are commanded to wear both head and arm *tefillin*. [The rewards for wearing *tefillin* are also great.] Whoever wears *tefillin* regularly will live long, as [implied by [[Isaiah 38#^38-16|Isaiah 38:16]]]: "God is upon them, they shall live." ^4-26
