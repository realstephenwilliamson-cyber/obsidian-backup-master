## The Authoritarian Personality

*4 May 1960*

\[…\] It may rather astonish you, in the context of a session devoted to the problems of authoritarianism and nationalism, to encounter a lecture that reveals an essentially psychological emphasis, or at least in the first instance seems to reveal such an emphasis. You may initially assume that this problem, in the way it is specifically posed in these sessions, really belongs in the realm of political science, or at least in the realm of sociology. Of course, I do not underestimate this aspect – and we shall come back to it later – but I wish to pursue something Freud once said about psychoanalysis in his early period.<sup>1</sup> In other words, I do not want to dismiss things which are generally acknowledged and generally accepted and replace them with more less faddish theories of my own. Rather, I would simply like to add something to the things you are already aware of – albeit something that has a distinctive value of its own in the context of objective social and political circumstances. On the other hand, I believe that I have very good reasons to foreground this psychological aspect more strongly than is usually done. I hope that I shall be able to justify this approach in the course of these reflections.

First of all, I should like to try and explain to you why the problem we are talking about in this session can also be addressed in terms of these psychological aspects. Let me just assume to begin with that, when we talk about psychological motivations in a very emphatic and specific sense, we are generally thinking of irrational motivations in contrast to the kind of conscious motivations that are connected with the ‘reality principle’,<sup>2</sup> with our everyday experience of reality, 

*[p. 99]* ^pg106

and which are therefore more obviously related to the objective social and political circumstances than tends to be the case with specifically psychological determinants. Yet we find that nationalism of every kind depends on expecting certain sacrifices from human beings, sacrifices which themselves answer to a certain kind of irrationality. Aggressive nationalism is only possible by demanding sacrifices of various kinds from human beings, beginning with those sacrifices that are connected with things such as military training and culminating in the immediate danger to the life of the individual and the group in the case of war. These are all sacrifices that are hardly compatible with rationality in the usual sense, namely with our interests in self-preservation or the immediate preservation of the interests the community to which the particular individual belongs.

History reveals abundant examples of how explosions of nationalism in particular have brought disaster rather than good fortune upon the nations involved. And when we look at the propaganda of the National Socialists today, we cannot fail to be struck by the remarkable fact that the promise of happiness and positive fulfilment plays a remarkably small role. What we find instead is a glorification of sacrifice, of negative renunciation, and ultimately of death – something which in Germany especially has a long pre-history in the glorification of everything we like to call ‘tragic’, a glorification on which our people, disastrously enough, has long prided itself. Now it may be that nationalism involves a certain contradiction between what human beings are enjoined to perform and their own rational ends. And this contradiction can only be managed by appealing to psychological dispositions and psychological potentials that already lie within human beings themselves and that explain why human beings are actually prepared to be used for ends and purposes that can spell personal destruction for themselves and may also threaten destruction for their people and even – we have to say today – for humanity as a whole. This is why the sphere of psychology, specifically as an irrational sphere, possesses a necessary function within the objective problematic of nationalism.

Nationalism, and especially militant aggressive nationalism, would actually be inconceivable without these psychological stimuli and motivations. To this extent, even if we are not at all tempted by psychologism – and I would explicitly underline that my own philosophical position, as many of you know, is utterly opposed to any psychologistic approach – we must still allow significant weight to the psychological aspects and elements that I have mentioned. I might just add here that psychologism – which as a general outlook or philosophical position is open to extremely serious objections – has 

*[p. 100]* ^pg107

of course nothing to do with the due recognition of psychological knowledge in the field where such insights and expertise actually belong. We can only properly speak of psychologism in relation to the attempt to explain forms of objectivity that do not essentially lie in the subjective sphere, or essentially in particular irrational dispositions, on the basis of entirely psychological motives. Thus I would like to insist from the start on this distinction between psychological knowledge that is aware of its own limits and psychologism as a specific philosophical position.

In this context we are talking about the problem of personalities that we might describe as ‘authority-oriented’, precisely because there is a very strong connection between this personality type – this characterological type, as we say – and objective political nationalism. I have already indicated – and perhaps this already leads us further into the central issue – that the objective function of the authority-oriented character is to allow human beings to be harnessed to irrational ends that may destroy these human beings themselves. We may also interpret this to imply that the specific character types in question are also those which tend to internalize the violence and compulsion visited upon them by the world as it is currently organized – in other words, to re-enact it upon themselves and, if possible, upon other human beings too, especially those who are weaker than themselves, and thereby pass on and extend this original pressure. The mechanism of adaptation to a world the meshes of which are so tightly woven that there is hardly any way out seems to be more successful, for understandable psychological reasons, the more human beings can somehow make this world and its demands their own – and I use this expression in a negative sense here – even though it is a heteronomous world that requires things of them that are quite incompatible with their own freedom and self-determination – and they basically do so in order to live and survive in this same world. But we have to recognize the validity of the general psychological observation that such adaptive tendencies also tend to acquire an independence of their own, tend to become sedimented as characterological structures in human beings themselves. We are talking here about a phenomenon that has been expressly formulated by depth psychology, and depth psychology in its strictest and most uncompromising form, namely in the theory of Sigmund Freud. The literal version of the formula actually derives from Freud’s daughter, Anna Freud, who among the various forms of repression or defence against hostile instinctual mechanisms specifically referred to the process she called ‘identification with the aggressor’.<sup>3</sup> This is a mechanism whereby the instinctual energies that we ourselves have 

*[p. 101]* ^pg108

to suppress are transformed in such a way – for these energies are indeed plastic and susceptible to such reshaping – that they come to serve the power or authority which is feared and to which we are utterly exposed, and which, as we also inwardly know, can only spell danger for us. I believe that I can now show you in a very simple way that this is certainly not a matter of far-fetched sectarian speculation – a charge so often levelled specifically at psychoanalysis in its strict form – since this is only a more telling formulation of something that is quite generally recognized and encountered. I have only to remind you of the type of person you will all have come across before: I am talking about the old soldier who recalls his time in the army, who tells you that he was put through it and really knocked into shape, that he was spared no imaginable hardship, but then adds that it was really the best time of his life.

Here you encounter a phenomenon that is described in the technical language of psychology as ‘ambivalent’. In other words, human beings who have to identify with the aggressor in this way and turn against their own interests do not succeed that easily or that quickly in all this precisely because it stands in direct contradiction to their own interest. Hence we also find here contrary resisting impulses at work, impulses which are hostile to the surrounding world. These hostile and aggressive impulses, provoked by the pressure that bears down on human beings, then come to serve the power or authority with which they identify. Thus the rage that is accumulated inwardly through the repression of their own instincts is now turned outwards towards some other group of people with whom they find themselves in contention. All of this is so irrational, when you think about it, that you can see that it is not readily explicable by looking directly at social factors on their own, for we must also look into the role of character in this connection.

And when we do so, we discover an extraordinarily serious psycho-social constellation, and I believe that it is imperative for our work to become aware of the seriousness of this question if we are not simply to congratulate ourselves on our own undoubtedly unanimous desire to reject the attitudes we are talking about and thereby overlook the real weight that attaches to these other tendencies that are still at work here. For, on the one hand, the world is in fact organized to a considerable extent in an authoritarian way. In the West, thank goodness, we do live under democratic forms of government, but for specific social reasons – that I cannot explore in this lecture, since I wish to focus on the psychological aspects of the question – the freedom of the individual here is still restricted to a very considerable degree. Because the network of society is so tightly 

*[p. 102]* ^pg109

woven, because human beings must constantly behave in accordance with the demands of the particular ‘social role’ they occupy – to use a currently popular expression from social psychology, one that derives from the work of Talcott Parsons.<sup>4</sup> The way in which our life is organized, and especially the way in which economic relations are organized, is itself authoritarian to such a degree that human beings just have to fit in, as they say today. This process of ‘fitting in’ is made somewhat easier for people if they already constantly tell themselves, either consciously or unconsciously: ‘You have to fit in, you have to fit in.’ I can still remember today how my parents once reprimanded our housemaid when she lectured me, as a very small child, about the need to fit in.<sup>5</sup> I remain grateful to my parents to this day for intervening in this way and restraining the authoritarian tendencies of the housemaid, tendencies that would later reveal themselves so drastically in the Third Reich. On the other hand, we find authoritarian dispositions within the masses themselves – dispositions towards an identification with power itself – to an immensely strong degree. The proof of this is simply the mass basis of the Third Reich, something that we have all experienced for ourselves. And it would be completely delusional to assume, on account of wholesale political and military defeat, that the psychological basis for the identification with authority has now simply disappeared from the masses. The situation in which we find ourselves is that, on one side, the authoritarian aspects and features of society are drawing closer together and converging with one another, apart from our political institutions themselves, and on the other side the same is true of the authoritarian tendencies within human beings themselves. As a result, the space of freedom in which we exist is in truth far smaller, far narrower, and far more limited than we imagine, not least because there is a certain taboo in the West around these authoritarian issues, and this I believe is precisely why it is imperative to study the question of the authoritarian personality.

Let me just add, however, to avoid any possible misunderstanding, that the authoritarian personality we are talking about is by no means a specifically German phenomenon. Fascism certainly came to power in Germany by means of the dispositions that we have mentioned. But that is, rather, to be explained from specific objective constellations, and similar dispositions are obviously to be found in other countries too. Indeed at our Institute for Social Research we originally developed the concept of the authoritarian personality essentially by reference to American material and American findings, and for those who are interested in pursuing this subject further I would draw your attention to the book *The Authoritarian* 

*[p. 103]* ^pg110

*Personality*, which we published in 1950.<sup>6</sup> It explores in an extremely detailed and comprehensive manner the things that I am only able to outline for you here in a cursory way and merely indicate the range of aspects involved. To illustrate the immediate contemporary relevance of all this, you have only to see that Chessman was obviously only executed<sup>7</sup> because the political authorities that might have pardoned him feared that such a pardon could cost them votes in the forthcoming autumn election.<sup>8</sup> And if you recognize how the irrational rage against a man like Chessman, whom God knows we can hardly describe as a saint, called for his death at any price, you may perhaps sense the magnitude of the danger when the authoritarian character finds an opportunity to express itself in legalized form, i.e., when it is rationalized by the legal order and officially established power and you witness the re-emergence of those destructive and genuinely disturbing elements that we already experienced during the Third Reich. And it would be remarkably superficial to imagine that these things have been eliminated once and for all by the recent turns of political fate. For the psychological as well as the ideological moments that are involved here possess a remarkably stubborn capacity to endure and persist even in situations that are intrinsically alien to them or appear to have nothing whatever to do with them.

The point where the authority-oriented character truly reveals itself is the phenomenon of prejudice. And the particular relevance that these things have acquired in Germany is indeed directly connected with prejudice, for I believe that we have all been rather encouraged to think about these things once again by the antisemitic excesses that took place in winter last year.<sup>9</sup> Perhaps I may be permitted in this connection, without inviting any suspicion of vanity, simply to refer you to the documentation that I presented in a lecture last November,<sup>10</sup> which was also attended by Professor Jacobsen,<sup>11</sup> where I discussed in some detail the potential for things of this kind even before we possessed any material evidence of these antisemitic excesses. This alone suffices to show that the theoretical reflections that I am presenting here are on the right track, even if they cannot be corroborated in all their details by so-called empirical data.

So let me just say a few things here about the problem of prejudice before proceeding to outline a phenomenology of the prejudiced character. In one sense, every one of you is familiar with prejudices and entertains prejudices. But, of course, you all know that, when we speak of prejudices in the particular context that has brought us together here, we also have something very specific in view. There is a sense in which we cannot live at all without prejudices or pre-judgements without some anticipation of the future, without 

*[p. 104]* ^pg111

making certain judgements that bear on something that has not yet come to pass, but that we nonetheless regard as a kind of certainty. When we cross the street, for example, when the traffic lights are on red, the car that can run us over is not yet there, but we would do well to judge in advance, to anticipate the possibility, that one might arrive in the fragment of a second and actually run us over. Without some such anticipatory orientations in the context of thought and perception, what we call experience would not be possible at all. In this regard I would just point out that, in Kant’s *Critique of Pure Reason*, a work that is certainly enlightened and anti-dogmatic in general outlook, the concept of an ‘anticipation of perception’, i.e., a specific expectation of future experience, plays an essential role as a necessary condition of present experience. For Kant, the anticipations of experience belong among the ‘principles’ on which the possibility of substantive experience itself rests.<sup>12</sup>

Thus when we speak of prejudices in the narrow sense we find a certain factor or element that distinguishes it from pre-judgements in the broader sense, namely that specific element of irrationality that I referred to earlier. The rational reflection or grounding that is present in the case of the red traffic light is lacking here, and a more or less ungrounded conviction takes its place, a conviction for which the prejudiced individual can summon up plenty of feeling but not much in the way of legitimate motivation. In England, for example, there is an extraordinarily common saying to the effect that the Welsh are all thieves.<sup>13</sup> There is of course no reason to assume that people who say this actually know the Welsh in the sense in which we can be said to know the traffic regulations in the context of our earlier example. It is also far from clear why the Welsh should have more thieves among them than people from any other part of the country. And, finally, it is a very questionable procedure, though one which is highly characteristic of the authority-oriented personality, to infer anything about an individual on the basis of a highly problematic generic concept and to claim that someone must be a thief because the person in question is Welsh. That is the same schema that was applied in Germany to the Jews as such. It was just this kind of imprecise general concept that was used to try and define the individual. In this way, the individual, which is never fully captured by the logical species or genus, is nonetheless treated merely as a species, merely as a generic being. And this of course is deeply bound up with nationalism, which regards individual persons solely as representatives of their biological species and attempts to deal with their particular qualities and characteristics solely in terms of the species.

*[p. 105]* ^pg112

But I believe that even what I have just said about the irrationality of prejudice in this sense still does not really get to the heart of the problem that is presented by the authority-oriented personality. For the crucial thing here is a certain kind of rigidity or inflexibility. We specifically introduced the idea of ‘pathic’ prejudice in this connection.<sup>14</sup> This is the kind of prejudice that is entertained by human beings who cannot be brought to abandon this prejudice even by experiences that would seem to counter or disconfirm it. I believe that if you want to specify the difference between prejudice as pre-judgement, as a legitimate anticipation of experience, and what we described here as pathic prejudice, this is precisely the point that allows you to do so. There is no other criterion than this: human beings inevitably anticipate experience and have their own schemata in order to respond to reality and make sense of it, but they are not pathogenic in the sense that they can in principle always allow themselves to be corrected by experience, whereas this is not the case with specifically prejudiced personalities on account of that peculiar rigidity and inflexibility that they exhibit. Generally speaking, these are people who lack a genuine living relationship to objects, a living ‘object-cathexis’ as they say in psychoanalysis,<sup>15</sup> people who absolutize the subject with its fixed hard and fast convictions and simply ignore anything different that the world has to offer. And this, incidentally, is one of the reasons why the turn towards the subject, which I am emphasizing in this lecture, is entirely legitimate, for these human beings, however objectively they present themselves, in reality do not allow the other human being, what is objectively Other, what is not merely the ‘I’, to reach or affect them, and therefore absolutize their ‘I’. That is why it is necessary to explore the conditions under which this kind of ‘I’ actually gets constituted in the first place.

These are human beings who actually absolutize themselves qua individual, or – to come to the heart of the matter – if this self-absolutization as a particular individual is not successfully achieved, come through a complex mechanism of identification to attach absolute significance to the collective to which they subscribe, whose member they feel themselves to be. The collective itself thus comes to resemble a gigantic inflated ‘I’ which they glorify and in whose name they now act. One could perhaps say that the authority-oriented character, the kind of character that represents the truly dangerous element that we are principally talking about here, is specifically characterized or distinguished by the incapacity to undergo experience or to learn through experience. This character type is framed in such a way that the world is already nothing for him but a sort of material that he works upon in a more or less manipulative manner, upon 

*[p. 106]* ^pg113

which he presses and in terms of which he projects that which is already present in him, but which is brittle and unyielding, inured against any experience of the new – probably because, at a very early stage of his development, the organs through which he could perceive and respond to the world were somehow blunted or desensitized, and he was thrown back upon himself and dammed up within himself as a result. If you will allow me to make a practical observation at this point, I would say that the cultivation of direct encounters with previously defamed minorities is probably of little help in this regard, given the well-known problem of ‘preaching to the converted’, for the people who desire such encounters are generally those who are more or less free of aggressive nationalistic sentiments in the first place. And even if one can get the other authority-oriented characters to participate in such encounters, either we find that they already see other human beings in terms of such clichés that they are unable to get close to them at all in relation to their actual experience, or we discover – and this is the far more frequent and specific case, and I have a lot of material to confirm this – that they say things such as ‘The individual Israelis that I have met are splendid people’, although this does nothing to change their view that Jews as a whole are a band of bloodsuckers, conspirators and idlers.

Thus I believe that the work we need to undertake here really has to start with the subject. In other words, everything depends on whether we can learn, already in the context of the very early phases of child development, to make human beings free and strengthen them in such a way that they become capable of experiencing in a particular way. Just how little authoritarian antisemitism actually relates to its object was very clearly revealed to us by the racist daubs that provided experimental confirmation, as it were, of an old thesis that Professor Horkheimer<sup>16</sup> and I had already developed in some detail in *Dialectic of Enlightenment*, in the section of the book entitled ‘Elements of Antisemitism’.<sup>17</sup> That modern antisemitism has hardly anything to do with the Jews was shown by the fact that none of those who daubed swastikas on the walls had ever come across an actual Jew in their entire lives and have no idea of what it means to be a Jew – an observation the full significance and implications of which I believe has yet to be appreciated in the context of social psychology.

Let me add a further thesis to this. I said earlier that the authority-oriented character represents a kind of abbreviated adaptation to a world that is organized in an authoritarian way. If you will allow me to employ a concept of philosophy here, one that derives from Hegel, namely the idea of an alienated and reified world, we can justifiably 

*[p. 107]* ^pg114

say that the authority-oriented character is basically someone who reproduces the image of this alienated and reified world within themselves. The world of the authority-oriented character is a world of reified consciousness and, indeed, a world of reified unconsciousness. And that is a kind of ‘conditioning’ that replaces the capacity to live a genuine life. This is what we might describe as Hegel’s moment of rigidification. This rigidification expresses itself in a whole series of aspects or moments, in the fact, for example, that such individuals, as I said before, in truth effectively absolutize their own subjectivity without knowing it but tend in external terms to damn the subjective element, the element of impulse, the human and spontaneous element of experience. These are the people who invented the charming expression ‘empty humanitarian dreaming’, and again the same people who invented the no less charming word *Realpolitik* and are always more than happy to operate with such categories.<sup>18</sup> They generally behave in a rigid, categorical and unapproachable way and usually react to arguments with rage, violently rejecting the arguments and branding the person who dares to present them as a traitor or an intrinsically inferior individual.

To characterize this personality type more clearly, I would just like to read you some material from a study concerning the relationship and attitudes of the German population to France.<sup>19</sup> An individual who exhibited a definite authority-oriented character responded to questioning as follows: ‘The Frenchman is slovenly; he doesn’t keep the house tidy; and if a table or chair needs repairing, he does nothing about it.’ Then at a later point in the interview the same man says: ‘I have generally encountered orderliness and cleanliness.’ The interesting thing in this connection is that the contradiction simply remains unaddressed, is not processed in any way, and is quite patent: on the one side the cliché, on the other the man’s experience that things are different, his own observation that they are different. But these elements are not integrated; they just lie there, side by side, unrelated or harshly juxtaposed to each other, and the unity that would otherwise relate such divergent elements, in other words, the requisite synthesis of experience is lacking here. Another person from the same investigation expressed himself this way: ‘As a nation, I have a very low view of the French. I reject them because, considered as a whole, as a political factor, they fall short of what is required, because they show no understanding towards us, they cannot forget what has been. I actually think highly of the Frenchman as an individual person, extremely highly in fact.’

You can see how the same kind of contradiction appears at very different points. We have a vast amount of material in regard to 

*[p. 108]* ^pg115

this contradiction from our American sources. We are talking not just about various contingent empirical observations here but about symptomatic utterances that express something essential about the issue in question. It is quite evident that we are talking about people who tend to privilege the practice of subsumption and rigid classification. It is not hard to find people who still speak, in the context of everyday language, of ‘the Russian’ or ‘the American’, when you hear people say things such as ‘the Russians are inferior’, ‘the Americans will never have any culture’, ‘the French are slovenly’, or whatever formula it happens to be. Of course, we can respond by saying: ‘These are just stereotypes left over from army life, and you are being overscrupulous in attempting to draw God knows what significant conclusions from this.’ In the first place, however, it is worth remembering that such categories first arose in such rigidly hierarchical systems as that of the old military class, but it is even more significant that they still survive in a world where you would think experience would have taught people that there is actually no such thing as ‘the’ American or ‘the’ French person, but only individual Americans and individual French people. But in spite of this the collective spirit, as it were, still clings to such formulae.

I would almost warn you against the objections of ‘ordinary common sense’ that are inclined to take such things too lightly, to see them as nothing more than *lapsus linguae*, as slips of the tongue, since I believe that hardened linguistic fixtures of this kind are generally symptomatic of relations that are actually very serious. Even more serious, however, is the tendency of this character type – a tendency that is closely bound up with the urge to classify everything – to think in essentially bivalent terms or, put more simply, to divide the world into the sheep and the goats, into the good and the saved on one side and the *a priori* evil ones, the judged and damned, on the other – without any mediating categories in between, and above all without asking whether the actual human beings who are subsumed in this way are fully captured without remainder in a concept such as this.

This bivalent approach is generally combined with an equally primitive schema, namely a differentiation in terms of ‘in-groups’ and ‘out-groups’.<sup>20</sup> In other words, the group to which we take ourselves to belong – and it may be relatively irrelevant what that group or that people happens to be – is invested with every imaginable positive characteristic and ranked among ‘the saved’, while the others, who are rejected in negative terms as aliens who must be subjugated, or at least not fully accepted as one of us, are seen as ‘the damned’. That is the out-group, or at least the minority who live inside one’s own country, with whom we still have to deal. Behind all this there lies 

*[p. 109]* ^pg116

that aspect or moment that the American psychologist \[Hermann Nunberg has described\] in terms of the concept of ‘ego-weakness’.<sup>21</sup> I cannot go into the rather complicated problem of the relationship 

between the social and psychological determinants that are involved here, but we are clearly dealing with a form of psychological deformation that is highly characteristic of the contemporary situation: all individual human beings are rigidified in some way or other; they find themselves confronted by institutional forces of overwhelming power, they feel themselves powerless in the face of such institutions, and therefore feel themselves damaged in terms of their own narcissism or, put more simply, in terms of their own vanity – taking vanity here exclusively in the sense in which it is understood in the context of depth psychology. When I talk of narcissism and vanity in this specific sense – and I say this for the psychologists among you – I am simply referring to that form of vanity in which the libido, the capacity for love, is withdrawn from external objects, or from other human beings, and gets transferred to one’s own ego, along with the specific tendency to absolutize this ego. Yet it is very difficult for the ego to absolutize itself in this way. So we have the mechanism whereby the ego identifies first of all with its own group, the in-group, and then ascribes to this latter the absolute character that it can hardly acquire for itself as this individual human being.

I would like to give you an example of this collective narcissism from this relationship to the French, and one which strikes me as particularly suitable for illustrating the point at issue, since it is not drawn directly from the firing zone of the problem of antisemitism and because it touches on something that no longer seems to play such a significant role in the context of German consciousness today. But the example clearly shows that very similar things present themselves in relation to an out-group such as the French. I would just point out, *en passant*, that we also find similar things in relation to various other groups that are experienced as out-groups in terms of one’s own group, such as refugees or foreign workers. Thus we hear things that confirm the idea of collective narcissism such as the following: ‘We \[the Germans\] are much more heavy-handed in the things we do, whereas the Frenchman is more free and easy in everything. I spent 150 Deutschmarks buying tiles, for example, in order to renew the roof, even though it is not my child who will inherit the house when I am gone. A Frenchman wouldn’t have done that. He would have just frittered the money away.’ You can see here how far from reality this judgement is, for anyone who knows the slightest thing about France knows that the sense of the importance of family and the sense of economic responsibility for the family are very 

*[p. 110]* ^pg117

highly developed indeed. But such things do not affect the stereotype in any way, which is why I am giving you these examples. Here is another one: ‘The French are lazy, and they are to blame for their own misfortune. Yet if things go bad for you in Germany, it’s really hard to find work.’

You can see here how the stereotyped division into good and evil and authoritarian nationalism fit together so easily. You can also see how this narcissism, this gratification that people derive by elevating and glorifying themselves, provides a kind of satisfaction that compensates for what they lack in reality, for what they are denied in reality. This is what is known in depth psychology as ‘substitute gratifications’, which are obtained when people fancy themselves to be higher or better individuals than others, along with the particular nuance that the sacrifice that they make is typically regarded as an especially good thing, even though it is never even asked whether the sacrifice in question is justified.<sup>22</sup> Instinctual gratification which is not expressly denied, by contrast, is already regarded as something unworthy and bad in itself. This is particularly relevant to the relationship to sexuality, something which in general the authority-oriented character does not readily allow to enter consciousness but which is ascribed to others as a dark, dangerous and corrupting impulse. Here we also find that such authority-oriented people reveal a tendency to believe that other people are secretly engaged in dark conspiracies of one kind or another. This whole idea of disaster being hatched in darkness is a projection that basically has to be explained in these terms.

I will give you another couple of relevant examples from the same investigation. I should point out that these remarks and observations were not prompted by particular questions but came up in the context of free interviews where people were given the opportunity to express themselves about their relationship to France. Thus we hear this about France:

Cleanliness is not that great; the women do themselves up more and prefer going to bars, while the family comes second. The French household is certainly not as valuable as the German one. They would rather spend a Mark on going to the cinema, for example, whereas a German housewife would rather save the money for a new tablecloth. And even when the French woman has no food in the house, there’s always money for cosmetics. They love to make up and to dress up. I really can’t stand all that. They look very stylish on the outside, but dress worse at home. Yet the French woman is free and easy. There are people like this everywhere of course, but in France there are far more than in Germany.

*[p. 111]* ^pg118

Or, again: ‘The Frenchman likes to get up late and to eat at midday; he loves light music, and marital fidelity doesn’t count for much. As a Nordic type of person, I reject that.’

You can see, therefore, that these people see themselves simply as representatives of some general concept, in this case that of ‘the Nordic type of person’, who even seems to object to the idea of a good lunch. What we find here is a mechanism of the authority-oriented character that I believe is quite fundamental, namely the mechanism of projection. This is the process by which we suppress certain impulses within ourselves, or at least fail to admit them to ourselves – whether through internal reasons of conscience, i.e., through censorship exercised by the super-ego, or through external social conventions – and proceed to transfer or ascribe these impulses to other human beings. This projectivity is a fundamental mechanism of the authority-oriented character and closely resembles what we observe in a particular form of mental illness, namely in cases of paranoia. Indeed we might say that there are strong structural affinities between authority-oriented characters and paranoid individuals, where the only difference is that the former are spared the individual experience of mental illness only because they have been socialized more broadly into the acceptance of particular deluded ideas, such as the idea that the Jews are somehow responsible for everything. Thus they appear to remain healthy as individuals precisely by subscribing to forms of objective insanity, such as antisemitism, the persecution of witches, or the persecution of black people in America – whatever it happens to be.

This process of collectivization gives rise to a remarkable social climate that is very characteristic for the community of authority-oriented individuals, one that we could describe as a sort of wink-wink collusion: ‘Of course, we all know who is to blame for everything. One doesn’t need to spell it out, but one isn’t allowed to say it.’ This secret, brooding sense of collusion on the part of the community of all clean, healthy, noble and right-thinking people already implies, one way or another, a readiness to set upon others who think differently. These people, who always think in hierarchical terms, namely in terms of the weak and the strong, who only dare to assert themselves when they feel protected by the strong, also have a tendency to say: ‘Well, you know that everyone else thinks like we do.’ You often find this attitude in the context of the discussion over antisemitism today, where you frequently hear people say: ‘Well, there’s always been antisemitism around,’ as if that already implied some kind of legitimation. All it actually tells us is that there have always been authority-oriented people around. The fact that something has long since existed hardly constitutes a justification.

*[p. 112]* ^pg119

But it is a distinctive feature of the personality type we are talking about that whatever has power, whatever asserts itself, whatever has proved strong and successful in historical terms, thereby already appears legitimate. We are talking about people who worship power in principle and tend to see reality itself hierarchically in terms of who is on top and who is below, who is strong and who is weak. You have only to think of the enormous significance that was ascribed by the National Socialists to the idea of a future hierarchy with all its elaborately distinguished grades and levels \[…\]<sup>23</sup>

But what is particularly characteristic and particularly dangerous about the paranoid personality structure is that these character types never exhaust their prejudice and their aggression. If I just remind you how I said at the beginning that these individuals generally posit their own ego as absolute and do not allow themselves to be affected or changed by new experiences, you find the reason for this right here. It is as if the mechanism of their own subjective sense of importance simply runs on and on in a completely uninterrupted way precisely because it encounters no resistance in terms of other human beings, in terms of the real material existence of other people. It is like the queen in the fairy story who cannot live as long as there is someone beyond the mountains, however far away, who is said to be more beautiful than she is. So it is that people of this kind know no limits. Once the prejudice is established, it just carries on and on. It is extremely characteristic for this whole mode of thinking that it often starts from very limited and relatively unimportant claims and observations from which the most appalling conclusions eventually come to be drawn.

I can provide you with characteristic evidence of this from another study.<sup>24</sup> Perhaps you will allow me to assume the validity of the scientific methodology and the justification of the methods that were employed in these particular investigations, for in the couple of hours we have at our disposal I must restrict myself here to communicating the most important results so that you can see what is at stake here. I would not want us to get distracted from the genuine substantive questions by methodological considerations that could not adequately be pursued in the present context. The following example comes from the investigation that was conducted in 1951. I quote:

That Jews slaughter Aryan children at Easter is not something that can just be plucked from nowhere, for things were much the same in earlier times, and people always said that the Jew started as a hawker and ended with a warehouse. This can’t just be cleverness on their part, for 

*[p. 113]* ^pg120

other people are certainly not all fools. They are more unscrupulous, the Jews are, and ready to walk over dead bodies. They don’t have the same inhibitions that other people have.<sup>25</sup> When you look more closely at utterances such as these, you can see how the metaphorical expression about ‘walking over corpses’, which in the first instance refers simply to the alleged and legendary unscrupulousness of the Jews in money matters, is also used, through the association of ‘corpses’, to legitimate deluded notions such as that of ritual slaughter and the murder of children. And if the Jews walk over corpses and do away with people, it is obviously only a short step in terms of such projection to the view that ‘the Jews must be done away with too.’ The projectivity involved here implies not only that we project our own transgressions and instinctual impulses onto others, onto out-groups, but that at the same time we also punish these transgressions and impulses and reap a certain sadistic pleasure from doing so.

We might also say that, with the personality types we are talking about here, the affects themselves are remarkably shallow or, to put it very simply, that they do not genuinely feel very much at all. We have studied such affective shallowness in America in considerable detail in terms of merely conventional responses.<sup>26</sup> We are talking about human beings who generally cleave to current conventions and with whom the positive values, namely the positive value judgements, almost always have a peculiarly external character. Thus we find that physical features such as health – the principle of *mens sana in corpore sano* – or physical cleanliness and similar qualities tend to play an exaggerated role. Thus one man expressed the following sentiments: ‘I wanted to travel to Paris on one occasion, but I had to step back from the idea. I heard from some travelling companions that they had been shocked by the toilet facilities, which are intolerable in terms of hygiene.’ In other words, mere hearsay about the toilets in France, which played a major role in this judgement, persuaded the man against travelling to France after all. And he officially gave his judgement the rather inflated and narcissistic expression: ‘I had to step back from the idea.’ But then the same man said, and this is very characteristic: ‘The French people that I have met were always very clean.’ That is just like the example I cited earlier: ‘But the Jews I have met were always nice people, but the other Jews, especially the ones who have come over from the East, are all criminals.’ Even in this selective mechanism the narcissistic element still plays a significant role: ‘My acquaintances, the ones I know, are naturally good people because I can somehow 

*[p. 114]* ^pg121

lay claim to them as my own, whereas the others are bad and evil people.’

This aspect of affective impoverishment has in fact been documented in an extraordinarily convincing way in the appalling biography of Höss, the commandant of Auschwitz.<sup>27</sup> This reveals the most extreme form of reified consciousness on the part of a man who ended up treating human beings merely as things, as raw material for factories, literally for factories of death, in which nothing was produced but corpses and the heaps of gold that were extracted from human teeth. We might say, in the most extreme case of the character type we have described here,<sup>28</sup> that such a person not only fails to live in a genuine sense himself, i.e., no longer enjoys living experiences himself, but is also incapable of perceiving other people as living beings; he sees them simply as material to be manipulated, simply as *Menschenmaterial*, or ‘human resources’, as they say.

I think I have now given you at least a rough idea of the authority-oriented personality. I still owe you some supplementary observations and further clarifications, and in particular an answer to the question as to why I have spent so much time discussing the psychological rather than the objective-social aspect of the problem. The only way I can actually respond to this question is by communicating some of the solid results of our American investigations into the authoritarian personality. In that specific context we employed various scales \[…\] – although I cannot go into the concept involved in any detail here – and various research methods that enabled us to measure the intensity of ideologies and character structures. Among these scales – and with regard to their theoretical validity you will have to take my word for this here – there was one specifically designed to measure antisemitism, and there was a characterological scale, the so-called F scale, that we are about to apply, now designated as the A scale, to the situation in Germany in order to discover more about the authority-oriented character in the present context; and then finally there was also a scale designed to measure the strength and nature of prevailing political and economic ideologies.<sup>29</sup> In the course of our research we discovered something rather remarkable, namely that antisemitism was more strongly correlated with the characterological dimensions that I have just briefly described for you than it was with political and economic dimensions. In other words, we found that more individuals with the character structure that I have outlined tended to exhibit militant antisemitism than was the case with individuals who entertained a specifically articulated ideology, such as a reactionary or extremely conservative ideology. There was some overlap of course, and individuals often displayed both traits 

*[p. 115]* ^pg122

at once. But the correlation on the characterological side was more extensive than it was on the side of political-economic ideology. And this led us to pay particular attention to this characterological issue.

I do not want you to misunderstand me here, as if I were attempting to explain the problems of authoritarian nationalism simply on the basis of character types. For I said to you at the beginning and continue to maintain that, as far as the actualization of authoritarian tendencies are concerned, objective political and economic factors are far more important. But in the mass society of today such tendencies can only be realized if they have a mass basis, and they have this basis precisely in the attitudes and dispositions that I have tried to describe for you. They are the potential, the reservoir, as it were, from which in the extreme case, in particularly acute political situations, these movements, as they like to call themselves, can readily draw.

We can draw a further conclusion here, incidentally, namely that the authority-oriented character is not necessarily limited to the political right or to far-right radicalism. For there is of course also a communist authoritarianism; and this too certainly relies on authority-oriented personalities that feel themselves to be communists simply because the totalitarian form of rule is particularly suited to this personality structure. I once heard a rather famous woman say that communists are always unassuming.<sup>30</sup> Thus if such people believe they are in possession of the right theory, and thus also in possession of higher wisdom, higher experience and higher intelligence, this is essentially the same as what I was saying to you about the authority-oriented person. But there has definitely been insufficient investigation into this question concerning the relationship between communist ideology and the authority-oriented character. And any such investigation would have to push through and beyond certain aspects of the humanistic heritage that has remained part of the communist ideology even today in the age of totalitarian dictatorship. It is not particularly easy to see exactly what shape the problem under discussion has assumed in this context, but it would be very much worth pursuing this question in more detail.

Now, by way of conclusion, I would just like to repeat that the prevailing objective conditions define the climate to which people are exposed, and that this climate tends in a huge number of ways to strengthen and encourage the authority-oriented character structure. I believe that you have only to thumb through our most popular illustrated papers and magazines to understand what I mean. This is also reflected in the fact that parents feel they have to justify the events of the Nazi period, in which they actively or passively participated, in 

*[p. 116]* ^pg123

the eyes of their children by emphasizing the supposedly good sides of Nazism.

Finally, I just want to mention very briefly another phenomenon that is characteristic of this whole outlook, namely the phenomenon of tolerated excess. There are human beings whose ‘discontent with civilization’, to quote Freud, is extraordinarily great, human beings who basically harbour extremely destructive tendencies that would gladly explode the whole edifice of civilization. Militant nationalism, which is certainly connected with instinctual pleasure, is oriented to these very tendencies. On the other hand, those of a sycophantic nature will only trust such excesses if the latter are already socially approved or sanctioned in some way. Thus Hitler, who started with his little band of seven trusted comrades, was only able to wreak the destruction that he did because he already sensed, and indeed correctly sensed, that very important and influential groups of people will be prepared to stand behind him.<sup>31</sup> This same system, therefore, also involves a perverse combination of violence, of injustice, of criminality, with a certain sense of legality. The fact that Hitler immediately felt the need to declare the murder of Röhm and his actual or supposed fellow conspirators on 30 June 1934 to be a legal act<sup>32</sup> is an extremely vivid historical expression of this tendency towards tolerated or permitted excess, to what we might call legalized excess. The whole political system typically involves the gratification that is produced when the controlled and contained instincts are released, albeit in the name of what formerly suppressed them, and one is permitted to indulge whatever one likes that one has otherwise denied oneself, and to do so with a good conscience.

The potential for all this is also probably always there in one way or another, although whether it is constant or changes over time is a very difficult question. I would think that the tighter the social network becomes, the greater the social pressure becomes too, even in times of prosperity. And indeed I would think, without wishing to be too definitive about this, that this pressure is actually growing. But we can say in any case that the general tendency of society is moving so strongly in a totalitarian direction that the selective mechanisms that lend themselves to the exploitation of these things have their greatest potential in this reservoir \[…\]<sup>33</sup>
