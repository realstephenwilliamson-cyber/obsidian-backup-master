# Ezekiel 19 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Ezekiel 18|← Ezekiel 18]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Ezekiel 20|Ezekiel 20 →]]

## Israel degraded

**19:1** As for you, raise up a lamentation for the princes of Israel, ^19-1

**19:2** and say: What a lioness was your mother among lions! She lay down among young lions, rearing her cubs. ^19-2

**19:3** She raised up one of her cubs; he became a young lion, and he learned to catch prey; he devoured humans. ^19-3

**19:4** The nations heard about him; he was caught in their pit, and they brought him with hooks to the land of Egypt. ^19-4

**19:5** When she saw that she was thwarted, that her hope was lost, she took another of her cubs and made him a young lion. ^19-5

**19:6** He prowled among the lions; he became a young lion, and he learned to catch prey; he devoured people. ^19-6

**19:7** And he ravaged their strongholds^[Tg: Heb *his widows*] and laid waste their towns; the land was appalled, and all in it, at the sound of his roaring. ^19-7

**19:8** The nations set upon him from the provinces all around; they spread their net over him; he was caught in their pit. ^19-8

**19:9** With hooks they put him in a neck collar and brought him to the king of Babylon; they brought him into custody, so that his voice should be heard no more on the mountains of Israel. ^19-9

**19:10** Your mother was like a vine in a vineyard^[Cn: Heb *in your blood*] transplanted by the water, fruitful and full of branches from abundant water. ^19-10

**19:11** Its strongest stem became a ruler’s scepter;^[Heb *Its strongest stems became rulers’ scepters*] it towered aloft among the clouds; it stood out in its height with its mass of branches. ^19-11

**19:12** But it was plucked up in fury, cast down to the ground; the east wind dried it up; its fruit was stripped off; its strong stem was withered; the fire consumed it. ^19-12

**19:13** Now it is transplanted into the wilderness, into a dry and thirsty land. ^19-13

**19:14** And fire has gone out from its stem, has consumed its branches and fruit, so that there remains in it no strong stem, no scepter for ruling. This is a lamentation, and it is used as a lamentation. ^19-14
