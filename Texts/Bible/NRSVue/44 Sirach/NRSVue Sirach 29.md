# Sirach 29 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue Sirach 28|← Sirach 28]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue Sirach 30|Sirach 30 →]]

## On lending and borrowing

**29:1** The merciful lend to their neighbors; by holding out a helping hand they keep the commandments. ^29-1

**29:2** Lend to your neighbor in his time of need; repay your neighbor when a loan falls due. ^29-2

**29:3** Keep your word and be honest with him, and on every occasion you will find what you need. ^29-3

**29:4** Many regard a loan as a windfall and cause trouble to those who help them. ^29-4

**29:5** One kisses another’s hands until he gets a loan and is deferential in speaking of his neighbor’s money, but at the time for repayment he delays and pays back with empty promises and finds fault with the time. ^29-5

**29:6** If he can pay, his creditor^[Gk *he*] will hardly get back half and will regard that as a windfall. If he cannot pay, the borrower^[Gk *he*] has robbed the other of his money, and he has needlessly made him an enemy; he will repay him with curses and reproaches and instead of glory will repay him with dishonor. ^29-6

**29:7** Many refuse to lend, not because of meanness, but from fear^[Other ancient authorities read *many refuse to lend, therefore, because of such meanness; they are afraid*] of being defrauded needlessly. ^29-7

**29:8** Nevertheless, be patient with someone in humble circumstances, and do not keep him waiting for your alms. ^29-8

**29:9** Help the poor for the commandment’s sake, and in their need do not send them away empty-handed. ^29-9

**29:10** Lose your silver for the sake of a brother or a friend, and do not let it rust under a stone and be lost. ^29-10

**29:11** Lay up your treasure according to the commandments of the Most High, and it will profit you more than gold. ^29-11

**29:12** Store up almsgiving in your treasury, and it will rescue you from every disaster; ^29-12

**29:13** better than a stout shield and a sturdy spear, it will fight for you against the enemy. ^29-13

## On guaranteeing debts

**29:14** A good person will be surety for his neighbor, but the one who has lost all sense of shame will fail him. ^29-14

**29:15** Do not forget the kindness of your guarantor, for he has given his life for you. ^29-15

**29:16** A sinner wastes the property of his guarantor, ^29-16

**29:17** and the ungrateful person abandons his rescuer. ^29-17

**29:18** Being surety has ruined many who were prosperous and has tossed them about like waves of the sea; it has driven the influential into exile, and they have wandered among foreign nations. ^29-18

**29:19** The sinner comes to grief through surety; his pursuit of gain involves him in lawsuits. ^29-19

**29:20** Assist your neighbor to the best of your ability, but be careful not to fall yourself. ^29-20

## Home and hospitality

**29:21** The necessities of life are water, bread, and clothing, and also a house to assure privacy. ^29-21

**29:22** Better is the life of the poor under their own crude roof than sumptuous food in the house of strangers. ^29-22

**29:23** Be content with little or much, and you will hear no reproach for being a guest.^[Lat: Gk *reproach from your family*; other ancient authorities lack this line] ^29-23

**29:24** It is a miserable life to go from house to house; as a guest you should not open your mouth; ^29-24

**29:25** you will play the host and provide drink without being thanked, and besides this you will hear rude words like these: ^29-25

**29:26** “Come here, stranger, prepare the table; let me eat what you have there.” ^29-26

**29:27** “Be off, stranger, for an honored guest is here; my brother has come for a visit, and I need the house.” ^29-27

**29:28** It is hard for a sensible person to bear scolding about lodging^[Or *scolding from the household*] and the insults of the moneylender. ^29-28
