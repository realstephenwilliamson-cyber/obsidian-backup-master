### 8.1–13: Mediator of a better covenant

*[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-1|Text]], p. 475* ^8-1

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-1|1]]:** *Seated at the right hand*, alludes to [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]. *Majesty*, see 1.3n. Priests are not typically seated while in divine service, nor are they envisaged as seated at God’s right hand; the imagery is a mixture of royal and priestly. ^8-1-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-2|2]]:** *Sanctuary*, plural in Gk, “the holy places.” *Tent*, Gk “skēnē,” namely, the tabernacle, [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-1|Ex 25.1–27.21]]; 36.1–40.38. Hebrews never uses the typical Greek words for the Temple (such as “naos” or “hieron”). The idea that the earthly Temple has a heavenly counterpart may perhaps be found in the Tanakh (see e.g., Isa 6), and becomes common in early Jewish writings; see e.g., *1 En.* 14.9–23; *2 Bar.* 4.5; Philo, *Heir* 112; *y. Ber.* 4.5 (8c). ^8-2

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-5|5]]:** [[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-9|Ex 25.9]],[[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-40|40]]. *Shadow*, cf. 10.1. God’s instructions for the tabernacle ([[Torah 19 - Terumah#^25-1|Ex 25.1–27.21]]) suggest the divine ideal; the description of its construction ([[Torah 22 - Vayakhel#^36-1|Ex 36.1–40.38]]) suggests the material form. Philo makes a similar argument (*Leg. all.* 3.102). ^8-5

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-6|6–7]]:** A supersessionist theology. Not only is Jesus the *mediator of a better covenant*, but the first covenant was faulty because it failed to create the perfect relationship between humans and God; see 7.28n. ^8-6

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-8|8–12]]:** [[Jeremiah 31#^31-31|Jer 31.31–34]] (Jer 38 LXX). There are notable differences between the MT and the LXX; most significant**:** in 31.32, the LXX has “I had no concern for them,” while the MT has “I was their husband” (v 9). In its original context, Jeremiah was suggesting that the Torah would be renewed after the Babylonian Exile by being implanted in people’s hearts or minds so they could instinctively observe it; therefore they would no longer sin and so there would not be another exile. *Remember their sins no more*, that atonement in the new covenant is permanent is essential to the argument; see 10.26. ^8-8

**[[NRSVue Hebrews 08#^8-13|13]]:** The term for *covenant* does not appear in this verse (the Gk reads “new one” and “first one”), but the author is clearly referring to the *covenant* in [[Jeremiah 08#^8-8|Jer 8.8–12]], which refers to the Mosaic covenant. [[Hebrews 09#^9-16|Heb 9.16–18]] offers an interpretation of the word “covenant” (Gk “diathēkē,” Heb typically “berit,” but “diathēkē,” has connotations “berit” does not have; see 9.15–22n.). For the idea that the new covenant renders the old *obsolete* see also [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-7|2 Cor 3.7–14]]. Jewish sources rarely develop Jeremiah’s “new covenant” since the first one is eternal (see [[LXX Sirach 17#^17-12|Sir 17.12]]; cf. *4 Ezra* \[2 Esd\] 9**.**36–37; *2 Bar.* 77**.**15; Philo, *Moses* 2.14–15; Josephus, *Ag. Ap.* 2.272); rather, they concentrate on the perfect obedience to Torah, the original covenant ([[LXX Baruch 02#^2-30|Bar 2.30–35]]; *Jub.* 1.15–28). ^8-13
