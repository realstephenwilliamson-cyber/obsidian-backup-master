### 10.1–13.13: Defense of apostolic authority

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 366* · The ironic and polemical tone of chs 10–13 contrasts sharply with that of the preceding chapters. Paul no longer refers to his opponents indirectly (see 2.17n.; 5.12n.), but pointedly. Chs 10–13 may be from another letter (see Introduction). ^10-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-1|10.1–6]]:** **Paul attacks his opponents.** Paul’s opponents criticize him for lack of power, but he insists on the ironic idea that power is demonstrated through weakness; see 4.7–12nn.; 11.30 (summarizing 11.21b–29); 12.9–10 (summarizing 12.1–8); [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-17|1 Cor 1.17–25]]. ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Meekness and gentleness of Christ*, see 8.9; [[Philippians 02#^2-6|Phil 2.6–8]]. *I who am . . . when I am away*, alludes to the charge against him; see also v 10. *Humble*, lit., “base” or “humiliated,” see 11.7n. This trait is attributed to Moses in [[Torah 36 - Beha'alotcha#^12-3|Num 12.3]], to whom Paul might be comparing himself. Moses spoke with God *face to face* ([[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^33-11|Ex 33.11]]) ^10-1-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Those who think . . .* , opponents Paul addresses only in the third person; see 10.10–12; 11.5,12–15,22–23,26; 12.11. ^10-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-3|3–5]]:** *Not merely human, but . . . divine power*, Paul and those like him live as *human beings*, but his *weapons* have superhuman power. *We destroy arguments*, Paul characterizes his opponents as debaters; see [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-19|1 Cor 1.19–20]]. *Wage war . . . destroy*, and *take . . . captive*, warfare imagery applied to the religious debate. ^10-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-6|6]]:** Paul’s threat to *punish* contrasts with his commendation in 7.15 for obedience. ^10-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-7|10.7–11]]:** **Paul’s defense.** ^10-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-7|7]]:** *Just as you belong to Christ, so also do we*, answers the opponents’ boasting that they “belong to Christ.” A similar declaration of unity is at [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-10|1 Cor 1.10–17]]. ^10-7-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-8|8]]:** *Boast*, Paul normally boasts of the Corinthians; see 1.12n., 3.12n.; here he boasts of himself, only by claiming authority as their founder. The proper content of boasting is a matter of contention in chs 10–13. *Building you up . . . tearing you down*, cf. [[Jeremiah 01#^1-10|Jer 1.10]]; [[Ecclesiastes 03#^3-3|Eccl 3.3]]. ^10-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-9|9–11]]:** *Letters* answer the opponents’ charge (v 10) that Paul’s appearances and speech, unlike his weighty letters, are unimpressive. *His speech contemptible*, see 11.6. ^10-9

### 10.12–18: The proper way to boast is within one’s territory

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-12|Text]], p. 367* ^10-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-12|12]]:** *We do not dare to classify or compare*, standard rhetorical device of calling attention to one’s praiseworthy acts in comparison with superior persons. Paul denounces the practice here but parodies it in his “fool’s speech”; see 11.21b–12.10. ^10-12-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-13|13–16]]:** *Field* (v 13), lit., “(measure of) rule (Gk “kanon”)” (also vv 15,16), probably refers to the proper jurisdiction, i.e., the Gentiles, assigned to Paul and Barnabas ([[Galatians 02#^2-9|Gal 2.9]]). Paul reminds readers that he founded the congregation legitimately, *without boasting of work already done in someone else’s sphere of action*. Paul thus accuses his opponents of overstepping their bounds. ^10-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-16|16]]:** *In lands beyond you*, Paul wanted to use Corinth as a base for missionary work. ^10-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-17|17]]:** [[Jeremiah 09#^9-23|Jer 9.23]], also cited in [[1 Corinthians 01#^1-31|1 Cor 1.31]]. ^10-17

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 10#^10-18|18]]:** *It is not those who commend themselves . . . ,* see *m. Avot* 2.8: “If you have learned much Torah, do not take credit for yourself, since for this purpose you were created.” ^10-18
