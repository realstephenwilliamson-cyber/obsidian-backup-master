# Colossians 2 (JANT notes)

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-1|2.1–5]]:** **Pastoral concerns.** ^2-1

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-1|1]]:** *Laodicea*, a neighboring city; see 4.13n.; [[Revelation 03#^3-14|Rev 3.14–22]]. Josephus records several letters regarding a petition from the Jewish high-priest Hyrcanus (ca. 76–40 BCE) to the proconsul that the Jews of Laodicea be allowed to observe their ancestral laws (*Ant*. 14.241–43). ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-2|2]]:** *Love* (Gk “agapē”), Paul’s term for the virtue that holds diverse parts in harmony (1 Cor 13), appears ten times in Ephesians, and five in Colossians (1.4,8,13; 2.2; 3.14). *Mystery*, see 1.26–27n. and 3.3n. ^2-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-4|4]]:** *Plausible arguments*, Paul’s word for the kind of intellectual argumentation he opposes is “wisdom” (Gk *“*Sophia”; 28 times in 1 Cor 1!). In Colossians, wisdom is a virtue, so the author uses “persuasive words” (Gk *“*pithanologia”) found only here in the NT. ^2-4

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-5|5]]:** *Firmness*, normally refers to the “firmament” of creation, but in conjunction with Gk “taxis” (“order,” NRSV “morale”) the image is military. This author views the church like an army, organized and disciplined. ^2-5

### 2.6–15: The Messiah’s role in salvation

*[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-6|Text]], p. 412* ^2-6

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-6|6–7]]:** *Live your lives . . . rooted and built up in him . . . established in the faith . . . abounding in thanksgiving*, four grammatically parallel ways that community members *God, both of the Father and of Christ* b Gk *to walk* should live. ^2-6-2

> [!note] Colossians and Torah Observance (col 2.13–23) *(p. 412)*
> Paul, in his own letters, considers Torah observance obligatory for Jews ([[Galatians 05#^5-3|Gal 5.3]]) and maintains as well that Gentiles are obligated to follow some principles of the Torah that define the sanctity of the community ([[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-2|1 Thess 4.2–7]]). The author of Colossians presents a different view: that the Colossians should avoid obeying the commandments of the Torah, in food and drink, in regard to the festivals of the Jewish year, to the celebrations of New Moons, or the observance of the Shabbat. These ritual obligations are seen as merely “shadows of what is to come” (2.17), namely a foreshadowing of the life of believers in the world to come. The death of the Messiah has located the Colossian believers outside of the mundane realm and, by implication, the Jewish realm.
>
> Paul had urged his Gentile readers to take seriously what the death and resurrection of the Messiah meant for them in God’s plans for the world. God’s covenant, the Torah, was obligatory for both Jews and Gentiles but in different ways: Jews were obliged to observe all of the statutes of the Torah ([[Galatians 05#^5-3|Gal 5.3]]); Gentiles are obliged to observe the Torah “written on their hearts” ([[Romans 02#^2-15|Rom 2.15]]). The author of Colossians, writing a generation or more later to an audience who still seem interested in observing at least some of the biblical commandments, insists that since Jesus’ death there is no Torah, no commandments, only a new existence in a new world ([[Colossians 02#^2-20|Col 2.20]]).

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-7|7]]:** *The faith*, see 1.23an. ^2-7

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Philosophy*, except for 4 Macc (1.1; 5.11,22; 7.9,21), the only biblical use of the term. Here philosophy is a negative concept, what Paul called “human wisdom” ([[1 Corinthians 02#^2-5|1 Cor 2.5]]), and Colossians associates with “human tradition” (cf. [[Mark 07#^7-8|Mk 7.8]]; [[Galatians 04#^4-3|Gal 4.3]]). The author simultaneously condemns Greek philosophical traditions, Jewish ritual, and pagan worship. Jewish tradition had a mixed response to Greek philosophy; Philo calls Plato “most holy” (*Good Person* 13), but Epicurus is the ep-onym in rabbinic literature for anti-rabbinic thought (e.g., *m. Sanh* 10.1: “ . . . these are the ones who have no portion in the world to come: He who says, the resurrection of the dead is a teaching which does not derive from the Torah, and the Torah does not come from Heaven; and an Epicurean”); *m. Avot* 2.14: “R. Eleazar says, ‘Be constant in learning of Torah, and know what to reply to an Epicurean . . .’ ”). ^2-8

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-9|9–10]]:** *Fullness*, see 1.19n. *Head*, see 1.18n. ^2-9

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-11|11]]:** *Circumcision*, see “Circumcision,” p. 673. These church members want to behave like Jews, not only as regards circumcision, but as regards the liturgical calendar as well (2.16–17). *Spiritual circumcision*, lit., “circumcision not done by hands.” *Putting off . . . flesh*, the author contrasts baptism and circumcision, urging the former and forbidding the latter. *Putting off* appears in [[Colossians 02#^2-11|Col 2.11]],[[Colossians 02#^2-15|15]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-9|3.9]], but nowhere else in the NT; the NRSV translates each appearance differently. ^2-11

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Buried .  .  . in baptism .  .  . also raised*, the unusual Gk word “baptismos,” “immersion,” appears otherwise in the NT only in [[Mark 07#^7-4|Mk 7.4]] and [[Hebrews 06#^6-2|Heb 6.2]], rather than the specifically Christian “baptisma,” “baptism,” which occurs 19 times; scribes tried to make the text conform to the more common usage. The passage loosely quotes [[Romans 06#^6-4|Rom 6.4]] concerning believers who through baptism identify with the Messiah in his dying and rising. Unlike Paul’s own letters, Colossians speaks of resurrection in the present. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-13|13]]:** *Uncircumcision* (Gk “akrobustia,” lit., “foreskin”), Paul ([[1 Corinthians 07#^7-18|1 Cor 7.18]]) is the only biblical author to use the technical medical term. Gentiles were dead with respect to baptism, and their men were dead with respect to circumcision, i.e., their foreskins were intact. *Forgave .  .  . our trespasses*, reminiscent of [[Matthew 06#^6-12|Mt 6.12]]; [[Luke 11#^11-4|Lk 11.4]]. ^2-13

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-14|14]]:** Record*,* Gk “cheirographon,” used only here in the NT, typically refers to a written contract (cf. [[LXX Tobit 05#^5-3|Tob 5.3]]; [[LXX Tobit 09#^9-5|9.5]]). *Demands*, Gk “dogma,” refers to the terms of the contract. The word appears here and in [[Ephesians 02#^2-15|Eph 2.15]], but never in Paul’s own letters. Humanity has a debt to God, repaid by the Messiah’s atoning death. ^2-14

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Rulers and authorities*, see 1.16n. *Disarmed .  .  . triumphing*, a parade of vanquished enemies, familiar to Romans. *Disarmed*, better “stripped naked” (cf. 2.11n.). *Public example*, the Gk word means “to humiliate” (cf. [[Matthew 01#^1-19|Mt 1.19]]); the text emphasizes the public nature by adding *triumphing*. ^2-15

### 2.16–23: Warnings against Jewish observance

*[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-16|Text]], p. 413* ^2-16

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-16|16]]:** The author complains about the Colossians observing Jewish dietary laws and calendar rituals (see [[Romans 14#^14-15|Rom 14.15]],[[Romans 14#^14-17|17]],[[Romans 14#^14-20|20]]). *Food and drink*, probably imitates [[Romans 14#^14-17|Rom 14.17]]. *Festivals* (Gk “heortē”; used in LXX chiefly for Heb “ḥag,” pilgrimage festival, e.g., [[Torah 15 - Bo#^10-9|Ex 10.9]]); the terms appears in no other NT letter. *New moons, or sabbaths*, a rare direct LXX echo (e.g., [[Ezekiel 04#^4-17|Ezek 4.17]]; [[Hosea 02#^2-13|Hos 2.11]]). Jewish new moon (Heb “rosh chodesh”) rituals (cf. [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^28-11|Num 28.11]]) are never mentioned in the NT but are mentioned by Josephus (cf. *Ant* 3.238) and the DSS (cf. 11Q5 27.7; 11Q19 43.20). In contrast *made* e Or *divested himself of* to the Tanakh, the Mishnah focuses on observing the new moon for establishing festival dates rather than on special observance, although *m. Zeb*. 5.3; 10.1 describes “rosh chodesh” sacrifices in conjunction with Shabbat sacrifices. Paul himself never mentions Sabbath or “rosh chodesh” observance. ^2-16-2

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-17|17]]:** *Shadow .  .  . substance*, despite its negative view of philosophy, Colossians shares with Hebrews and Philo the Platonic view that perceptible reality is a shadow of ultimate reality, only what exists in the mind of God is real and unbiased (Heb 8.5n.; 10.1; Philo, *Leg. all*. 3.96: “But the shadow of God is his word, which he used like an instrument when he was making the world. And this shadow, and, as it were, model, is the archetype of other things”). ^2-17

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-18|18]]:** *Self-abasement*, the NRSV perhaps connects Gk *“*tapeinosphrosune,” “humility,” with asceticism or athletic training. Everywhere else in the NT ([[Colossians 02#^2-23|Col 2.23]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-12|3.12]]; [[Acts 20#^20-9|Acts 20.9]]; [[Ephesians 04#^4-2|Eph 4.2]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-3|Phil 2.3]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-5|1 Pet 5.5]]) the NRSV translates “tapeinosphrosune” as “humility”; in [[Colossians 02#^2-23|Col 2.23]] it means a degree of humility with which the author is uncomfortable, an outcome of following Jewish traditions (cf. 3.12; [[Ephesians 04#^4-2|Eph 4.2]]; [[Philippians 02#^2-3|Phil 2.3]]). *Worship of angels*, both Paul and the authors of the DSS believed that angels were present during the congregation’s worship ([[1 Corinthians 11#^11-10|1 Cor 11.10]]; 1QSa 2.9; see “Supernatural Beings,” p. 682). *Visions,* glimpses of the heavenly realm (see [[2 Corinthians 12#^12-1|2 Cor 12.1–10]]). *Puffed up*, see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-6|1 Cor 4.6]],[[1 Corinthians 04#^4-18|18]],[[1 Corinthians 04#^4-19|19]]. **19**: *Head . . . body*, see 1.18n. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-20|20]]:** *Died to the elemental spirits*, see vv 8–15; 2.8n. *As if you still belonged to the world*, continues the idea that Jesus’ followers are under the Messiah’s rule while outsiders are under supernatural powers governing this world (cf. [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-12|1 Cor 15.12–28]]). ^2-20

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-21|21]]:** *Handle . . . taste . . . touch*, the author mocks Jewish purity laws as trivial. Paul took exception to Gentiles participating in distinctly Jewish practices, but he never viewed Torah as trivial. ^2-21

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-22|22]]:** *Human commands and teachings*, possibly an echo of Jesus’ complaint against the Pharisees in [[Mark 07#^7-7|Mk 7.7]] || [[Matthew 15#^15-9|Mt 15.9]], or, less likely another rare LXX reference ([[Isaiah 29#^29-13|Isa 29.13]].) ^2-22

**[[NRSVue Colossians 02#^2-23|23]]:** *Humility*, the same word translated “self-abasement” in 2.18 (see 2.18n.). *Self-indulgence* (Gk “plēsmonē”), a crux for translators, who have difficulty explaining how piety and humility fail to check self-indulgence. It is best to take the sentence as continuing the mocking tone and satirizing an attitude that understands these virtues as vices, where the author quotes his opponents’ ironic use of “wisdom” back to them. ^2-23
