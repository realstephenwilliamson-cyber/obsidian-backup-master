He was a student, as I have said, of Pherecydes of Syros. After his death he ~ came to Samos and studied with Hermodamus, a descendant of Creophylus, who was already old. Being young and eager to learn he left home and was initiated in all the religious rites both Greek and barbarian. (3) Thus he went to Egypt with a letter of introduction from Polycrates himself to king Amasis. And he jearned their language, as Antiphon says in his On Those of Outstanding Virtue, and he visited the Chaldeans and Magi. Then he went down to the cave of Ida in Crete with Epimenides, but also he entered the sanctuaries in Egypt, and he learned the ineffable mysteries of their gods. Then he returned to Samos, and finding his country ruled by the tyrant Polycrates, he departed to Croton in Italy. And there he prescribed laws for the Italians and was honored together with his disciples, who, being about three hundred in number, governed so nobly that the government was in effect an aristocracy.
