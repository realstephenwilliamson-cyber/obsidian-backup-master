## About

An English translation of the Latin Vulgate, made by English Catholic exiles at the English College, Douai, as a Counter-Reformation effort. The vault's text is Bishop Challoner's 18th-century revision, in an American printing of 1899.

- **Makers:** the English College, founded at Douai in 1568 by William Allen. The translation was chiefly Gregory Martin's. Allen, Richard Bristow, William Reynolds and Thomas Worthington proofread it and supplied notes. Britannica credits Worthington with the Old Testament annotations; Wikipedia says many of them.
- **1582:** the New Testament was published at Rheims, where the college had moved in 1578. It came out in one volume with extensive notes.
- **1609–10:** the Old Testament was published at Douai in two volumes. Genesis to Job came out in 1609; Psalms to 2 Maccabees, with three apocrypha from the Vulgate appendix, followed in 1610.
- **1749–52:** Richard Challoner's revision: the New Testament in 1749, the whole Bible in 1750 and a further New Testament in 1752. He smoothed the Latinate English (borrowing heavily from the King James Version), cut the notes and dropped the three apocrypha. Sources differ on when he stopped revising: Wikipedia elsewhere says he revised between 1749 and 1777, Britannica gives 1749–72.
- **1899:** an American edition of the Challoner text, published by the John Murphy Company of Baltimore with the imprimatur of Cardinal James Gibbons. Its chronology followed Ussher (creation in 4004 BC) and was replaced in Murphy's 1914 edition; TAN Books reprinted the 1899 edition in 1971. eBible titles its text the American edition of 1899 but names no publisher. Murphy's is the only 1899 edition Wikipedia mentions.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia calls it the first complete English Bible made under Catholic auspices, and says the Rheims New Testament influenced the King James translators. Britannica says it was the only authorized English Bible for Catholics until the 20th century.
