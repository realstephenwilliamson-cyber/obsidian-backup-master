# Revelation 20 (JANT notes)

[[JANT Revelation 19|← Revelation 19]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · [[JANT Revelation 21|Revelation 21 →]]

Text: [[NRSVue Revelation 20|NRSVue]] · [[Revelation 20|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Revelation - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-1|20.1]]:** *Bottomless pit*, the Satan-dragon from ch 12 is bound, like most great demons, in a pit (see [[LXX Tobit 08#^8-3|Tob 8.3]]; *1 En*. 10.3–6; 14.5; 18.16; 21.3–6; *Jub*. 10.7–11; see [[Mark 03#^3-27|Mk 3.27]]). See 9.1n.; 9.11n. ^20-1

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-2|2]]:** *Dragon*, see 12.1–2n.; 12.3–4n. *Thousand years*, while *1 En*. 21.6 specifies the period of the fallen angels’ binding with the huge number of 10,000 years, John envisions the Satan-dragon’s thousand-year imprisonment as a temporary reprieve for the world. ^20-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-3|3]]:** *He must be let out for a little while*, the dragon’s release (20.7–10) reignites the eschatological war. ^20-3

### 20:4–6: First resurrection and thousand-year reign

*[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-4|Text]], p. 570* ^20-4

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-4|4]]:** *Thrones*, symbols of authority (see chs 4–5). *Beheaded*, a new detail of the heavenly witnesses’ suffering (cf. 6.9) that reflects both Roman execu- tions and the seeds of a martyrological tradition that focuses on the torments of the righteous (2.13; 6.9–10; see 2 Macc 7; *Ascen. Isa.* 5). This new detail should not be taken as reflecting systematic legal persecution of Jesus’ believers during the author’s time, as no evidence for such persecution exists. ^20-4-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-5|5–6]]:** *First resurrection*, John’s depiction of two resurrections, first of the saints and then (v 12) of the rest of humanity, maintains the distinctive glory of the saints (vv 13,15) and guarantees their immunity from any further punishment (i.e., the *second death*, vv 14b–15). The ninth-century rabbinic text *Pirqe R. El.* (ch 34), which drew on both Christian and Islamic eschatology, describes a second resurrection and a second death to reward the righteous and condemn heretics. *Priests*, implying both purity and divine intimacy—qualities of the heavenly state achieved by the beheaded martyrs, see 1.6; 5.10. ^20-5

### 20.7–10: End of the millennial rule

*[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-7|Text]], p. 571* ^20-7

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-7|7]]:** That *Satan will be released* does not result in a heavenly battle as in 12.7–9 (see [[Isaiah 27#^27-1|Isa 27.1]]; [[Isaiah 51#^51-9|51.9–10]]; [[Psalms 074#^74-12|Ps 74.12–15]]) but rather a great amassing of armies, recalling the war against Gog and Magog in Ezek 38–39 (see also [[Revelation 19#^19-17|Rev 19.17–21]]). The Satan-dragon serves as the instrument of deception behind the armies rather than as the direct opponent (see 16.13–16). Large-scale eschatological war often takes precedence in Jewish apocalyptic tradition over battles between individual angels and monsters ([[Daniel 11#^11-40|Dan 11.40–12.1]]; [[Mark 13#^13-7|Mk 13.7–8]]; 1QM), although sometimes the traditions are combined: [[Daniel 12#^12-1|Dan 12.1]]; *4 Ezra* 13. ^20-7-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-8|8]]:** *Gog and Magog*, in Ezek 38–39 Gog is a prince of the land of Magog; here they represent the nations of the world arrayed under Satan. The final assault by and defeat of Gog and Magog became absorbed into rabbinic eschatology (e.g., *m. Ed.* 2.10; *Lev. Rab.* 19). ^20-8

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-9|9]]:** *Camp* (Gk “parem-bole”; Heb “maḥaneh”), see [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-11|Deut 23.10–14]], reflects the holy-war context for John’s notions of sainthood, purity, and conflict (see Rev 14.4n.). *Beloved city*, Jerusalem. ^20-9

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-10|10]]:** *Devil*, see 2.10n.; 12.3–4n. *Lake of fire*, see 19.19–20n. Eternally *tormented*, see *1 En.* 21.6–10; see also [[Daniel 12#^12-2|Dan 12.2]]. Some rabbis envisioned eternal punishment for heretics alone (i.e., those who preached two gods rather than one God; *t. Sanh*. 13.5). ^20-10

### 20:11–15: Last judgment

*[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-11|Text]], p. 571* ^20-11

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-11|11]]:** *White throne*, symbolizing purity and authority. *One who sat on it*, God, left purposely vague in appearance (cf. 21.5), in contrast to the many other heavenly beings in Revelation. *Earth and the heaven fled* like a human in fear before the divine presence (see [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-15|Ex 20.18–19]]; [[Isaiah 06#^6-5|Isa 6.5]]; Jon 1.3). This image builds on the subjugation of earth and heaven as throne and footstool in [[Isaiah 66#^66-1|Isa 66.1]], but here they are no longer God’s furniture but frightened witnesses. ^20-11-2

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-12|12]]:** John’s interest in heavenly *books* (see 1.3; 6.14; 10.8–11; 22.7,18–19) reflects a broader Jewish tradition (e.g., [[Daniel 07#^7-10|Dan 7.10]]; *4 Ezra* 6.20; *1 En*. 47.3; *Lev Rab.* 14). *Book of life*, see 3.5n. ^20-12

**[[NRSVue Revelation 20#^20-13|13–15]]:** *The sea*, as the place of lost bodies reflects John’s attention to maritime culture (see 8.9; 18.11–20). *Death and Hades*, hitherto presented *heaven,* or *out of heaven from God* as mounted agents of God’s vengeance (6.7–8), are themselves regarded as demonic forces and consigned to eternal punishment. *Lake of fire*, see 19.19–20n.; 14.10n. ^20-13
