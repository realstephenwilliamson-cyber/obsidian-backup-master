# Rashi 49 - Ki Teitzei

← [[Rashi 48 - Shoftim]] · [[Rashi (Metsudah)]] · [[Rashi 50 - Ki Tavo]] →

Rashi on *[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-10|Deuteronomy 21:10–25:19]]* · Text: [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei]]

## Chapter 21

### 21:10

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-10|Deuteronomy 21:10]]

**If you should go to war.** Optional warfare is Scripture’s subject; for, with regard to the war for Eretz Yisroel, it would be inappropriate to say, “and you capture prisoner from them,” since it has already said, “Do not allow a soul to remain alive.”

**And you capture prisoners from them.** This includes the Canaanites among them, although they are members of the seven nations.

### 21:11

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-11|Deuteronomy 21:11]]

**A wife.** Even someone’s wife.

**You may take her as your wife.** The Torah speaks only in deference to the wicked inclination, for if the Holy One, blessed is He, would not permit her, he would live with her illicitly. However, if he does marry her, he will ultimately hate her, as it is further said, “If a man has, etc.” And, ultimately, he will father, from her, a wayward, rebellious son. This is why these chapters adjoin one another.

### 21:12

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-12|Deuteronomy 21:12]]

**And allow her nails to grow.** She must allow them to grow so that she becomes ugly.

### 21:13

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-13|Deuteronomy 21:13]]

**She must discard her prisoner’s garb.** As they are attractive, since the young Gentile women beautify themselves during warfare, to invite promiscuous behaviour with them.

**And she will remain in your home.** In the home he makes use of. He enters and stumbles over her, leaves and stumbles over her, notices her weeping and disgrace, so that she becomes repulsive to him.

**And weep for her father.** Why to such an extent? So that the Israelite daughter may be joyous, while this one is melancholy; the Israelite daughter may beautify herself, while this one becomes ugly.

### 21:14

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-14|Deuteronomy 21:14]]

**But it shall come to pass, that if you do not desire her.** Scripture informs you that you will ultimately hate her.

**You may not exploit her.** You may not utilize her. In the Persian language, servitude and utilization is verbalized as *imra’ah.* From the commentary of R’ Moshe Hadarshan I have learned this.

### 21:17

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-17|Deuteronomy 21:17]]

**Doublefold.** Equivalent to two brothers.

**Of all that he possesses.** From here [we derive] that the firstborn does not inherit doublefold of receivables due after the father’s death, as he does of property in his possession.

### 21:18

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-18|Deuteronomy 21:18]]

**Wayward.** Who strays from the path.

**And rebellious.** Who rebels against his father’s instructions. The word means the same as “rebels.”

**Though they chasten him.** They warn him before three [magistrates] and have him flogged. The wayward, rebellious son is not liable unless he steals and consumes a *tartamor* of meat, and drinks half a *log* of wine, as it is said, “gluttonous and drunken,” and it is said, ”Do not be among the wine guzzlers, among those who gluttonously consume meat for themselves.” The wayward, rebellious son is executed in consideration of his ignoble end. The Torah penetrates to the logical conclusion of his thought — ultimately, he will squander his father’s fortune, attempt to apply himself to his studies and fail, station himself near an intersection and plunder his fellow creatures; the Torah declares: Let him die in innocence rather than die laden with guilt.

### 21:21

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-21|Deuteronomy 21:21]]

**Let all Yisroel hear and be fearful.** From here [we derive] that there must be a proclamation at the courthouse: ‘So-and-so was stoned for having been a wayward, rebellious son’!

### 21:22

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-22|Deuteronomy 21:22]]

**If a man is guilty of a capital offense.** The proximity of the chapters teaches that if his mother and father take pity on him, he will ultimately develop into a degenerate, commit transgressions, and be sentenced to death by the courts.

**You shall hang him from a tree.** The Rabbis remark that all those who are stoned are also hung, for it is said, “for a hanging corpse is an affront to God,” and someone who curses God is stoned.

### 21:23

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^21-23|Deuteronomy 21:23]]

**For a hanging corpse is an affront to God.** It is an affront to the king, for man is fashioned in His image, and the Israelites are His children. This is compared to twin brothers who were identical to each other. One became king, and one was apprehended as a thief and hanged. Everyone who saw him exclaimed, ‘The king has been hanged!’ [The king issued a command, and he was brought down.] קְלָלָה throughout Scripture connotes slighting and insult, as in, “He insulted me explicitly.”

## Chapter 22

### 22:1

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-1|Deuteronomy 22:1]]

**And conceal yourself.** Avert your eyes as if you don’t notice it.

**You may not observe . . . and conceal yourself.** You may not observe it so as to conceal yourself. This is its plain meaning. The Rabbis, however, comment: At times, you may conceal yourself, etc.

### 22:2

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-2|Deuteronomy 22:2]]

**Until your brother seeks.** Would it seem reasonable to return it to him before he seeks it? But, rather, inquire after him, lest he is a swindler.

**When you must return it to him.** Provided there is a return — but not if it eats in your home the equivalent of its value, which you then claim from him. From here, they ruled: Everything which produces and eats, shall produce and eat; which does not produce and eat, shall be sold.

### 22:3

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-3|Deuteronomy 22:3]]

**You may not conceal yourself.** By averting your eyes as if you don’t notice it.

### 22:4

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-4|Deuteronomy 22:4]]

**You must surely lift it up with him.** This refers to loading — to load the cargo which fell from it.

**With him.** [With] its owner. But if he walked off and sat down, and said to him, ‘Since you were commanded, load if you will,’ he is exempt.

### 22:5

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-5|Deuteronomy 22:5]]

**A man’s attire may not be on a woman.** So that she resembles a man, in order to mingle with men — this can only be for the purpose of promiscuous behavior.

**Nor may a man wear a woman’s garment.** In order to take a place among women. Another interpretation: He may not shave the pubic hairs, or under the armpits.

**For the abomination.** The Torah prohibited only garments leading to abomination.

### 22:6

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-6|Deuteronomy 22:6]]

**If you should chance.** This excludes a nest owned beforehand.

**Do not take the mother.** While on top of her young.

### 22:7

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-7|Deuteronomy 22:7]]

**So that you will benefit . . .** If, with an easily fulfilled commandment, involving no financial loss, the Torah declares, “So that you will benefit and live long”; then certainly with the reward for the difficult commandments.

### 22:8

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-8|Deuteronomy 22:8]]

**When you build a new home.** If you fulfill the commandment of “sending from the nest,” you will ultimately build a new home and fulfill the commandment of [making] a fence, since one mitzvah engenders another mitzvah. And you will attain a vineyard, a field, and beautiful clothing. This is why these chapters adjoin each other.

**A fence.** A fence around the roof. Onkelos translates תְּיָקָא, like תִּיק, “a container,” which protects its contents.

**For someone who should fall may fall.** He deserves to fall, but still, let his death not come about through you, as benefit is brought about through the meritorious, and injury through the guilty.

### 22:9

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-9|Deuteronomy 22:9]]

**Mixed species.** Wheat, barley, and grapeseed, within a hand fling.

**Lest . . . becomes obnoxious.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, “becomes obnoxious.” Anything obnoxious to a person, whether laudable, as with sacred objects, or detestable, as with forbidden objects, is described by the term “obnoxious,” as in, “Do not approach me, for I have rendered you obnoxious.”

**The expansion.** This refers to the fullness and enlargement generated by the seed.

### 22:10

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-10|Deuteronomy 22:10]]

**You may not plow with an ox and a donkey.** This applies, as well, to any two species in the universe, and applies, as well, to leading them together, harnessed as a pair, in transporting any kind of burden.

### 22:11

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-11|Deuteronomy 22:11]]

**A mixture of threads.** The meaning is “a mixture.” The Rabbis interpret, combed, spun, and woven.

### 22:12

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-12|Deuteronomy 22:12]]

**Make yourself pendant threads.** Even out of the כִּלְאַיִם mixture — this is why Scripture places them near each other.

### 22:13

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-13|Deuteronomy 22:13]]

**He has relations with her and hates her.** he will ultimately . . .

### 22:14

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-14|Deuteronomy 22:14]]

**berate her with calumnies.** One transgression engenders another. He transgressed “Do not hate” — he will ultimately reach the point of malicious slander.

**This woman.** We derive from here that no statement may be made except in the presence of the defendant.

### 22:15

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-15|Deuteronomy 22:15]]

**The girl’s father and mother.** Those who nurtured the evil branch must suffer humiliation over it.

### 22:16

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-16|Deuteronomy 22:16]]

**The girl’s father should assert.** This teaches that a woman is not permitted to speak before a man.

### 22:17

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-17|Deuteronomy 22:17]]

**Let them spread the cloth.** This is figurative — the matter is as clearcut as a cloth.

### 22:18

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-18|Deuteronomy 22:18]]

**And chastise him.** Flogging.

### 22:20

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-20|Deuteronomy 22:20]]

**However, if the allegation is true.** Through witnesses, and with a pre-warning, that she committed adultery after her אֵירוּסִין — marriage.

### 22:21

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-21|Deuteronomy 22:21]]

**To the entrance of her father’s home.** Look at the branch you nurtured.

**The people of her city.** In the presence of all the people of her city.

**Behaving promiscuously [in] her father’s house.** As if it had said “in” her father’s house.

### 22:22

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-22|Deuteronomy 22:22]]

**Even both of them shall die.** This excludes lascivious activity from which the woman does not derive pleasure.

**Even.** This adds those following them. (Another version of Rashi’s text: “those committing sodomy.”) Another interpretation: “Even both of them” adds the fetus — if she was pregnant, we do not wait until she gives birth.

### 22:23

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-23|Deuteronomy 22:23]]

**And a man encounters her in the city.** This is why he was intimate with her — the breach summons the thief. But if she had remained at home, this would not have occurred to her.

### 22:26

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^22-26|Deuteronomy 22:26]]

**For, just as if a man rises up, etc.** According to the plain interpretation, this is its meaning; ‘For she was coerced, he overpowered her, similar to a man who rises up against his fellow murderously.’ Our Sages expound, here, ‘This appears to teach, but subsequently learns, etc.’

## Chapter 23

### 23:1

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-1|Deuteronomy 23:1]]

**A man may not marry.** He cannot effect marriage with her — marriage with her is legally invalid.

**And may not expose the edge of his father’s garment.** His father’s prospective levirate partner, appropriate for his father. But he was already warned concerning her, from “the nakedness of your father’s brother.” Evidently, to transgress, with her, against two prohibitions, and to place, next to her, “the bastard may not enter,” to teach that the bastard is only [someone born] of those liable to *kares* excision; certainly judicial execution, as no act of incest or adultery subject to judicial execution is exempt from *kares* excision.

### 23:2

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-2|Deuteronomy 23:2]]

**With injured or crushed.** Whose testicles were injured or crushed.

**Or with a gash causing spilling.** The organ was gashed, and no longer ejects a stream of semen, but spills and dribbles, and is sterile.

### 23:3

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-3|Deuteronomy 23:3]]

**A base-born may not enter into Adonoy’s community.** He may not marry an Israelite woman.

### 23:4

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-4|Deuteronomy 23:4]]

**An Ammonite may not enter.** He may not marry an Israelite woman.

### 23:5

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-5|Deuteronomy 23:5]]

**Because of the matter.** Because of their advice concerning you, to incite you to sin [as written in the affair of Bilam].

**On the way.** When you were harried.

### 23:7

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-7|Deuteronomy 23:7]]

**Do not pursue their peace.** By inference from what is said, “He may remain with you, in your midst,” perhaps this one as well? The Torah therefore teaches, “Do not pursue their peace.”

### 23:8

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-8|Deuteronomy 23:8]]

**Do not despise the Edomite.** Completely, although it would be appropriate for you to despise him, as he advanced towards you with the sword.

**Do not despise the Egyptian.** Totally, although they hurled your males into the river. What is the reason? Because they received you hospitably during a time of need. Therefore:

### 23:9

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-9|Deuteronomy 23:9]]

**Children who will be born to them in the third generation, etc.** The other nations, however, are permitted immediately. Thus we learn that someone who incites a man to sin is worse than someone who murders him. For the one who murders him, murders him in this world, while the one who incites him to sin, drives him out of this world and the World to Come. Therefore, Edom, who advanced towards them with the sword, is not to be despised. Similarly Egypt, which drowned them. These, however, who incited them to sin, are despicable.

### 23:10

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-10|Deuteronomy 23:10]]

**When you go out . . . take precautions.** As Satan incriminates in times of peril.

### 23:11

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-11|Deuteronomy 23:11]]

**A nocturnal incident.** Scriptural law is expressed in common circumstances.

**He must go out of the camp.** This is a positive commandment.

**He may not enter into the midst of the camp.** This is a negative commandment. He is not permitted to enter the camp of the Levites, certainly not the camp of the *Shechinah.*

### 23:12

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-12|Deuteronomy 23:12]]

**As sunset nears.** Close to the setting of the sun, he should immerse himself, as he does not become pure without the setting of the sun.

### 23:13

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-13|Deuteronomy 23:13]]

**Have a designated place.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders, similar to “everyone at his place.”

**Outside the camp.** Outside the cloud.

### 23:14

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-14|Deuteronomy 23:14]]

**In addition to your weapons.** Aside from your other utensils.

**Your weapons.** Similar to “your instruments of force.”

### 23:15

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-15|Deuteronomy 23:15]]

**Let Him not see within you.** [Let] the Holy One, Blessed is He [not see] disgraceful objects.

### 23:16

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-16|Deuteronomy 23:16]]

**Do not deliver a slave.** As Targum [Onkelos] renders. Another interpretation: Even a Gentile slave owned by a Jew, who fled from outside the land to Eretz Yisroel.

### 23:18

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-18|Deuteronomy 23:18]]

**There shall not be a harlot.** Promiscuous, dedicated to and designated for lecherous relations.

**And there shall not be a male prostitute.** Designated for homosexual relations. Onkelos renders, ‘An Israelite woman shall not be kept by a slave,’ for she, too, engages promiscuously in lecherous intimate relations, since his marriage is ineffective with her, as they are compared to the donkey, as it is said, “Remain here with the donkey” — a people compared to the donkey. ‘And an Israelite man shall not marry a maid servant,’ for he, too, becomes a male prostitute through her, since all of their intimate relations amount to lechery, as his marriage is ineffective with her.

### 23:19

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-19|Deuteronomy 23:19]]

**A harlot’s fee.** [If] he gave her a lamb as her harlot’s fee, it is disqualified as a sacrificial offering.

**Or payment for a dog.** If he exchanged a sheep for a dog.

**Even both.** This adds alterations in form, as, for example, wheat kernels ground into flour.

### 23:20

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-20|Deuteronomy 23:20]]

**You may not pay usury.** This prohibits the borrower from paying interest to the lender. [Afterward, the prohibition to the lender: ‘You may not lend him your money for usury.’]

### 23:21

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-21|Deuteronomy 23:21]]

**You may pay usury to the stranger.** But not to your brother. A negative commandment deduced from the positive constitutes a positive commandment — so that he transgresses, for this, two negative commandments and one positive commandment.

### 23:22

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-22|Deuteronomy 23:22]]

**Do not delay in discharging it.** For three festivals. The Rabbis derive this from Scripture.

### 23:24

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-24|Deuteronomy 23:24]]

**Keep what you have articulated and perform.** This specifies a positive commandment with the negative.

### 23:25

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-25|Deuteronomy 23:25]]

**When you enter your fellow’s vineyard.** Scripture refers to a laborer.

**As you desire.** As much as you desire.

**To your satisfaction.** But not to gorge yourself.

**But you may not place it in your container.** From here [we derive] that the Torah refers only to harvest time, when you place it into the owner’s receptacle. However, if he enters to till or to hoe, he may not eat.

### 23:26

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-26|Deuteronomy 23:26]]

**When you enter your fellow’s standing grainstalks.** With this, as well, Scripture refers to the laborer.

## Chapter 24

### 24:1

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-1|Deuteronomy 24:1]]

**For he found in her something lecherous.** He is then commanded to divorce her, and that she should not find favor in his eyes.

### 24:2

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-2|Deuteronomy 24:2]]

**To another man.** This one is not like the first, as he ousted the evil woman from his home, while this one brought her in.

### 24:3

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-3|Deuteronomy 24:3]]

**If [lit. and] her last husband hates her.** Scripture informs him that he will ultimately hate her. If not, then she will bury him, as it is said, “Or, if [the last husband] dies.”

### 24:4

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-4|Deuteronomy 24:4]]

**After having been defiled.** This adds the adulteress who was secluded [with the adulterer].

### 24:5

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-5|Deuteronomy 24:5]]

**A new wife.** One that is new to him, even if she is a widow. This excludes someone who remarries his divorced wife.

**It shall not impose itself over him.** The military command.

**In any matter.** Which is needed by the army — not to supply water and food, or to repair the roads. However, those who return from the combat formations by declaration of the *kohein* — for example, one who built a home but did not inaugurate it, or betrothed a woman but did not marry her — supply water and food, and repair the roads.

**He shall be . . . for his home.** Even for the sake of his home. If he built a home and inaugurated it, or planted a vineyard and redeemed it, he need not budge from his home for the war effort.

**For his home.** This refers to his [inaugurated] home.

**He shall be.** This adds his [redeemed] vineyard.

**He shall bring joy.** He shall cause his wife to rejoice. Targum also renders “He shall bring joy to his wife.” But one who translates, “He shall rejoice with his wife” is mistaken, for this is not the translation of וְשִׂמַּח, but of וְשָׂמַח .

### 24:6

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-6|Deuteronomy 24:6]]

**He may not secure as collateral.** If he demands security from him for his debt in court, he may not secure implements of food preparation.

**A mortar.** This refers to the lower [utensil].

**Or pestle.** This refers to the upper [utensil].

### 24:7

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-7|Deuteronomy 24:7]]

**If a man is apprehended.** Through witnesses, and after a warning. Similarly, every “apprehended” in the Torah.

**And he exploited him.** He is only liable if he uses him.

### 24:8

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-8|Deuteronomy 24:8]]

**Take precautions with the leprosy affliction.** So as not to pluck the symptoms of defilement, or remove the leprous ulcer.

**In accordance with everything that . . . shall instruct you.** Whether to place in confinement, condemn, or purify.

### 24:9

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-9|Deuteronomy 24:9]]

**Remember what the Adonoy, your God, did to Miriam.** If you intend to take precautions to avoid suffering *tzora’as,* do not engage in slander. Remember what was done to Miriam, who spoke about her brother, and was afflicted with leprous ulcers.

### 24:10

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-10|Deuteronomy 24:10]]

**If you become your fellow’s creditor.** You obligate your fellow.

**For any amount of debt.** The slightest debt.

### 24:12

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-12|Deuteronomy 24:12]]

**Do not repose with his collateral.** Do not repose with his collateral in your possession.

### 24:13

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-13|Deuteronomy 24:13]]

**At sunset.** If it is a night garment. If it is a day garment, return it in the morning. That is already written in “These are the laws” — “Until sunset, return it to him.” Throughout the day, return it to him, at sunset, you may take it.

**And bless you.** And even if he does not bless you, still, “It will be to your credit.”

### 24:14

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-14|Deuteronomy 24:14]]

**Do not steal from the wage earner.** But isn’t it already written? Evidently, to violate, against the destitute, two negative commandments: ‘Do not steal the pay of the wage earner who is poor or destitute;’ and, against the wealthy, he has already been warned, “Do not steal from your fellow.”

**Destitute.** In want of all things.

**Your converts.** This refers to the righteous proselyte.

**In your cities.** This refers to the alien settler, who eats meat which was not ritually slaughtered.

**Who are in your land.** This is added to include rental fees for animals and tools.

### 24:15

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-15|Deuteronomy 24:15]]

**And he places his life in jeopardy for it.** For these wages, he places himself in mortal danger — he climbs a wall, or suspends himself from a tree.

**And you will have a sin.** Regardless, except that punishment is swiftly dispatched when someone cries out.

### 24:16

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-16|Deuteronomy 24:16]]

**Fathers shall not be executed through sons.** Through the testimony of the sons. Perhaps through the sin of the sons? It has already been said, “A man is executed by his own sin.” However, one who is not a man may die by his father’s sin — minors may die by their father’s sin, by Heavenly decree.

### 24:17

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-17|Deuteronomy 24:17]]

**Do not be devious in the judgment of the proselyte or orphan.** Concerning the wealthy, he has already been warned, “Do not judge deviously.” It is repeated with the poor so that he violates two prohibitions, since it is easier to distort the judgment of the poor than that of the wealthy. Thus, he warns, and repeats the warning.

**You may not secure as collateral.** Not at the time of the loan.

### 24:18

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-18|Deuteronomy 24:18]]

**Remember.** On this condition I redeemed you — so that you keep my statutes even when the matter entails monetary loss.

### 24:19

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-19|Deuteronomy 24:19]]

**And you forget a sheaf.** But not a heap. From here we derive that a sheaf containing two *seahs* that was forgotten is not *shikchah.*

**In the field.** This is added to include forgotten standing grainstalks, some of which he forgot to reap.

**You may not return to take it.** From here we derive that what is behind him is *shikchah,* but what is before him is not *shikchah,* as it is not included in “you may not return.”

**In order that . . . will bless you.** Although it came into his possession unintentionally — certainly, with someone who acts intentionally. You may thus conclude that if a *sela*-coin dropped from his hand and was found by a pauper who gained sustenance from it — he is blessed for it.

### 24:20

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-20|Deuteronomy 24:20]]

**You may not strip it of its glory.** Do not remove its glory from it. From here [we derive] that one must leave unharvested the outer edge of the tree.

**Behind you.** This refers to *shikchah.*

### 24:21

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-21|Deuteronomy 24:21]]

**You may not harvest pygmy vines.** If you discover pygmy vines, you may not take them. What are pygmy vines? Anything lacking the shoulder and the droplets. If it has either of them, then it belongs to the owner. I have seen in the Jerusalem Talmud, ‘What is the shoulder? clusters atop one another. The droplets? Those hanging down from the main stem.’

## Chapter 25

### 25:1

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-1|Deuteronomy 25:1]]

**If a quarrel should occur.** They will ultimately approach the court of justice. You may thus conclude that tranquility does not evolve from quarreling. What caused Lot to take leave of the saint? You may say, it was quarreling.

**And they convict the villain.** Perhaps everyone found liable in court is subject to flogging? The Torah therefore teaches, ”Should, the wicked one deserve flogging.” At times he is flogged, and at times he is not flogged. Who, then is liable to flogging? Learn from the context, “You may not muzzle an ox while it threshes” — a negative commandment unattached to a positive commandment.

### 25:2

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-2|Deuteronomy 25:2]]

**The judge shall incline him.** This teaches that he is not flogged standing or sitting, but inclined.

**Before him in the amount befitting his crime.** And behind him, in the amount befitting a double crime. From here it is derived that he is flogged two thirds from behind, and one third at the front.

**With the number near.** This is not vocalized בַּמִסְפָּר, indicating that it is attached, so that we read “With the number near forty,” but not with the total of forty. Rather, the number which concludes and completes up to forty, meaning forty minus one.

### 25:3

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-3|Deuteronomy 25:3]]

**He may not add.** From here we derive that one is forbidden to strike one’s fellow.

**When your brother will be slighted.** He is constantly described as a criminal, but after the flogging, he is entitled “your brother.”

### 25:4

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-4|Deuteronomy 25:4]]

**You may not muzzle an ox.** Scripture speaks in commonplace situations, but this applies as well to all domesticated animals, wild animals and fowl, engaged in work involving food. If so, why is the ox mentioned? To exclude man.

**While it threshes.** Perhaps one may muzzle it while outside? The Torah therefore teaches, “You may not muzzle an ox,” anywhere. Then why is threshing said? To teach you: Just as threshing is, specifically, something done when the work was not complete, and with produce of the earth; so, with everything similar. This excludes milking, and cheese and butter making, since they are not products of the earth. It excludes kneading and shaping, since its work was completed for the *challah* obligation. It excludes prying apart dates and figs, since their work was completed for tithing.

### 25:5

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-5|Deuteronomy 25:5]]

**If brothers reside together.** Provided they shared a concurrent residence in this world — this excludes the wife of his brother who did not share his world.

**Together.** Together in their inheritance. This excludes maternal brothers.

**Having no son.** Scrutinize him — a son or daughter, a son’s son or daughter, or a daughter’s son or daughter.

### 25:6

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-6|Deuteronomy 25:6]]

**It shall be that the firstborn.** The oldest brother shall perform levirate marriage with her.

**When she is capable of bearing children.** This excludes the *eilonis,* who cannot bear children.

**Shall be established in place of his deceased brother.** The one who performed levirate marriage with his wife, shall receive the deceased’s share in his father’s estate.

**So that his name may not be obliterated.** This excludes the wife of a eunuch, whose name is already obliterated.

### 25:7

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-7|Deuteronomy 25:7]]

**To the portal.** As Targum [Onkelos] translates, “To the portal of the court of law.”

### 25:8

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-8|Deuteronomy 25:8]]

**He shall stand.** While standing.

**And declare.** In the sacred language. She, too, must speak in the sacred language.

### 25:9

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-9|Deuteronomy 25:9]]

**And spit before him.** On the ground.

**Who will not build.** From here we derive that someone who performed *chalitzah* may not retract and perform levirate marriage, as it does not say, “Who *did* not build,” but “Who *will* not build.” Since he did not build, he may no longer build.

### 25:10

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-10|Deuteronomy 25:10]]

**It will be entitled, etc.** It is a mitzvah incumbent on all those standing there to proclaim, “The divesture of the shoe.”

### 25:11

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-11|Deuteronomy 25:11]]

**If men engage in an altercation.** Ultimately, they will come to blows, as it is said, “From his assailant.” No peace can result from altercation.

### 25:12

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-12|Deuteronomy 25:12]]

**You shall sever her hand.** Monetary payment for his humiliation, varying according to the one who caused the humiliation, and the one who suffered it. But perhaps this refers literally to her hand? It is said here, “have no compassion,” and it is said elsewhere, concerning scheming witnesses, “Have no compassion.” Just as there, the reference is to monetary payment, here, too, the reference is to monetary payment.

### 25:13

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-13|Deuteronomy 25:13]]

**Varying weight-stones. [lit. stones]** Weights.

**Large and small.** Where the larger contradicts the smaller, so that he will not receive with the larger, and return with the smaller.

### 25:14

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-14|Deuteronomy 25:14]]

**You shall not have.** If you do this, you shall not have anything.

### 25:15

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-15|Deuteronomy 25:15]]

**A fully accurate, just weight, you shall have.** If you fulfill this, you shall have abundance.

### 25:17

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-17|Deuteronomy 25:17]]

**Remember what . . . perpetrated against you.** If you were deceitful with your measures and weights, you should dread harrassment by the enemy, as it is said, “Deceitful scales are abhorred by Adonoy,” and it is written afterward, “The malicious arrive, then comes mortification.”

### 25:18

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-18|Deuteronomy 25:18]]

**When they chanced upon you en route.** This connotes a chance occurrence. Another interpretation: The connotation is semen and ritual defilement — they defiled them with homosexual relations. Another interpretation: The meaning is “cold,” as opposed to heat. They cooled you, moderated you to tepidness from seething heat. For all the nations were afraid of waging war against you, until they commenced, preparing the way for others. This is compared with a boiling bath into which no creature could enter. One villain came and leaped into it. Although he was scalded, he cooled it for the others.

**They struck down your appendage.** Striking at the appendage — they severed the circumcision, and flung it heavenward.

**All the feeble ones behind you.** Enfeebled because of their sins, as the cloud ejected them.

**And You were exhausted and wearied.** Exhausted by thirst, as it is written, “The people thirsted there for water,” and afterward it is written, “Amalek came.”

**And wearied.** By travel.

**They had no fear.** Amalek, of God, so as not to harm you.

### 25:19

[[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-19|Deuteronomy 25:19]]

**You shall obliterate the memory of Amalek.** Man and woman, child and infant, ox and sheep, so that the name “Amalek” will never be remembered, even with regard to an animal, by saying, ‘This animal belonged to Amalek.’
