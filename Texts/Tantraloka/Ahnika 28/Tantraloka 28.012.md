## Mūla

अब्धिरक्षीन्दु वैष्णव्या ऐन्द्र्यास्त्वस्त्रं त्रयोदशी ।
वाराह्या रन्ध्ररुद्रौ द्वे चण्ड्या वस्वक्षियुग्मकम् ॥ ^mula

abdhirakṣīndu vaiṣṇavyā aindryāstvastraṃ trayodaśī |
vārāhyā randhrarudrau dve caṇḍyā vasvakṣiyugmakam || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. X
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 10 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v10 03 - Chapter Twenty-Eight, part 1#^pg25|p. 8]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 10.pdf#page=25>))
>
> There, (out of these,) we will (now) talk about the procedures (observed) on propitious days (parvan). These are of two kinds, namely, Kula (parvans) and Akula (parvans). It is said in the venerable Yogasaṁcara that the former is made up of the group of the eight (Mothers of the) Kulas. (These are as follows). 1) The Moon of the Ocean (abdhīndu, i.e. the fourteenth) and the Sage’s lunar day (muni i.e. the seventh) are those of Māheśī; 2) the first and fifteenth are those of (Brāhmī,) who belongs to Brahmā’s family; 3) the Sentiments (rasa i.e. the sixth) and the Fires (i.e. the third) belong to Kaumarī; 4) the Ocean (the fourth) and the Moon of the Eyes (akṣīndu i.e. the twelfth) to Vaiṣṇavī; 5) the Weapon (astra i.e. the fifth) and the thirteenth to Aindrī; 6) the Cavities (randhra i.e. the ninth) and the Rudras (i.e. the eleventh) to Vārāhī; 7) the Vasus (i.e. the eighth) and the Eyes (akṣi i.e. the second) to Caṇḍī (i.e. Cāmuṇḍā). Thus, each (of the Mothers) has two days. The only exception is 8) Yogeśī, who has, on her own, only the tenth day, and she also has (in common with Cāmuṇḍā,) the eighth. It is for this reason that she is said to have two days. By others, the auspicious Akula days (akulaparvan) are also (metaphorically called Kula,) characterized in reverse. (with 28.10–14ab)
