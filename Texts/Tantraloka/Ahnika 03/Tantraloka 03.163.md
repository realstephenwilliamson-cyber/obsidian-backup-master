## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. II
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 02 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v02 09 - Chapter Three, part 6#^pg294|p. 278]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 02.pdf#page=294>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 3.163cd–164ab.
>
> The will assumes three states, according to whether it is devoid of an object (karman) or is coloured by an object of desire (iṣṭavya), (the latter being of two kinds) according to whether it is momentary (śīghra) or stable. The object of desire (iṣyamāṇa) thus resides within this (will) in three ways, according to whether it has not unfolded (anunmiṣata), is in the course of unfolding (unmīlat), or is fully unfolded (pronmīlita), without this entailing a fall from (the will’s) inherent nature (tādrūpya) (as the object of desire). That same (will,) externalizing itself by virtue of its own ‘heat’ (ūṣman), that is, by the (autonomous) impulse of its innate freedom, clearly becomes the three (sibilants) (ūṣman) Ś, Ṣ and S. (with 3.162cd–165ab)
