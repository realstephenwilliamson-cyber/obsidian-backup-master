### 21.1–11: Triumphal entry

*[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-1|Text]], p. 48* · ([[Mark 11#^11-1|Mk 11.1–11]]; [[Luke 19#^19-28|Lk 19.28–40]]; [[John 12#^12-12|Jn 12.12–19]]). ^21-1

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-1|1]]:** *Bethphage*, the location cannot be identified. *Mount of Olives*, east of Jerusalem, was linked to messianic and eschatological ideas ([[Ezekiel 11#^11-23|Ezek 11.23]]; [[Zechariah 14#^14-4|Zech 14.4]]). ^21-1-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-2|2]]:** *Donkey . . . and a colt*, [[Zechariah 09#^9-9|Zech 9.9]], followed by *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 98#98a|98a]], that the Messiah will come “riding upon an ass.” ^21-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-4|4–5]]:** *The prophet*, Matthew combines [[Isaiah 62#^62-11|Isa 62.11]] and [[Zechariah 09#^9-9|Zech 9.9]]. ^21-4

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-7|7]]:** *He sat on with the baptism that I am baptized with?* b Other ancient authorities lack *Lord* c Or *‘The Lord needs them and will send them back immediately.’ them*, likely the cloaks, not two animals. ^21-7

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-8|8]]:** *Cut branches*, see [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-39|Lev 23.39–40]]; [[II Kings 09#^9-13|2 Kings 9.13]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 10#^10-5|2 Macc 10.5–8]]; only [[John 12#^12-13|Jn 12.13]] mentions palms. ^21-8

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-9|9]]:** *Hosanna*, Heb “save now” ([[Psalms 118#^118-26|Ps 118.26]]). A form of this term (“Help!”) is earlier used in connection with King David ([[II Samuel 14#^14-4|2 Sam 14.4]]). *Son of David*, see 9.27n. *Blessed is the one who comes,* from the “hallel” (“praise”) psalms (Ps 115–18) recited during Passover according to rabbinic tradition (*t. Sukk*. 3.2; *b. Arak*. [[Arakhin 10#10a|10a]]). ^21-9

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-10|10]]:** *Whole city was in turmoil*, see 2.3. ^21-10

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-11|11]]:** *Prophet Jesus*, the crowds do not address him as Messiah. *From Nazareth in Galilee*, Matthew favors Galilee over Jerusalem. ^21-11

### 21.12–17: Temple incident

*[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-12|Text]], p. 49* · ([[Mark 11#^11-15|Mk 11.15–19]]; [[Luke 19#^19-45|Lk 19.45–48]]; [[John 02#^2-13|Jn 2.13–22]]). ^21-12

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-12|12–13]]:** *Selling and buying*, to purchase animals for sacrificial offering (*m. Seqal*. 1.3; *m. Ker*. 1.7). *Money changers*, converted foreign currency into Tyrian shekels, high-quality silver coinage accepted by the Temple. *Doves*, [[Torah 24 - Vayikra#^5-7|Lev 5.7]]. ^21-12-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-13|13]]:** *House of prayer*, See [[Isaiah 56#^56-7|Isa 56.7]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 07#^7-37|1 Macc 7.37]]; see also 6.5; [[Psalms 141#^141-2|Ps 141.2]]; [[LXX 2 Maccabees 10#^10-26|2 Macc 10.26]]. *Den of robbers*, [[Jeremiah 07#^7-11|Jer 7.11]] (12.6), a place where criminals feel safe. ^21-13

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-14|14]]:** *Blind and the lame*, present in the Temple, contrast [[II Samuel 05#^5-8|2 Sam 5.8]], “The blind and the lame shall not come into the house.” ^21-14

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-15|15]]:** *Hosanna*, see 21.9n. ^21-15

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-16|16]]:** [[Psalms 008#^8-2|Ps 8.1–2]] \[HB vv 2–3\]; 11.25n.; 17.23n. ^21-16

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-17|17]]:** *Bethany*, town east of Jerusalem (26.6). ^21-17

### 21.18–22: The fig tree

*[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-18|Text]], p. 49* · ([[Mark 11#^11-12|Mk 11.12–14]]; [[Luke 13#^13-6|Lk 13.6–9]]). ^21-18

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-19|19]]:** *Fig tree*, here symbolizing judgment ([[Isaiah 34#^34-4|Isa 34.4]]; [[Jeremiah 08#^8-13|Jer 8.13]]; [[Jeremiah 24#^24-1|24.1–10]]; [[Hosea 02#^2-14|Hos 2.12]]; [[Joel 1#^1-7|Joel 1.7]]). *Withered*, Temple leaders and the people of Jerusalem are condemned because they lack faith in Jesus (24.2). ^21-19

### 21.23–27: Jesus’ authority is questioned

*[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-23|Text]], p. 49* · ([[Mark 11#^11-27|Mk 11.27–33]]; [[Luke 20#^20-1|Lk 20.1–8]]). ^21-23

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-23|23]]:** Temple authorities challenge Jesus (see 9.34; 12.22–24; 19.3–9; 22.15–22,34–46). *Chief priests*, Gk “archiereus,” in sing. “high priest,” in pl. “chief priests,” including previous high priests. [[Luke 03#^3-2|Lk 3.2]] mentions “the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas”; Annas, deposed by the Romans in 15 CE, was succeeded by his sons and his son-in-law, Caiaphas, who served as high priest 18–36 CE. ^21-23-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-25|25]]:** *Baptism of John*, see 3.1–12. ^21-25

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-26|26]]:** *Regard John as a prophet*, see 17.12–13n. ^21-26

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-28|21.28–32]]:** **Parable of the two sons.** See 20.1–16. ^21-28

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-29|29]]:** *Changed his mind*, the first son represents those who repent. ^21-29

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-30|30]]:** *But he did not go*, the second son, a liar and a hypocrite, represents those who preach but do not practice (23.2–3)**.** ^21-30

### 21.33–46: Parable of the vineyard

*[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-33|Text]], p. 50* · ([[Mark 12#^12-1|Mk 12.1–12]]; [[Luke 20#^20-9|Lk 20.9–19]]). See *Gos. Thom*. 65. ^21-33

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-33|33]]:** *Vineyard*, a reworking of [[Isaiah 05#^5-1|Isa 5.1–2]],[[Isaiah 05#^5-7|7]]; see 20.1–16n. *Tenants*, chief priests and Pharisees (21.45). ^21-33-2

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-34|34]]:** *Slaves*, prophets of Israel (22.3). ^21-34

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-37|37]]:** *His son*, Jesus (21.41,43; see 7.16–20n.). ^21-37

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-39|39]]:** *Out of the vineyard*, Jesus was crucified outside Jerusalem’s walls (27.32–34; [[Mark 15#^15-20|Mk 15.20]]; [[Luke 23#^23-26|Lk 23.26–33]]; [[John 19#^19-17|Jn 19.17]]; see also [[Hebrews 13#^13-12|Heb 13.12–13]]). ^21-39

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-41|41]]:** See 8.11; [[Acts 13#^13-46|Acts 13.46]]. The leaders condemn themselves. ^21-41

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-42|42]]:** [[Psalms 118#^118-22|Ps 118.22–23]], also from the Hallel psalms (see 21.9n.); see also [[Acts 04#^4-11|Acts 4.11]]; [[1 Peter 02#^2-7|1 Pet 2.7]]. ^21-42

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-43|43]]:** *People that produces the fruits*, followers of Jesus. ^21-43

**[[NRSVue Matthew 21#^21-46|46]]:** *Prophet*, see 21.11. Later rabbinic sources claimed that prophecy had ceased centuries earlier (e.g., *b. B. Bat*. [[Bava Batra 12#12a|12a]] cites the destruction of the Temple; *Avot de R. Natan* B47: prophecy ceased due to harsh treatment by the people; *Pesiq. Rab*. 35: the lack of exiles returning to Israel caused prophecy to end). Josephus and others suggest a widespread belief that prophecy continued through the late Second Temple period. ^21-46
