## About

Abhinavagupta's Sanskrit treatise on the Trika school of Kashmir Shaivism, written in the 10th century. In 37 chapters it treats both doctrine and ritual; Wikipedia says it synthesizes the 64 monistic āgamas and the various schools of tantra, and is regarded for its size and scope as an encyclopedia of nondual Hindu tantra.

- **Author:** Abhinavagupta. Wikipedia gives c. 950–1016 CE (citing Muller-Ortega and Rastogi); the IEP (David Peter Lawrence) gives c. 975–1025; Britannica says he flourished in 1014. The IEP calls him a disciple of a disciple of Utpaladeva and says he used the categories of the Pratyabhijñā ("recognition") school to interpret Trika and to draw other Shaiva and Shakta material into it.
- **Title:** "Elucidation of Tantra" (Wikipedia).
- **Contents:** the first chapter condenses the essential teaching. Topics include the 36 tattvas and six paths, the four means (upāyas) and three impurities (malas), grace (śaktipāta), kuṇḍalinī, the theory of the alphabet, reflection (pratibimba), liberation, the origin of the tantras, Trika mandalas, and the Kaula ritual of chapter 29.
- **Commentary and abridgements:** Jayaratha's commentary, the *Viveka*; Wikipedia dates him 1150–1200 and calls him Abhinavagupta's most important commentator. Abhinavagupta summarized the work in prose as the *Tantrasāra*, which the *Tantroccaya* shortens further; the *Tantravaṭadhānikā* is a very short summary.
- **Editions and translations:** vol. 1, with Jayaratha's commentary, was edited by Mukund Rām Shāstrī (Allahabad, 1918). Raniero Gnoli's complete Italian translation is in its second edition. Mark Dyczkowski published a complete English translation with Jayaratha's commentary in 2023. John R. Dupuche translated chapter 29 with the commentary, and Swami Lakshmanjoo's teaching on chapters 1–4 has appeared in three volumes (2017–2023).
- **Why it matters:** the IEP says it set out to give a critical philosophical structure to tantric theology that was not itself philosophical. Wikipedia credits the Kashmir Series of Texts and Studies, and Lakshmanjoo's teaching, with its wider prominence.
