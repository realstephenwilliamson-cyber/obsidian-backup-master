# The Pharmakon of Digital Memory

> [!abstract] Definition
> Originating in Plato's *Phaedrus* and elaborated by Jacques Derrida and Bernard Stiegler, the *pharmakon* designates an artifact that is simultaneously poison and cure. A technology possesses no neutral essence; the prescription is determined entirely by the surrounding social, political, and bodily practices.

## Philosophical Lineage
1. **Plato's *Phaedrus*:** King Thamus rejects writing because Theuth offers it as a *pharmakon* for memory. Thamus argues it is a poison that produces forgetting (*lethe*) and the illusion of wisdom, rather than true remembrance (*anamnesis*).
2. **Derrida's *Dissemination*:** Demonstrates that the *pharmakon* resists binary logic—it cannot be resolved into pure poison or pure remedy.
3. **Stiegler's General Pharmacology:** In [[Stiegler - What Makes Life Worth Living|What Makes Life Worth Living: On Pharmacology]] (pp. [[What Makes Life Worth Living 02 - Section#^pg10|2]], [[What Makes Life Worth Living 02 - Section#^pg33|25]]; cf. Ricoeur, [[Memory, History, Forgetting 07 - PART II HISTORY, EPISTEMOLOGY, part 1#^pg160|MHF p. 141]]), Stiegler insists that every technical object exteriorizes human memory, initially deskilling or poisoning organic faculties, while simultaneously offering the only ground upon which new noetic and spiritual practices can be built.

## Application to Digital Curation
In [[Cybernetic Anamnesis text 2025-11-05|Cybernetic Anamnesis]], the digital archive is identified as a pharmakon:
- **As Poison:** It enables echo-chambers, deepfakes, algorithmic addiction, and [[Frictionless Expropriation]].
- **As Cure:** When governed by curatorial literacy, critical hermeneutics, and communal disciplines, it provides the substrate for [[Cybernetic Anamnesis (concept)]]—critical remembering that works through rather than against technical media.
