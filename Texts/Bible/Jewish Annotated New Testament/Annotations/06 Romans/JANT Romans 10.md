### 10.1–21: Some Jews do not comprehend the times

*[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-1|Text]], p. 306* · Paul continues at least through v 5 to defend Jews who have not joined him as heralds. The degree of culpability Paul intends is difficult to assess. ^10-1

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-1|1–5]]:** Continuation of argument and elliptical style from 9.30–33. ^10-1-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-1|1]]:** *Them*, Jews not sharing Paul’s view. While critiquing fellow Jews, Paul sees these *brothers and sisters* as being righteous and on a course set for them, but in need of protection; the comparison (9.32–33) is to messengers who trip while running, and who need to be stabilized so they do not fall but return to running to achieve the goal. *That they may be saved*, lit., “for their protection.” ^10-1-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-2|2]]:** *Not enlightened*, lit., “not possessing complete comprehension of the sort that the following verses clarify.” ^10-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-3|3]]:** Not realizing God’s righteousness (in Christ), they attend to the righteousness signified by circumcision, as if this were required to be counted among the righteous. *Their own*, “their own righteousness” in some manuscripts, i.e., Israelite identity and thus circumcision as the necessary sign of righteousness. ^10-3

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-4|4]]:** *End*, see ”Christ is the Goal of Torah,” p. 306. *Law* (“nomos”), here signifying circumcision. ^10-4

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-5|5]]:** [[Torah 29 - Achrei Mot#^18-5|Lev 18.5]], but the language is closely linked with Deut 30, esp. vv 11–14, which Paul develops in the next verses. For Paul, observing Torah involves being faithful to the revelation of Christ; he is not arguing for Torah’s abolition but for recognizing its goals. Cf. [[LXX Sirach 17#^17-11|Sir 17.11]]; Philo, *Prelim. Studies* 84–85. *Person*, Israelite. ^10-5

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-6|6–8]]:** A paraphrase of [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-11|Deut 30.11–14]] (the first clause is from [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^9-4|Deut 9.4]]; cf. [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^8-17|Deut 8.17]]). Philo used this passage to discuss similar topics, including proclamation to the nations; he had a positive assessment of Gentiles who turn to the One God, and offers instructions for virtuous life for those who join the people of God, includ- ing claiming that this is not too hard to achieve; see Philo, *Virtues* 175–86 (esp. 183), *Rewards* 79–84 (esp. 80; 83–84 includes a foot-race comparison), *Names* 235–39, *Dreams* 2.180, and *Good Person* 68. See also [[Bava Metzia 59#59b|*b. B. Metz.* 59b]], which similarly employs [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-10|Deut 30.10–11]] in intra-Jewish disputes to assert that the answer is near, in this case, the rabbinic majority decision. ^10-6

> [!note] Christ Is the Goal of Torah (ROM 10.4) *(p. 306)*
> Traditionally, Paul’s claim that “Christ is the end of the law” has been interpreted to mean that Torah no longer has any purpose and thus is obsolete. But “end” (Gk *telos*) is better read as “goal”: understood as the goal of Torah, the Christ represents the purpose toward which the circumcised righteous ones are set apart. They are to announce to the nations the words God has entrusted to them about Christ: “For the goal of (the) convention (of circumcision, to set apart Israel for the role assigned on behalf of the nations) is Christ (providing) righteousness to everyone who is being faithful.” Paul’s message resonates with [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^9-4|Deut 9.4–6]] (Paul cites 9.4 in v 6), in that Israel’s entering the land is not on account of Israel’s righteousness, but the covenant promise of God. See notes to 2.26–3.2.

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-6|6]]:** *Faith*, or faithfulness. ^10-6-2

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-7|7]]:** *Abyss*, i.e., “depths of the sea,” the underworld, Heb “Sheol” or Gk “Hades,” but [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-13|Deut 30.13]] simply refers to crossing the “sea.” The reference to the abyss/Hades is attested in [[Psalms 107#^107-26|Ps 107.26]] (LXX “abyssos”) and in *Tg. Neof.* 1. On desiring news brought back from the dead, see Homer, *Od.* , 10.487–574; 11.163–65; [[Luke 16#^16-19|Lk 16.19–31]]. ^10-7

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-8|8]]:** [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-14|Deut 30.14]]. ^10-8

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-9|9]]:** *If you*, Gk implies “would.” *Confess with your lips*, proclamation is the theme throughout; at issue is that some Israelites are not proclaiming this news. *Raised him*, part of the news is to announce to the nations that the end of the ages has begun, as resurrection indicates. *Saved*, or kept safe/protected. ^10-9

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-10|10]]:** *Heart . . . justified*, righteousness is an expression of one’s intent, of doing right for the right reasons. *Confesses . . . saved*, manifestation of righteousness by proclaiming it to others; *saved*, or protected. Paul is alluding to the elements of heart and mouth in the citations from Deut. If speaking to a herald he believes to be stumbling, the heart represents the intention to behave faithfully to what God has revealed in Christ, and the mouth is the proclamation of that message to the nations. Paul may even be drawing a graphic contrast for Israelites between faithfulness, which results in righteousness (standing upright after stumbling), and proclamation, which results in restoration (to running the course once stabilized). There may also be a contrast with declaring Caesar as Lord. ^10-10

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-11|11]]:** [[Isaiah 28#^28-16|Isa 28.16]], already cited in 9.33; Isaiah’s original meaning of trust in God to avoid fear here becomes a promise of vindication. ^10-11

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-13|13]]:** See [[Joel 3#^3-5|Joel 2.32]]\[LXX 3.5\]. *Saved*, or protected. ^10-13

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-14|14–21]]:** Similar to the issues in ch 3. The topic implicit in the adaptation of [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-11|Deut 30.11–14]] in vv 6–8 is now addressed: what is the proof that this message is known to Israel now? Throughout Paul plays on Gk “akouō” (“hear”) and “akoē” (“what is heard”). This corresponds to Heb “shema,” signifying obedient hearing. Paul plays on the citation of [[Isaiah 53#^53-1|Isa 53.1]] in v 16. ^10-14

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-15|15]]:** [[Isaiah 52#^52-7|Isa 52.7]] validates the line of questioning, or alternatively, is an answer. In Isa 52 a herald runs to bring news of deliverance from Babylon, the return of God’s reign, and the restoration to the land. The nations will then see God acting to restore Israel ([[Isaiah 52#^52-10|Isa 52.10]]). *Beautiful*, or timely. ^10-15

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-16|16]]:** [[Isaiah 53#^53-1|Isa 53.1]]; this chapter concerning the suffering servant played a significant role in the early Christ-believing community. *Obeyed*, lit., “listened (obediently).” ^10-16

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-17|17]]:** Or “Therefore faithfulness (results) from (responding faithfully to) a report.” *Word of Christ*, “God” in some manuscripts. ^10-17

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-18|18]]:** [[Psalms 019#^19-5|Ps 19.4]]\[LXX 18.5\]; the psalm states that apart from speech, the heavens declare God’s glory; in vv 5–6, the sun is represented as a bridegroom leaving its chamber to run its course, so that nothing is hidden. Verse 14 calls the Lord “rock and redeemer,” which may connect to Paul’s stumbling stone image. ^10-18

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-19|19]]:** [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-21|Deut 32.21]], the Song of Moses. ^10-19

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-20|20]]:** [[Isaiah 65#^65-1|Isa 65.1]]. ^10-20

**[[NRSVue Romans 10#^10-21|21]]:** [[Isaiah 65#^65-2|Isa 65.2]]. *Disobedient*, or “not persuaded.” In all of these citations, Paul recontextualizes verses that had a different meaning in their original historical context. Paul is doing Pesher-like readings: the “real” meaning of Scripture is only fulfilled later, and perhaps even the original prophet did not understand it. Unlike the thrust of chs 9 and 11, ch 10 appears to suggest his fellow Jews are guilty for not joining him in professing to the nations that Jesus is the Messiah. ^10-21
