### 16.1–9: Parable of the dishonest manager

*[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-1|Text]], p. 148* ^16-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-1|1]]:** *Rich man*, see 12.16n. *Squandering*, see 15.13. ^16-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-6|6]]:** *Make it fifty*, some commentators, but not the parable, suggest the manager was removing the interest charge ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-24|Ex 22.25]]; [[Torah 32 - Behar#^25-36|Lev 25.36–37]]; [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-20|Deut 23.19]]; [[Psalms 015#^15-5|Ps 15.5]]). ^16-6

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-8|8]]:** *His master* (Gk “kyrios,” “lord,” with no possessive), scholars disagree as to whether this “master” is the rich man, or the “lord” (i.e., here, Jesus; see 1.17n.). *Commended*, the rich man can do little else: the steward has created for him the reputation of *with* c Other ancient authorities add *Treat me like one of your hired servants* generosity, which he would not want to counter. However, the parable defies any fully satisfactory explanation. *Children of light*, see [[John 12#^12-36|Jn 12.36]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-8|Eph 5.8]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-5|1 Thess 5.5]]; 1QM. ^16-8

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-9|9]]:** *Wealth*, lit., “mammon,” Gk transliteration of a Semitic word for money (1QS 6.2; CD 14.20; *m. Avot* 2.12 \[“R. Yose says, ‘Let your fellow’s money {Heb “mammon”} be as precious to you as your own’ ”\]; *Tg. Onq.* on [[Torah 09 - Vayeshev#^37-26|Gen 37.26]]; [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^18-2|Ex 18.2]]). ^16-9

### 16.14–15: Criticism of Pharisees

*[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-14|Text]], p. 149* · See “Pharisees in Luke,” p. 122. ^16-14

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-14|14]]:** *Lovers of money*, conventional Greco-Roman insult. For Luke, love of money is a major sin. See [[1 Timothy 06#^6-10|1 Tim 6.10]]; [[Hebrews 13#^13-5|Heb 13.5]]; *1 En*. 92–105. ^16-14-2

### 16.16–17: Torah’s permanence

*[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-16|Text]], p. 149* · ([[Matthew 05#^5-18|Mt 5.18]]; cf. [[Luke 21#^21-33|Lk 21.33]]). ^16-16

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-16|16]]:** *Law and the prophets*, the first two parts of the Tanakh (“Torah” and “Neviʾim”). See 16.29; 24.27,44; Sir (Prologue, 0.1); [[LXX 2 Maccabees 15#^15-9|2 Macc 15.9]]. *Good news*, see 1.19n. ^16-16-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-17|17]]:** *One stroke* . . . *to be dropped*, Torah remains, although for the church it is interpreted through Jesus. Similar comments on the eternal letters appear in rabbinic Judaism, e.g., *y. Sanh*. 2.6 (see *Ex. Rab*. 6.1; *Lev. Rab*. 19.2) recount that when Solomon annulled the smallest letter, *yod*, in attempting to thwart the commandment “the king must not acquire many wives” \[[[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-17|Deut 17.17]]\]), and the letter complained, God replied, “Solomon and a thousand like him will disappear but the smallest speck of a tail of you will never be annulled.” ^16-17

### 16.18: Forbidding remarriage after divorce

*[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-18|Text]], p. 149* · ([[Matthew 19#^19-9|Mt 19.9]]; [[Mark 10#^10-11|Mk 10.11–12]]). *Marries another*, the injunction may be against divorce designed to facilitate remarriage; see 3.19–20n. Jews generally agreed that divorce was permissible (given [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-1|Deut 24.1–4]]) but debated the grounds; Bet Hillel (the School of Hillel) permitted divorce for flimsy reasons; Bet Shammai (the School of Shammai) permitted it only in cases of unchastity (*m. Git*. 9.10). See “Jewish Family Life,” p. 604. Because Jewish women had marriage contracts (“ketubot”), divorce was financially prohibitive; it was also not desirable ([[Malachi 2#^2-16|Mal 2.16]]; *b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 22#22a|22a]]: “Even \[God\] shares tears when anyone divorces his wife”). ^16-18

### 16.19–31: Parable of the rich man and Lazarus

*[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-19|Text]], p. 149* ^16-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-19|19]]:** *Rich man*, commonly called “Dives” (Lat “rich man”); see 6.24; 12.16n. *Purple*, expensive dyed cloth ([[Proverbs 31#^31-22|Prov 31.22]]; [[Lamentations 4#^4-5|Lam 4.5]]; [[Daniel 05#^5-7|Dan 5.7]],[[Daniel 05#^5-16|16]],[[Daniel 05#^5-29|29]]; see also [[Mark 15#^15-17|Mk 15.17]],[[Mark 15#^15-20|20]]; [[John 19#^19-2|Jn 19.2]],[[John 19#^19-5|5]]; [[Acts 16#^16-14|Acts 16.14]]). ^16-19-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-20|20]]:** *Lazarus*, the only named character in a parable, connecting [[Luke 16#^16-19|Luke 16.19–31]] to [[John 11#^11-1|Jn 11.1–44]]; [[John 12#^12-1|12.1]],[[John 12#^12-9|9]]. ^16-20

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-21|21]]:** *Longed to satisfy his hunger*, see 14.13. ^16-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-22|22]]:** *To be with Abraham*, paradise (13.28–29; *b. Qidd*. [[Kiddushin 72#72a|72a]]/b); Lazarus’s salvation is not contingent upon Jesus’ sacrificial death. Lazarus’s own moral character is ignored. ^16-22

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-23|23]]:** *Hades*, see 10.15n. ^16-23

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-24|24]]:** *Send Lazarus*, the rich man knows Lazarus’s name: he thus cannot claim he was ignorant of Lazarus’s suffering. Despite their different fates, he regards Lazarus as a servant. ^16-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-25|25]]:** *He is comforted*, see 13.30. ^16-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-29|29]]:** *Moses and the prophets*, see v 16n. Israel’s Scriptures provide needed guidance. ^16-29

**[[NRSVue Luke 16#^16-31|31]]:** *Rises from the dead*, evoking Jesus’ resurrection; see [[John 11#^11-45|Jn 11.45–46]]. ^16-31
