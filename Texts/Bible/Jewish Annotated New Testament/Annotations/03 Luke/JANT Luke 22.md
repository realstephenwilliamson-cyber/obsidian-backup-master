### 22.1–6: Judas Iscariot

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-1|Text]], p. 160* · . ^22-1

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-1|1]]:** *Festival of Unleavened Bread* ([[Matthew 26#^26-2|Mt 26.2–5]]; [[Mark 14#^14-1|Mk 14.1–2]]; [[John 11#^11-47|Jn 11.47–53]]), see [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-17|Ex 12.17]]; [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-15|23.15]]; [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-18|34.18]]; [[Torah 31 - Emor#^23-5|Lev 23.5–6]] (listing the Passover and the Festival of Unleavened Bread as separate holidays); [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^28-17|Num 28.17]]; [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^16-16|Deut 16.16]] (apparently equating the two holidays); [[II Chronicles 08#^8-13|2 Chr 8.13]], etc. *Called the Passover*, perhaps an explanation for Gentile readers. The Talmud (*b. Sanh*. [[Sanhedrin 43#43a|43a]]) also connects Jesus’ death to the time of Passover. ^22-1-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-3|3–6]]:** See [[Matthew 26#^26-14|Mt 26.14–16]]; [[Mark 14#^14-10|Mk 14.10–11]]. *Satan*, see 4.3n.; his reappearance is the “opportune time” of 4.13; see also [[John 13#^13-2|Jn 13.2]]. *Judas*, see 6.16n. *Chief priests*, see 19.47n. ^22-3

### 22.7–13: Passover preparations

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-7|Text]], p. 160* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-17|Mt 26.17–19]]; [[Mark 14#^14-12|Mk 14.12–16]]; [[John 13#^13-1|Jn 13.1]]). ^22-7

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-7|7]]:** *Day of Unleavened Bread*, see 22.1n. *Passover lamb*, see [[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-8|Ex 12.8]],[[Torah 15 - Bo#^12-21|21]]. For Passover, see 2.41n. ^22-7-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-10|10]]:** *A man carrying a jar of water*, suggestions that he would be notable because carrying water was women’s work cannot be confirmed. It indicates local support; see 19.30n. ^22-10

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-11|11]]:** *Guest room* (Gk “kataluma”), see 2.7. ^22-11

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-13|13]]:** *Prepared*, including obtaining a lamb sacrificed in the Temple, unleavened bread, and bitter herbs (details in *m. Pesah*.). *Passover meal*, according to the Synoptic Gospels, the Last Supper is a Passover meal; in John’s Gospel, it is not. ^22-13

### 22.14–20: Last Supper

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-14|Text]], p. 160* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-26|Mt 26.26–29]]; [[Mark 14#^14-22|Mk 14.22–25]]; cf. [[John 06#^6-51|Jn 6.51–58]]). ^22-14

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-14|14]]:** *The hour*, of the meal. ^22-14-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-17|17]]:** *Giving thanks*, blessing before drinking. Only Luke presents the cup prior to the bread as well as after. ^22-17

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-18|18]]:** *Kingdom of God comes*, focusing on the future rather than present kingdom (contrast 17.21). ^22-18

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-19|19]]:** *Loaf*, Gk “artos,” “bread,” could refer to unleavened bread. *Given thanks*, see 9.16n. *This is my body*, see [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-23|1 Cor 11.23–26]]. Jesus associates the bread with his flesh, which could suggest cannibalism ([[John 06#^6-60|Jn 6.60]]). ^22-19

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-20|20]]:** *New covenant*, see [[Jeremiah 31#^31-31|Jer 31.31]]; [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-25|1 Cor 11.25]]; [[2 Corinthians 03#^3-6|2 Cor 3.6]]; [[Hebrews 08#^8-8|Heb 8.8]],[[Hebrews 08#^8-13|13]]; [[Hebrews 09#^9-15|9.15]]; [[Hebrews 12#^12-24|12.24]]. On the connection of covenant and blood, see [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^24-8|Ex 24.8]]. ^22-20

### 22.21–23: Predicting the betrayal

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-21|Text]], p. 160* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-21|Mt 26.21–25]]; [[Mark 14#^14-18|Mk 14.18–21]]; [[John 13#^13-21|Jn 13.21–30]]). ^22-21

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-21|21]]:** *His hand is on the table*, possible allusion to [[Psalms 041#^41-10|Ps 41.9]] (the friend who shares a meal). ^22-21-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-22|22]]:** *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. *Woe to that one*, despite Jesus’ foreordained and willing death, Judas is nevertheless responsible. R. Akiva said, “Everything is foreseen, and free choice is given” (*m. Avot* 3.15). verses 19b-20 (*which is given . . . in my blood*) ^22-22

### 22.24–30: Servant leadership

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-24|Text]], p. 161* · ([[Matthew 20#^20-24|Mt 20.24–28]]; [[Mark 10#^10-41|Mk 10.41–45]]; cf. [[John 13#^13-3|Jn 13.3–16]]). ^22-24

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-25|25]]:** *Benefactors* (Gk “euer-getai,” lit., “good works doers”), a title typically bestowed on kings (Josephus, *J.W*. 3.469 \[Vespasian\]; 4.111 \[Titus\]). ^22-25

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-27|27]]:** See 12.37n. ^22-27

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-30|30]]:** *At my table*, see 11.3n. *Sit on thrones*, see [[Matthew 19#^19-28|Mt 19.28]]; see also [[Psalms 122#^122-5|Ps 122.5]]. ^22-30

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-32|32]]:** *I have prayed*, see 3.21n. *Faith may not fail*, suggesting one can resist Satan. ^22-32

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-34|34]]:** See vv 54–62. ^22-34

### 22.35–38: Preparation for Jesus’ arrest

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-35|Text]], p. 161* ^22-35

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-35|35]]:** See 9.3; 10.4. ^22-35-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-37|37]]:** See [[Isaiah 53#^53-12|Isa 53.12]] in the context of the suffering servant (see also [[Acts 08#^8-32|Acts 8.32–35]]). ^22-37

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-38|38]]:** *Two swords*, Jesus’ followers are armed (see 22.49–50; Jesus rejects violent reprisal). ^22-38

### 22.39–46: Anguish on the Mount of Olives

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-39|Text]], p. 161* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-36|Mt 26.36–46]]; [[Mark 14#^14-32|Mk 14.32–42]]; [[John 18#^18-1|Jn 18.1–2]]; Luke avoids Ara-maisms, like “Gethsemane”). ^22-39

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-39|39]]:** *His custom*, see 21.37. *Mount of Olives*, see 19.29n. ^22-39-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-40|40,46]]:** *Time of trial*, see 11.4n. ^22-40

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-41|41]]:** *Prayed*, see 3.21n. ^22-41

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-42|42]]:** *Cup*, symbolizing destiny ([[Psalms 011#^11-6|Ps 11.6]]; [[Psalms 016#^16-5|16.5]]) and not divine wrath ([[Isaiah 51#^51-17|Isa 51.17]]; [[Psalms 075#^75-9|Ps 75.8]]). ^22-42

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-43|43–44]]:** *Anguish . . . sweat became like great drops of blood*, although attested in late second century manuscripts, the verses are lacking in major third and fourth century texts. With developing Christology, some followers of Jesus rejected the idea that he would have such strong emotion, especially if it appeared he was fearful of the cross. ^22-43

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-45|45]]:** *Because of grief*, Luke tempers the disciples’ failure to remain awake. ^22-45

### 22.47–53: Jesus’ arrest

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-47|Text]], p. 161* · ([[Matthew 26#^26-47|Mt 26.47–56]]; [[Mark 14#^14-43|Mk 14.43–52]]; [[John 18#^18-2|Jn 18.2–12]]). ^22-47

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-47|47]]:** *Kiss*, see [[II Samuel 20#^20-9|2 Sam 20.9]]; the greeting of Jesus’ followers ([[Romans 16#^16-16|Rom 16.16]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-20|1 Cor 16.20]]; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-12|2 Cor 13.12]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-26|1 Thess 5.26]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-14|1 Pet 5.14]]). ^22-47-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-48|48]]:** *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. ^22-48

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-49|49]]:** *Sword*, see 22.36–38. ^22-49

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-52|52]]:** *Chief priests*, mentioned only in Luke’s arrest account (see 19.47n.). *Bandit* (Gk “lēstēs”), not a simple robber but one who uses violence (10.30,36; 19.46). ^22-52

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-53|53]]:** *Power of darkness*, see 1.79; 11.35–36; 23.33; [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]; Luke depicts the chief priests’ authority as cosmically evil (see also [[Psalms 082#^82-5|Ps 82.5]]; [[Psalms 107#^107-10|107.10]],[[Psalms 107#^107-14|14]], and elsewhere). ^22-53

### 22.66–71: Sanhedrin condemnation

*[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-66|Text]], p. 162* · ([[Matthew 27#^27-1|Mt 27.1]]; [[Matthew 26#^26-59|26.59–65]]; [[Mark 14#^14-55|Mk 14.55–63]]; [[Mark 15#^15-1|15.1]]; [[John 18#^18-13|Jn 18.13–14]],[[John 18#^18-19|19–23]]). ^22-66

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-66|66]]:** *Council* (Gk “synedrion,” Sanhedrin), the judicial authority. *M. Sanh*. records a “Great Sanhedrin” of seventy-one that met in the Temple; it was ruled by the high priest but consisted of sages (rabbis). This report’s historicity is questionable, given the Mishnah’s tendency to promote the sages’ authority over that of the priests. The Mishnah also indicates the illegality, and indeed unlikelihood of a full Sanhedrin trial on the first night of Passover (e.g., *m. Sanh*. 4.2 indicates the Sanhedrin was forbidden to hold a capital trial on the eve of a Sabbath or festival). John’s Gospel, lacking a Sanhedrin trial, depicts only a hearing before Annas. ^22-66-2

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-67|67]]:** *If you are the Messiah*, a charge against Jesus in 23.2. ^22-67

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-68|68]]:** *You will not answer*, perhaps an evocation of 20.7: the leaders are unable to respond to him without convicting themselves. By their silence, however, they are already convicted. ^22-68

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-69|69]]:** *Son of Man*, see 5.24n. *Seated at the right hand*, see [[Psalms 110#^110-1|Ps 110.1]]; see also [[Colossians 03#^3-1|Col 3.1]]; [[Hebrews 08#^8-1|Heb 8.1]]. *Power of God*, the phrase evokes [[LXX Judith 13#^13-19|Jdt 13.19]] (image of salvation); [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 07#^7-25|Wis 7.25]] (image of wisdom); [[LXX 2 Maccabees 02#^2-24|2 Macc 2.24]],[[LXX 2 Maccabees 02#^2-28|28]] etc. (defeat of enemies) as well as returns to the infancy account ([[Luke 01#^1-35|Lk 1.35]]). ^22-69

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-70|70]]:** *You say*, Jesus does not answer directly (contrast [[Mark 14#^14-62|Mk 14.62]]). ^22-70

**[[NRSVue Luke 22#^22-71|71]]:** No charge directly applies to Jesus; he has technically neither blasphemed nor engaged in sedition. ^22-71
