# Garments of the Absolute in Mei HaShiloach

> [!abstract] Definition
> The radical mystical theology of Rabbi Mordechai Yosef Leiner of Ishbitz (*Mei HaShiloach*, 1860), asserting that even the revealed commandments (*mitzvot*) and biblical narratives are external "garments" covering an undivided divine reality wherein God is the sole true actor in the cosmos.

## Core Hasidic Metaphysics
In *Mei HaShiloach*:
- **The Divine Will behind the Law:** Beyond the outer halakhic boundaries lies the deeper *ratzon Hashem* (inner divine will). In moments of profound crisis or illumination, the inner will can break through the outer garment.
- **Transcendence of the Ego:** Human autonomy is ultimately an illusion; true spiritual awakening occurs when the individual recognizes that all deeds, thoughts, and suffering are energized exclusively by the Divine.

## Resonance with the Gospel of Recognition
In [[Gospel of Recognition text 2026-08-05|The Gospel of Recognition]]:
- The Ishbitzer provides an indispensable Jewish hermeneutic for breaking through institutional, legalistic exteriority (*the velvet rope*) to reach the undivided divine presence.
