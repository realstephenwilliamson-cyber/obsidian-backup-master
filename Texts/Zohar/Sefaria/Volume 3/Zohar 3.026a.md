# Zohar 3.026a

← [[Zohar 3.025b]] · [[Zohar (Soncino)]] · [[Zohar 3.027a]] →

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 7.03 - Parashat Va-Yiqra#^z3-26a|Parashat Va-Yiqra, at 3:26a]]

*Volume 3, folio 26a* · Vayikra, Tzav

*Passage Vayikra 66:445 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 3.025b#^vayikra-66-445|Vayikra 66:445]].*

**Vayikra 66:446** “And let the skies pour down righteousness”: since when the heavens receive this rain from the place above, then the skies pour down righteousness. What is meant by “skies” (*shehakim*)? The place where manna is ground (*shahak*) for the righteous, where Victory and Majesty grind manna for the place called Righteous, or, rather, for the two Righteous Ones *Zaddik and Zedek*. ^vayikra-66-446

**Vayikra 66:447** Then “the earth opens” (*Ibid.*) beneath, and mankind “are fruitful with salvation”. “Righteousness springs forth together”: mercy and kindness abound and man’s food is provided, and so joy is added to joy and all worlds are blessed.’ Said R. Aha: ‘Had I come but to hear this, it would have been sufficient.’ ^vayikra-66-447

**Vayikra 67:448** R. Jose then discoursed on the verse: “My heart is towards the governors of- Israel that offered themselves willingly among the people, bless ye the Lord” (Judges 5, 9). ‘If a man’, he said, ‘desires earnestly to pray that blessings should be poured on earth from above, he should concentrate his mind on that recondite Stream, the deepest recess of all, where is the very beginning of the union of Father and Mother (*Hokmah* and *Binah*). So it says here, “My heart is to the governors of Israel”, to wit, to those, the Father and Mother, who trace out laws for the Holy Israel who flows out from between them. ^vayikra-67-448

**Vayikra 67:449** “That offer themselves willingly (*mithnadevim*) among the people”: these are the Fathers who are called *nedivim*. Then, “bless ye the Lord”, that blessings may flow from him below and fill the world. Happy are Israel that God pours down blessings upon them and listens to their prayer; of them it is written, “He hath regarded the prayer of the destitute and hath not despised their prayer” (Ps 102, 18). ^vayikra-67-449

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Tzav 1:1–1:4.*
