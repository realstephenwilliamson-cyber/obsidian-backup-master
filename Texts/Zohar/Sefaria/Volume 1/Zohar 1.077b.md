# Zohar 1.077b

Pritzker translation (Matt): [[Zohar 2.02 - Parashat Lekh Lekha#^z1-77b|Parashat Lekh Lekha, at 1:77b]]

*Volume 1, folio 77b* · Lech Lecha

*Passage Lech Lecha 2:8 begins on the previous side: [[Zohar 1.077a#^lech-lecha-2-8|Lech Lecha 2:8]].*

**Lech Lecha 2:9** At that moment a certain flash springs forth from the side of the North and strikes the four quarters of the world and comes down and alights between the wings of the cock, which is thereby awakened and begins to crow. But none are stirred save those truly pious ones who rise and remain awake and study the Torah, and then the Holy One, blessed be He, and all the righteous in the Garden of Eden listen to their voices, as it is written, “Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the companions hearken for thy voice, cause me to hear it” (S. S. 8, 13) ^lech-lecha-2-9

**Lech Lecha 4:16** AND THE LORD SAID TO ABRAM. Just before this it is written, “And Haran died in the presence of Terah his father” (Gen. 11, 28). The connection is as follows. Up to that time no one had ever died in the lifetime of his father. Haran, however, was killed at the time when Abram was cast into the furnace. Then it says, “And Terah took Abram his son and Lot the son of Haran, etc…. and they went forth with them from Ur of the Chaldees.” ^lech-lecha-4-16

**Lech Lecha 4:17** We should expect “and they went forth with *him״*, referring to Terah; why, then, does it say “with them”? The reason is that Terah and Lot went forth with Abram and Sara, who led the way in departing from that sinful district; for when Terah saw that Abram was rescued from the fire, he began to be guided by Abram, and therefore we read, “and they went forth with them”, i.e. Terah and Lot with Abram and Sara. ^lech-lecha-4-17

**Lech Lecha 4:18** Also it was “to go into the land of Canaan”, where *they* wanted to go. We learn further from this text that whoever makes an effort to purify himself receives assistance from above. For no sooner is it written, “to go to the land of Canaan” than we read “and God said to Abram, Get thee forth”; this message was not given to him until he made the first move himself. ^lech-lecha-4-18

**Lech Lecha 4:19** For the upper world is not stirred to act until an impulse is given from the lower world. The prototype of this process is that the blackish light is not caught up by the white light until it has first itself begun to mount;[^1] but when it does so, forthwith the white light rests upon it, ^lech-lecha-4-19

**Lech Lecha 4:20** and therefore it is written, “O Lord, keep not thou silence, hold not thy peace and be not still, O God” (Ps. 83, 2), in order that the white light may never be withheld from the world. So, too, it says, “Ye that are the Lord’s remembrancers, keep not silence” (Is. 62, 6), in order to give the impulse from below for the influence to descend from the upper world. Similarly the prophetic spirit rests upon man only when he has first bestirred himself to receive it. So here, too, when once Abram and his family had left Ur of the Chaldees, then God said to him, “Get thee forth”; ^lech-lecha-4-20

**Lech Lecha 5:21** the word “thee” here, says R. Eleazar, means “for thine own advantage, to prepare thyself, to perfect thy degree”. “Get thee forth”: that is, it is not fitting for thee to remain here among these sinners. ^lech-lecha-5-21

**Lech Lecha 5:22** The real truth of the matter is this. God inspired Abram with a spirit of wisdom so that he was able to discover by means of certain tests the characters of all the inhabited countries of the world. He surveyed them and weighed them in the balance, and discovered the (heavenly) powers to which each is entrusted. ^lech-lecha-5-22

*Not translated in the Soncino edition: Lech Lecha 7:41–7:42.*

[^1]: v. p. 163.
