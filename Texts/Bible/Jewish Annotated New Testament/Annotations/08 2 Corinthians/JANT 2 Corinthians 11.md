### 11.1–12.10: A fool’s speech

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 367* · Paul parodies his opponents’ penchant for comparison after criticizing them for doing the same (10.12). ^11-1

### 11.1–11: Appeal as a fool

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-1|Text]], p. 367* ^11-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-1|1]]:** *Foolishness* (Gk “aphrosynē”), the opposite of “moderation” or “sober-mind-edness” (Gk “sophrosynē”); see “madman” (Gk “paraphronōn”), 11.23; 1 Cor 1–3 uses different words for “foolishness” (Gk “mōria,” “mōros”), whose opposite is “wisdom” (Gk “sophia”). ^11-1-3

> [!note] Synagogue Discipline *(p. 367)*
> Of the three judiciary punishments Paul endured (11.24–25), lashes and stoning alone are known from Jewish sources. Stoning is one of early Judaism’s four prescribed methods of capital punishment (alongside strangulation, beheading, and burning); this clearly is not what Paul experienced. Flogging was ancient Judaism’s only form of corporal punishment, and it is the subject of an entire tractate of the Mishnah (*Makkot*, “Lashes”). The use of lashes as a judicial punishment derives from [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-1|Deut 25.1–3]], which sentences a guilty party to a maximum of forty lashes, “if more . . . are given, your neighbor will be degraded in your sight.” The “forty minus one” Paul describes reflects the rabbinic reading of [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-3|Deut 25.3]] as allowing up to, but not to include, the fortieth lash (*m. Makk.* 3.10). Later exegetes explain that imposing only thirty-nine lashes assures that an error in counting will not result in the biblical limit’s being surpassed. Depending on the nature of the crime and state of health of the individual, fewer than thirty-nine lashes might also have been inflicted. Prior to the flogging, the person’s health was assessed and a number of lashes that would not kill or irrep-arably injure him was determined. The appropriate number was set by the rabbis of the Mishnah as a multiple of three, another reason that a maximum of thirty-nine lashes could be inflicted (*m. Makk.* 3.11).
>
> Flogging was imposed for violations of negative commandments that involved physical action (*m. Makk.* 3.1–6), e.g., inappropriate sexual relations, violations of Temple purity, self-mutilation as a form of mourning, and tattooing. While the Mishnah does not contemplate mere speech or thoughts as grounds for flogging, the Babylonian Talmud (*Tem.* 3a-b) specifies flogging for one who makes a false oath or takes God’s name in vain, thus violating [[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-7|Ex 20.7]] and [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-11|Deut 5.11]]. While Scripture states that God will never exonerate such an individual, the Talmud conceived that the human court, through lashes, could do so. Later rabbinic law does not fully clarify exactly how or in what setting Paul’s actions might have been adjudicated and a corporal punishment imposed by a court.

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-2|2]]:** *Divine jealousy*, a zeal inspired by God; cf. [[I Kings 19#^19-10|1 Kings 19.10]], Elijah’s proclamation that he has been “zealous for the Lord”; in Heb as in Gk, the same verbal root conveys the sense both of jealousy and zealousness. *Promised you in marriage*, Paul presents himself as father of the *chaste* bride, that is, the founder of the congregation; see 1.19. ^11-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-3|3]]:** *Serpent*, Satan; see 2.11; 11.14; [[1 Corinthians 07#^7-5|1 Cor 7.5]]. *Deceived Eve*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^3-13|Gen 3.13]]. In connection with “marriage” and “virginity” (v 2), Paul is probably thinking of the tradition that Satan sexually seduced Eve (*2 En*. 31.6). ^11-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-4|4]]:** *Another Jesus . . . different spirit . . . different gospel*, reminiscent of [[Galatians 01#^1-6|Gal 1.6–9]], where Paul declares that there is no different Gospel or other Jesus, even if there are individuals who wish to confuse the community and pervert the true Gospel that Paul teaches; see 10.13–16n. Cf. [[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-2|Deut 13.1–5]]. ^11-4

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-5|5]]:** *Super-apostles*, likely a sarcastic title Paul assigns his opponents, though some commentators hold that the reference is to the apostles chosen by Jesus in his lifetime. ^11-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-6|6]]:** *Untrained in speech*, see 10.10. *Knowledge*, see [[Ephesians 03#^3-2|Eph 3.2-4]]; divine revelation communicated to Paul the mystery of Christ; he knows the Gospel as well as the apostles who knew Jesus in person. ^11-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-7|7]]:** *Humbling*, lit., “debasing”; see also 12.21; [[Philippians 04#^4-12|Phil 4.12]]. The adjective appears in 10.1. Phil 2.8 in the Gk describes the Christ as “debased.” Since Greco-Roman philosophers viewed craftsmen as debased, Paul’s use of the word here might refer to his continual activity as a craftsman to support himself (see [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|1 Cor 4.12]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-9|1 Thess 2.9]]). The rabbis did not share the Greco-Roman view; men were instructed to teach their sons a trade (*m. Qidd.* 4.14), and the Talmudic literature contains no negative statements regarding craftsmen, who are viewed positively at [[Berakhot 58#58a|*b. Ber*. 58a]], *b. Moʾed Qat*. 13a, and [[Arakhin 10#10b|*b. Arak*. 10b]]. Still, some later rabbis locate craftsmen in a different, lower social class, from which they expect to remain separate even in the world-to-come ([[Chullin 54#54b|*b. Hul*. 54b]]; *Eccl. Rab*. 3.11). ^11-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-8|8]]:** *Robbed other churches*, Paul took from others, primarily the Philippian church, more than their fair share of his expenses. ^11-8

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-9|9]]:** *I did not burden*, see 12.13; 1 Cor 9 on self-support; see [[Philippians 04#^4-16|Phil 4.16]],[[Philippians 04#^4-18|18]]; [[1 Thessalonians 02#^2-9|1 Thess 2.9]] for Macedonian support. Accepting support obligated recipients (clients) to benefactors (patrons). By refusing their support, Paul denies the Corinthians the status and authority of patronage; by accepting Macedonian support, Paul is seen as slighting the Corinthians; see 12.12–18nn. The rabbis of earliest rabbinic Judaism asserted that, alongside studying Torah, one should earn a living through work and not be supported by others (*m. Avot* 2.2*)*. Some later sources taught that one should labor only in Torah and that, as a result, earthly needs will take care of themselves (see *Avot de R. Natan* 6.5). ^11-9

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-10|10]]:** *Boast*, see 1.12n. and 1.14n. ^11-10

### 11.12–15: Characterization of Paul’s opponents

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-12|Text]], p. 368* ^11-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-13|13]]:** *False apostles*, cf. the Tanakh’s treatment of false prophets ([[Torah 47 - Re'eh#^13-2|Deut 13.1–5]]; 18.20–22). Rabbinic law dealt harshly with teachers who led people into false belief or instructed them to act contrary to the decided law (see *m. Sanh.* 11.1). At the same time, the rabbis accepted and legitimated disagreement, carefully preserving minority opinions and perspectives that had been rejected through rabbinic debate. ^11-13

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-14|14]]:** *Satan disguises himself*, see 2.11. A Talmudic source depicts Satan’s disguising himself as a woman to entangle in sin even such rabbis as Meir and Akiva, who had declared that people can easily subdue their evil inclination ([[Kiddushin 81#81a|*b. Qidd*. 81a]]). ^11-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-15|15]]:** *Ministers*, Jewish sources, which generally use the term “satan” to refer to an impersonal source of evil, do not speak of *ministers* or other assistants of Satan. *End will match their deeds*, this idea is commonplace in biblical and Talmudic literature; see, e.g., [[Ecclesiastes 10#^10-8|Eccl 10.8]]; [[Avodah Zarah 5#5b|*b. Avod. Zar.* 5b]]; *b. B. Kamma* 17a. ^11-15

### 11.16–30: Paul’s hardships validate his authenticity

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-16|Text]], p. 369* ^11-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-16|16–21]]:** *Fool*, see 11.1n. One who boasts of human accomplishments is a fool, for these things are meaningless compared to the true life offered in the Christ. Paul, responding to the super-apostles (11.5; 12.11), ironically lists his achievements: he will speak as a fool and boast *according to human standards*. ^11-16-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-22|22]]:** *Hebrews . . . Israelites . . . descendants of Abraham*, suggests that Paul’s opponents boast of their Jewish pedigree. Paul declares his own Jewish heritage whenever necessary; see [[Romans 09#^9-4|Rom 9.4]]; [[Romans 11#^11-1|11.1]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-5|Phil 3.5]], but cf. 11.24n. These three terms are never used together in the Tanakh. In the NT the three terms for Jews are used interchangeably, with no clear difference in implication. The Tanakh uses the term “Hebrew” and, most commonly in the Tanakh and rabbinic literature, “children of Israel” or simply “Israelites” to refer to the people later known as Jews. In the Tanakh, the term “descendants (lit., “seed”) of Abraham” does not appear in the sense of Jew, and it appears only rarely in later Jewish literature, likely because not all of Abraham’s descendants were Jews. ^11-22

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|23a]]:** *Madman*, see 11.1n. ^11-23

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|23b–27]]:** Catalogue of hardships used ironically to show Paul’s qualifications; see 1.3–7n.; 4.8–9n.; 10.1n. ^11-23-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-24|24]]:** *Forty lashes minus one*. In the Tanakh (e.g., [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^25-1|Deut 25.1–3]]) and rabbinic literature (e.g., *m. Makk*. 1.3), lashes are a judicial punishment imposed by a court for violating communal standards. *From the Jews*, Paul, who just boasted of his Jewish identity, now depicts himself as at odds with the Jewish community, a common tactic in his writings (e.g., [[Romans 15#^15-30|Rom 15.30]]; [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-19|1 Cor 9.19–22]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-11|Gal 5.11]]). ^11-24

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-25|25]]:** *Beaten with rods*, Roman, not Jewish, punishment. *Stoning*, in Jewish sources, a form of capital punishment; the convicted is pushed from a high place and then, if he is still alive, crushed with a single, heavy boulder dropped on him (*m. Sanh*. 6.1–4). There is no evidence that Jewish courts, under Greek and then Roman control, ever had the authority to administer a death penalty. What Paul is referring to, clearly not a form of capital punishment, is unattested in the rabbinic literature. *Shipwrecked*, cf. Acts 27. ^11-25

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-30|30]]:** *Boast*, a familiar theme; see Introduction, 1.12n. and 1.14n. ^11-30

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 11#^11-32|32–33]]:** See [[Acts 09#^9-23|Acts 9.23–25]]. *Aretas* IV ruled the Nabateans from 9 BCE until his death ca. 41 CE. The Nabateans took Damascus in 37 CE, soon after which Paul escaped from the city *in a basket*. ^11-32
