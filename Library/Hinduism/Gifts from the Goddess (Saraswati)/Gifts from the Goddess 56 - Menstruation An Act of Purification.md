B Menstrual cycles are in rhythm with nature. Nature is full of such cyclical periods—inhaling and exhaling, morning and evening, light and darkness, as well as cycles in time. Because we are in the northern hemisphere, *dakṣiṇāyaṇa*<sup>206</sup> occurs when the sun 206  The six-month period between the summer and winter is going south and the night grows shorter, and *uttarāyaṇa*<sup>207</sup> occurs when the sun solstices. is going north and the day grows longer. Of all these cycles, however, the one most important and visible for us is *chandramāna*—the lunar cycle. Every 28 days, 14 plus 14, 207  The six-month period between the winter and summer a new cycle begins. In ancient times, before so-called civilization took hold, women solstices. used to menstruate in harmony with the lunar cycle. Women were associated with the moon.

The menstrual cycle takes place when the production of new life has failed to occur, and that which has not conceived has to be flushed out. It is this process of flushing out which produces the menstrual blood. So it is an act of purification, not “impurification.” It is a preparation for conception, which is very sacred. In Hindu dharma there are four *puruṣārthās*: dharma, *artha*, *kāma* and moksha. *Kāma* is considered pure because it leads to conception. Life wants to sustain itself; it doesn’t want to give up. And how do you conceive? Through *kāma*. The time of ovulation is most auspicious, and the process of flushing out is a preparation for that. Conception may or may not occur, but it is still associated with *kāma*.

This whole taboo against menstruating women indulging in sexual activity is also wrong. If you want to follow family planning, then there is good reason to indulge during this time. Of course, if it makes the woman feel uncomfortable or if the man is turned off by the sight of the blood or fears he is hurting the woman, then it is counterproductive. But if they have no such inhibitions, then there is nothing wrong in it.
