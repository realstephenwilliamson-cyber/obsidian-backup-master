## About

A dialogue attributed to Plato. Socrates talks with the young Alcibiades, who is about to address the Athenian assembly, shows him that he knows nothing of justice or politics, and urges him to know himself first.

- **Characters and setting:** Socrates, Alcibiades' first lover, who has not spoken to him for years; and Alcibiades, proud of his looks, birth, friends, wealth and his tie to Pericles. A private conversation, set as Alcibiades is about to enter public life (Wikipedia; Cambridge University Press). The sources consulted give no dramatic date.
- **Argument:** there is no advantage without justice; Alcibiades' real rivals are the kings of Sparta and Persia; the Delphic maxim *know thyself* applies, and what rules in a person is the soul, not the body. Alcibiades ends by taking Socrates as his teacher, and Socrates doubts that either can withstand the power of the city.
- **Authenticity:** never doubted in antiquity; doubts began with Friedrich Schleiermacher. Wikipedia reports stylometric support for Plato, and Nicholas Denyer's Cambridge edition (2001) defends it; his reviewer Mark Joyal (BMCR, 2003) has grave doubts. The IEP lists it among the dubia, and a 2015 NDPR review says most commentators of the last century treated it as spurious.
- **Date of composition:** traditionally early; Gerard Ledger's stylometry puts it in the 390s BC. Denyer dates it to the 350s (Wikipedia), the early 350s (BMCR). R. S. Bluck suggested about 343/2 BC, after Plato's death. One hypothesis has an Academy member write the first two-thirds and Plato finish it.
- **Place among the dialogues:** Diogenes Laertius puts it first in Thrasyllus's fourth tetralogy, subtitled "On the Nature of Man", and says some readers began Plato with it (3.59, 3.62). Ancient readers took it as the best introduction to Plato (Wikipedia; Cambridge).
- **Why it matters:** it was a common ancient starting point for reading Plato, and its argument identifies the self that rules with the soul. Wikipedia says its popularity declined after Schleiermacher.
