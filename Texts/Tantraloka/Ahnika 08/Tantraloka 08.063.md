## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 04 - Chapter Eight, part 2#^pg129|p. 111]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=129>))
>
> Jambudvīpa extends all around below Meru up to the ocean of salt, for just one hundred thousand (leagues), and it is divided into nine (regions) by the mountains on the borders (that run across it). (with 8.62cd–63ab)
>
> The three (chains) to the south (of Meru) are those of Niṣadha (Bull), Hemakūṭa (Golden Peak), and Himavat (the Himalaya – Abode of Snow). They are, respectively, one hundred thousand, ninety thousand and eighty thousand (leagues long). Again, to the north, there are (another three chains) that are the same (length), namely, Nīla (Blue), Śveta (White) and Triśrṇga (Triple Peak). These six (chains of) mountains, which serve as boundaries, extend from east to west. To the east and west (perpendicular to these chains are the chains) called (east) Mālyavat (Possessing Garlands) and (west) Gandhamādana (Inebriating Smell). They extend from south to north for a length of thirty-four thousand (leagues). Apart from these, there are eight others which, in pairs, are in the east (south, west and north), in due order. They are called, respectively, 1) (east) Jāthara (Belly), (Hema)kūta, 2) (south) (Kailāsa), Himavat, 3) (west) (Niṣadha), (Pāri)yātra, 4) (north) Jārudhi and Śṛṅgavat (Possessing a Peak). Such is the division here (of the mountains), described in order to establish (the extent and geography of the nine) continents (varsa).). (with 8.63cd–67)
