## Mūla

उच्यते परिपूर्णं चेद्भावीयं रूपमुच्यते ।
तद्विभुर्भैरवो देवो भगवानेव भण्यते ॥ ^mula

ucyate paripūrṇaṃ cedbhāvīyaṃ rūpamucyate |
tadvibhurbhairavo devo bhagavāneva bhaṇyate || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 11 - Chapter Ten, part 2#^pg365|p. 346]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=365>))
>
> Tt is said, (in response to this query,) that if we are talking about the perfectly full (and complete) nature of things, then that is said to be the Blessed One (bhagavat) Himself, the all-pervasive Lord Bhairava. Now, (if we are reflecting on how) He (bears within Himself and) tolerates the progressive differentiation of His aspects (amśāmśikākrama) that is conceived by the glory (māhātmya) of His own (power), what does this conception (of the distinction between these levels), as outlined above, effectively do (to either compromise Bhairava’s plenitude or undermine our views)? (with 10.80–81)
