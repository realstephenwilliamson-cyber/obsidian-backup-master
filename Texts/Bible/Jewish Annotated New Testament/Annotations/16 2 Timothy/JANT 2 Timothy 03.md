### 3.1–5: The sinners of the last days

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 445* ^3-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-1|1]]:** *Last days*, see [[Isaiah 02#^2-2|Isa 2.2]]; [[Jeremiah 23#^23-20|Jer 23.20]]; [[Hosea 03#^3-5|Hos 3.5]]; [[Micah 4#^4-1|Mic 4.1]]; increasing depravity is a sign of the last days, e.g., *1 En.* 91.5 observes: “. . . violence must increase on the earth, and a great chastisement be executed on the earth, and all unrighteousness come to an end.” According to the later tradition found in [[Sanhedrin 98#98a|*b. Sanh.* 98a]], “Rabbi Yochanan said: ‘The Son of David will not come except in a generation which is either completely righteous or completely guilty.’” ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-2|2–4]]:** Vice lists were common in Hellenistic codes of behavior. See Philo, *Sacr.* 32; Dio Chrys. 1.15–35; 3.40–41; Cf. [[Romans 13#^13-12|Rom 13.12–14]]; [[1 Corinthians 05#^5-10|1 Cor 5.10–11]]; [[1 Corinthians 06#^6-9|6.9–10]]; [[2 Corinthians 12#^12-20|2 Cor 12.20–21]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-19|Gal 5.19–21]]; [[Ephesians 05#^5-3|Eph 5.3–5]]; [[Colossians 03#^3-5|Col 3.5–8]]; [[1 Timothy 01#^1-9|1 Tim 1.9]]; [[1 Timothy 06#^6-4|6.4]]; [[Revelation 21#^21-7|Rev 21.7–8]]. ^3-2

> [!note] Inspiration *(p. 445)*
> Although God’s inspiration in the Tanakh is attributed to human beings like prophets ([[Torah 40 - Balak#^24-2|Num 24.2]]; [[Ezekiel 02#^2-2|Ezek 2.2]]; [[Micah 3#^3-8|Mic 3.8]]), leaders ([[Judges 03#^3-10|Judg 3.10]]; [[Judges 06#^6-34|6.34]]), etc., the divine origin of the Tanakh, either given directly by God (the Torah, to Moses at Sinai) or inspired by God (rest of the canon), was a common belief in first-century and later rabbinic Judaism. Josephus writes: “The Prophets have written the original and earliest accounts of things as they learned them of God Himself by inspiration” (*Ag. Ap*. 1.37). The Gk *epipvoia* literally means “breathing upon.” [[2 Timothy 03#^3-16|2 Tim 3.16]] uses the cognate *theopneustos*, literal- ly “God-breathed” (see Philo, *Leg. all.* 1.36–40). A condition for inclusion in the canon was that the book was considered to be inspired by God (*t. Yad.* 2.14; *Song Rab.* 1.1, no. 5), although it might be more accurate to say that any book that was canonical was deemed inspired. See “The Canon,” p. 695. After the Torah was given to the Jewish people, Rabbi Yehoshua affirmed it was up to rabbis and the people, free from divine intervention, to interpret it ([[Bava Metzia 59#59b|*b. B. Metz.* 59b]]). Translations of the Tanakh (such the LXX and the Targum) were also considered inspired in some circles (Philo, *Life of Moses* 7.37–40; [[Megillah 3#3a|*b. Meg.* 3a]]); see “The Septuagint,” p. 703.

### 3.6–9: False teaching

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-6|Text]], p. 446* ^3-6

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-6|6–7]]:** *Silly women*, Gk “gynaikaria,” a derogative diminutive, better “gullible, little women,” perhaps referring to the same group as in [[1 Timothy 05#^5-14|1 Tim 5.14–15]] targeted by the false teachers. These women are contrasted with Eunice and Lois in 1.5 and Priscilla and Claudia in 4.19,21. ^3-6-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-8|8]]:** *Jannes and Jambres*, names given in Jewish tradition to the Egyptian magicians who opposed Moses in [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^7-11|Ex 7.11–12]] (CD 5.18–19 \[“Jannes and his brother”\]; *Tg. Ps*–*J*. on [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^1-15|Ex 1.15]]; [[Torah 14 - Vaera#^7-10|7.10–12]]; [[Torah 40 - Balak#^22-2|Num 22.2]]; see Introduction). ^3-8

### 3.10–13: Paul’s example and the promise to the faithful

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-10|Text]], p. 446* · After a list of vices to avoid, the author, in Paul’s name, presents a list of virtues as exemplified by his own life, a model for Timothy to follow. See [[1 Corinthians 11#^11-1|1 Cor 11.1]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-17|Phil 3.17]]; see also [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-7|2 Thess 3.7]],[[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-9|9]]. ^3-10

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-11|11]]:** *Persecutions*, see [[Romans 08#^8-35|Rom 8.35]]; [[1 Corinthians 04#^4-12|1 Cor 4.12]]; [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-23|2 Cor 11.23–32]]; [[2 Corinthians 12#^12-10|12.10]]. *Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra*, see [[Acts 13#^13-14|Acts 13.14–14.23]]. ^3-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-12|12]]:** *Will be persecuted*, early Christian teaching emphasized the suffering of the faithful. See [[Matthew 10#^10-23|Mt 10.23]]; [[John 15#^15-20|Jn 15.20]]; [[Acts 07#^7-52|Acts 7.52]]; [[1 Peter 04#^4-12|1 Pet 4.12]]. ^3-12

### 3.14–17: The nature and purpose of scripture

*[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-14|Text]], p. 446* ^3-14

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-15|15]]:** *From childhood*, in rabbinic tradition, Torah instruction begins when children were five (*m. Avot* 5.21) or six/seven years old ([[Bava Batra 21#21a|*b. B. Bat.* 21a]]), but Rabbi Joshua’s mother is praised (*m. Avot* 2.8) because “she never removed his cradle from the study hall” (*y. Yeb*. 1.6,3a). The Tanakh highlights the importance of teaching children (e.g., [[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-19|Deut 11.19]]); see also [[Ephesians 06#^6-4|Eph 6.4]]. *Sacred writings* (Gk “hiera grammata”), designates the incipient Jewish Bible, as in Philo (*Life of Moses* 2.290,292) and Josephus (*Ant.* 1.13; 10.210). *For salvation*, [[Luke 01#^1-71|Lk 1.71]],[[Luke 01#^1-77|77]]; [[John 04#^4-22|Jn 4.22]]; [[John 05#^5-39|5.39]]. ^3-15

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-16|16]]:** *All scripture*, likely referring to the LXX. An alternative translation is: “Every/all . . . inspired/given by God’s Scripture is also useful . . . ” See [[2 Peter 01#^1-21|2 Pet 1.21]]. ^3-16

**[[NRSVue 2 Timothy 03#^3-17|17]]:** *Everyone who belongs to God*, Gk “ho tou theou anthropos,” a person of God/man of God, see LXX [[LXX Deuteronomy 33#^33-1|Deut 33.1]]; [[Joshua 10#^10-14|Josh 10.14]]; [[Joshua 14#^14-6|14.6]]; [[Judges 13#^13-6|Judg 13.6]]; [[I Samuel 02#^2-27|1 Sam 2.27]]; [[I Samuel 09#^9-6|9.6–10]]; [[I Kings 13#^13-5|1 Kings 13.5]]; [[I Kings 14#^14-3|14.3]]; [[II Chronicles 11#^11-2|2 Chr 11.2]]; [[II Chronicles 30#^30-16|30.16]], and elsewhere in the LXX. ^3-17
