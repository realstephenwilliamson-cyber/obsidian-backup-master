**1.** A person who prays must be careful to tend to [the following] eight matters.[^1] [However,] if he is pressured,[^2] confronted by circumstances beyond his control,[^3] or transgresses[^4] and does not attend to one them, they are not[^5] of absolute necessity.[^6] They are:  
1) standing;  
2) facing the Temple;  
3)[^7] preparation of his body;  
4)[^8] proper clothing;  
5)[^9] proper place;  
6)[^10] control of his voice;  
7)[^11] bowing;[^12] and  
8) prostration.[^13] ^5-1

**2.** Standing: What is implied?  
[Generally,] one should pray only while standing.[^14] [Thus,] a person sitting in a boat or in a carriage, if able to stand, should do so;[^15] if not, he may sit in his place and pray.[^16]  
A person who is ill[^17] may pray even while lying on his side,[^18] provided he is able to have the proper intention.[^19] Similarly, one who is thirsty or hungry[^20] is considered as one who is ill.[^21] [Therefore,] if he is able to concentrate properly he should pray. If not, he should not pray until he has eaten or drunk.[^22]  
One riding an animal should not descend [from the animal][^23] - even if he has someone to hold his animal. Rather, he should sit in his place and pray so his mind will be settled.[^24] ^5-2

**3.** Facing the Temple: What is implied?  
A person standing in the Diaspora should face *Eretz Yisrael* and pray.[^25]  
One standing in *Eretz Yisrael* should face Jerusalem.[^26]  
One standing in Jerusalem should face the Temple.[^27]  
One standing in the Temple should face the Holy of Holies.[^28]  
A blind person, one who is[^29] unable to determine direction, or one travelling in a boat[^30] should direct his heart towards the Divine Presence[^31] and pray.[^32] ^5-3

**4.** The preparation of one's body: What is implied?  
When one stands in prayer, he should place his feet together side by side.[^33] He should set his eyes downwards as if he is looking at the ground, and his heart upwards as if he is standing in Heaven.[^34]  
His hands should be resting on his heart, with the right hand clasped over the left hand.[^35] He should stand like a servant before his master,[^36] in fear, awe, and dread.[^37] He should not rest his hand on his hips [during the *Amidah*].[^38] ^5-4

**5.** Proper clothing: What is implied?  
One should adjust his clothing and make himself neat and presentable before [praying], as [implied by [[Psalms 029#^29-2|Psalms 29:2]]]: "They bow to God in resplendent holiness."  
One[^39] should not pray wearing [only] his undershirt,[^40] bareheaded,[^41] or barefoot[^42] - if it is the custom of the people of that place[^43] to stand before their most respected people[^44] with shoes.[^45]  
In all places,[^46] one should not hold *tefillin* in his hand or a *Sefer Torah* in his arms during the *Amidah*, since he will worry about them.[^47] [Similarly,] one should not hold utensils or money in his hand.[^48] However, he may pray while holding his lulav on Sukkot,[^49] since it is the commandment of the day.[^50]  
If one is carrying a burden of less than four *kabbim* on his head[^51] when the time for the *Amidah* arrives, he should throw it over his shoulder and pray.[^52] If it is larger than four *kabbim*,[^53] he should place it on the ground and then pray.  
It is customary for all Sages and their students to pray only when wrapped in a *tallit*.[^54] ^5-5

**6.** Proper place: What is implied?  
One should stand in a low place[^55] and turn his face towards the wall.[^56] Also, one should open windows or doors[^57] that face Jerusalem and pray opposite them,[^58] as \[[[Daniel 06#^6-11|Daniel 6:11]]] states: "...and he had windows open in his room facing Jerusalem."  
A[^59] person should establish a fixed place where he always prays.[^60] One should not pray in a destroyed building,[^61] nor [should one pray] behind a synagogue, unless he turns his face towards the synagogue.[^62]  
It is forbidden to sit down next to someone in the midst of the *Amidah*[^63] or to pass in front of him,[^64] except at a distance of four cubits.[^65] ^5-6

**7.** One should not stand in a place three or more handbreadths high[^66] and pray.[^67] [Similarly, he should not pray while standing] on a bed, bench, or chair.[^68]  
A raised platform that has a surface area of four cubits by four cubits which is the [minimum] size of a house,[^69] is considered like an attic.[^70] Thus, one is permitted to pray there.[^71] Similarly, if it is surrounded by walls, even if it is not four cubits by four cubits,[^72] one may pray there, since its height is not noticeable, because it constitutes an area unto itself.[^73] ^5-7

**8.** Craftsmen[^74] working at the top of a tree, or on top of a board[^75] or wall when the time of the *Amidah* arrives must descend in order to pray,[^76] and then return to their work.[^77] If they were at the top of an olive or fig tree, they may pray where they are, because of the excessive effort [involved in descending].[^78]  
What is it that they pray? If they are working for meals alone, they recite three prayers of 19 blessings.[^79] If they are working for wages, they recite "Give us understanding."[^80] In either case, they do not lead the congregation[^81] or lift up their hands [to bless the people].[^82] ^5-8

**9.** Control of one's voice: What is implied?  
A person should not raise his voice during his *Amidah*,[^83] nor should he pray silently.[^84] Rather, he should pronounce the words with his lips, whispering in a tone that he can hear.[^85]  
He should not make his voice audible[^86] unless he is sick or cannot concentrate otherwise.[^87] In such a case, it is permitted except when in a congregation, lest the others be disturbed by his voice.[^88] ^5-9

**10.** Bowing:[^89] What is implied?  
One praying bows five times in each and every *Amidah*:  
In[^90] the first blessing, at the beginning and at the end;  
in[^91] the blessing of thanks,[^92] at the beginning and at the end;[^93] and  
upon completing the *Amidah*, one bows and takes three steps backwards[^94] while bowing.[^95] He takes leave[^96] from his left and afterwards, from his right.[^97] Then, he lifts his head up from the bowed position.  
When he bows the [other] four times, he does so at [the utterance of the word] "Blessed" and straightens up when [reciting] G‑d's name.[^98][^99]  
To whom does the above apply? To an average person. However, the High Priest bows at the beginning and end of each and every blessing.[^100] A king bows at the beginning [of the *Amidah*] and does not lift his head until he completes his whole *Amidah*.[^101] ^5-10

**11.** Why should one take leave from the left first?[^102] Because one's left is to the right [side][^103] of His countenance; i.e., just like when one stands before a[^104] king, he takes leave from the right of the king, and then afterwards from the left of the king. Thus, they[^105] established that one should withdraw from the *Amidah* in the same manner as he withdraws from before a king. ^5-11

**12.** All these bows require that one bow until the vertebrae in his spine protrude and he makes himself like a bow.[^106]  
However, if one bows slightly [to the extent that] it causes him pain and he appears to have bowed with all of his power, he need not worry.[^107] ^5-12

**13.** Prostration, what is implied?  
After one lifts his head from the fifth bow, he sits on the ground, falls with his face towards the earth, and utters all the supplications[^108] that he desires.[^109]  
"Kneeling" always refers to [falling to] one's knees;[^110] "bowing," to bending over on one's face;[^111] and "prostration," to stretching out on one's hands and feet until he is flat with his face on the ground.[^112] ^5-13

**14.** When uttering the supplication[^113] after the *Amidah*, there are those who bow and there are those who prostrate themselves.[^114]  
It is forbidden to prostrate oneself on stones except in the Holy Temple, as we have explained in *Hilchot Avodat Kochavim*.[^115]  
An important person is not permitted to fall on his face unless he is certain that he is as righteous as Yehoshua.[^116] Rather, he should tilt his face slightly, but not press it to the ground.[^117]  
One may pray in one place and offer this supplication in another[^118] ^5-14

**15.** It is an accepted custom among the entire Jewish people not to utter the supplication on Sabbaths or festivals. Nor [does one utter it] on Rosh Hashanah, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah or Purim[^119] or in Minchah on the eve of Sabbaths or holidays, nor in the Evening Prayer of any day.[^120] There are [however,] individuals who do utter the supplication in the Evening Prayer.[^121]  
On Yom Kippur only, one utters the supplication prayer in every prayer, since it is a day of supplication, requests, and fasting.[^122] ^5-15
