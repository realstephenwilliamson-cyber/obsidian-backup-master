## About

The prayer book of the Episcopal Church in the United States from 1928 until the 1979 book replaced it. It revised the 1892 book; Britannica notes that the church had used its own prayer book since 1789.

- **Makers:** the General Convention of the Episcopal Church. A revision commission formed in 1913, chaired by Cortlandt Whitehead and, after his death in 1922, by Charles L. Slattery. The revision was completed at the 1925 Convention and finally approved in 1928.
- **Changes from 1892:** the penitential sentences before the Daily Offices gave way to a seasonal emphasis. Wikipedia says "extreme Calvinism" was removed and human sinfulness less stressed. The marriage vows of husband and wife were made more alike, and the third Good Friday collect was altered to remove a slur against Jews. The Visitation of the Sick was rewritten in a more hopeful tone, prayers for the dead entered the burial office, and fifteen new collects were added.
- **Contents:** Morning and Evening Prayer at the front; Holy Communion with the collects, epistles and gospels in the middle; baptism, confirmation, marriage, the sick and burial toward the back, followed by the Psalter, the Ordinal, a Catechism, family prayers and the Articles of Religion.
- **Later changes:** the Standard Book, designed by Daniel Berkeley Updike at the Merrymount Press, dates from 1930; a 1936 printing enlarged the type of the later services (598 pages became 611). Wikipedia says a new daily lectionary was approved on 5 October 1943; the Justus text prints both the 1928 tables and those used from 1945.
- **After 1979:** the 1979 General Convention let bishops permit the 1928 services, with the 1979 lectionary; the 2000 Convention reclassified the 1928 book alongside the supplemental liturgies. Justus says it served the church for fifty years, 1928 to 1978.
- **Why it matters:** it was the Episcopal Church's prayer book for half a century, and its Psalter was reused in the traditional-language *Anglican Service Book* of 1991.
