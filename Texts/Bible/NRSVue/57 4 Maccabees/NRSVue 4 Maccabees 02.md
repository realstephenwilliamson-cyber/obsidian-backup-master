# 4 Maccabees 2 (NRSVue)

[[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 01|← 4 Maccabees 1]] · [[NRSVue|index]] · [[NRSVue 4 Maccabees 03|4 Maccabees 3 →]]

## Compatibility of the law with reason

**2:1** And why is it amazing that the desires of the soul for the enjoyment of beauty are rendered powerless? ^2-1

**2:2** It is for this reason, certainly, that the temperate Joseph is praised, because by mental effort^[Another ancient authority adds *in reasoning*] he overcame the prospect of pleasure. ^2-2

**2:3** For when he was young and in his prime for intercourse, by reason he nullified the frenzy^[Gk *he swatted the gadfly*] of the passions. ^2-3

**2:4** Reason is proved to rule not only over the frenzied urge of sexual desire but also over every desire.^[Or *all covetousness*] ^2-4

**2:5** Thus the law says, “You shall not covet your neighbor’s wife or anything that is your neighbor’s.” ^2-5

**2:6** In fact, since the law has told us not to covet, I could prove to you all the more that reason is able to control desires. Just so it is with the passions that hinder one from justice. ^2-6

**2:7** Otherwise how could it be that someone who is habitually a solitary eater, a glutton, or even a drunkard can learn a better way, unless reason is clearly lord of the passions? ^2-7

**2:8** Thus, as soon as one adopts a way of life in accordance with the law, even though a lover of money, one is forced to act contrary to natural ways and to lend without interest to those who plead for assistance and to cancel the debt when the seventh year arrives. ^2-8

**2:9** If one is greedy, one is ruled by the law through reason so that one neither gleans the harvest nor gathers the last grapes from the vineyard. In other matters we can recognize that reason rules the passions. ^2-9

**2:10** For the law prevails even over affection for parents, so that virtue is not abandoned for their sakes. ^2-10

**2:11** It overrules love for one’s wife so that one rebukes her when she breaks the law. ^2-11

**2:12** It overrules love for children so that one punishes them for misdeeds. ^2-12

**2:13** It is sovereign over the relationship of friends so that one rebukes friends when they act wickedly. ^2-13

**2:14** Do not consider it paradoxical when reason, through the law, can prevail even over enmity. The fruit trees of the enemy are not cut down, but one preserves the property of enemies from marauders and helps raise up what has fallen.^[Or *the beasts that have fallen*] ^2-14

**2:15** It is evident that reason rules even^[Other ancient authorities read *through*] the more violent passions: lust for power, vanity, boasting, arrogance, and malice. ^2-15

**2:16** For the temperate mind repels all these malicious passions, just as it repels anger—for it is sovereign over even this. ^2-16

**2:17** When Moses was angry with Dathan and Abiram, he did nothing against them in anger but controlled his anger by reason. ^2-17

**2:18** For, as I have said, the temperate mind is able to get the better of the passions, to correct some and to render others powerless. ^2-18

**2:19** Why else did Jacob, our most wise father, censure the households of Simeon and Levi for their irrational slaughter of the entire tribe of the Shechemites, saying, “Cursed be their anger”? ^2-19

**2:20** For if reason could not control anger, he would not have spoken thus. ^2-20

**2:21** Now when God fashioned humans, he planted in them passions and inclinations, ^2-21

**2:22** but at the same time he enthroned the mind among the senses as a sacred governor over them all. ^2-22

**2:23** To the mind he gave the law, and one who lives subject to this will rule a kingdom that is temperate, just, good, and courageous. ^2-23

**2:24** How is it then, one might say, that if reason is master of the passions, it does not control forgetfulness and ignorance? ^2-24
