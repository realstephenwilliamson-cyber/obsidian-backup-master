## Chapter 38

**38:1** In those days Hezekiah fell dangerously ill. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came and said to him, “Thus said the LORD: Set your affairs in order, for you are going to die; you will not get well.” ^38-1

**38:2** Thereupon Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD. ^38-2

**38:3** “Please, O LORD,” he said, “remember how I have walked before You sincerely and wholeheartedly, and have done what is pleasing to You.” And Hezekiah wept profusely. ^38-3

**38:4** Then the word of the LORD came to Isaiah: ^38-4

**38:5** “Go and tell Hezekiah: Thus said the LORD, the God of your father David: I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears. I hereby add fifteen years to your life. ^38-5

**38:6** I will also rescue you and this city from the hands of the king of Assyria. I will protect this city. ^38-6

**38:7** And this is the sign for you from the LORD that the LORD will do the thing that He has promised: ^38-7

**38:8** I am going to make the shadow on the steps, which has descended on the dial[^1] of Ahaz because of the sun, recede ten steps.” And the sun[’s shadow] receded ten steps, the same steps as it had descended. ^38-8

**38:9** A poem by King Hezekiah of Judah when he recovered from the illness he had suffered: ^38-9

**38:10** [^2]I had thought:  
I must depart in the middle of my days;  
I have been consigned to the gates of Sheol  
For the rest of my years. ^38-10

**38:11** I thought, I shall never see Yah,[^3]  
Yah in the land of the living,  
Or ever behold men again  
Among those who inhabit the earth. ^38-11

**38:12** My dwelling is pulled up and removed from me  
Like a tent of shepherds;  
My life is rolled up like a web  
And cut from the thrum.  
[^4]Only from daybreak to nightfall  
Was I kept whole, ^38-12

**38:13** Then it was as though a lion  
Were breaking all my bones;  
I cried out until morning.  
(Only from daybreak to nightfall  
Was I kept whole.) ^38-13

**38:14** I piped like a swift or a swallow,  
I moaned like a dove,  
As my eyes, all worn, looked to heaven:  
“My Lord, I am in straits;  
Be my surety!” ^38-14

**38:15** What can I say? He promised me,  
And He it is who has wrought it.  
[^5]All my sleep had fled  
Because of the bitterness of my soul. ^38-15

**38:16** My Lord, for all that and despite it  
My life-breath is revived;  
You have restored me to health and revived me. ^38-16

**38:17** Truly, it was for my own good  
That I had such great bitterness:  
You saved my life  
From the pit of destruction,  
For You have cast behind Your back  
All my offenses. ^38-17

**38:18** For it is not Sheol that praises You,  
Not [the Land of] Death that extols You;  
Nor do they who descend into the Pit  
Hope for Your grace. ^38-18

**38:19** The living, only the living  
Can give thanks to You  
As I do this day;  
Fathers[^6] relate to children  
Your acts of grace: ^38-19

**38:20** “[It has pleased] the LORD to deliver us,[^7]  
That is why we offer up music[^8]  
All the days of our lives  
At the House of the LORD.” ^38-20

**38:21** When Isaiah said, “Let them take a cake of figs and apply it to the rash, and he will recover,” ^38-21

**38:22** Hezekiah asked, “What will be the sign that I shall go up to the House of the LORD?” ^38-22

[^1]: Heb. “steps.” A model of a dial with steps has been discovered in Egypt.
[^2]: Meaning of verse uncertain. in part.
[^3]: I.e., visit His Temple. For “Yah” see 12.2; 26.4.
[^4]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^5]: Meaning of Heb. uncertain.
[^6]: Heb. singular.
[^7]: Heb. singular.
[^8]: Neginothai is a poetic form of neginoth.
