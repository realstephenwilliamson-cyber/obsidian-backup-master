# Synoptic parallels

[[Crosswalks]] · [[Bible]] · [[Synoptic reader]] · [[JANT Table - Parallel Texts|the JANT table]]

The passages that Matthew, Mark and Luke share, one row per pericope. Each reference opens the first verse in the BSB chapter note; [[Synoptic reader]] shows a row's passages side by side in the NRSVue, the BSB or the Douay-Rheims, and reads its rows from this table.

- **Rows with a number** are the rows of the table of parallel texts in the *Jewish Annotated New Testament* (2nd ed., pp. 776–777), as printed there and in that order: rows 1–27 the passages of Mark that Matthew and Luke share, rows 28–48 the “Q” passages of Matthew and Luke. References in brackets are the JANT’s own brackets (a partial parallel). The JANT gives no John column.
- **Rows with a letter** (8a, 8b …) split a JANT row that covers more than one NRSVue section of Mark into those sections, titled with the NRSVue headings. A Matthew or Luke passage is given in such a row only where the NRSVue’s own cross-references for those Mark verses point into the JANT row’s Matthew or Luke range; the passage is then the NRSVue section of Matthew or Luke holding the verse referred to, cut to the JANT range. Each passage found so was then read against the Mark section: a link of theme or wording between two different episodes is left out, and where the NRSVue section of Matthew or Luke is another episode but the verse referred to is a true parallel, only that verse is given, in brackets (both listed under the table). An empty cell in these rows does not mean there is no parallel: the JANT row above gives the whole range. Since 15 Oct 2026 (decision D31a) the Matthew and Luke cells this method left empty are filled from the parallel references the Berean Standard Bible prints under its section headings in Mark (BSB USFM, release v5.16, the source of the vault's BSB notes), cut to the JANT row's Matthew or Luke range, and used only where the row holds at least half of the BSB section (counted within the JANT row's Mark range); where that gives nothing, from the NRSVue section of Matthew or Luke that has the row's own heading, cut the same way. The *source* column says which. Two section rows still give Mark only: 17e and 19d.
- **Verse numbers** are the NRSVue’s and the BSB’s. The Douay-Rheims numbers a few chapters differently (see the notes under the table).
- **Where these rows leave the BSB's parallels** (24 Oct 2026; audit C-05, Stephen's decision 17 of 19 Oct): rows 14a, 19e, 19g, 23f–23g and 25a–25b take their Matthew or Luke range from the gospel text and the NRSVue's sections, not from the BSB's printed parallels, whose section breaks differ there. 14a adds Mt 10:1, the parallel of Mk 6:7; 19e runs to Mt 17:20, since 17:19–20 parallel Mk 9:28–29; 19g stops at Lk 9:48, leaving 9:49–50 to 19h; Lk 20:19, the parallel of Mk 12:12, belongs to 23f, not 23g; Lk 21:10–11, the parallel of Mk 13:8, belongs to 25a, not 25b.

## Notes on the table

**Read from the OCR of the printed page.** Some printed ranges look wrong against the text. All six were checked against the page images of the JANT (pp. 776–777) on 14 Oct 2026: the book prints them so. Since 15 Oct 2026 (Stephen's decision) the table links the passages the text supports, and the reader shows those; each such row keeps a *check* link to this list of what the JANT prints:

- Row 11 (Stilling the storm): Luke 8.22–35 runs into the next row’s Luke 8.26–56; the stilling of the storm is Luke 8.22–25.
- Row 13 (Rejection in Nazareth): Matthew 13.51–52 ends the parables; the rejection at Nazareth is Matthew 13.53–58.
- Row 20 (Sayings of Jesus): printed out of Mark’s order (between Mark 9 and 10); Luke 10.25–38 takes in the Good Samaritan and the first verse of Martha and Mary, and the row for Mark 11.1–12.37 gives Luke “(10.25–28)”.
- Row 29 (Temptation of Jesus): Luke 3.3–12 is John’s preaching; the temptation is Luke 4.3–12.
- Row 36 (Healing of a centurion’s servant): the centurion’s servant is Matthew 8.5–13; 8.14–15 is Peter’s mother-in-law.
- Row 45 (Parable of the marriage feast): the parable runs Matthew 22.1–14 and Luke 14.16–24; the JANT prints the shorter ranges here, but its own annotations give Matthew 22.1–14 and Luke 14.15–24.

**Section rows: passages left out or cut to the verse referred to.** The NRSVue cross-reference is given first, then the reason.

- 9d (A lamp under a bushel basket): cut to the verse referred to (Mk 4.25 → Mt 13.12): Mt 13.12 is the saying of Mk 4.25; the rest of its NRSVue section (Mt 13.10–13) answers Mk 4.10–12.
- 19f (Jesus again foretells his death and resurrection): left out Mt 16:21–23 (Mk 9.31 → Mt 16.21): Mt 16.21–23 is the first passion prediction, with Mk 8.31–33; the second, Mt 17.22–23, is not among the references.
- 22d (The request of James and John): left out Mt 19:16–30 (Mk 10.37 → Mt 19.28): the link is the saying about thrones (Mk 10.37, Mt 19.28); Mt 19.16–30 is the rich young man, and the request of James and John is Mt 20.20–28.
- 23a (Jesus’s triumphal entry into Jerusalem): cut to the verse referred to (Mk 11.1 → Mt 21.17; Mk 11.11 → Mt 21.17): Mk 11.11 and Mt 21.17 both end the day at Bethany; the rest of Mt 21.12–17 is the cleansing of the temple.
- 23c (Jesus cleanses the temple): left out Mt 21:33–46 (Mk 11.18 → Mt 21.46): the link is the leaders’ fear of the crowd (Mk 11.18, Mt 21.46); Mt 21.33–46 is the parable of the tenants (row 23f), and Matthew’s cleansing is Mt 21.12–13.
- 23f (The parable of the wicked tenants): left out Mt 22:15–22 (Mk 12.12 → Mt 22.22): the link is the closing phrase “they left him and went away” (Mk 12.12, Mt 22.22); Mt 22.15–22 is the question about taxes.
- 23i (The first commandment): cut to the verse referred to (Mk 12.34 → Mt 22.46): Mk 12.34 and Mt 22.46 close the questioning (no one dared to ask him more); the rest of Mt 22.41–46 is the question about David’s son.
- 23i (The first commandment): cut to the verse referred to (Mk 12.28 → Lk 20.39): Lk 20.39 (“Teacher, you have spoken well”) answers to the scribe of Mk 12.28, 32; the rest of Lk 20.27–40 is the question about the resurrection.
- 25c (The desolating sacrilege): left out Lk 21:7–19 (Mk 13.21 → Lk 21.8): the link is the warning against false claimants (Mk 13.21, Lk 21.8); Lk 21.7–19 runs with Mk 13.3–13, and Luke’s desolating sacrilege is Lk 21.20–24.
- 26i (Jesus before the council): left out Mt 26:1–5 (Mk 14.54 → Mt 26.3): the link is the high priest’s court (Mk 14.54, Mt 26.3); Mt 26.1–5 is the plot to kill Jesus.
- 26k (Jesus before Pilate): cut to the verse referred to (Mk 15.1 → Lk 22.66): Mk 15.1 and Lk 22.66 are the council meeting at daybreak; the rest of Lk 22.66–71 is the hearing before the council.

**Douay-Rheims numbering.** In these chapters the Douay-Rheims (which follows the Vulgate) ends on a different verse number from the NRSVue and the BSB, so some of its verse numbers point to other words: Matthew 17 (26 verses; NRSVue/BSB 27), Mark 4 (40; 41), Mark 8 (39; 38) and Mark 9 (49; 50), John 6 (72; 71). The reader shows the Douay-Rheims by its own numbers and says so in those chapters. It also has verses that the NRSVue and the BSB leave out (for example Mark 9:44 and 9:46); in those two the reader shows such a verse as “—”.
