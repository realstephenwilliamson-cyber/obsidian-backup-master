# The Scapegoat Reflex — Culprits vs Conditions

> [!abstract] Definition
> The recurrent civilizational impulse to blame complex systemic collapses on visible, named villains (*culprits*) rather than analyzing the fragile, interconnected infrastructural and ecological networks (*conditions*) that brought about the failure.

## The Bronze Age Paradigm
In [[Sea Peoples flowchart 2026-07-24|Let's Just Blame the Sea Peoples]] (Movement One, Piece 1):
- The Late Bronze Age collapse (~1200 BCE) saw the sudden fall of the Mycenaeans, Hittites, and Levantine city-states.
- Popular historiography blames the enigmatic "Sea Peoples."
- Modern archaeology (Eric Cline, *1177 B.C.*) demonstrates that the Sea Peoples were as much a symptom of a systemic megadrought, famine, trade fragility, and internal revolt as a cause. Blaming them is a scapegoat reflex.

## Modern Application
The same reflex governs contemporary discourse: blaming foreign hackers, algorithms, or individual politicians for democratic crisis, while ignoring the systemic erosion of epistemic trust, media architecture, and economic fragility.
