## Mūla

यस्येच्छातः सत्त्वादिगुणशरीरा विसृजति रुद्राणी ।
अनुकल्पो रुद्राण्या वेदी तत्रेज्यतेऽनुकल्पेन ॥ ^mula

yasyecchātaḥ sattvādiguṇaśarīrā visṛjati rudrāṇī |
anukalpo rudrāṇyā vedī tatrejyate'nukalpena || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. V
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 05 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v05 08 - Chapter Eight, part 6#^pg295|p. 277]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 05.pdf#page=295>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 8.270cd–271ab.
>
> Desiring here to withdraw (the created order into himself), (Śrīkanṭha) first penetrates (samāviṣya) into (the being of) the four-faced (Brahmā), who is born from the lotus (of Viṣṇu’s navel). Then, having burnt the four worlds (to ashes), he creates the worlds of living beings again. By his will, Rudrāṇī, whose body is the qualities, sattva and the rest, emits (the universe). (The goddess) Vedī, (who personifies the sacrificial altar), represents (anukalpa) Rudrāṇī. There the gods Indra, Upendra and Brahmā, perceiving that along with the Gandharvas, Yakṣas, Rākṣas, fathers and Munis, sacrifice in accord with the procedure (anukalpenay to Paśupati. Accordingly, the sacrifices are of various sorts. (with 8.269–271)
