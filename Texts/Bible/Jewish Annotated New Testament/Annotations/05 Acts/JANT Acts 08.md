# Acts 8 (JANT notes)

### 8.1–3: Church developments

*[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-1|Text]], p. 239* · Stephen’s death marks a narrative turning point. For the first time the church experiences widespread opposition, which drives followers from Judea and helps to expand the gospel. The paired themes of persecution and expansion become a motif that dominates the rest of the work. While some Jews could be openly hostile toward Jesus’ followers (Paul is a good example, cf. [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]), Luke’s unmistakable editorial hand calls for an appreciation of these stories for their literary and theological value, and not as a record of historical events. ^8-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-1|1]]:** *Judea and Samaria*, see 1.8. The stories of Philip and Peter in chs 8–10 dramatize the expansion. ^8-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-3|3]]:** Paul reports his persecution of the church ([[1 Corinthians 15#^15-9|1 Cor 15.9]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]],[[Galatians 01#^1-23|23]]). ^8-3

### 8.4–25: Preaching in Samaria

*[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-4|Text]], p. 239* · Samaritans, like Jews, worshipped YHWH and recognized the Pentateuch as holy scripture, but they identified Mt. Gerizim, rather than Jerusalem, as their religious center where a sanctuary stood until its destruction by John Hyrcanus I in 128 BCE (Josephus, *Ant.* 13.255). Samaritans appear in Luke’s Gospel as faithful to Jesus’ teaching ([[Luke 10#^10-33|Lk 10.33]]; [[Luke 17#^17-16|17.16]]; cf. 9.52). ^8-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-4|4]]:** *Scattered*, like Jews in the Diaspora. ^8-4-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-5|5]]:** *Philip*, one of the seven, see 6.5. *City of Samaria*, no exact location is given; Luke may have had in mind Sebaste, formerly the city of Samaria (today Sebastiya), once the capital of the kingdom of Israel, but rebuilt and renamed by Herod in honor of the emperor Augustus (Josephus, *J.W.* 1.403; *Ant.* 15.217, 292–98). ^8-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-6|6–7]]:** Exorcisms and healings, see 3.7; 5.12; 9.34; 14.18; 16.18. ^8-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-9|8.9–25]]:** **Simon.** Luke distinguishes between the false “great power” of Simon (v 10), with the true great powers, “dunameis” (translated as “miracles”) of Philip (v 13). Christians act as the legitimate conveyers of divine power in contrast to those who practice magic (13.6–11; 16.16–24; 19.13–20,23–40). ^8-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-9|9]]:** *Practiced magic*, hence the name often given as Simon “Magus,” indicating a member of the Persian priestly class (see [[Matthew 02#^2-1|Mt 2.1–12]]), but sometimes in pagan, Jewish, and Christian writings carrying a pejorative connotation. ^8-9-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-10|10]]:** *Power of God that is called Great,* the phrasing indicates that the crowd takes Simon to be a god. ^8-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-12|12]]:** *Kingdom of God*, in Acts, the term refers to the content of Christian preaching; see 19.8; 20.25; 28.23,31. ^8-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-14|14]]:** Jesus’ instructions concerning Samaria (1.8) are fulfilled. The apostles continue to oversee the church’s expansion (15.1–21). **15–16**: The apostles enjoy a unique command of and relation with the Holy Spirit (cf. 10.44; 13.2; 16.6; 19.6). ^8-14

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-18|18–19]]:** Simon’s offer is the origin of the term “simony,” meaning an attempt to buy a church office. ^8-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-23|23]]:** *Gall of bitterness*, the allusion to [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^29-17|Deut 29.18]] \[HB 29.17\] implies that Simon is guilty of serving other gods. ^8-23

### 8.26–40: The Ethiopian official

*[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-26|Text]], p. 240* ^8-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-26|26]]:** *Angel of the Lord*, God, through agents such as angels and the Spirit, directs the expansion of the church (10.19–20; 13.4; 16.6,7). ^8-26-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-27|27]]:** *Ethiopian eunuch*, Ethiopia evokes a distant land ([[Ezekiel 29#^29-10|Ezek 29.10]]; Homer, *Od.* 1.23). *Candace*, royal title for *queen of the Ethiopians* rather than a personal name. *Jerusalem to worship*, the form of worship is not indicated. Varying attitudes toward eunuchs appear in the Tanakh and later Jewish traditions. Eunuchs were excluded from participation in Temple rituals ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^21-20|Lev 21.20]]) and entrance into the community of Israel ([[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^23-2|Deut 23.1]]; Josephus, *Ant.* 4.290–91). Rabbinic law prohibits eunuchs from serving in the Sanhedrin ([[Sanhedrin 36#36b|*b. Sanh.* 36b]]). In contrast, Isaiah speaks of eunuchs who observe the covenant as being honored by God ([[Isaiah 56#^56-5|Isa 56.5]]), persons whom God will admit to the Temple and accept their offerings ([[Isaiah 56#^56-7|Isa 56.7]]). The deuterocanonical Wisdom of Solomon accords eunuchs special favor, including a place in the Temple ([[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 03#^3-14|Wis 3.14]]), and rabbinic traditions accept eunuchs serving as priests (*m. Yebam.* 8.6). Gentiles interested in Judaism appear often in Acts (10.2; 13.16; 16.14; 18.7). ^8-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-28|28]]:** *Isaiah*, often quoted in Luke-Acts ([[Luke 03#^3-4|Lk 3.4]]; [[Luke 04#^4-17|4.17]]; [[Acts 28#^28-25|Acts 28.25]]) or alluded to (2.3; 3.13-14; 7.58; 8.26). ^8-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-29|29]]:** *The Spirit*, see 8.39; 10.19; 11.12; 13.2,4; 15.28; 16.6,7; 21.4,11; 28.25. ^8-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-30|30]]:** *Heard him*, reading was typically aloud. ^8-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-32|32–33]]:** See [[Isaiah 53#^53-7|Isa 53.7–8]]. ^8-32

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-35|35]]:** See [[Luke 24#^24-27|Lk 24.27]]. For Luke, although Jews and Christians possess the same sacred text, only Christians interpret it correctly. Qumran’s Teacher of Righteousness expresses a similar plural b Gk *after they* c Or *go at noon* d Other ancient authorities add all or most of verse 37, *And Philip said, “If you believe with all your heart, you may.” And he replied, “I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.”* view of privileged reading in 1QpHab. ^8-35

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-39|39]]:** *Snatched . . . away,* signaling divine favor (see Elijah, [[II Kings 02#^2-1|2 Kings 2.1–11]]; [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-58|1 Macc 2.58]]). ^8-39

**[[NRSVue Acts 08#^8-40|40]]:** *Azotus*, between Gaza and Joppa; *Caesarea*, Caesarea Maritima, the major harbor city built by Herod the Great and the seat of Roman administration in the province of Judea. ^8-40
