### 4.1–42: The Samaritan woman

*[[NRSVue John 04#^4-1|Text]], p. 183* ^4-1

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-2|2]]:** *Not Jesus . . . who baptized*, this contradicts 3.22 which states that Jesus did baptize. This contradiction may be evidence of the complex process of composition involving more than one writer. ^4-2

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-4|4]]:** *Had to go* . . . *Samaria*, between Judea and Galilee. The main route from Judea to Galilee led through Samaria, though an alternate route via the Jordan valley was possible. Samaritans are descendants of the remnant of the ten tribes associated with the Northern Kingdom of Israel who were not deported when the Kingdom fell in 722 BCE and Babylonians and Medians brought to the region by the Assyrian conquerors (see [[II Kings 17#^17-24|2 Kings 17.24–41]]). Tension between Samaritans and the Jews who returned from Babylonian exile was created in part by the Samaritans’ opposition to the rebuilding of the Temple ([[Ezra 04#^4-6|Ezra 4.6–24]]). ^4-4

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-5|5]]:** *Sychar*, the site is not known; some commentators believe that the reference should be to Shechem; *Jacob had given to . . . Joseph*, [[Joshua 24#^24-32|Josh 24.32]]. ^4-5

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-6|6]]:** *Well*, in biblical stories, encounters between men and women at wells often result in marriage ([[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^29-10|Gen 29.10–20]]; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-16|Ex 2.16–21]]). *Noon*, the timing contrasts the woman favorably with Nicodemus, who came at night (3.2). ^4-6

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-7|7]]:** *Give me a drink*, see 19.28, at the crucifixion, also at noon. ^4-7

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-9|9]]:** *Do not share*, in the Gospel writer’s view, Jews and Samaritans would not eat together. ^4-9

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-10|10]]:** *Living water*, flowing water, as a spring or river ([[Torah 39 - Chukat#^19-17|Num 19.17]]) and refreshing ([[Jeremiah 02#^2-14|Jer 2.14]]; [[Jeremiah 17#^17-13|17.13]]). ^4-10

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-11|11]]:** Paralleling Nicodemus (3.4), the woman takes Jesus literally and therefore misunderstands him. ^4-11

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-12|12]]:** *Greater than* . . . *Jacob*, Johannine irony, for the Gospel would certainly assert that Jesus is greater than Jacob. ^4-12

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-13|13]]:** *Everyone who drinks,* in [[LXX Sirach 24#^24-21|Sir 24.21]], Wisdom promises that those who taste wisdom will thirst for more. ^4-13

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-18|18]]:** *Five husbands*, this detail seems to be important primarily to showcase Jesus’ prophetic ability. Symbolic interpretations (five husbands as the five books of Moses for example) are not convincing. ^4-18

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-19|19]]:** *Prophet*, not one who predicts the future but one with God-given insight. ^4-19

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-20|20]]:** *This mountain*, Mount Gerizim, the Samaritans’ holy place (see [[LXX 2 Maccabees 06#^6-2|2 Macc 6.2]]). ^4-20

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-21|21]]:** *Nor in Jerusalem*, a prophecy of a future time when (as the Gospel writer hopes) all worship will be concentrated on Jesus as the Son of God. In the context of the Gospel, this is an example of replacement theology (Jesus replaces the Temple); this reference is another indication of the Gospel’s post-70 date (see 2.20n.; see also 2.21). ^4-21

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-22|22]]:** *Salvation is from the Jews*, the Gospel’s only unequivocally positive statement about Jews; though he is later accused of being a Samaritan (8.48), Jesus here may be indirectly affirming his own Jewish origins, and stating that salvation — that is, he as Messiah and Son of God — is from or of the Jews. *From*, may imply “arising among” or “originating in.” ^4-22

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-25|25]]:** *Messiah*, the Samaritans believed in the return of a prophet like Moses ([[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^18-15|Deut 18.15]]) whom they refer to as the Taheb (*Memar Marqa*). ^4-25

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-26|26]]:** *I am*, John’s Jesus uses the emphatic “I am” (Gk “ego eimi”) frequently in his discourses (4.26; 6.20,35,41,48,51; 8.12,18,23\[twice\],24,28,58; 10.7,9,11,14; 11.25; 13.19; 14.6; 15.1,5; 18.5,6,8). In several cases (4.26; 6.20; 8.24,28,58; 13.19; 18.5,6,8) the phrase is used without a predicate, recalling God’s emphatic self-revelation to Moses at the burning bush ([[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-14|Ex 3.14]]: “I AM WHO I AM.” He said further, “Thus you shall say to the Israelites, ‘I AM has sent me to you’ ”). ^4-26

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-27|27]]:** *Speaking with a woman*, implying that they were not accustomed to seeing Jesus speak with women. While some Second Temple and rabbinic texts warn against speaking with a woman ([[LXX Sirach 09#^9-1|Sir 9.1–9]]; *m. Avot* 1.5), other sources indicate that men, including teachers, did so (e.g., *b. Eruv*. 53b in which Rabbi Yosi the Galilean initiates a conversation with a learned woman named Beruriah). In speaking with a woman, John’s Jesus is simply behaving according to the Jewish norms of his time. In Jn 11, no eyebrows are raised when Jesus speaks to both Mary and Martha. ^4-27

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-29|29]]:** *Come and see*, the woman is depicted as an apostle who brings others to Jesus (see 1.41,45). ^4-29

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-32|32]]:** *Food to eat*, the disciples have brought Jesus food; he responds to them as he did to the Samaritan woman, by changing the terms of the conversation from simple nourishment (food, water) 22 is plural b Gk *I am* c Or *the Christ* which they might provide to him, to the spiritual nourishment which he can provide to them. ^4-32

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-35|35]]:** Perhaps a proverbial saying; see v 37. *Harvesting*, harvest imagery may refer to the encounter with the Samaritans, resulting in their faith, recounted in 4.39–42. ^4-35

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-36|36–38]]:** *Sower . . . reaper*, ordinarily those who sow and those who reap are the same, but here the reapers (perhaps those spreading the gospel) are, like the Israelites in [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^6-10|Deut 6.10–11]], benefiting from the labors of their predecessors, or those whom they displaced. The sower is Jesus, the reapers, the disciples, perhaps reflecting a post-Easter perspective. *Wages*, the harvest of believers; see [[Matthew 09#^9-37|Mt 9.37–38]]. ^4-36

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-41|41]]:** *His word*, his teaching, taking place in person, as opposed to the testimony of the Samaritan woman. ^4-41

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-42|42]]:** *Savior* (Gk “soter,” in LXX for Heb “yesha”), basis for Yeshua (Jesus); see [[Matthew 01#^1-21|Mt 1.21]]; the word could mean “deliverer” (from enemies) and was a common title for rulers. The Samaritan term is “Taheb” which is translated as the “restorer,” the “returning one,” or the “repentant.” ^4-42

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-43|4.43–54]]:** **Second sign: healing of the official’s son** ([[Matthew 08#^8-5|Mt 8.5–13]]; [[Luke 07#^7-1|Lk 7.1–10]]). ^4-43

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-44|44]]:** See [[Mark 06#^6-4|Mk 6.4]]; [[Luke 04#^4-24|Lk 4.24]]. *Prophet has no honor* ([[Matthew 13#^13-53|Mt 13.53–58]]; [[Mark 06#^6-1|Mk 6.1–6]]; [[Luke 04#^4-22|Lk 4.22–24]] and *Gos. Thom*. 31), in the Johannine context, the proverb contrasts the generally warm reception Jesus receives in Galilee with the suspicion that his activities arouse in Judea. ^4-44

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-45|45]]:** *Galileans*, a geographical designation; since they were *at the festival* in *Jerusalem* they are Jews. **46**: *Cana*, see 2.1–11. *Royal official*, likely serving Herod Antipas, tetrarch of Galilee 4 BCE–39 CE. ^4-45

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-48|48]]:** *You*, is plural. *Signs and wonders*, if “signs” only produce awe, and not faith, they have failed. ^4-48

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-50|50]]:** *Your son will live*, see [[I Kings 17#^17-23|1 Kings 17.23]] (Elijah) and [[II Kings 08#^8-9|2 Kings 8.9]] (Elisha). ^4-50

**[[NRSVue John 04#^4-54|54]]:** *Second sign*, referring either to the second of a series of signs perhaps taken from an earlier signs-source (see Introduction), or else the second sign that took place in Cana, the turning of water into wine being the first one (2.1–11). *36The reaper is receiving* b Both instances of the Greek word for *you* in this verse are plural ^4-54
