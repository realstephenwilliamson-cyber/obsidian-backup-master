# Mei HaShiloach I - Korach

← [[Mei HaShiloach I - Sh'lach]] · [[Mei HaShiloach]] · [[Mei HaShiloach I - Chukat]] →

*Volume I · Numbers · Korach* · Torah: [[Torah 38 - Korach]] · [[Mei HaShiloach]]

## Teaching 1

*“And Korach took .…”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-1|Bamidbar, 16:1]])  
The Midrash (in *Bamidbar* Rabba, *Korach*, 18:17) tells us, “he saw the great chain of generations that would come out of him.” Since he saw this he became arrogant, daring to believe that surely he was the greatest of God’s servants. Of this it is said ([[Proverbs 27#^27-1|Mishlei, 27:1]]), “Do not boast of tomorrow, for you don’t know what the day may bring forth.” Even if a man understands that has hope (promise of future rewards or success), he is to beware of putting his trust in this for he does not know how God may conduct Himself in any present moment.

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-1|Numbers 16:1]]

## Teaching 2

*“And Korach took .…”*  
It is said in the Midrash (*Bamidbar* Rabba, *Korach*, 18:2), “Why does the passage about Korach follow immediately after the section on *tsitsit*? Because Korach took a *talit* made entirely of *techelet*,[^1] came before Moshe, and asked, ‘does this *talit* require *tsitsit* or not?’”[^2]*Techelet* teaches of the fear of God, and Korach asserted that the fear of God was something completely revealed to him at all times. He understood that everything comes from God, even the fear of God.[^3] If so, how could anyone ever do anything against God’s will? Since all will and all action come from Him, may He be blessed, how could anyone do something not according to His will? For this reason Korach asserted that a *talit* made entirely of *tchelet* (i.e., constantly conscious of the fear of God) would be exempt from *tsitsit*, because *tsitsit* teaches of the fear of God. Yet in truth, God’s will as it exists in this world depends on how each one sees it. This relates to what is said in the Gemara (*Chagiga*, 13b), “The prophet Yechezkel prayed on the face of the ox [on the chariot], and it turned into a cherub.”[^4]  
“Ox” teaches of wisdom that is greatly refined, and at the deepest level everything really does come from God, our free choice is no more consequential as a peel of garlic, only according to one’s particular state of mind. God hid His way from mankind because He desires that we serve Him, and if everything (in God’s consciousness) were clear to us, we would not experience the desire to serve Him. Because of this Yechezkel prayed and changed the face to a cherub, that God’s way would be hidden, and it would appear to mankind that we are able to make choices. In this way the desire to serve Him would come to our hearts. This is the meaning of (ibid.) “the cherub is a small face.” [^5], [^6]

**Torah:** [[Torah 37 - Sh'lach#^15-38|Numbers 15:38]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-1|Numbers 16:1]]

## Teaching 3

*“… and why do you raise yourselves above the community of God?”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Bamidbar, 16:3]])  
Here Korach’s assertion was that there is no favoritism in Israel, that God doesn’t elevate one above the other, for God dwells among them all equally. This is similar to what is said in the Midrash (Gemara *Ta’anit*, 31a), “In the future God will make a circle dance of the righteous, and all will point to God saying, ‘this is our God whom we have hoped for.’” A circle dance, for all points in a circle are equidistant from the center, no one closer than another. Korach asserted that now it is also like this, yet concerning this King Shlomo said ([[Proverbs 20#^20-26|Mishlei, 20:26]]), “a wise king scatters the wicked, returning the wheel over them.” This means, concerning the “crown”[^7] that God gave them, that is, the crown of the *Levi’im* that gave the sons of Levi an exalted status, concerning this, “he returns the wheel over them.” That means, He answers them (return, *meishiv* in Hebrew, also means answers), “when I gave you an elevated status as *Levi’im* you didn’t refuse it and claim that there is no one in Israel who receives special status above another.”

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Numbers 16:3]]

## Teaching 4

*“And Korach took .…”*  
This relates to “why did your heart take you away …” ([[Job 15#^15-12|Job, 15:12]]). That is, that his heart took him. Concerning this Moshe our teacher cried out in prayer (*Tehilim*, [[Psalms 090#^90-1|90:1]]), “God, you have been our dwelling place in every generation. Before the birth of the mountains, and the formation of the earth …” Mountains signify powers and desires. On this he prayed to God, for He is the one who gives power and the desire to act, thus if one cannot overcome his desire he is not culpable, for all his power comes from God. Concerning this he said, “teach us to number our days,” which is now interpreted (*yameynu* is days, *yamIno* is power) as asking God to give us the power to overcome our desires, and not to receive desires greater than the power to control it. Rather, our desires should be on the level of our restraining power. “When will you Return, God, and console your servant?” In other words, that God should grant Korach the understanding that he can use to overpower his desire (for honor or control).

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-1|Numbers 16:1]]

## Teaching 5

*“For all the congregation is holy and God is among them .…”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Bamidbar, 16:3]])  
This relates to the verse ([[Proverbs 12#^12-19|Mishlei, 12:19]]), “a lip of truth shall be established forever, but a lying tongue is for but a moment.” Those who spoke truth will endure forever, and the lies of their mouth will be immediately nullified. From this verse they (the sons of Korach) will be rectified in the future. Because King David established the lineage of the sons of Korach to their father (in *Tehilim* 44–49), they were returned to his community. If they would not derive any good from being called in their father’s name, then they would not have been specifically related to this evil, God forbid. As it is said in the Gemara (*Shabbat* [[Shabbat 55#55b|55b]] on another matter), “could it be that he sinned and still the Scriptures relate his progeny?” This is one of the thirteen principles with which the Torah is expounded, “everything that was in the general principle, but was taken out … you may not return it to its general principle until the verse does it explicitly.” Here King David returned them to his community (*klal*, also general principle) explicitly. This is seen specifically in the enactment of the Ari”zal to say the chapter of *Tehilim* is “a song of the sons of Korach” before blowing the *shofar* on Rosh Hashanah, a custom that has been accepted by all the communities of Israel.[^8]

**Torah:** [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-12|Leviticus 22:12]], [[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-13|Leviticus 22:13]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Numbers 16:3]]

## Teaching 6

*“Why do you raise yourselves above the congregation of God?”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Bamidbar, 16:3]])  
It is written (Yesahya, 51:15), “And I will place my word in your mouth, and I will cover you in the shadow of My hand.” There is a good and precious point in every soul of Israel specifically given by God. However, in this world, that point is dressed in a garment that makes it appear to be the opposite of its true nature, and this is called “covered in the shadow of My hand.” It is also this way with precious souls, for God made garments which cause them to look like the opposite of their preciousness. It is like this with Moshe Rabeynu, of whom the Torah testifies that he was “the most humble of all men,” and this was covered with a garment that made the opposite seem true. Therefore they claimed that he wanted to raise himself above the rest.  
This is also the case with David haMelech, whose essential attribute was the betterment of all of Israel, as is written in the Midrash Sochar Tov (*Tehilim* 1), “the one who shall request Will is David haMelech, who loved Israel.” Of him it was written (*Devarim*, [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-15|17:15]]), “From among your brethren appoint a king over yourselves,” (“from among your brethren”) meaning one who has love for the entire community of Israel. On the surface it seemed that David was the opposite, as it is written (Gemara, *Bereshit* Rabba, 63:11), “at the time Shmuel haNavi saw the red-haired David he feared that he might be one who would shed blood, so God answered him, ‘see how he is with beautiful eyes.’”[^9]

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-3|Numbers 16:3]], [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^17-15|Deuteronomy 17:15]]

## Teaching 7

*“And Moshe said to Korach, you and all your congregation were [*hayu*] before Hashem .…”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-15|Bamidbar, 16:15]])  
“*Hayu*.” With these words were they cast down into hell. These three letters (of “were,” *hayu, Hei Yud Vav*) form a holy name that signifies the “seal of that which is below,” as is written in the *Sefer Yetsira*,[^10] the “Book of Creation,” “the direction below is sealed with Hei Yud Vav.” When Moshe saw that their Divine decree had been sealed (to cast them below), he chose these words, “were (*hayu*) before God.”

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-15|Numbers 16:15]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^16-16|Numbers 16:16]]

## Teaching 8

*“… you have killed the people of God.”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-6|Bamidbar, 17:6]])  
Truly, the people knew that this act had come from God, yet their claim was God visited upon them because Moshe and Aharon hated them. This is why Moshe Rabeynu said to Aharon, “take the incense censer [to stop the plague],” to show the people God’s great love for them.[^11]

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-6|Numbers 17:6]]

## Teaching 9

*“The dead in the plague were fourteen thousand and seven hundred, besides those that died in the matter of Korach.”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-14|Bamidbar, 17:14]])  
Here we find that whenever *Kedusha* (sanctity) descends into the world, it does not come “for free,” but comes at the great cost of lives of Israel. For instance, before David haMelech merited knowing the place of the Mikdash (Holy Temple in Jerusalem), many lives were lost and many tragedies suffered. Thus it was before the world merited the *birrurim* (clarifications) involved in recognizing the soul of Aharon haCohen, for this was not made entirely clear until after the controversy of the staves, as is known (see *Bamidbar*, [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-16|17:16–26]]). This was also acquired at a great price, namely 15,000 lives, for 14,700 died in the plague, 250 were swallowed by the earth in the controversy of Korach, along with fifty people close to Datan, Aviram, and Korach. This 15,000 is hinted at in the verses in *Bereshit* concerning the creation of light. From the verse, “and God said, let there be light” (verse 3) until the completion of light (the end of verse 5), we find the letter *aleph* fifteen times.[^12] This hints that before the light of Aharon haCohen was made completely clear in this world, fifteen thousand lives of Israel had to be lost. Then afterwards, the light of Aharon haCohen emerged clear and refined.[^13]

**Torah:** [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-3|Genesis 1:3]], [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^1-5|Genesis 1:5]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-14|Numbers 17:14]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-16|Numbers 17:16–26]]

## Teaching 10

*“And God said to Moshe … take from them a staff, a staff from each father’s house .…”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-16|Bamidbar, 17:16]])  
Why did God command them to take staves after the Korach affair? The staff (or branch) hints at life as is known, as it is written ([[Zechariah 08#^8-4|Zechariah, 8:4]]), “every man with his staff in his hand from many days” (long life). God commanded them to take staves for in the future God will show each one that he will arrive at his place and true portion of eternal life, and will not desire the portion of his neighbor. For before it will be clear to each one what his portion is, he will want the portion of his fellow. It was like this in the controversy of Korach, as he longed for the portion of Aharon haCohen. This is the meaning of the staves, that God showed each and every one his place, that everyone has a good place, and that no one would complain about (be jealous of) the portion of Aharon. This is seen in the Aramaic translation of Onkelos, “and they saw, and each man took his staff.” “Saw” is translated as “and he made it known.”[^14]  
That is, that they understood and established in the clarity of the situation, recognizing the depth of the root and source of each one. Even though it only says that Aharon’s staff blossomed, really all the staves blossomed, yet Aharon’s staff went on to give fruit. The portion of Aharon is the completeness of every action in this world, and he would not have done anything that would have to go through another process of purification. Not so with the other tribes, whose actions achieve this purification only after time. This is so even though there are other tribes whose preciousness is greater than that of *Kehuna*, priesthood, like the tribe of Yehuda (from which grows King David’s line). Nonetheless, their completion takes time, and this was once against the opinion of Aharon, who at first considered himself the greatest, and this troubled him greatly.  
Therefore God had Israel give him the twenty-four gifts of *Kehuna*, to show him that all the actions of Israel have to be in this world according to the level of Aharon haCohen, for in this world God chooses the action in its completeness. On this it is written in the *Sefer Yetsira*, “the Book of Creation,”[^15] that there are twelve permutations of the four letters of God’s name (each tribe associated with one permutation). The permutation for Aharon is in its natural and complete order, *Yud Hei Vav Hei*, for this is the name that rules in this world.

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-16|Numbers 17:16]], [[Torah 38 - Korach#^17-17|Numbers 17:17]]

## Teaching 11

*“And the Levites shall perform the service .…”* ([[Torah 38 - Korach#^18-23|Bamidbar, 18:23]])  
It is written in the Holy Zohar (*Bamidbar*, [[Zohar 9.06 - Parashat Qorah#^z3-178b|178b]]), “the Levite completes the left side by means of his service.” At a time when man’s heart is overcome with lust, and he overpowers it with all his strength and prevents himself from it, then he succeeds in completing himself by filling the deficiency that he has carried from his birth. For everyone is born with a deficiency that he must fix. This is why the prophet Yeshayahu said ([[Isaiah 48#^48-8|Yeshaya, 48:8]]), “you are called a criminal from the womb.” This is called the “left side,” which God leaves for man to fix.

**Torah:** [[Torah 38 - Korach#^18-23|Numbers 18:23]]
