## Mūla

यज्ज्ञेयमात्रं तद्बीजं यद्योगाद्बीजता स्वरे ।
तस्य बीजस्य सैवोक्ता विसिसृक्षा य उद्भवः ।
यतो ग्राह्यमिदं भास्यद्भिन्नकल्पं चिदात्मनः ॥ ^mula

yajjñeyamātraṃ tadbījaṃ yadyogādbījatā svare |
tasya bījasya saivoktā visisṛkṣā ya udbhavaḥ |
yato grāhyamidaṃ bhāsyadbhinnakalpaṃ cidātmanaḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. II
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 02 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v02 07 - Chapter Three, part 4#^pg204|p. 188]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 02.pdf#page=204>))
>
> The seed (bīja) is just (pure) objectivity alone (jñeyamātra) (of Śiva consciousness that needs be known), which is not (yet) differentiated into the (succeeding) forms of consciousness beginning with ‘sovereignty’ (īṣaṇā), (symbolized by the letter Ī). The seed state (bījatā) is present in the vowels by virtue (of their) association (yoga) with this (pure, inner objectivity). (with 3.85cd–86ab)
>
> The intent to emit (visisṛkṣā) mentioned previously is the outpouring (udbhava) of that seed, due to which this object of knowledge (grāhya) (ultimately) manifests as if it were separate from the conscious nature. This is arousal (kṣobha). The act of arousing (kṣobhaṇā) is that which causes the consciousness of other perceivers who are silent (tūṣṇīṁbhūta) (in the sense that their attention is not directed outside themselves) to be forcefully (haṭhāt) (drawn away and) fall from the detached (transcendental) aspect (audāsīnyāṁśa) of consciousness by its (inherent) power. (with 3.86–87)
