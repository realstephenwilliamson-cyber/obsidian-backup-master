# Philippians 3 (JANT notes)

Text: [[NRSVue Philippians 03|NRSVue]] · [[Philippians 03|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT Philippians - Introduction]]

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-1|3.1a]]:** *Finally*, seen by some to indicate that one letter ended here (see 4.8; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-11|2 Cor 13.11]]); better gauged as transitional toward subject matter not yet taken up (see [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-1|1 Thess 4.1]]; [[2 Thessalonians 03#^3-1|2 Thess 3.1]]). ^3-1

### 3.1b–11: Warnings against false teachers

*[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-1|Text]], p. 403* ^3-1-2

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-1|1b]]:** *The same things*, of which Paul had warned others (see [[Galatians 06#^6-12|Gal 6.12–15]]) he now warns the Philippians—most likely regarding Gentile-Christian Judaizers who may try to enter Philippi to urge its Gentile believers to submit to circumcision. ^3-1-3

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-2|2]]:** *Dogs* (see “Beware of the Dogs,” p. 404). *Beware . . .  those who mutilate*, referring to Gentile circumcision. ^3-2

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-3|3]]:** *We . . . are the circumcision*, suggests that the Philippians themselves are Gentiles. This verse essentially summarizes the core of Paul’s epistle to the Galatians. See [[Romans 02#^2-28|Rom 2.28–29]], echoing [[Torah 51 - Nitzavim#^30-6|Deut 30.6]]. ^3-3

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-4|4]]:** *I have more*, Paul is a truer son of Israel than are his opponents, so he can not only refute but preempt their contention that observing the Law will bring salvation from bondage to sin. ^3-4

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-5|5]]:** *Circumcised on the eighth day*, not in his maturity (as with male proselytes). *Of the people of Israel*, not grafted in (see [[Romans 11#^11-17|Rom 11.17]]). *Of the tribe of Benjamin*, the source of Israel’s first king (Saul) and the sole Northern tribe loyal to Judah when the Northern Kingdom broke away ([[I Kings 12#^12-21|1 Kings 12.21–23]]). *Hebrew of . . . Hebrews*, from Hebrew parentage (unlike proselytes). *Pharisee*, more expert in the Law than his opponents (see “Pharisees,” p. 619). ^3-5

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-6|6]]:** *Persecutor*, expressive of Paul’s zeal before his conversion (see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-9|1 Cor 15.9]]; [[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13]]). *Blameless*, upstaging Paul’s opponents who champion the Law instead of faith; that Paul is a follower of Jesus cannot be ascribed to any incapacity on his part to fulfill Torah. ^3-6

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-11|11]]:** *If somehow*, expresses humility, not doubt. *Attain the resurrection*, to become accounted worthy of, and possessing the sure hope of, securing the reward of resurrection from the dead ([[Luke 20#^20-35|Lk 20.35]]; see [[1 Peter 01#^1-3|1 Pet 1.3]]) upon Christ’s return ([[1 Corinthians 15#^15-23|1 Cor 15.23]]; [[1 Thessalonians 04#^4-15|1 Thess 4.15]]). ^3-11

### 3.12–4.1: Confession and further exhortation

*[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-12|Text]], p. 404* ^3-12

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-18|18]]:** *Many live as enemies of the cross*, but there is no evidence that they are present in Philippi. ^3-18

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-19|19]]:** *Is the belly*, likely libertines pandering to sensual pleasures (see [[Romans 16#^16-18|Rom 16.18]]). ^3-19

**[[NRSVue Philippians 03#^3-21|21]]:** *Conformed to*, fashioned like. *Body of his glory*, spiritual, upon resurrection (see [[1 Corinthians 15#^15-44|1 Cor 15.44]]). ^3-21

> [!note] “Beware of the Dogs” (phil 3.2–9) *(p. 404)*
> The ancient world was generally unfamiliar with warm interactions of masters with domesticated varieties of “dog” (see [[Isaiah 56#^56-10|Isa 56.10–11]]), a term usually meant as insulting ([[Revelation 22#^22-15|Rev 22.15]]); see also Greek “cynics” (from *kyon*, *kynarion*, “dog”). Vicious or otherwise detestable, most dogs were uninhibit-edly unclean in their shameful public scavenging ([[II Kings 09#^9-10|2 Kings 9.10]],[[II Kings 09#^9-36|36]]), so that terming anyone “dog” was derogatory ([[I Samuel 17#^17-43|1 Sam 17.43]]; [[I Samuel 24#^24-15|24.14]]; [[II Samuel 09#^9-8|2 Sam 9.8]]; [[II Samuel 16#^16-9|16.9]]; [[II Kings 08#^8-13|2 Kings 8.13]]; [[Matthew 07#^7-6|Mt 7.6]]; [[2 Peter 02#^2-21|2 Pet 2.21–22]]). Here Paul applies the epithet to adversaries from outside Philippi who *might* arrive, and different from earlier opponents ([[Philippians 01#^1-15|Phil 1.15–18]] and 1.28). They were either Jewish or Gentile followers of Jesus who were promoting adherence to the Law, and less likely Jewish missionaries bent on winning Gentile God-fearers (who loosely attached themselves to Judaism as quasi-converts, attending synagogue, worshipping the Jewish God, observing varying degrees of Jewish practices but without becoming Jewish converts by submitting to circumcision). Facing their adamancy that male Gentile followers of Jesus undergo circumcision (3.2–3), Paul sarcastically terms them mutilators (perhaps obliquely referring to the self-inflicted wounds by the prophets of Baal \[see 1 Kings 28\] or devotees of the Phrygian mystery goddess, Cybele, who would slash themselves amid religious frenzy; or a barbed suggestion that those promoting circumcision would mutilate their genitalia).
>
> Paul was wary due to experiences in other early churches where rival teachers promoted circumcision of Gentile men (see [[Galatians 02#^2-1|Gal 2.1–16]]; [[Galatians 05#^5-2|5.2–6]],[[Galatians 05#^5-12|12]]); likely also because of Philippi’s vulnerable short distance to the Egnatian Way—rendering this church susceptible to such subversives showing up. His bitter tirade reflects conviction that Gentile believers in Christ should not convert to Judaism lest they nullify their new freedom, thereby hindering God’s plans for Gentile salvation and clouding their perception that justification comes solely by the grace of God through the efficacy of the cross. If one could become righteous by one’s own works, Christ would not have needed to die ([[Galatians 02#^2-21|Gal 2.21b]]).
>
> Instead, the Philippians should heed Paul as expert in preempting his enemies—given his own consummate possession of the very privileges of which his adversaries boast (see 3.3–6nn.). Apprais-ing his impeccable privileges at their true value (namely, as less than nothing for paling before the gift of Christ), Paul gladly relinquished them in favor of the extreme gain of knowing Christ Jesus as Lord.
>
> Elsewhere ([[Romans 09#^9-4|Rom 9.4–5]]) Paul declares that “to them \[the Israelites\] belong the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the worship, and the promises; . . . the patriarchs, and from them, according to the flesh, comes the Messiah.” In Philippians, his contrasting depreciation of his Jewish background should be interpreted as in the service of his polemic against the “dogs” who seek to discredit him.
