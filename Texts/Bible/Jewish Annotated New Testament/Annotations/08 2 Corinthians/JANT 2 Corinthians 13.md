### 13.1–10: Previous themes resume in a warning of harsh discipline

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-1|Text]], p. 372* ^13-1

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-1|1]]:** *Third time*, see 12.14n. *“Any . . . witnesses,”* see [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^19-15|Deut 19.15]]. Paul asserts that his repeated warnings on multiple visits constitute the “two or three witnesses” Deuteronomy requires to sustain an accusation. In its original context, by contrast, the verse requires the testimony of more than one person to convict the accused. *B. Rosh Ha-Shanah* 21b argues that even Solomon was disallowed by God from passing judgments absent additional witnesses. ^13-1-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-3|3–4]]:** *Christ . . . is not weak . . . but is powerful*, the weakness–powerful paradox summarized; see 10.1n. ^13-3

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-5|5]]:** *Examine yourselves . . . test yourselves*, a central rabbinic theme, e.g., [[Berakhot 5#5a|*b. Ber*. 5a]]: “If a person sees that sufferings afflict him, let him examine his deeds.” *Jesus Christ is in you*, cf. [[Ezekiel 37#^37-14|Ezek 37.14]]: “I will put within my spirit you, and you shall live.” Some rabbinic texts see in this verse a depiction of the messianic resurrection of the dead (*Gen. Rab.* 96.5; *Ex. Rab.* 48.4; *Song Rab.* 1.9)*.* ^13-5

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-7|7]]:** *Have met the test*, see 2.9n. The Corinthians are to determine if Christ indeed is in them; if not, Paul’s work with them failed. ^13-7

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-10|10]]:** Strong warning so that Paul will not have to resort to *severe* discipline, precisely what the opponents accused Paul of doing (10.10–11). Paul reminds the Corinthians that this is so for the purpose of *building up*, not *tearing down*; see 10.8; 12.19. ^13-10

### 13.11–13: Benediction

*[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-11|Text]], p. 372* ^13-11

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-11|11]]:** *Farewell* (or “rejoice”) *. . . put . . . listen . . . agree . . . live . . .* , parting exhortations, a standard ending in Pauline letters, which feature greetings, a holy kiss, and a final benediction. *God of love and peace*, the exact formulation does not appear in Jewish sources, although midrashic literature and the liturgy routinely characterize the relationship between God and Israel as one of love, familiar from Jewish daily prayer (“Barukh ʾatah Adonai, ha-boḥer beʿamo Yisraʾel beʾahavah,” “Blessed are You, O Lord, who in your \[his\] love choose your \[his\] people Israel”). God’s role in bringing peace is also a central theme. The final benediction of the “Amidah” praises God for blessing the people of Israel with peace. ^13-11-2

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-12|12]]:** *Holy kiss*, see [[Romans 16#^16-16|Rom 16.16]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-20|1 Cor 16.20]]; [[1 Thessalonians 05#^5-26|1 Thess 5.26]]. Cf. *Gen. Rab*. 70.12, “All kissing is indecent, except in three instances: the kiss of high office, the kiss of reunion, and the kiss of separation.” *Saints*, in the Septuagint, the same Gk root renders the Heb term for “holy.” Paul thus refers to those who are sanctified through their relationship with Christ, an idea parallel to the Tanakh’s view of the Israelites as a holy nation, sanctified through their covenant with God (e.g., [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^20-26|Lev 20.26]]; [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^7-6|Deut 7.6]]). ^13-12

**[[NRSVue 2 Corinthians 13#^13-13|13]]:** A triadic benediction found nowhere else in Paul’s letters. ^13-13
