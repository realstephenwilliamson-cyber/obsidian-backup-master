## Mūla

संविन्न किल वेद्या सा वित्त्वेनैव हि भासते ।
जाग्रदाद्यास्तु सम्भाव्यास्तिस्रोऽस्याः प्राग्दशा यतः ॥ ^mula

saṃvinna kila vedyā sā vittvenaiva hi bhāsate |
jāgradādyāstu sambhāvyāstisro'syāḥ prāgdaśā yataḥ || ^iast

## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. VI
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 06 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v06 14 - Chapter Ten, part 5#^pg490|p. 471]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 06.pdf#page=490>))
>
> No second Fourth state (i.e. Fourth-fourth) is possible in this (state). Consciousness (saṁvid) is not objectively perceptible (vedyā). It only manifests as the conscious sentience (vittva) (of the perceiver). (with 10.275cd–276ab)
>
> The three states of waking and the rest can arise (in the Fourth state as well), because, as it is the prior (initial) state (prāgdaśā), the three exist by its grace. Therefore, it is said about it in the Trikaśāsana (i.e. the Mālinīvijayottara) that it is variously (bhedatah) (called) ‘Beyond Mind’ (manonmana) (which is waking in the Fourth State), ‘Endless’ (Ananta) (which is dreaming in the Fourth State), and ‘All Things’ (Sarvārtha) (which is deep sleep in the Fourth State). (with 10.276cd–277)
