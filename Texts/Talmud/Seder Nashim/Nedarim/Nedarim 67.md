## 67a

> [!torah]- Torah Or (67a)
> - [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-7|Numbers 30:7]] (line 5)
> - [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]] (line 6)

> [!xref]- Ein Mishpat (67a)
> - [Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, Negative Commandments 242](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Mitzvot_Gadol,_Negative_Commandments.242) (line 4)
> - [Tur, Yoreh De'ah 234](https://www.sefaria.org/Tur,_Yoreh_De'ah.234) (lines 4, 6)
> - [Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 234:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.234.6) (line 4)
> - [Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 234:35](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.234.35) (line 6)

If **the father nullified** her vow **and the husband did not nullify** it, or if **the husband nullified** it **and the father did not nullify** it, then the vow **is not nullified. And needless to say,** it is not nullified **if one of them ratified** the vow. ^67a-1

**GEMARA:** The mishna states that if the father nullified her vow and the husband did not nullify it, or if the husband nullified it and the father did not nullify it, then the vow is not nullified. The Gemara asks: **Is this** not the same as **the first clause** of the mishna, which states: **Her father and her husband nullify her vows?** The Gemara answers: The second clause is necessary, **lest you say:** The mishna **is teaching** that **either her father or her husband** can nullify her vows, but there is no need for both of them to do so, which is also a possible interpretation of the Hebrew phrase used. Therefore, the mishna **teaches us** that it means that both of them must nullify the vow. ^67a-2

At the end of the mishna it is stated: **And needless to say,** it is not nullified **if one of them ratified** the vow. The Gemara asks: **Why do I** need the mishna **to teach** this? **Now,** it **was stated** that if **one** of them **nullified** the vow **without the other,** it **is nothing,** her vow is not nullified. If **one of them ratified** it, **why do I** need it to state that her vow is not nullified? Is it **necessary to teach** this? ^67a-3

The Gemara answers: **It was necessary for** the mishna to mention this **in** a case **where one of them nullified** the vow **and** the other **one ratified** it, **and the one who ratified** the woman’s vow **retracted and requested** dissolution **of his ratification** from a halakhic authority, who dissolved it. **Lest you say: That which he ratified** is what **he uprooted,** by asking the halakhic authority to dissolve his ratification, and therefore the vow is no more, the mishna **teaches us that they both must nullify** it **together.** ^67a-4

§ The mishna teaches: **And** with regard to **a betrothed young woman, her father and her husband nullify her vows.** The Gemara asks: **From where do we** derive this *halakha*? Since she is still in her father’s house, he should be authorized to nullify her vows by himself. **Rabba said: The verse states: “And if she be to a husband, and her vows are upon her…**But if her husband disallows her on the day that he hears it” ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-7|Numbers 30:7–9]]). **From here** can be derived **with regard to a betrothed young woman that her father and her husband nullify her vows.** The Gemara asks: Is it not possible to **say** that **this verse is written with regard to a married** woman? ^67a-5

The Gemara answers: No, **if** you say that it is written **due to** a need to teach the *halakha* of **a married woman,** it cannot be, as **a different verse is written** for that purpose: **“And if** a woman **vowed in her husband’s house”** ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]]). The earlier verses therefore refer to a betrothed young woman, who is not yet in her husband’s house. The Gemara suggests: **Say** that **both** sets of verses are written **with regard to a married woman. And if you would say: Why do I** need **two verses** written **with regard to a married woman?** It is **to say that the husband cannot nullify earlier** vows made before her marriage but only those made “in her husband’s house.” ^67a-6

## 67b

> [!torah]- Torah Or (67b)
> - [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-4|Numbers 30:4]] (line 3)
> - [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-7|Numbers 30:7]] (line 2)
> - [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]] (lines 1, 5, 6)

> [!xref]- Ein Mishpat (67b)
> - [Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, Negative Commandments 242](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Mitzvot_Gadol,_Negative_Commandments.242) (line 7)
> - [Tur, Yoreh De'ah 234](https://www.sefaria.org/Tur,_Yoreh_De'ah.234) (line 7)
> - [Shulchan Arukh, Yoreh De'ah 234:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh,_Yoreh_De'ah.234.5) (line 7)

The Gemara rejects this, stating: **And do you not learn it by itself,** from the words “And if she vowed in her husband’s house” ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]])? As the verse indicates that her husband can nullify only vows made after the couple is fully married, and not those made beforehand, the earlier verse is unnecessary. ^67b-1

The Gemara suggests an alternative method of demonstrating that the first verse is referring to a betrothed woman: Or **if you wish, say that** the words “and if she be to a husband” ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-7|Numbers 30:7]]) must be referring to a betrothed woman, since the usage of the term **“she be” indicates betrothal** rather than marriage. ^67b-2

The Gemara proposes: **Say** that **a father can nullify** the vows of his betrothed daughter **on his own.** The Gemara responds: **If so, why do I** need the verse to teach that in a case where **she binds herself with a bond in her father’s house,** her father can **disallow her,** i.e., nullify her vow (see [[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-4|Numbers 30:4–6]]). **Now** when **it can be said** that **in the presence of a betrothed,** i.e., when she is betrothed, the **father nullifies** his daughter’s vows **on his own, is** it **necessary** to state that he can do so **where there is no betrothed?** Therefore, the fact that the Torah specifically states that the father nullifies her vows by himself when she is not betrothed indicates that he does not have that power when she is betrothed. ^67b-3

The Gemara suggests: **Say** that **the father requires the betrothed’s** participation in order to nullify his daughter’s vows **but** that **the betrothed can nullify** them **on his own. And if you would say:** If the woman’s betrothed can nullify them on his own, **why do I** need the reference to the **father that the Merciful One writes** with regard to the vows of a betrothed young woman, implying that the participation of the father is necessary to nullify her vows. One can explain that the need to mention the father **is necessary** in order to teach us **that if** the father **ratified** the vow, it is **ratified,** and her betrothed can no longer nullify it. ^67b-4

The Gemara rejects this suggestion: **If so, for what** purpose **did** the Torah **write** “And if **she vowed in her husband’s house”** ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]]), which indicates that a married woman’s husband nullifies her vows on his own? That could be derived by **an *a fortiori*** inference: **If in the presence of the father, a betrothed man** nevertheless **nullifies** her vows **on his own,** then **when** she is **no** longer **in the presence of the father,** i.e., she is married and no longer subject to his authority, **is it necessary** to state that her husband nullifies her vows on his own? ^67b-5

The Gemara suggests: **Say** that the betrothed can nullify her vows by himself, and the words **“And if she vowed in her husband’s house”** ([[Torah 42 - Matot#^30-11|Numbers 30:11]]) are in fact not necessary to teach that a fully married husband can nullify her vows on his own. Rather, they come **to say,** i.e., to teach, **that the husband cannot nullify** vows that **preceded** the betrothal. ^67b-6

The Gemara answers: **But from that,** i.e., from the fact that the verse precludes only the full-fledged husband from nullifying vows that preceded the betrothal, one may infer that **the betrothed can nullify** by himself vows that **preceded** the betrothal. Such a conclusion is unreasonable, as the fully married man has greater authority over her than the betrothed. ^67b-7

**Rather, is it not** the case that the betrothed cannot nullify vows on his own, and his ability to do so is only **because of his partnership with the father?** ^67b-8
