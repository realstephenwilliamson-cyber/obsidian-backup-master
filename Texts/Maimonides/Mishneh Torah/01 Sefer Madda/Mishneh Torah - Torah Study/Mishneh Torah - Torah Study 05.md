**1.** Just as a person is commanded to honor his father and hold him in awe,[^1] so, too, is he obligated to honor his teacher and hold him in awe.[^2]  
[Indeed, the measure of honor and awe] due one's teacher exceeds that due one's father. His father brings him into the life of this world,[^3] while his teacher, who teaches him wisdom, brings him into the life of the world to come.[^4]  
[Accordingly,] if he saw a lost object belonging to his father and one belonging to his teacher, the lost object belonging to his teacher takes precedence.[^5] If his father and his teacher are both carrying loads, he should relieve his teacher's load, and then his father's.[^6] If his father and his teacher are held as captives, he should redeem his teacher, and afterwards, redeem his father.[^7] However, if his father is [also] a Torah sage,[^8] he should redeem his father first.[^9]  
[Similarly,] if his father is a Torah sage[^10] - even if he is not equivalent to his teacher - he should return his lost article, and then that belonging to his teacher.  
There is no greater honor than that due a teacher, and no greater awe than that due a teacher. Our Sages declared:[^11] "Your fear of your teacher should be equivalent to your fear of Heaven."  
Therefore,[^12] they said:[^13] Whoever disputes the authority of his teacher[^14] is considered as if he revolts against the Divine Presence, as implied [by [[Torah 41 - Pinchas#^26-9|Numbers 26:9]]]: "...who led a revolt against[^15] God."  
Whoever engages in controversy with his teacher is considered as if he engaged in controversy with the Divine Presence, as implied [by [[Torah 39 - Chukat#^20-13|Numbers 20:13]]]: "...where the Jews contested with God and where He was sanctified."  
Whoever[^16] complains against his teacher is considered as if he complains against the Divine Presence, as implied [by [[Torah 16 - Beshalach#^16-8|Exodus 16:8]]]: "Your complaints are not against us, but against God."  
Whoever thinks disparagingly of his teacher is considered as if he thought disparagingly of the Divine Presence, as implied [by [[Torah 39 - Chukat#^21-5|Numbers 21:5]]]: "And the people spoke out against God and Moses." ^5-1

**2.** What is meant by disputing the authority of one's teacher?[^17] A person who[^18] establishes a house of study [where] he sits, explains, and teaches[^19] without his teacher's permission[^20] in his teacher's lifetime.[^21] [This applies] even when one's teacher is in another country.[^22]  
It is forbidden to ever render a halachic judgment[^23] in one's teacher's presence.[^24] Whoever renders a halachic judgment in his teacher's presence is worthy of death.[^25] ^5-2

**3.** If a person asked [a student] regarding a halachic question and there were twelve *mil*[^26] between him and his teacher, he is permitted to answer.[^27] [Furthermore,] to prevent a transgression, it is permitted to give a halachic judgment even in the presence of one's teacher.[^28]  
What does the above imply? For example, one saw a person perform a forbidden act because he was unaware of the prohibition or because of his perversity, he should [try to] prevent him [by] telling him: "This is forbidden."[^29] [This] applies even in his teacher's presence and even though one's teacher had not given him permission. Wherever the desecration of God's name is involved, no deference is paid to a teacher's honor.[^30]  
When does the above apply?[^31] With regard to a matter that came up incidentally.[^32] However, establishing oneself as a halachic authority to sit and reply to all who ask concerning halachic matters is forbidden,[^33] even if [the student] is at one end of the world and the teacher at the other,[^34] until either:  
a) the teacher dies;[^35] or  
b) the student receives permission from his teacher.[^36]  
Not everyone whose teacher dies is permitted to sit and render judgment concerning Torah law; only one who is a student worthy of rendering judgment.[^37] ^5-3

**4.** Any student who is not worthy of rendering halachic judgments and does so is foolish, wicked, and arrogant.[^38] \[[[Proverbs 07#^7-26|Proverbs 7:26]]:] "She has cast down many corpses" applies to him.[^39]  
[Conversely,] a sage who is worthy of rendering halachic judgments[^40] and refrains from doing so holds back [the spread of] Torah[^41] and places stumbling blocks before the blind.[^42] "How prodigious are those she slew" [*ibid.*] applies to him.[^43]  
These underdeveloped students who have not gathered much Torah knowledge, seek to gain prestige in the eyes of the common people and the inhabitants of their city [by] jumping to sit at the head of all questions of law and halachic judgments in Israel. They spread division, destroy the world, extinguish the light of Torah, and wreak havoc[^44] in the vineyard of the God of Hosts.[^45] In his wisdom, Solomon alluded to them [as follows, ([[Song of Songs 2#^2-15|Song of Songs 2:15]])]: "Take for us foxes, little foxes that spoil the vineyards, [our vineyards are blooming.]" ^5-4

**5.** It is forbidden for a student to refer to his teacher by name,[^46] even outside his presence.[^47] He should not mention his name[^48] in his presence, even when referring to others with the same name as his teacher[^49] - as he does with the name of his father. Rather, he should refer to them with different names, even after their death.[^50]  
The above applies when the name is unusual, and all will thus know to whom it refers.[^51]  
[A student] should not greet his teacher or respond to the latter's greeting, as is customary when two friends exchange greetings. Rather, he should bow before him and say with awe and reverence: "Peace be upon you, my master."[^52] If his teacher greeted him, he should respond: "Peace be upon you, my teacher and master."[^53] ^5-5

**6.** Similarly, he should not remove his *tefillin* in the presence of his teacher,[^54] nor should he recline in his presence.[^55] Rather, he should sit before him as one sits before a king.[^56]  
A person should not pray either in front of his teacher,[^57] behind his teacher, or at his teacher's side.[^58] Needless to say, one should not walk by his side.[^59] Rather,[^60] he should distance himself behind his teacher without standing directly behind him and then pray.[^61]  
One should not enter a bathhouse together with his teacher[^62] or sit in his teacher's place.[^63] One should not side against his teacher's opinion[^64] in his presence[^65] or contradict his statements.[^66]  
One should not sit in his presence until he tells him to sit.[^67] One should not stand before him until he tells him to stand or until he receives permission to stand.[^68]  
When one departs from his teacher, one should not turn his back to him.[^69] Rather, one should walk backwards while facing him. ^5-6

**7.** A person is obligated to stand before his teacher from the time he sees him - as far away as he can see - until [he passes beyond his field of vision] and is hidden: his figure no longer visible. Then, [the student] may sit.[^70]  
A person is obligated to visit his teacher during the festivals.[^71] ^5-7

**8.** Deference should not be shown to a student while in the presence of his teacher,[^72] unless it is customary for his teacher also to show him deference.[^73]  
All the services which a servant performs for his master should be performed by a student for his teacher. [However,][^74] if [the student] was in a place where he was not recognized and was not wearing *tefillin*[^75] - should he[^76] suspect that people[^77] will say he is a servant[^78] - he need not put on [his teacher's] shoe or remove it.[^79]  
Whoever prevents his student from serving him withholds kindness from him and takes away his fear of heaven.[^80] Any student who deals lightly with a matter related to the honor of his teacher causes the Divine Presence to depart from Israel.[^81] ^5-8

**9.** [A student who] saw his teacher transgress the words of the Torah should[^82] tell him: you have taught us such and such.[^83]  
Whenever he[^84] mentions a teaching in his[^85] presence, he should tell him: "You have taught us the following, master."[^86] He should not mention a concept which he did not hear from his teacher unless he mentions the name of the person who authored it.  
When his teacher dies, he should rend all his garments until he reveals his heart.[^87] He should never mend them.[^88]  
When does the above apply?[^89] To one's outstanding teacher from whom one has gained the majority of his wisdom.[^90]  
However, a person who has not gained the majority of his wisdom under a teacher's instruction is considered to be both a student and colleague.[^91] He[^92] is not obligated to honor him in all the above matters. Nevertheless,[^93] he should stand before him, rend his garments at his [death], as he does for all the deceased for whom he is obligated to mourn.[^94] Even if he learned only one thing from him, whether it be a small or great matter,[^95] he should stand before him[^96] and rend his garments at his [death].[^97] ^5-9

**10.** Every student with a proper character[^98] will not speak in front of anyone who is wiser than he is,[^99] even though he has not learned anything from him. ^5-10

**11.** An outstanding teacher may, if he desires, forgo his honor[^100] with regard to any or all of the above matters[^101] to any or all his students.[^102]  
Even though he forgoes [these honors], the student is obligated to respect him[^103] at the time he forgoes [respect].[^104] ^5-11

**12.** Just as students are obligated to honor their teacher, a teacher is obligated to honor his students and encourage them.[^105] Our Sages[^106] declared: "The honor of your students should be as dear to you as your own."  
A[^107] teacher should take care of his students and love them, because they are like sons[^108] who bring him pleasure in this world[^109] and in the world to come.[^110] ^5-12

**13.** Students increase their teacher's wisdom and broaden his horizons. Our Sages declared:[^111] "I learned much wisdom from my teachers and even more from my colleagues. However, from my students[^112] [I learned] most of all."  
Just as a small branch is used to light a large bough,[^113] so a small student sharpens his teacher's [thinking processes], until, through his questions, he brings forth brilliant wisdom.[^114] ^5-13
