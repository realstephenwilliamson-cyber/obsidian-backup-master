## About

A Sanskrit scripture of the Pāñcarātra, a Vaiṣṇava tradition, devoted to the goddess Lakṣmī as the power (śakti) of Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa. In the chapter 6 passage Wikipedia quotes, Śakra asks and Śrī answers, calling herself Hari's primordial I-hood.

- **Date:** Wikipedia places the final redaction in the late 12th or early 13th century, reasoning that the text extensively quotes the 11th-century *Saṃvitprakāśa* and reflects the ideas of Rāmānuja and Kṣemarāja. Wisdom Library's description says it may date to the 9th century.
- **Length:** Wisdom Library's online Sanskrit text runs to chapter 57.
- **Contents:** Pāñcarātra philosophy and cosmogony, the yoga based on it, and mantra-śāstra (Wikipedia; publisher's description of Gupta's translation). It traces creation through the vyūhas Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha, each paired with a form of the goddess, and lists Lakṣmī's forms in Viṣṇu's avatars, such as Sītā with Rāma and Bhūdevī with Varāha. Wikipedia says it does not cover temple ritual.
- **Philosophy:** Wikipedia says it expounds qualified non-dualism (Viśiṣṭādvaita) yet is heavily inspired by Kashmiri Śaiva Pratyabhijñā thought, and at times quotes the *Bhagavad Gītā* word for word.
- **Translation:** Sanjukta Gupta, *Lakṣmī Tantra: A Pāñcarātra Text*, translation with notes (Leiden: Brill, 1972; Orientalia Rheno-Traiectina 15). It was also her Utrecht doctoral thesis, supervised by Jan Gonda. Motilal Banarsidass reprinted it in Delhi in 2000, and a third edition followed in 2013. The publisher's description says she left out the ritual esoterism to keep the book manageable.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia notes close parallels with the Pratik Rahasyam of the *Devī Māhātmya* and calls such Vaiṣṇava assimilation of Śākta lore extremely rare. The publisher's description says its account of śakti and of reliance on God's grace carries it beyond sectarian lines.
