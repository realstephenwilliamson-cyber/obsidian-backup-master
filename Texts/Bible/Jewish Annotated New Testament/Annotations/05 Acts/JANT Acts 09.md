### 9.1–31: Conversion of Saul/Paul

*[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-1|Text]], p. 241* · Repeated, with minor variations, in 22.1–21; 26.9–18. Paul states that he persecuted the church ([[Galatians 01#^1-13|Gal 1.13–14]]; [[Philippians 03#^3-6|Phil 3.6]]) but makes no mention of his actions being sanctioned by the high priest. With the exception of the priests who came to believe (6.7), Acts presents the priesthood as staunchly antagonistic towards Christians (4.1; 5.17; 22.30; 23.14; 25.2). ^9-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-1|1]]:** *High priest*, Acts ascribes to the high priest legal authority that he did not possess over Jews in Diaspora communities. ^9-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-2|2]]:** *Damascus,* a large city in Syria, with a sizeable Jewish community (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.561). Paul spent considerable time in Damascus ([[Galatians 01#^1-17|Gal 1.17–18]]). *Way*, title for a Christian community (19.9,23; 22.4; 24.14,22), possibly meant to portray Christians as following the divinely appointed path (see [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-30|Deut 5.33]]; 10.12; 30.16; [[Isaiah 40#^40-3|Isa 40.3]]; [[Isaiah 42#^42-24|42.24]]; [[Zechariah 03#^3-7|Zech 3.7]]). The Qumran community employed the Hebrew equivalent, “derekh,” as a self-identification (CD 20.18; 1QS 9.17–18; 10.21). ^9-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-3|3–7]]:** The scene includes several details common to divine appearances, such as flashing light ([[LXX 4 Maccabees 04#^4-10|4 Macc 4.10]]), falling to the ground ([[Ezekiel 01#^1-28|Ezek 1.28]]; [[Daniel 10#^10-9|Dan 10.9]]), double naming ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-11|Gen 22.11]]; [[Torah 11 - Vayigash#^46-2|46.2]]; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-4|Ex 3.4]]; [[I Samuel 03#^3-4|1 Sam 3.4]],[[I Samuel 03#^3-10|10]]), and commission ([[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^12-1|Gen 12.1]]; [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-2|22.2]]). ^9-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-5|5]]:** *Lord*, a title of respect as well as of divinity. ^9-5

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-8|8]]:** Connection between physical and spiritual sight ([[Luke 24#^24-16|Lk 24.16]],[[Luke 24#^24-31|31]]). ^9-8

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-9|9]]:** *Neither ate nor drank*, fasting in this case may be understood as an act of intense spiritual engagement ([[Luke 04#^4-2|Lk 4.2]]; [[LXX Judith 08#^8-6|Jdt 8.6]]) or of supplication or penance ([[Ezra 10#^10-6|Ezra 10.6]]; [[LXX Tobit 12#^12-8|Tob 12.8]]; [[LXX Sirach 34#^34-26|Sir 34.26]]; *Pss. Sol.* 3.8; *Jos. Asen.* 10.1; *b. Ber.* 17a). ^9-9

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-10|10]]:** *Disciple,* Acts does not report how Christianity spread to this Syrian city. *Vision*, divine instruction often takes place in visions (10.3,17,19; 16.9). *Here I am*, conventional response to God ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-1|Gen 22.1]]; [[Torah 07 - Vayetzei#^31-11|31.11]]; [[Torah 11 - Vayigash#^46-2|46.2]]; [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^3-4|Ex 3.4]]). ^9-10

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-11|11]]:** *Tarsus*, city of Cilicia in southeast Asia Minor. ^9-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-12|12]]:** *Lay his hands*, technique used in healing ([[Luke 04#^4-40|Lk 4.40]]; [[Luke 13#^13-11|13.11–13]]). ^9-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-13|13]]:** *Saints*, lit., “holy ones,” a standard term for members of the Christian community. It is rare in Acts (9.32; 26.10), but frequent in Paul (e.g., [[Romans 01#^1-7|Rom 1.7]]; [[Romans 15#^15-25|15.25]]; [[1 Corinthians 16#^16-1|1 Cor 16.1]]; [[2 Corinthians 08#^8-4|2 Cor 8.4]]; [[2 Corinthians 13#^13-13|13.13]]; [[Philippians 01#^1-1|Phil 1.1]]). ^9-13

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-15|15]]:** *Gentiles . . . kings . . . Israel*, Paul accomplishes this mission. Jesus offers a similar command to his disciples (see [[Matthew 10#^10-18|Mt 10.18]]; [[Matthew 28#^28-19|28.19]]; [[Mark 13#^13-9|Mk 13.9–10]]). ^9-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-16|16]]:** *Suffer*, the mission will be difficult; Paul recites his ordeals in [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-24|2 Cor 11.24–29]]. ^9-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-18|18]]:** *Scales*, see [[LXX Tobit 03#^3-17|Tob 3.17]]; [[LXX Tobit 11#^11-13|11.13]]. ^9-18

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-22|22]]:** *Jesus was the Messiah*, this preaching provokes opposition among Jews (17.3–5; 18.5–6,28). ^9-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-23|23–25]]:** *Jews plotted to kill him*, Paul states ([[2 Corinthians 11#^11-32|2 Cor 11.32–33]]) the threat came from an official of the Nabatean king, not from Jews. ^9-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-26|26]]:** *Come to Jerusalem*, Paul reports that after the Damascus Road incident, he traveled to Arabia and did not return to Jerusalem for three years ([[Galatians 01#^1-17|Gal 1.17–18]]). ^9-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-27|27]]:** *Barnabas*, see 4.36n. He joins Paul as a missionary (12.25; 13.2–50; 14.12–20; 15.2–39; see also [[1 Corinthians 09#^9-6|1 Cor 9.6]]; [[Galatians 02#^2-1|Gal 2.1–13]]). *Apostles*, Paul reports that on his initial visit to Jerusalem he met only with Cephas (Peter) and James ([[Galatians 01#^1-18|Gal 1.18–19]]). *Spoken boldly*, see 4.13n. ^9-27

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-29|29]]:** *Hellenists*, Greek-speaking Jews, as in 6.1. ^9-29

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-30|30]]:** *Tarsus*, Paul’s hometown, see v 11. ^9-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-31|31]]:** A typical summary reporting the church’s expansion in size and location (2.41,47; 5.12–16; 19.20). ^9-31

### 9.32–43: Peter’s travels

*[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-32|Text]], p. 242* · The story returns to Peter who performs two miraculous healings that prompt many to become believers (vv 35,42). Elsewhere in Acts, healings rarely result in conversion (13.11–12), and more often stir interest (5.16) and misunderstanding (14.11–12; 28.6). ^9-32

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-32|32–35]]:** Peter heals Aeneas, a paralytic, similar to what Jesus does ([[Mark 02#^2-3|Mk 2.3–12]]). ^9-32-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-32|32]]:** *Saints*, see 9.13n. ^9-32-3

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-34|34]]:** *Get up*, miraculous deeds are often accompanied by special verbal formulas ([[Mark 01#^1-25|Mk 1.25]]; [[Mark 05#^5-8|5.8]]; [[Mark 09#^9-25|9.25]]; [[Luke 07#^7-14|Lk 7.14]]; [[Luke 08#^8-54|8.54]]; *Greek Magical Papyri* 4.1243). ^9-34

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-35|35]]:** *Lydda*, in Greek mean *a gazelle* on the road from Jerusalem to Joppa (present-day Lod). ^9-35

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-36|36–42]]:** Peter resuscitates Tabitha, similar to actions by Elijah ([[I Kings 17#^17-17|1 Kings 17.17–24]]), Elisha ([[II Kings 04#^4-18|2 Kings 4.18–37]]), and Jesus ([[Luke 07#^7-11|Lk 7.11–17]]; [[Luke 08#^8-41|8.41–42]],[[Luke 08#^8-49|49-56]]). ^9-36

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-36|36]]:** *Joppa*, present day Yafo/Yaffa, adjacent to Tel Aviv. *Tabitha*, only woman in the NT explicitly called a disciple. Like Paul and John Mark (12.12,25), she has both a Hebrew or Aramaic and a Greek (or Latin) name (see translators’ note *g*). ^9-36-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-39|39]]:** *Widows*, for widows who take active roles in prayer and mourning, see [[Luke 02#^2-37|Lk 2.37]]; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-5|1 Tim 5.5]]. Tabitha is sometimes thought to be a widow as well, but this is not certain. *Tunics and other clothing*, like other women in Acts (e.g., Lydia, 16.14), Tabitha is responsible for the production of woven goods and might be imagined as a benefactor of the widows. ^9-39

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-40|40]]:** *Get up*, see v 34. ^9-40

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-41|41]]:** *Saints,* see v 13. ^9-41

**[[NRSVue Acts 09#^9-43|43]]:** *Tanner*, tanners had dubious reputa-tions largely because of the smell produced by the tanning process (*b. Pesah.* 65a; *b. Ketub.* 77a; *b. Qidd.* 82b). ^9-43
