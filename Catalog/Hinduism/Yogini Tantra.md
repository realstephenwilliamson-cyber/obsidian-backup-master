## About

A long Sanskrit Tantra by an unknown author, devoted to the goddesses Kali and Kamakhya, with religious, philosophical and some historical matter. Wikipedia calls it especially important for left-hand (vamachara) Tantric worship, and warns against confusing it with the Buddhist class of texts called Yogini Tantras.

- **Place:** written in Assam or Cooch Behar.
- **Date:** usually the 16th century, because it mentions the Koch dynasty, called "kuvacha" and said to be born of a Mech woman; Nath (1989), citing chapter 12, verse 3, puts it in about the 16th century. Some authors place it in the 17th. Hugh Urban called it a product of 17th-century Assam in 2011, and a 16th-century text from Cooch Behar in 2015.
- **Contents:** the worship of Kali and Kamakhya. Urban calls its account of sexual rites one of the most explicit in Tantric literature. Wikipedia's article on the Kamakhya Temple, citing Kakati, says it ignores the *Kalika Purana*'s story of Kamakhya's origin, links her with Kali, stresses the creative symbolism of the yoni, and states that the religion of the Yogini seat is of Kirata (tribal) origin, in a verse cited as 2/9/13.
- **Relation to the Kalika Purana:** the same article calls it a later work than the *Kalika Purana*, the older text on Kamakhya.
- **Editions:** a Sanskrit edition by Biswanarayan Shastri (Delhi: Bharatiya Vidya Prakashan, 1982), whose English preface and introduction discuss the manuscript and earlier printings. Some printings add a Hindi translation. Wikipedia reports no published English translation, only a partial online summary.
- **Why it matters:** with the *Kalika Purana*, it is one of the texts Wikipedia's Kamakhya article cites for the goddess's Tantric cult and her Kirata roots; its mention of the Koch dynasty is the main evidence for its date.
