# Ecclesiastes 05

← [[Ecclesiastes 04]] · [[Tanakh]] · [[Ecclesiastes 06]] →

Compare: [[NRSVue Ecclesiastes 05|NRSVue]] (Heb 5:1–19 = NRSVue 5:2–20; NRSVue 5:1 = Heb 4:17)

## Chapter 5

**5:1** Keep your mouth from being rash, and let not your throat[^1] be quick to bring forth speech before God. For God is in heaven and you are on earth; that is why your words should be few. ^5-1

**5:2** Just as dreams come with much brooding, so does foolish utterance come with much speech. ^5-2

**5:3** When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it. For He has no pleasure in fools; what you vow, fulfill. ^5-3

**5:4** It is better not to vow at all than to vow and not fulfill. ^5-4

**5:5** Don’t let your mouth bring you into disfavor, and don’t plead before the messenger[^2] that it was an error, but fear God;[^3] else God may be angered by your talk and destroy your possessions. ^5-5

**5:6** [^4]For much dreaming leads to futility and to superfluous talk. ^5-6

**5:7** If you see in a province oppression of the poor and suppression of right and justice, don’t wonder at the fact; for one high official is protected by a higher one, and both of them by still higher ones. ^5-7

**5:8** Thus the greatest advantage in all the land is his: he controls a field that is cultivated.[^5] ^5-8

**5:9** A lover of money never has his fill of money, nor a lover of wealth his fill of income. That too is futile. ^5-9

**5:10** As his substance increases, so do those who consume it; what, then, does the success of its owner amount to but feasting his eyes? ^5-10

**5:11** A worker’s[^6] sleep is sweet, whether he has much or little to eat; but the rich man’s abundance doesn’t let him sleep. ^5-11

**5:12** Here is a grave evil I have observed under the sun: riches hoarded by their owner to his misfortune, ^5-12

**5:13** in that those riches are lost in some unlucky venture; and if he begets a son, he has nothing in hand. ^5-13

**5:14** Another grave evil is this: He must depart just as he came.[^7] As he came out of his mother’s womb, so must he depart at last, naked as he came. He can take nothing of his wealth to carry with him. ^5-14

**5:15** So what is the good of his toiling for the wind? ^5-15

**5:16** Besides, all his days he eats in darkness,[^8] with much vexation and grief and anger. ^5-16

**5:17** Only this, I have found, is a real good: that one should eat and drink and get pleasure with all the gains he makes under the sun, during the numbered days of life that God has given him; for that is his portion. ^5-17

**5:18** Also, whenever a man is given riches and property by God, and is also permitted by Him to enjoy them and to take his portion and get pleasure for his gains—that is a gift of God. ^5-18

**5:19** For [such a man] will not brood much over the days of his life,[^9] because God keeps him busy enjoying himself. ^5-19

[^1]: Heb. leb, lit. “heart,” sometimes designates the organ of speech; cf. Isa. 33.18; 59.13; Ps. 19.15; 49.4; Job. 8.10.
[^2]: Some ancient versions read “God.”
[^3]: Moved up from v. 6 for clarity.
[^4]: Meaning of verse uncertain. Emendation yields “Much brooding results in dreams; and much talk in futilities”; cf. v. 2.
[^5]: I.e., the high official profits from the labor of others; but meaning of verse uncertain.
[^6]: Some ancient versions have “slave’s.”
[^7]: Moved up from v. 15 for clarity.
[^8]: Septuagint reads “are [spent] in darkness and mourning.”
[^9]: The thought of which is depressing; see v. 16.
