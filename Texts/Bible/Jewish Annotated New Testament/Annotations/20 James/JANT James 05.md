# James 5 (JANT notes)

[[JANT James 04|← James 4]] · [[Jewish Annotated New Testament|JANT index]] · —

Text: [[NRSVue James 05|NRSVue]] · [[James 05|BSB and SBLGNT]] · Introduction: [[JANT James - Introduction]]

### 5.1–6: The emptiness of riches

*[[NRSVue James 05#^5-1|Text]], p. 498* ^5-1

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-1|1]]:** *Rich*, see 1.9n., wealth leads to corruption. *Miseries . . . coming to you*, riches are of no use at the end, whether at one’s individual death or on the day of judgment (see [[Matthew 19#^19-23|Mt 19.23]]). ^5-1-2

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-2|2–3]]:** *Riches . . . rotted . . . gold and silver . . . rusted*, see [[Matthew 06#^6-19|Mt 6.19–21]]. *Treasure for the last days*, see *y. Peʾah* 1.1: treasure of deeds is for the next world, that of wealth for this world. ^5-2

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-4|4]]:** *The wages . . . cry out*, see [[Torah 49 - Ki Teitzei#^24-14|Deut 24.14–15]]. *Lord of hosts*, reference to [[Haggai 2#^2-8|Hag 2.8]] “The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, says the Lord of hosts.” ^5-4

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-5|5]]:** *You have fattened . . . slaughter*, see [[Torah 53 - Ha'Azinu#^32-15|Deut 32.15]] for indulgence leading to rebellion. “Fat” is occasionally a metaphor for intransigent wickedness, e.g., [[Psalms 119#^119-70|Ps 119.70]]. ^5-5

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-6|6]]:** *Murdered the righteous one*, the poor, righteous person is condemned by the protected rich; see [[LXX Wisdom of Solomon 02#^2-10|Wis 2.10–20]]. ^5-6

### 5.7–12: Patience and care in speaking

*[[NRSVue James 05#^5-7|Text]], p. 498* ^5-7

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-7|7–8]]:** *Coming of the Lord*, generally taken as a reference to the expectation that Jesus would return. The ensuing verses speak of the Lord, i.e., God (compassionate and merciful after [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6–7]]) and the reference here might be to God’s salvation or judgment day ([[Malachi 3#^3-23|Mal 4.5]]; [[Isaiah 59#^59-20|Isa 59.20]]). *Early and the late rains*, October and April ([[Torah 46 - Eikev#^11-14|Deut 11.14]]; [[Jeremiah 05#^5-24|Jer 5.24]]; [[Joel 2#^2-23|Joel 2.23]]). ^5-7-2

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-9|9]]:** *Do not . . . judged*, see *m. Sot*. 1.7 and *b. Shabb*. 27b for the same idea phrased positively: God is the judge ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-25|Gen 18.25]]) whose verdict is final. *The Judge is standing at the doors*, probably Jesus, possibly God. ^5-9

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-10|10]]:** *Suffering . . . the prophets*, prophets (e.g., see Jer 38, and the first-century Gk *Lives of the Prophets*) were persecuted. *M. Taʾan*. 2.4 lists prophets and holy people who endured suffering and were saved. ^5-10

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-11|11]]:** *Endurance of Job*, James refers to Job 1–2; some rabbis saw the book as a parable, composed by Moses, teaching that God tries the one God loves (*b. B. Bat.* 15a). Endurance (Gk “hupomone”; also in [[Hebrews 12#^12-1|Heb 12.1]]) is correctly rendered “endurance” as opposed to “patience” as in KJV. *Compassionate and merciful*, e.g., [[Torah 21 - Ki Tisa#^34-6|Ex 34.6]]; [[Psalms 103#^103-8|Ps 103.8]]. ^5-11

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-12|12]]:** *Do not swear*, reflects [[Matthew 05#^5-34|Mt 5.34–37]]; see also *b. B. Metz*. 49a; [[Ecclesiastes 05#^5-4|Eccl 5.4]]. ^5-12

### 5.13–20: Prayer, praise, and following the truth

*[[NRSVue James 05#^5-13|Text]], p. 498* ^5-13

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-13|13]]:** *Suffering . . . pray . . . cheerful . . . praise,* speech should be appropriate to the circumstance. ^5-13-2

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-14|14]]:** *Elders* of the church, (Heb “zekeynim”; Gk “presbyteroi”), see Acts 15; [[1 Timothy 05#^5-17|1 Tim 5.17]],[[1 Timothy 05#^5-19|19]]; [[Titus 01#^1-5|Titus 1.5]]; [[1 Peter 05#^5-1|1 Pet 5.1]],[[1 Peter 05#^5-5|5]]; 2 Jn 1; 3 Jn 1, *b. Sanh*. 101a, [[Proverbs 15#^15-29|Prov 15.29]]. *Church* (Gk “ekklesia”), a congregation or assembly, synonymous with “synagogue”; Heb “edah*”* or “qahal*”* (see LXX [[LXX Proverbs 05#^5-14|Prov 5.14]]). *Pray over them*, *b. B. Bat*. 116a speaks of the sage praying for the ill person, presumably at bedside. *Anointing*, used in healing, see [[Mark 06#^6-13|Mk 6.13]]; James seems to speak of not holy oil but of medicinal magic (cf. [[Torah 28 - Metzora#^14-15|Lev 14.15–18]]) or of medicinal oil ([[Isaiah 01#^1-6|Isa 1.6]]). *Name of the Lord*, perhaps referring not to the name of Jesus (see [[Mark 16#^16-17|Mk 16.17–18]]) but to God’s name (see LXX [[LXX Psalms 118#^118-26|Ps 118.26]]). *Y. Maʿas. S*. 2.1 (repeated in *y. Shabb.* 14.4) refers to ancient healing practices by intoning enchantments; James says to use the name of God (likely to exclude names of angels or other spirits) when anointing with oil. The Talmudic passage remains unclear if the healer puts the oil on his own head or on the patient’s. ^5-14

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-15|15]]:** *Prayer of faith will save the sick . . . raise them up . . . be forgiven*, see e.g., 4Q521 \[“Messianic Apocalypse”\] “for the heavens and the earth will listen to his *anointed one*. . . Heal the sick, resurrect the dead, announce glad tidings to the poor.” The “Amidah” prayer states: “Lord. . . you uphold the falling . . . heal the sick . . . free those in bondage.” *Committed sins . . . forgiven*, The last rights, “extreme unction,” of the Catholic Church originated in the twelfth century. The Catholic Church may have adapted these verses to institute the rite. ^5-15

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-16|16]]:** *Confess your sins to one another*, Jewish sources know of confession to God ([[Psalms 038#^38-16|Ps 38.15–18]]) and to wronged parties [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], [[Torah 45 - Vaetchanan#^5-14|Deut 5.14]] and [[LXX Sirach 05#^5-14|Sir 5.14]] (“Forgive your neighbor the wrong he has done and then your sins will be pardoned when you pray”). [[Nehemiah 09#^9-2|Neh 9.2]] suggests communal confession: they “stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their ancestors.” *And pray for one another*, when you ask for another, your needs are given (*b. B. Kamma* 92a based on [[Job 42#^42-10|Job 42.10]]). *Prayer of the righteous*, *b. Taʾan*. 8a recommends having a pious person pray for you. ^5-16

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-17|17–18]]:** *Elijah . . . harvest*, see [[I Kings 17#^17-1|1 Kings 17.1]] and 18.41–46, although the account does not explicitly quote Elijah’s praying for drought (the standing posture implies it) or for it to cease (his beseeching \[“taḥanun”\] posture implies it). ^5-17

**[[NRSVue James 05#^5-20|20]]:** *Brings back a sinner*, see [[Daniel 12#^12-3|Dan 12.3]] (“those who lead many to righteousness”); the message concludes by stressing the benefits of repentance, a major Jewish theme from Deuteronomy onwards. A*nd will cover a multitude of sins*, see [[Psalms 032#^32-1|Ps 32.1]] (“Happy are those whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered”) and [[Proverbs 10#^10-12|Prov 10.12]] (cited by [[1 Peter 04#^4-8|1 Pet 4.8]], “love covers a multitude of sins”) for near linguistic parallel. ^5-20
