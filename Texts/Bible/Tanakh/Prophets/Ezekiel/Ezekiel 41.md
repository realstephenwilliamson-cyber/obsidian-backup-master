## Chapter 41

**41:1** He then led me into the great hall. He measured the jambs, 6 cubits on either side; such was the depth[^1] of each jamb.[^2] ^41-1

**41:2** The entrance was 10 cubits wide, and the flanking walls of the entrance were each 5 cubits wide. Next he measured the depth [of the hall], 40 cubits, and the width, 20 cubits. ^41-2

**41:3** And then he entered the inner room. He measured each jamb of the entrance, 2 cubits [deep]; the entrance itself, 6 cubits across; and the width of [the flanking wall on either side of][^3] the entrance, 7 cubits. ^41-3

**41:4** Then he measured the depth, 20 cubits; and the width at the inner end of the great hall was also 20 cubits. And he said to me, “This is the Holy of Holies.” ^41-4

**41:5** Then he measured the wall of the Temple. [It was] 6 cubits [thick] on every side of the Temple, and the side-chamber measured 4 cubits [across].[^4] ^41-5

**41:6** The side chambers were arranged one above the other, in 33 sections.[^5] All around, there were projections in the Temple wall to serve the side chambers as supports, so that [their] supports should not be the Temple wall itself. ^41-6

**41:7** The winding passage[^6] of the side chambers widened from story to story; and since the structure was furnished all over with winding passages from story to story, the structure itself became wider from story to story.[^7] It was by this means that one ascended from the bottom story to the top one by way of the middle one. ^41-7

**41:8** I observed that the Temple was surrounded by a raised pavement—the foundations of the side chambers; its elevation was a rod’s length, or 6 cubits. ^41-8

**41:9** The outer wall of the side chamber was 5 cubits thick, and that which served as a walk between the Temple’s side chambers ^41-9

**41:10** and the chamber complexes[^8] was 20 cubits wide all around the Temple. ^41-10

**41:11** Of entrances to the side chambers giving on the walk, there was one entrance on the north side and one entrance on the south side; and the space[^9] of the walk was 5 cubits thick all around. ^41-11

**41:12** And the structure that fronted on the vacant space at the [Temple’s] western end was 70 cubits deep;[^10] the walls of the structure were 5 cubits thick on every side; and it was 90 cubits wide.[^11] ^41-12

**41:13** He measured the [total] depth of the Temple, 100 cubits;[^12] and the depth of the vacant space and of the structure, with its walls, also came to 100 cubits.[^13] ^41-13

**41:14** The front side of the Temple, like the vacant space on the east, was 100 cubits wide.[^14] ^41-14

**41:15** He also measured the width[^15] of the structure facing the vacant space in the rear, inclusive of its ledges,[^16] 100 cubits.  
Both the great hall inside and the portico next to the court— ^41-15

**41:16** the thresholds[^17]—and the windows with frames and the ledges[^18] at the threshold, all over the three parts of each, were completely overlaid[^19] with wood. There was wainscoting from the floor to the windows, including the window [frame]s ^41-16

**41:17** and extending above the openings, both in the inner Temple and outside.[^20] And all over the wall, both in the inner one and in the outer,[^21] ran a pattern.[^22] ^41-17

**41:18** It consisted of cherubs and palm trees, with a palm tree between every two cherubs. Each cherub had two faces: ^41-18

**41:19** a human face turned toward the palm tree on one side and a lion’s face turned toward the palm tree on the other side. This was repeated all over the Temple; ^41-19

**41:20** the cherubs and the palm trees were carved on[^23] the wall from the floor to above the openings.  
As regards the great hall, ^41-20

**41:21** the great hall had four doorposts; and before the Shrine was something resembling ^41-21

**41:22** a wooden altar 3 cubits high and 2 cubits long and having inner corners;[^24] and its length[^25] and its walls were of wood. And he said to me, “This is the table[^26] that stands before the LORD.” ^41-22

**41:23** The great hall had a double door, and the Shrine likewise had ^41-23

**41:24** a double door, and each door had two swinging leaves:[^27] two for the one door and two such leaves[^28] for the other. ^41-24

**41:25** Cherubs and palm trees were carved on these—on the doors of the hall—just as they were carved on the walls; and there was a lattice[^29] of wood outside in front of the portico. ^41-25

**41:26** And there were windows with frames[^30] and palm trees on the flanking walls of the portico on either side [of the entrance] and [on] the Temple’s side chambers and [on] the lattices.[^31] ^41-26
