## About

A book by Vine Deloria Jr. (1933–2005), a Standing Rock Sioux writer and scholar, subtitled *A Native View of Religion*. It sets tribal religions, rooted in particular places, against Christianity and the American culture shaped by it.

- **Author:** born in Martin, South Dakota, near the Pine Ridge Reservation. Wikipedia gives a theology degree from Augustana Theological Seminary (1963) and a law degree from the University of Colorado (1970). He led the National Congress of American Indians (Wikipedia: 1964–67; EBSCO: 1966) and taught at the University of Arizona (Wikipedia: 1978–90; EBSCO: 1979–90) and the University of Colorado, Boulder (1990–2000).
- **First edition:** New York: Grosset & Dunlap, 1973 (Wikipedia; the 1974 *Horizons* review lists it at iv + 376 pages). The publisher's blurb for the anniversary edition says it was first published in 1972.
- **Later editions:** a second edition (Golden, Colo.: North American Press, 1992; 313 pages), described in the Historical Society of Pennsylvania catalogue as an updated version. EBSCO mentions a revised edition reviewed by George Tinker in 1994. A 30th-anniversary edition followed from Fulcrum in 2003.
- **Argument:** Wikipedia summarizes it as treating religion as rooted in place rather than universal, and as linking Christian anthropocentrism and American economic thinking to environmental damage. The 1992 contents include chapters titled "Thinking in Time and Space", "Spatial Problem of History" and "Sacred Places and Moral Responsibility". EBSCO says it examines why and how Native people were converted and calls them to revitalize their earlier beliefs.
- **Reception:** EBSCO calls it one of his most controversial works and the first of its kind from the Indian community. Wikipedia reports that in 1974 *Time* named Deloria one of the primary "shapers and movers" of Christian theology.
