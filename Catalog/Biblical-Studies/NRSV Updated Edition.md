## About

The NRSV Updated Edition (NRSVue) is a revision of the New Revised Standard Version of 1989, made under the Society of Biblical Literature (SBL) for the National Council of Churches (NCC), which holds the copyright. Its scholars stress that it is a revision, not a new translation (RNS).

- **Base text:** the NRSV (1989), itself a revision of the Revised Standard Version, made by an ecumenical committee of about thirty Orthodox, Catholic, Protestant and Jewish scholars. Bruce Metzger wrote its preface; its rule was "as literal as possible, as free as necessary."
- **Process:** launched at the SBL Annual Meeting in 2017; planning in 2017, review 2018–20, vetting 2020–21 (SMU). Seven general editors in three teams and 56 book editors; Wikipedia says more than fifty scholars. The NCC board approved it in October 2021.
- **Dates:** presented on 16 November 2021 (RNS). Wikipedia gives 25 December 2021 for the digital release and 2022 for the first print editions; RNS says publishers could issue it from 1 May 2022.
- **Changes:** RNS reports more than 20,000 revisions, some 12,000 of them substantive; SMU gives about 20,000 changes, which it also calls substantive edits. The aims were to use advances in textual criticism since 1989 (some changes rest on Dead Sea Scrolls evidence), improve the textual notes, and revise style. Examples: *an enslaved woman* for *slave woman* (Galatians 4:22), *magi* for *wise men*, *purification offering* for *sin offering*, and person-first wording for disability (Matthew 4:24).
- **Editions:** Protestant, Catholic and Ecumenical. It is the base text of the SBL Study Bible and the Westminster Study Bible, and a sixth edition of the New Oxford Annotated Bible is planned on it. On 29 September 2025 Friendship Press announced a USCCB imprimatur for the Catholic Edition, for private use and study.
- **Why it matters:** Wikipedia says the NRSV is widely used by biblical scholars. The SBL's executive director, John Kutsko, called the update suitable in Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox and Jewish settings (RNS).
