### 28.1–10: Paul on Malta

*[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-1|Text]], p. 278* · Paul is shown to have miraculous abilities to escape death and heal others (13.9–11; 14.9–11; 20.9–12). ^28-1

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-1|1]]:** *Malta*, island off Sicily’s southern coast. ^28-1-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-4|4]]:** *Justice*, possibly a personification, the deity Justice seen as exacting retribution. ^28-4

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-6|6]]:** *Say that he was a god*, in Iconium, Paul’s healing of a paralytic prompts the residents to refer to him as Hermes (14.12). ^28-6

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-8|8]]:** *Putting his hands*, a common action associated with healing; see 9.12; 14.3; 19.6; [[Luke 04#^4-40|Lk 4.40]]; [[Luke 13#^13-11|13.11–13]]. ^28-8

### 28.11–31: Paul in Rome

*[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-11|Text]], p. 278* ^28-11

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-11|11]]:** *Three months later*, ships had to wait out the winter months when nav-igation was not possible (Josephus, *J.W.* 2.203). *Alexandrian ship*, see 27.6. *Twin Brothers*, Dioscuri, the brothers Castor and Pollux, often considered as guardians of sailors (Euripides, *Helen* 1663; Horace, *Odes* 1.3.2; 1.12.25). A figurehead, a carved image in the form of a deity, animal, or other object, was often placed on a ship’s bow (Lucian, *Navigium* 5). ^28-11-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-12|12–14]]:** The final part of the journey takes Paul along the eastern coast of Sicily (Syracuse) to Rhegium, the port on the Italian peninsula opposite Sicily, up the western coast of Italy to the port of Puteoli, near Naples, and finally to Rome. ^28-12

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-15|15]]:** *Forum of Appius*, town 43 mi south of Rome. *Three Taverns*, resting spot 33 mi south of Rome. ^28-15

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-16|16]]:** *Live by himself*, the description is similar to the treatment afforded to Agrippa I while living in Rome (Josephus, *Ant.* 18.235), and is consistent with the deference Roman authorities frequently show to Paul (21.40; 22.29; 24.23). ^28-16

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-17|17–20]]:** Paul’s remarks to the Jewish leaders summarize his position: he is loyal to Judaism and the Jewish people (22.3); he committed no crimes against Rome (22.3–21; 24.11–13); Jesus is the fulfillment of Israel’s traditions (23.6; 24.15; 26.6–8). *Romans,* Paul’s acquittal by the Romans mirrors Jesus’ acquittal by Pilate ([[Luke 23#^23-4|Lk 23.4]],[[Luke 23#^23-15|15]],[[Luke 23#^23-22|22]]). ^28-17

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-19|19]]:** *Jews objected,* confirming that Jews and not Romans are responsible for *about seventy-six* b Gk *place of two seas* c Gk *brothers* Paul’s imprisonment. *Appeal to the emperor*, see 25.11. *No charge to bring against my nation*, Paul is more honorable than his Jewish opponents since he refuses to accuse them of bringing false accusations and attempting to kill him. ^28-19

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-22|22]]:** *Sect*, see 9.2; 24.14*. Everywhere . . . spoken against*, a perception that better reflects the time in which Acts was written rather than when Paul was in Rome (see Pliny, *Letters* 10.96–97). ^28-22

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-23|23–29]]:** The final scene dramatically presents the related themes that the failure of the Jews to receive salvation both was foretold by the prophets and is a consequence of their own rejection of Jesus (13.12,45–46). ^28-23

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-23|23]]:** *Law of Moses and from the prophets*, see 26.22. ^28-23-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-26|26]]:** [[Isaiah 06#^6-9|Isa 6.9–10]]. ^28-26

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-28|28]]:** *Sent to the Gentiles*, on two previous occasions Jewish opposition led Paul to announce a turn to Gentiles (13.46; 18.6). While Paul continued to engage Jews, the inclusion of a lengthy passage from Isaiah (vv 26–27) and the report that he received all persons (v 30) make it appear that any Jewish mission is now over. ^28-28

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-30|28.30–31]]:** Acts ends without narrating Paul’s death, although the text often alludes to it (19.21; 20.22–25; 23.11; 26.32; 27.24). Acts may have avoided this part of the story, since it was understood by contemporary Christians that Roman authorities were responsible (*Acts of Paul*; Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 2.25.5–8), and thus maintaining the emphasis on Jews as those responsible for the persecution of Paul. ^28-30

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-30|30]]:** *Two whole years*, see also 24.27. The time reference suggests that Paul’s accusers had failed to follow up on their intentions to prosecute him, or possibly having done so the charges were subsequently dismissed. In either case, the ending of the book confirms Paul’s innocence of any crime. ^28-30-2

**[[NRSVue Acts 28#^28-31|31]]:** *Kingdom of God*, see 8.12. *Boldness*, see 4.13. Paul’s open preaching in the capital of the empire fulfills the programmatic command of Jesus to spread the gospel to the ends of the earth (1.8), with the city of Rome representing the empire’s wide expanse. *he had said these words, the Jews departed, arguing vigorously among themselves* d Or *in his own hired dwelling* ^28-31
