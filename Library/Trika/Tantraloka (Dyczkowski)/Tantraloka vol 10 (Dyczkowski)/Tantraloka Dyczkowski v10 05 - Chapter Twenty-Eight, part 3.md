### TĀ 28.188–189

One should worship (all) that, conscious and unconscious, serves as an aid, directly or indirectly, to the attainment of the desired goal, be it liberation or worldly benefits. (Moreover,) one should also worship the (various) means that serve to attain their nature and the means to that (means), that is, the sacred image (mūrti), the (auspicious) time, ritual action, and so on. (188-189)

What is ‘conscious and unconscious’ is (the conscious) Self and (the unconscious) vital breath and the rest (of the psycho-physical organism). This is the meaning. ‘The (various) means’ are knowledge and yoga etc. “The sacred image’ is, for example, a Liṅga. ‘The (auspicious period of) time’ is, for example, a Kulaparvan. ‘Ritual action’ (includes) bathing, visualization (of the deity) and the like.

#### Viveka on 28.191

‘This is especially so’, because in the world (of daily life), the means and the goal differ from one another, and so it is possible that (things) could be otherwise.
