## Translation

> [!source] Dyczkowski, vol. I
> [[Library/Trika/Tantraloka (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka vol 01 (Dyczkowski)/Tantraloka Dyczkowski v01 12 - Chapter One, part 11#^pg454|p. 440]] ([pdf](<Library/Trika/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka translation/Dyczkowski - Tantraloka, vol. 01.pdf#page=454>))
> Dyczkowski numbers this verse 1.265cd–266ab.
>
> (If, for example, we take the inference: ‘on this mountain there is fire because there is smoke’,) the enunciation is (simply) ‘this mountain’. The object to be defined is ‘there is fire because there is smoke’. Examination (in this case) is the conscious analysis (vijñānajāla) of the (invariable) concomitance (vyāpti) (between the terms of the inference) and the rest. Or else (analogously), with respect to postulation (as, for example, ‘this animal, which is similar to a cow, is called a wild cow’), the enunciation is the initial (notion) ‘this (animal)’ and the definition is ‘that which is similar to a cow is called a wild cow (gavaya)’. The examination (concerns) the rest (of the features of the animal). Similarly, (the same may be said of, for example, the statement: “he who desires heaven should sacrifice with Agniṣṭoma.”) The enunciation (in this case is) such: ‘he who desires heaven’, its definition ‘should sacrifice’, and the examination the rest, that is, ‘with Agniṣṭoma etc’. (with 1.264–266)
