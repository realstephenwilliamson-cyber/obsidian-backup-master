**1.** There are eight requirements in the making of *tefillin*. All of them are halachot transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai[^1] and, therefore, it is necessary to fulfill them all. If one deviates with regard to any of them, the [*tefillin*] are unacceptable.[^2] They are:  
a) The *tefillin* must be square[^3] and must be sewn closed in a square.[^4] [Both] diagonals[^5] must be equal,[^6] and thus all four angles will be equal.[^7]  
b) The leather of the head [*tefillin*] should have a *shin* embossed on both its right and left sides.[^8]  
c) The passages should be wrapped in fabric.[^9]  
d) A hair should be wound around that fabric. Afterwards, they should be placed in their compartment.[^10]  
e) They should be sewn [closed] with the sinews [of an animal].[^11]  
f) The leather compartment in which they are placed should have a place for the straps to pass through so that they can be moved through the [*tefillin*'s] handle.[^12]  
g) The straps should be black.[^13]  
h) The knot with which they are tied should be the renowned knot that is formed like a *dalet*.[^14] ^3-1

**2.** How are the head *tefillin* made? We take a cubic wooden block.[^15] [It need not, however, be a perfect cube]. If its height is [slightly] more or less than its width, it is of no consequence. We are required to take care only that its width and length are alike.[^16]  
Three grooves are carved into it so that four projections will be made as depicted.[^17] Leather is taken[^18] and soaked in water, and then, the mold is placed within it.[^19] The leather is inserted in between the grooves.[^20]  
While [the leather] is still wet, it is plucked and squeezed[^21] until the shape of a *shin*[^22] with three heads[^23] is formed on the right side of the *tefillin* as they will be worn, and the shape of a *shin* with four heads[^24] is formed on the left side of the *tefillin* as they will be worn.[^25] ^3-2

**3.** The leather is then left on the mold until it dries, and then it is removed. Thus, the leather will be [formed into a block] with four empty compartments.[^26]  
One of the passages from the Torah is placed[^27] in each compartment,[^28] and then a portion of the leather is folded over beneath them,[^29] and then they are sewn closed on all four corners.[^30]  
Within this lower piece of leather, a place should be left for the straps to be inserted,[^31] like a handle.[^32] It is called a *ma'aboret*.[^33] ^3-3

**4.** How are the *tefillin* of the arm made? We take a wooden block whose length is equal to its width[^34] and is a fingerbreadth[^35] - or slightly more or slightly less[^36] - high, and place wet leather around it.[^37]  
The leather is left on this mold until it has dried, and then it is removed. The four passages[^38] are deposited in the place left by the mold.[^39] A portion of the leather is folded over beneath them, and then they are sewn closed on all four corners.[^40] A piece of leather, like a handle, should be left for the straps [to be inserted].[^41] ^3-4

**5.** What is the order of the passages? For the head *tefillah*, the final passage, *V'hayah im shamo'a*,[^42] is placed in the first compartment on the right side of the person putting on the *tefillin*. *Shema* is placed next to it. *V'hayah ki y'viacha* is placed in the third compartment next to *Shema*, and *Kadesh Li* is placed in the fourth compartment, on the left side of the person putting on the *tefillin*.[^43]  
Thus, a person who is facing the person wearing the *tefillin* will read them in the following order.[^44] If their order is altered, they are not acceptable.[^45] ^3-5

**6.** [The passages for] the arm *tefillin* are written on four columns[^46] on a single piece of parchment[^47] like a Torah scroll,[^48] according to the order in which these passages are found in the Torah,[^49] in the following manner:  
If[^50] they were written on four separate pieces of parchment and placed in the same compartment, one fulfills one's obligation. There is no need to glue them together.[^51] ^3-6

**7.** When the passages[^52] - both of the head and the arm *tefillin* - are rolled closed, they should be rolled from the end to the beginning,[^53] so that were the passage to be rolled open, it would be possible to read each portion from the beginning to the end.[^54] ^3-7

**8.** Before the passages are placed in their compartments, they should be wrapped in a fabric,[^55] and hair should be wound around them. Afterwards, they may be placed in their compartments.[^56]  
This hair should be from a kosher species of animal or beast.[^57] Even when these animals died without being ritually slaughtered or were *treifah*,[^58] [their hair is nevertheless acceptable].[^59] It has already become a universally accepted custom to wind hair from the tail[^60] of a calf[^61] [around these parchments].[^62] ^3-8

**9.** When the *tefillin* are sewn closed, they may be sewn only with sinews[^63] from a kosher species of animal or beast.[^64] [Sinews taken from] animals which died without being ritually slaughtered or which were *treifah* [are nevertheless acceptable].[^65]  
It is customary to take sinews from the heels of kosher animals and beasts. They are white in color. If they are too firm, they are softened by [pounding them with] stones and the like until they become like flax.[^66] Afterwards, they are spun and twisted into threads[^67] and used to sew together *tefillin* and the sheets of Torah scrolls.[^68] ^3-9

**10.** When the *tefillin* are sewn closed,[^69] they should be sewn as a square.[^70] It is a widely accepted practice for there to be three stitches on each side, so that there will be twelve stitches in all.[^71] This applies for both the arm *tefillin* and the head *tefillin*. If, however, one made ten or fourteen stitches, there is no difficulty.[^72]  
For each of the stitches, the thread[^73] must pass through from both sides.[^74] ^3-10

**11.** The groove between [each of the compartments] of the head *tefillin* should reach the stitches [which sew the *tefillin* closed].[^75] [Nevertheless,] if the groove is discernible, so that the [division into] four compartments is openly visible, [the *tefillin*] are acceptable even if the groove does not extend until the stitches.[^76] If, however, the groove is not discernible, [the *tefillin*] are not acceptable.[^77]  
It is necessary to pass a thread or cord through each of the grooves on [the outer side of] the leather [compartments] to separate between the compartments.[^78] It is common custom to pass one of the sinews used to sew [the *tefillin* closed] between each of these three grooves.[^79] ^3-11

**12.** How are the straps made? We take leather straps[^80] [at least] the length of a barley-corn in width.[^81] If they are wider than that, they are acceptable. The length of the straps of the head *tefillin* should be sufficient to surround the head, tie the knot,[^82] and extend on either side of the head until they reach the navel or slightly above it.[^83]  
The length of the strap of the arm should be sufficient to surround the forearm,[^84] tie its knot,[^85] and extend[^86] until it can be wound three times around the middle finger and tied.[^87] If the straps are longer than this, they are acceptable.[^88] ^3-12

**13.** One places the straps through their handle,[^89] leaving space for the [circumference of] one's head, and ties[^90] a square knot, which resembles a *dalet*.[^91] Every Torah scholar should learn how to tie this knot.[^92] It is impossible to describe this knot in writing. Rather, it must be seen.[^93]  
The straps of the hand *tefillin* should be tied with a knot that resembles a *yud*.[^94] This knot should[^95] allow the strap to pass through it so that it can be widened or narrowed while one is tying the *tefillin* on one's arm. ^3-13

**14.** The outer surface of the straps of both the head and the arm *tefillin* must be black.[^96] This is a halachah transmitted to Moses on Mount Sinai.[^97]  
In contrast, with regard to the inner surface, since it faces the inside, it is acceptable if it is green or white.[^98] One should not make this [side of the straps] red, since it will be embarrassing for him if they become overturned.[^99]  
The back of the straps should be the same color as the compartment;[^100] if it is green, they should be green; if it is white, they should be white.[^101] It is attractive for *tefillin* to be entirely black, the compartments[^102] and the entire strap.[^103] ^3-14

**15.** The leather used to cover the *tefillin*[^104] and from which the straps are made should come from a kosher species of animal, beast, or fowl. Even when these animals died without being ritually slaughtered or were *treifah*, [their hides are nevertheless acceptable].[^105] If, however, leather from a non-kosher species[^106] was used or if they were covered with gold, they are not acceptable.[^107]  
The leather used for the straps must be processed with the intent that it be used for the mitzvah.[^108] In contrast, the leather used to cover the *tefillin*[^109] need not be processed at all.[^110] It is even acceptable if it is made from *matzah*.[^111] [Indeed,] this is the practice in many communities.[^112] ^3-15

**16.** *tefillin* may be made only by a Jew, since making them is equivalent to writing [the passages],[^113] because of the *shin* [embossed] in the leather [compartment] mentioned above.[^114] Therefore, if they were made by a gentile or sewn closed by him, they are unacceptable.[^115]  
Similarly, they may not be made by any others whose writing [of the passages] is not acceptable.[^116] ^3-16

**17.** A head *tefillah* may not be made into an arm *tefillah*,[^117] but an arm *tefillah* may be made into a head *tefillah*,[^118] because an article should not be lowered from a higher level of holiness[^119] to a lesser one.[^120] Similarly, the strap of a head *tefillah* should not be used for an arm *tefillah*.[^121]  
When does the above apply? After one has worn them. However, if head *tefillin* have never been worn, one may make them into arm *tefillin*.[^122] How is this done? One drapes leather around them until they become a single [compartment] and [then, one can] tie them on his hand.[^123] ^3-17

**18.** [The following laws apply when] the stitches of the *tefillin*[^124] become torn: If two stitches which are located next to each other become torn, or three stitches become torn even though they are not located next to each other, [the *tefillin*] are unacceptable.[^125]  
When does the above apply? With regard to old [*tefillin*]. With regard to new [*tefillin*], however, if their base remains intact, they are acceptable.[^126] [*tefillin* are considered] to be "new" as long as the leather remains strong and does not tear when one takes hold of a portion of the leather where the stitch was torn and hangs the *tefillin*. If the leather is not fit to hang the *tefillin* because it will tear, the [*tefillin* are considered] "old."[^127] ^3-18

**19.** Should a strap be torn, [the pieces] should not be tied or sewn together.[^128] Rather, it should be removed and entombed,[^129] and another one [substituted for it].[^130]  
The remnants of [torn] straps are not acceptable[^131] unless their length and width meets[^132] - or exceeds - the minimum requirements.  
At all times, a person should be careful that the external surface of the straps faces upward[^133] when he ties them on his arm and head.[^134] ^3-19
