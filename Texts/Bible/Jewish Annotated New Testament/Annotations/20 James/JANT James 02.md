### 2.1–7: Favoring the rich

*[[NRSVue James 02#^2-1|Text]], p. 493* ^2-1

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-1|1–4]]:** [[Torah 48 - Shoftim#^16-19|Deut 16.19–20]], “You shall not judge unfairly: you shall show no partiality; you shall not take bribes, for bribes blind the eyes of the discerning and upset the plea of the just. Justice, justice shall you pursue, that you may thrive and occupy the land that the Lord your God is giving you,” is one of several passages in the Tanakh admonishing judges to not favor one litigant over another. *Midr. Tanh*. on this passage explains: “Do not say this one is rich while this one is poor . . . this one \[morally worthy yet financially destitute\] should sit beneath \[me\], and do not have it that the poor stand and that the rich sit . . . God stands with the poor and not with those who oppress them.” *Assembly*, Gk “synagogue.” The NRSV tends to translate it as “synagogue” when non-Jesus-followers are involved, and “assembly” when Jesus-followers are in view. The root Aram sense expressed here is “bei knishta” or a place of assembly, like Heb “beit knesset.” James describes a building used for judicial hearings as well as for study, sermons, Torah reading, and punishing (see [[2 Corinthians 11#^11-24|2 Cor 11.24]]). While some scholars have questioned the use the synagogue for prayer in the first century, casual references in Josephus (*Ag. Ap.* 1.209–211) and Philo (*Flaccus* 120–123) suggest that prayer was also a function of the institution, especially in the Diaspora. The Gk word “proseuche” (see [[Acts 16#^16-13|Acts 16.13]]) designates the same institution for the same purposes. (See L. I. Levine, “The Second Temple Synagogue: The Formative Years,” in *idem*., \[ed.\] *The Synagogue in Late Antiquity* \[Philadelphia: ASOR, 1987\] 13–14.) ^2-1-2

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-5|5]]:** *Chosen the poor . . . heirs of without acts of favoritism* c Gk *Sit under my footstool* d Gk *brothers the kingdom*, although a Jewish interpretation (*m. Sanh.* 10.1; see *m. Ber.* 2.2) of [[Isaiah 60#^60-21|Isa 60.21]] lies behind the concept of inheriting the world to come (Heb “olam ha-ba*”*) this reference to “kingdom” suggests familiarity with the Jesus traditions ([[Matthew 05#^5-3|Mt 5.3]]; [[Luke 06#^6-20|Lk 6.20]]) in referring to the eschaton. See also [[Matthew 05#^5-5|Mt 5.5]] and compare [[Psalms 037#^37-11|Ps 37.11]] on the meek inheriting the land. According to *m. Sanh*. 10.1, “all Israel,” with few exceptions, have a share in the next world. ^2-5

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-6|6]]:** *Drag you into court*, [[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^23-3|Ex 23.3]] and [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-15|Lev 19.15]] warn judges against favoring the poor (or rich) in a lawsuit. ^2-6

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-7|7]]:** *Blaspheme the excellent name*, a possible reference to “ḥillul ha-Shem*”*—publicly making hollow or desecrating the divine name ([[Torah 31 - Emor#^22-32|Lev 22.32]]), in this case, “Elohim,” which at times connotes “judges” ([[Torah 18 - Mishpatim#^22-7|Ex 22.8]]). *B. Yoma* 86b classifies this sin as grievous beyond expiation. ^2-7

### 2.8–13: Wholeness under the law

*[[NRSVue James 02#^2-8|Text]], p. 494* ^2-8

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-8|8]]:** *Royal law*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], cf. *Midr. Sifra* to [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]], which calls this commandment “the supreme rule” that is served by all of scripture’s tenets. (See “The Concept of Neighbor,” p. 645.) For [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-18|Lev 19.18]] see also [[Matthew 22#^22-39|Mt 22.39]]; [[Mark 12#^12-31|Mk 12.31]]; [[Luke 10#^10-27|Lk 10.27]]. ^2-8-2

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-9|9]]:** *Partiality*, favoring one law or litigant and neglecting another. *Convicted by the law*, [[Torah 30 - Kedoshim#^19-15|Lev 19.15]]. **10–11**: *Fails in one point . . . transgressor*, the sentiment that to break one is to break all is unique for any properly explained Jewish text; perhaps James is thinking of [[Torah 50 - Ki Tavo#^27-26|Deut 27.26]]: “Cursed be he that confirms not all the words of this law to do them. And all the people shall say, Amen.” *T. Sebu*. 3.6 offers an almost identical list of transgressions and concludes: “\[Who is a complete lawbreaker?\] . . . Even he who does one transgression denies the One who commanded it.” Like James, *Midr. Pitaron Torah Kedoshim* claims one fulfills “love of neighbor” every time one refrains from breaking an ethical injunction in Torah. ^2-9

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-11|11]]:** *You shall not commit adultery* \[[[Torah 17 - Yitro#^20-13|Ex 20.13]]\], *. . . You shall not murder* \[[[LXX Exodus 20#^20-15|Ex 20.15]]\]. ^2-11

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-12|12]]:** *Law of liberty*, see 1.25n. The point of the law is to free humanity from the domination of evil lures. As one chooses, so God rewards and punishes. According to Kinyan Torah (now connected to *m. Avot,* as 6.2), the only free people are those who engage in Torah study. ^2-12

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-13|13]]:** *For judgment will be . . . no mercy*, rabbis called this “measure for measure,” and it is the major principle of divine justice (see [[Sanhedrin 90#90a|*b. Sanh*. 90a]]), already appearing in the Tanakh (e.g., [[Torah 13 - Shemot#^2-20|Ex 2.20–23]]). *Mercy triumphs over judgment*, although the expression *mercy conquers* (Heb “kovesh”) *judgment* (*Sifre Num*. 134) appears with a proof text from [[Micah 7#^7-19|Mic 7.19–20]] alongside many similar rabbinic teachings, these passages do not express James’s central point. His point concerns the moral background of the one seeking divine mercy. The best rabbinic parallel is found in *Gen. Rab*. 33: “Woe to the wicked who turn around the power of mercy (Heb “raḥamim”) to the power of severe judgment (Heb “din”) (e.g., the unmitigated violence perpetrated by, and in turn the divine wrath executed upon, the generation of the flood) . . . Fortunate are the righteous who turn around the power of severe judgment to the power of mercy” (e.g., Noah by feeding every living thing on the ark). ^2-13

### 2.14–26: Faith as expressed in works

*[[NRSVue James 02#^2-14|Text]], p. 494* ^2-14

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-14|14]]:** *Faith*, for James a mental construct entailing theological certainties, or creeds. For James, these creeds must be shown by obedience to revealed law and innate moral notions. ^2-14-2

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-15|15]]:** *Naked . . . food*, see [[Isaiah 58#^58-7|Isa 58.7]]: “Is it not to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover them, and not to hide yourself from your own kin?” ^2-15

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-18|18]]:** *I by my works will show you my faith*, only by doing good works, especially at the cost of personal deprivation, can one demonstrate trust in God’s system. This section can be seen as a correction of those who understood Paul ([[Romans 04#^4-5|Rom 4.5–6]]) as dismissing the importance of works. James mentions only ethical rather than ritual laws but we cannot draw any conclusions about his personal behavior from his silence on the ritual laws. ^2-18

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-19|19]]:** *Even the demons believe*, belief in the existence of the one God. ^2-19

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-21|21–24]]:** Abraham is *justified* (lit., “made righteous”) *by works* in his willingness to sacrifice Isaac (see Gen 22). *Our ancestor Abraham . . . Isaac on the altar*, that is, Abraham our father (Heb “Avraham avinu”), the epithet is found everywhere in Jewish writings, ancient and modern. Like James, the thirteenth-century Spanish commentator Nahmanides *(Commentary* on [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|Gen 15.5–6]]) observes that [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-15|Gen 22.15–17]] completes the demonstration of Abraham’s faith so Abraham could now receive the promise of numerous descendants. In [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-5|Gen 15.5–6]] Abraham is promised“ ‘Look toward heaven and count the stars, if you are able to count them . . . so shall your descendants be.’ And he believed the Lord; and the Lord reckoned it to him as righteousness.” When Abraham was told to sacrifice his only son Isaac ([[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-2|Gen 22.2]]) he somehow believed God would still give him many descendants from this son. After binding Isaac to an altar he is told in [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^22-16|Gen 22.16–18]], “Because you have done this, and have not withheld your son, your only son, . . . I will make your offspring as numerous as the stars of heaven . . . by your offspring shall all the nations of the earth gain blessing for themselves, because you have obeyed my voice.” He has no doubt he would have descendants, and this promise was confirmed because he acted to obey the test. The repetition of this promise underscores James’s point that faith is demonstrated in works. See also [[LXX 1 Maccabees 02#^2-5|1 Macc 2.5]] and Philo, *Abr.* 262, which both preceded James on this interpretation. Although Paul ([[Romans 04#^4-18|Rom 4.18–23]]; [[Galatians 03#^3-6|Gal 3.6]]) and apparently before him Nehemiah ([[Nehemiah 09#^9-7|Neh 9.7–8]]), did not see Abraham’s obedience as having any bearing on the claim of [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]]—that by his belief (Heb “emunah”; Gk “pistis”) was his justification (Heb “hatsdakah”)—this does not suggest the interpretation would be unacceptable to Jews. The French commentator Rashi (eleventh century) in his commentary to [[Torah 03 - Lech Lecha#^15-6|Gen 15.6]] likely held that view. ^2-21

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-23|23]]:** *Scripture* . . . *reckoned to him as righteousness . . . friend of God*, see [[Isaiah 41#^41-8|Isa 41.8]]; and [[II Chronicles 20#^20-7|2 Chr 20.7]]. The Palestinian targumic tradition (*Tg. Neof.* 1, *Frg. Tg. Ps. -J.* ) and Philo (*On Sobriety* 56) explained [[Torah 04 - Vayera#^18-17|Gen 18.17]] (God confiding his plans for Sodom and Gomorrah) by recourse to this friendship (“Shall I hide from Abraham what I am about to do”). ^2-23

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-25|25]]:** *Rahab the prostitute*, [[Joshua 02#^2-8|Josh 2.8–11]]. According to [[Megillah 14#14a|*b. Meg.* 14a]]-b, she married Joshua; see also [[Matthew 01#^1-5|Mt 1.5]]. ^2-25

**[[NRSVue James 02#^2-26|26]]:** *Body . . . spirit*, see [[Torah 01 - Bereshit#^2-7|Gen 2.7]] (human life is dependent on spirit/breath). For the rabbis, the body needs the soul to live ([[Berakhot 10#10a|*b. Ber*. 10a]]; *b. Moʾed Qat*. 28b–29a). ^2-26
